# Lessons learned and design guidance: manifest positivity and the finite-horizon programme

Claude (Fable 5.1), for Teddy. 11 September 2026. A lessons-learned and design document written at the close of the storage-depth v0.3 work, after the barrier theorem and ladder conjecture were drafted (`NOTE_barrier_theorem_and_ladder_conjecture_20260911.md`) and the depth strategy was discussed (`NOTE_depth_strategy_20260911.md`). This is in the event that we move on from the current project formulation and consider alternative approaches. It is meant to be read before brainstorming a possible next formulation: it records what the finite-horizon programme established, why its coordinates do not make Weil positivity manifest, which parts of it are structural and which are computed, how statements equivalent to RH should be handled, and what any replacement should be required to do. It is an opinion informed by the parts of the programme I have worked through in detail — the first-slab preprint, weil-depth, storage-depth, and the Ihara laboratory records — and less closely by the margin, string and defect-depth manuscripts.

## 0. Summary

The programme set out to find a formulation in which Weil positivity is manifest. The finite-horizon operator formulation is not one. In its coordinates `Q_{0,L}` is the compression to `L^2(0,L)` of an indefinite symbol; positivity is a compression effect; and the reason the compression is positive lives on the other side of the explicit formula, in the zero measure, which the prime-side operator never sees. The certificates are true and useful; they are verifications, not explanations. The one mechanism in the programme that produces positivity — Suzuki's support lemma for shifts `omega > 1/2` — is the Euler product in operator clothing and yields only the trivial zero-free region; the shift-energy identity transfers positivity but does not create it.

This is a characterization of a coordinate system, not a failure of the idea, and it leaves behind concrete design criteria. Recommendation: freeze the finite-horizon work as a complete body of method results (certified positivity through `L_2`, the barrier theorem, the ladder conjecture with its test, and an exact account of what certificates can see), stop investing in depth, and return to the drawing board with the criteria of Section 6 and the experiments of Section 7.

## 1. Where the programme stands

Certified: strict positivity of the shifted first-slab forms `Q_{omega,L}` for `L <= log 2`, `0 <= omega <= 1/2` (first-slab preprint, Theorem B, with the shift-energy identity as Theorem C); two-sided enclosures of `lambda_min(Q_{0,L})` at `L = log 2, ..., log 7` and `9/5` (weil-depth v0.4); and at `L_q = (3/4) log 14` and `L_2 = log(56)/2` by the residual-controlled continuation with 96-mode slabs (storage-depth v0.3). The eigenvalue ladder runs from `1.3e-3` at `log 2` to `1.9e-30` at `L_2`.

Understood: the dimension needed by any certificate that treats the arithmetic operator separately from the gamma part is `N > pi L e^{lambda_max(T_L)}`, with `lambda_max(T_L)` growing like `8 e^{L/2}/L` (barrier theorem; `N > 689` at `L = 3`, `36400` at `L = 4`); a certificate at horizon `L` constrains zeros only below height about `e^{2L}`, so no reachable horizon says anything about zeros beyond what is known; and `-ln lambda_min(L)` is linear in `e^L` across nine horizons and 27 orders of magnitude (`11.4 e^L - 17`, residual `+-1`), each new integer under the horizon costing the same factor whether or not it is a prime power.

## 2. Why positivity is not manifest in these coordinates

The clearest way to see the situation is the multiplier representation. For `f` supported in `(0,L)` the truncation in the translation operators is automatic in the quadratic form, so the arithmetic operator is `T_L = P_L M_{w_L} P_L` with `w_L(tau) = 2 sum_{n<e^L} Lambda(n) n^{-1/2} cos(tau log n)`, the sharp-cutoff partial sum of `-2 Re zeta'/zeta(1/2 + i tau)`, and the whole form is the truncated Wiener–Hopf operator `Q_{0,L} = P_L M_{g - w_L} P_L` plus a rank-two pole term, where `g(tau) = Re psi(1/4 + i tau/2) - log pi`.

Four facts about this representation, all now checked, together say that positivity is invisible on the prime side.

First, the symbol `g - w_L` is indefinite: negative near `tau = 0`, where its value is `-5.4 - W_L` with `W_L ~ 4 e^{L/2}`, and negative again at every Kronecker return of the prime sum, which are not rare — the pointwise inequality `g >= w_L` fails up to `tau ~ 1.4e6` at `L = 3`. Positivity of `Q_{0,L}` is therefore a property of the compression, an uncertainty-principle effect, not of the symbol.

Second, the explicit formula says exactly what the compression does: on test functions of exponential type `L/2` the indefinite prime-side symbol agrees with the positive measure `2 pi sum_gamma delta_gamma`. That is Weil's criterion in this language, and it makes the source of positivity explicit — the reality of the zeros, and nothing else. When a prime power enters, the prime-side symbol changes by `O(1)` at every frequency while the zero side does not change at all. A formulation in which positivity is manifest would have to show that this `O(1)` change is harmless; the finite-horizon operator has no vocabulary for that.

Third, the near-null vectors are functions tuned to vanish at the low zeros (transform of size `1e-28 ... 1e-18` at the first thirteen zeros at `L_q`), with the residual `3e-29` carried by the endpoint-generated tail at heights 85–130. Any manifestly positive decomposition of `Q_{0,L}` would have to exhibit these vectors as almost annihilated by every positive piece, which is to say it would have to encode the low zeros implicitly. The ladder makes this quantitative: the certified eigenvalues follow `exp(-11.4 e^L)`, so positivity in these coordinates is "barely true" in a precise sense, and a structure that made it manifest would have to make an `e^{-kappa e^L}` sampling constant natural.

Fourth, the continuation experiments confirm the diagnosis from the other side. The residual-controlled step (`R*F^{-1}R <= theta H_J`, direct-floor lemma) has the right shape for an inductive argument — relative, dominated by the old form, losing only a bounded factor — and its premise at each step, `M_theta >= m`, turned out to be a fresh positivity statement every time, not a consequence of the previous one. The barrier theorem then shows that the arithmetic operator has purely essential spectrum, so no finite head can lower its top, and the only way past the resulting dimension wall (a frequency-resolved prolate certificate) buys one more unit of `L` at the cost of a redesign. Neither observation is about `zeta`; both are about the coordinates.

## 3. What is structural in the programme, and what it does

The shift-energy identity `I - V_omega^* V_omega = 2 int_0^omega V_s^* Q_s V_s ds` is a genuine identity and a good one; the C1/C2 protocol correctly placed it among Lyapunov and dissipation identities (KYP, Redheffer). Its role is to transfer positivity: given `Q_s >= 0` on `(0, omega)` it delivers strict contractivity, hence the zero-free half-plane. It does not produce positivity of `Q_s`, and the whole difficulty is `Q_s >= 0`.

Suzuki's support lemma (contractivity of `H_{omega,a}` for `omega > 1/2`) is the one place positivity is manifest, and it is the Euler product: the half-plane `Re s > 1` is zero-free because the product converges absolutely, and in operator terms because the kernel's support forces strict contractivity. This is real structure, but it is the trivial region. Between `omega = 1/2` and the critical line the programme has certificates (first slab), margins (psi-omega-margin), and an inverse-spectral family with an RH endpoint (omega-string); as far as I have seen, none of these carries a mechanism that moves positivity inward in `omega` or outward in `L`. The classical inward move, de la Vallée Poussin's `3 + 4 cos theta + cos 2 theta`, is itself a manifest-positivity trick and gains only `c / log t`; it is a useful measure of how much a trick of that kind can buy.

The Ihara laboratory points in the same direction from below. For finite graphs the RH analogue (the Ramanujan property) is sometimes false, and the laboratory found what one expects: a detector (non-Stieltjes entries in the string) that fires when the property fails. The graphs where the property is a theorem — Lubotzky–Phillips–Sarnak — get it from Deligne's theorem on the automorphic side. The inverse-spectral formulation detects positivity; the extra structure proves it.

## 4. Statements equivalent to RH: how to treat them

Three points from the discussion of the ladder conjecture belong on record, because they bear on what a redesign should look for.

Positivity of `lambda_min(L)` for every `L` is RH (Weil's criterion; `lambda_min` is non-increasing in `L`, so positivity for all large `L` suffices). The ladder conjecture asserts a rate, so it contains RH. Its lower-bound half implies RH; under RH its weak form (`e^L <~ -ln lambda_min <~ L e^L`) is plausibly provable from sampling theory, making the weak form an effective Weil criterion; only the sharp form — the power of `L` and the constant — says something RH does not. The conjecture is RH plus a rate, and the two parts do not help each other: the direction carrying RH's difficulty (rate implies positivity) is trivial, the direction requiring work (positivity implies rate) is conditional analysis. The analogy is Li's criterion and the asymptotic `lambda_n ~ (n/2) log n`.

None of this means RH-equivalent statements are unprovable. Weil proved the Riemann hypothesis for curves over finite fields by proving a positivity statement, the Castelnuovo–Severi inequality, made manifest by intersection theory, and Deligne's proof of the Weil conjectures is again positivity made manifest by structure. A structural inequality such as `Q_{0,L}[f] >= c_L (|f(0)|^2 + |f(L)|^2)`, or a relative coupling bound for the near-null subspace, is not a lemma one can prove independently — the uniform family is the theorem — but it is a possible *form* of the theorem, and the question is whether its inductive step `P(L) => P(L+h)` can be proved. Nothing rules that out; what is known is that the one obvious derivation of the step, Cauchy–Schwarz for the enlarged form, presupposes the conclusion.

The ladder does guide such a search in the way scaling exponents guide an induction on scale: the inductive hypothesis must carry a floor `e^{-kappa e^L}`; the step must be relative rather than norm-based (norm-based Schur arguments lose `O(1)` against a floor of `1e-29`); the loss must be the same whether or not a prime power enters; and the per-unit-height cost is a constant. What it cannot supply is the content of `P(L)`, because the content is the zeros.

## 5. Lessons learned

*On what numerics can decide.* Certified finite-horizon computations can settle three kinds of question: whether a specific finite object is positive (they did, through `L_2`); how a family of such objects scales (the ladder, the barrier); and what a certificate at a given horizon can see (zeros below `~e^{2L}`). They cannot decide whether positivity is manifest; that is a property of the formulation, visible from the structure of the form before any computation. The multiplier representation of Section 2 could have been written down on day one and would have predicted the dimension wall, the ladder's height scaling, and the collapse of the continuation premises. Lesson: before certifying anything, write the form as explicitly as possible and ask where positivity would have to come from; compute only what that analysis cannot settle.

*On certificates versus explanations.* A certificate is a proof of a finite statement and an explanation of nothing. The storage-depth experience — floors understated by four orders of magnitude, a continuation whose premises were positivity itself, a second step that failed for reasons that had nothing to do with the mathematics — is what a certificate-driven programme looks like when the structure is absent: every step is a fresh verification, and improvements are improvements in bookkeeping. Lesson: when successive certificates get harder without a structural reason becoming visible, the coordinates are being verified, not the theorem.

*On the value of a negative result.* The barrier theorem, the "what the certificate sees" computation, and the ladder are the most informative products of the depth programme, and all three are negative in the sense that they bound what the method can do. Each was cheap once the right question was asked, and each redirected effort more than any positive step did. Lesson: budget for the question "what is the ceiling of this method, and why" early, and treat a sharp answer as a result to be written up, not as a disappointment.

*On sharpness as a signal.* The v0.3 arithmetic weight bounds turned out to be within 2–4 % of the true `lambda_max(T_L)`, and the Legendre gamma-tail floor within `log 2` of the diagonal value. When the analytic bounds in a certificate are already nearly sharp and the method still cannot proceed, the obstruction is structural, and effort spent sharpening bounds is wasted. Lesson: measure the slack in every analytic ingredient against the true quantity before deciding where to work.

*On the ladder as a fingerprint.* The nine enclosures cost weeks of computation and their most useful output is a two-parameter law. Any candidate formulation can be fingerprinted the same way at small horizons in double precision in an afternoon: compute the bottom of its spectrum at a few horizons, see whether the decay is `exp(-kappa e^L)`, and read off whether the low zeros are being encoded. A formulation in which positivity is manifest should either show no such collapse or explain it. Lesson: use the ladder as a cheap first test of any new system before building certified machinery for it.

*On test benches.* The Ihara laboratory is the most reusable asset in the programme: a family of systems in which the RH analogue is sometimes a theorem, sometimes false, and always checkable. It separates "detects positivity" from "proves positivity" mechanically. Lesson: keep it, and run every candidate structure through it before investing.

*On process.* The referee-style review rounds, the independent recomputation of constants, the drafts-versus-archive convention, and the large-files policy all worked; the one recurring process failure was letting the numerical work run ahead of the structural question it was meant to answer. Lesson: pair every numerical campaign with a written statement of which structural question it decides and what answer would stop it.

## 6. Design criteria for a replacement formulation

The finite-horizon programme leaves behind five tests that any new formulation should pass before it is worth developing.

1. It must reproduce the trivial zero-free region manifestly. The present formulation does (Suzuki's support lemma); a replacement that cannot recover the Euler product has lost ground.

2. It must have a mechanism by which each prime contributes a piece that is positive, or dominated, rather than an indefinite `O(1)` change of a symbol. This is where the present coordinates fail, and it is the same obstacle Connes–Consani met after the archimedean place — the first-slab theorem is the finite-horizon twin of their archimedean positivity, and the finite places are the open problem in both settings.

3. It must make the ladder natural. A sampling constant of size `exp(-kappa e^L)` should appear as a concentration phenomenon (Slepian/Landau–Widom) rather than as a numerical accident. This points at prolate spheroidal structure and at de Branges spaces built on `xi`; Connes–Moscovici's prolate operator, whose ultraviolet spectrum reproduces the low zeros, is the nearest existing object.

4. It must pass the Ihara test bench: it should make the Ramanujan property manifest for the graphs where it is a theorem and fail to be manifest where the property fails.

5. Negative criterion: a formulation whose only route to positivity beyond the trivial region is a finite certificate has the same status as the present one, however different it looks.

A quick evaluation procedure for a new idea, in the order that saves the most effort: write the object as a quadratic form and identify where its positivity would come from; check the trivial region; ask what happens when one prime enters; fingerprint the spectrum at small horizons; run the Ihara or function-field twin; and only then ask what a finite certificate would see and cost.

## 7. Drawing-board experiments

Two experiments are cheaper than any further depth step and directly test whether the coordinates or the object are the obstacle.

*Function-field twin.* Run the finite-horizon Weil form for the zeta function of a curve over `F_q`, where RH is Weil's theorem, the zeros are `2g` points periodic in `tau`, and the arithmetic side is a sum over closed points. Ask whether, in these coordinates, positivity is manifest — whether the compressed operator has a visible sum-of-squares structure coming from the intersection pairing — or whether even there one sees only compression positivity with small eigenvalues and near-null vectors tuned to the zeros. If the latter, the coordinates discard the structure that makes Weil's proof work, and one learns exactly which structure that is. If the former, one learns what the analogue for `zeta` would have to look like.

*Spectral side as target.* The ladder analysis identifies the natural objects: Paley–Wiener spaces of type `L/2`, the zero set as a sampling sequence, and the decay of its sampling constant. The finite-horizon data can now calibrate a quantitative Hilbert–Pólya question — which operator built from primes has the low zeros as approximate spectrum with controlled error — and the ladder gives the error scale such an operator must achieve at each horizon. This is the direction in which positivity is manifest by construction (`Q = sum |F(gamma)|^2` for a self-adjoint operator with spectrum `{gamma}`), and the question becomes whether the prime side can be made to produce the operator rather than to verify its consequences.

Two smaller items. First, before the ladder conjecture is circulated, read Zhu's compact-window paper (arXiv:2608.24827, cited in the first-slab preprint) for its Landau–Widom decay law; it may already contain the ladder's upper-bound half or the law itself, and the conjecture should be stated relative to it. Second, formulate and test the candidate inductive hypotheses of Section 4 against the nine near-null vectors already computed (endpoint values against `sqrt(lambda_min)`, window masses at distance `log n` from the end, relative coupling to a slab); this shows which structural properties hold with room to spare, which is the only place a non-circular step could find slack.

## 8. Recommendation for the existing work

Freeze the finite-horizon programme as a body of method results: certified positivity through `L_2` with two-sided enclosures; the barrier theorem with its exact constant `lambda_max(T_L)` and the corrected dimension law `N_sep = pi L e^{lambda_max(T_L)}`; the ladder conjecture with its data, its sampling interpretation, and the `log 8` / `log 11` test; and the account of what a certificate at horizon `L` sees and cannot see. Replace `rho^+_L` by `lambda_max(T_L)` in storage-depth §6 and state the barrier there; do not pursue sharper arithmetic norm bounds or further depth beyond the planned `log 8` step, which is worth doing only as the ladder test.

Keep the framing honest in the manuscripts: the numerics study finite-horizon positivity as an extremal problem and develop certified methods; the RH-relevant content is the characterization of what the coordinates cannot do, and that characterization is what motivates the return to the drawing board.
