\[
 k=pq^2d_1t_1t_2=pq^2a,\qquad

## 3. Complete Fourier kernel, with the sharp row mask intact

Freeze a native presentation class, orientation, common aspect and fixed
ray factors. For (3), the row quotient is a primitive character on
\(\mathcal O_F/f\), times its retained fixed scalar phase:

\[
 \psi_u(k)\overline{\psi_u(k')}=\gamma(k,k')\beta(u),\qquad
 \beta=\overline{\chi_p}\chi_q
       \chi_{d_1}\chi_{t_1}\chi_{t_2}
       \overline{\chi_{d_2}\chi_{t_3}\chi_{t_4}}.
 \tag{8}
\]

A negative native orientation conjugates the whole displayed character.
Every character remains zero extended. At this pair no local difference
is zero modulo six, so its complete physical zero mask is exactly the
zero extension at every prime dividing \(f\). No canceled character is
silently replaced by one on a nonunit. The factor \(\gamma\) contains
the actual fixed ray and frozen norm-height phases; it is retained below.

Let \(Q=Nf\), choose a primitive additive character \(e_f\) on the
finite residue ring, and write

\[
 G_\beta(j)=Q^{-1/2}\sum_{x\bmod f}\beta(x)e_f(jx),\qquad
 \tau_\beta=G_\beta(1).
 \tag{9}
\]

Every local factor of \(\beta\) is nonprincipal and primitive. Elementary
finite Gauss algebra gives

\[
 G_\beta(j)=\overline{\beta(j)}\tau_\beta,\quad
 |\tau_\beta|=1,\quad G_\beta(0)=0.
 \tag{10}
\]

For the exact physical weight \(W_*(u)=\Omega(Nu/U)1_{\mathcal R_*}(u)\),
periodize its actual row lattice, including its native representative
class, by

\[
 W_f(x)=\sum_{u\equiv x\; (f)}W_*(u),\qquad
 \widetilde W_f(j)=\sum_{x\bmod f}W_f(x)e_f(-jx).
 \tag{11}
\]

Then the **complete kernel of this pair**, without smoothing or removing
any row predicate, is exactly

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal H_{W_*}(k,k')=
  \frac{\gamma(k,k')\tau_\beta}{\sqrt Q}
  \sum_{j\bmod f}\overline{\beta(j)}\widetilde W_f(j).}
 \tag{12}
\]

This is finite Fourier inversion of the periodized sharp weight, not a
radial lattice Poisson formula. It needs no smoothness of the conductor
or defect predicate. Its cost is that the complete unknown mask Fourier
coefficients remain in (12). Additional finite native classes have their
own periodizations and fixed factors and are summed without changing them.

CRT retains all opposite-valuation Gauss coupling. Under the explicit
integer residue conventions used in the finite checker, write
\(Q_i=Q/N\mathfrak p_i\). Its phase is

\[
 \tau_\beta=\prod_i\beta_i(Q_i)\tau_{\beta_i}.
 \tag{13}
\]

For a native additive character the CRT projection is the same finite
operation, with its actual local additive characters; the numerical
integer formula is not an assertion identifying those phases with the
source's self-dual lattice convention. Formula (12) retains
\(\tau_\beta\) whichever compatible convention is used. In particular
the opposite factors at \(p,q\) leave a nonzero primitive Gauss mode,
rather than annihilating one another. One must not replace their phases
by their positive Gauss norms when summing over varying labels.

The required new information is covariance of
\(\widetilde W_f(j)\) with the normalized character mode
\(\beta(j)/\sqrt{\varphi(f)}\), and subsequently cancellation of
these covariances against the actual annular coefficients and Gauss
phases over the entire pair block. Ordinary Parseval only gives
\(|\mathcal H|\le\sqrt{\varphi(f)}\|W_f\|_2\), which does not close
the requested moment. Reintroducing a smooth unfiltered row ball would
change (11) and require an independently paid signed comparison.

There is a quantitative warning against a uniform arbitrary-mask claim.
On one complete residue period take the nonnegative mask
\(W_f(x)=1_{\beta(x)=1}\). The character is surjective onto sixth
roots of unity, so

\[
 \sum_x W_f(x)=\varphi(f)/6,\qquad
 \sum_xW_f(x)\beta(x)=\varphi(f)/6.
 \tag{14}
\]

All physical zeros remain. Thus primitive nonprincipal characters and
opposite local valuations do not force cancellation for arbitrary sharp
weights. The checker verifies (10)–(14) in genuine products of finite
split-prime residue fields. **The mask in (14) is not the physical
\(W_*\).** This disproves a uniform arbitrary-mask inference; it proves
no coherent bias or power obstruction for the actual residual.

## 4. A weaker sufficient target: high-plain inverse mass

Keep the actual full zero/profile bin \(\mathcal C\), common witnesses,
whole physical prime product \(Q_I\), and original inverse. Suppose the
source simultaneous witness rows \(\mathcal W\subset\mathcal C\) obey

\[
 |M_uS_u|^2\ge cU^{d(r+m)-\lambda_1},\qquad
 |Q_I(u)|^2\ge cU^{2G_I-\lambda_2}.
 \tag{17}
\]

Record literal pointwise costs rather than absorbing them in an epsilon:

\[
 |M_u|^2\le C U^{dr+e_M}H^{b_M},\qquad
 |S_u|^2\le C U^{dm+e_S}H^{b_S}.
 \tag{18}
\]

Here the source all-length/profile, height, and nonprincipal hypotheses
remain imported. No lower bound on either individual response is assumed
in (17). Dividing the product bound by the inverse **upper** bound gives
the legitimate necessary condition

\[
 |S_u|^2\ge (c/C)U^{dm-\lambda_1-e_M}H^{-b_M}
       =:\mathfrak s_{\min}\quad(u\in\mathcal W).
 \tag{19}
\]

Define the actual high-plain restricted inverse mass

\[
 \mathcal J=\sum_{u\in\mathcal C}|M_uQ_I(u)|^2
                   1_{|S_u|^2\ge\mathfrak s_{\min}}.
 \tag{20}
\]

This uses the original inverse and all original slots. It is not an
imported marked inverse theorem on a newly favored coefficient class.
The source inverse moment only gives \(\mathcal J\ll U^{1+\varepsilon}H^b\).
The new arithmetic request is

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal J\ll U^{1-g+\varepsilon}H^b.}\tag{21}
\]

On a witness row, dividing (17) by the plain upper bound in (18) gives

\[
 |M_uQ_I(u)|^2\ge(c^2/C)
 U^{dr+2G_I-\lambda_1-\lambda_2-e_S}H^{-b_S}.
\]

Consequently (21) proves the complete count implication

\[
 \#\mathcal W\ll
 U^{1-dr-2G_I-g+\lambda_1+\lambda_2+e_S+\varepsilon}H^{b+b_S}.
 \tag{22}
\]

The desired gain can therefore be tapered to the actual count deficit:

\[
 g\ge\max\{0,1-R_{\rm goal}-dr-2G_I+
                         \lambda_1+\lambda_2+e_S\},
 \tag{23}
\]

with a strict reserve for the mesh, capacity, union, and eventual height
conversion costs. Negative right-hand sides need only the old inverse
mass, not a new correlation theorem. Common separating parameters and
the event in (20) must be controlled uniformly before rowwise witness
selection; Poisson on the sharply gated event is not asserted.

For the ideal slot gain \(2G_I=dx(1-r)\), let
\(\eta=1/5000\) and \(R_{\rm new},r_{\rm new}\) have their existing
two-rebalance definitions. Their exact identity yields

\[
 A_I(r)-R_{\rm new}
 =\eta-d(1-x)(r-r_{\rm new}),\qquad
 A_I(r)=1-d[x+(1-x)r].
 \tag{24}
\]

Thus the required gated inverse-mass gain before losses is
\(\max\{0,\eta-d(1-x)(r-r_{\rm new})\}\), not a uniform
\(1/5000\) over the entire application wedge. The actual slot defect
\(dx(1-r)-2G_I\) and the losses in (23) must be added literally.
For \(r-r_{\rm new}=0,\eta/[2d(1-x)],\eta/[d(1-x)]\), the ideal
required gains are respectively \(\eta,\eta/2,0\). The checker
verifies this identity with exact rational arithmetic across the hard
\((d,x)\) box. It also reproduces the exact available count reserve
\(169987/20124130000\) from the uniform saving, which is only about
\(8.45\cdot10^{-6}\). The literal witness and height costs cannot
be paid out of an unrelated fourth-route reserve.

This is a genuinely weaker sufficient obligation than a full mixed
moment. For example, a full second-moment bound with saving \(s\)
implies (21) with gain approximately
\(g=s-\lambda_1-e_M\), with the gate constants and height factors
retained. The reverse implication fails. In a positive-measure model,
place inverse mass \(U^{1-g}\) on rows with
\(|S|^2=U^{dm}\), and mass \(U-U^{1-g}\) on rows with
\(|S|^2=U^{dm}/2\). A gate at \(3U^{dm}/4\) satisfies (21),
but the full mixed moment remains at least
\(U^{1+dm}/2\), with no power saving. This is a logical example,
not a native character counterexample. It explains why controlling
all plain-weighted inverse mass can be more than the detector needs.
For a gate with a small positive power width, the outside contribution
can still limit the full mixed saving to that width.

The direct witness count is weaker still. If one can prove the actual
count on \(\mathcal W\), there is no need to prove (21). Source detector
coverage over all witness subdivisions, physical slots, common aspects,
heights and the remaining bin classes is required in every version.

## 5. Research decision and verification

arithmetic remains the most developed local application, while geometry
consolidation is the first near-term theorem audit. The stronger conclusion
that the fourth route is now close to an estimate is unsupported. The
new native block survives its current column cuts, and its complete
sharp transform returns an unknown coherent covariance. Another partition
or a positive Gauss norm would not resolve that covariance.

The first mixed application checkpoint should be the weaker target in
item 1. The fourth-route block scout in item 2 remains worthwhile only
when it can supply an actual estimate beyond the existing envelope:

1. A uniform high-plain inverse-mass estimate (21), or the direct witness
   count, with the tapered loss budget (23) on the buffered application
   wedge and positive whole-slot products.
2. A power bound for a complete varying-label block of (12), retaining
   its actual annular ownership sums and sharp periodization, followed by
   an explicit sum over the complementary allocations in (15). A finite
   Gauss calculation or a fixed-label identity alone does not qualify.

If the next analytic scout returns only the generic envelope or an
uncontrolled character-mode projection, narrow the fourth investigation
and give more effort to the weakest count target and the short-family
cross-product alternative. No new estimate here justifies downgrading
those alternatives or claiming a boundary improvement.
