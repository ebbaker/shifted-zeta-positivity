     \ll_\varepsilon U^{1+dr-1/540+\varepsilon}H^b.

 \tag{1}
\]

## 2 The remaining signs after the gcd decomposition

The [ratio-core note](10_MOBIUS_RATIO_CORE_FOURTH_20261009.md) writes

     \text{ squarefree and pairwise coprime}.
\]
Their good ratio radical is \(\mathfrak f=\mathfrak a\mathfrak b\),
their Möbius product is \(\mu_F(\mathfrak f)\), and their physical
character product retains \(|\psi_u(\mathfrak g)|^2\).
This gives exactly
\[
 F_{J,>1/2}
 =D^{-1}\sum_{\substack{\mathfrak f\ {\rm squarefree}\\
                         \mathrm N\mathfrak f>U^{1/2}}}
            \mu_F(\mathfrak f)\mathcal H_J(\mathfrak f),
 \qquad D=U^r,
 \tag{4}
\]
with the original selected fourth weight inside \(\mathcal H_J\).
The original annuli require balanced factor norms and
\(\mathrm N\mathfrak g\asymp D/\sqrt{\mathrm N\mathfrak f}\).
Each core sum is real by ordered-factor reversal.

Growing one-prime cores cannot meet annular balance. Balanced two-prime
cores do meet it and attain the existing absolute column-pair count
exponent, even with the actual squarefree inverse support. Their raw
pair count is \(\gg D\sqrt V/(\log V)^2\) below a ratio cap \(V\).
This is a sharpness result for the absolute pair majorant; it is not a
lower bound for the selected energy.

The complete-family positive inverse sieve also loses the required

fourth target by \(0.1218625343738\ldots\). The available pointwise

The next direct proof task is therefore the signed estimate
