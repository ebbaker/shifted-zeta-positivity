## 1. The exact count statement and the missing reuse package

Write $B_{\rm fam}$ for an input boundary for the family actually used,
including its required reciprocal or direct growth statements. A zeta-only
boundary $B_\zeta$ is a different hypothesis. Write $d=2a-1$ for the
zero-bin parameter; an admissible witnessed bin has $d\le2B_{\rm fam}-1$.
This $d$ is distinct from an outer physical exponent in $U=Z^\omega$.

For the original whole-slot product, suppose the simultaneous witness has

\[
 |M_uS_u|^2\ge cU^{d(r+m)-\lambda_1},\qquad
 |Q_I(u)|^2\ge cU^{2G_I-\lambda_2}.
\]

An energy bound $E_{\mathcal C}\ll U^{1+dm-s+\varepsilon}H^b$
then gives

\[
 \#\mathcal W_{r,m,I}\ll
 U^{1-dr-2G_I-s+\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\varepsilon}H^b.       \tag{1}
\]

Relative to the old count exponent $R_*$, its actual count improvement is

\[
 \Delta_{\rm count}=R_*-1+dr+2G_I+s-\lambda_1-\lambda_2.   \tag{2}
\]

For a requested count $R_{\rm new}$, the precise condition is

\[
 s\ge1-R_{\rm new}-dr-2G_I+\lambda_1+\lambda_2.             \tag{3}
\]

The ideal rebalancing has $R_{\rm new}=R_*-F_x\eta$,

\[
 F_x=\frac{(5/6-d)(2-8x/9)}
 {(5/6-d)(3-17x/9)+d(2-8x/9)(1-x)}.
\]

On the current hard box, $F_x\eta\ge5456/50310325$ at
$\eta=1/5000$. This is approximately $0.00010845$, before the
application's remaining losses. Formula (3), rather than that ideal
number alone, governs actual slots. The energy is over the full fixed bin;
the lower witness need only hold on the subset being counted. A proposed
bound for an artificially witness-selected energy would change the task.

To reuse the current detector recipe at a new boundary, the following
package must be proved at that boundary. These are obligations for this
recipe, not claims that every possible RH proof must have this form.

| Obligation | Exact scope needed |
| --- | --- |
| Analytic input family | The Hecke functions, quotient characters, finite presentations and growth controls used by reflection and kernel estimates; a strip for zeta alone does not replace these. |
| Operational moments | The actual bins, permitted derivatives, profiles, physical slots and all required lengths; (3) must survive slot rounding and witness losses. |
| Complete bin coverage | The mixed box plus the existing proofs for its complement, floor bins, small rows, exceptional characters and terminal ranges. |
| Detector recombination | One must repeat the outer high estimate with the improved witness count and both rebalances. With $U=Z^\omega$, a count saving becomes $Z^{\omega\Delta_{\rm count}}$ inside that estimate, not automatically a shift of the global boundary by $\Delta_{\rm count}$. |
| Strict global contradiction | Both complete high and low estimates must lie strictly below the retained nonzero common signal, with the principal correction, normalizer and Euler domain justified at the proposed output boundary. |
| Feedback closure | The conclusion must concern the same required family and growth package, or a separate theorem must restore missing growth before another application. |
| Height quantifiers | Internal polynomial height orders are fixed before the eventual height choice and external tail orders. Every target height is covered; a bounded-height count is not zero exclusion. |

These conditions yield a map only after the global estimates have been
established. A boundary map $F(B)<B$ needs a non-stalling property, for
example continuity and strictness on the relevant interval, or a positive
gain on every compact interval above $1/2$. Constants and thresholds may
depend on a fixed stage. One need not impose uniform constants over an
infinite number of stages: each fixed forbidden half-plane can be excluded
at a finite valid stage. Conversely, one fixed improvement at $7/8$ is
not such a map.

## 3. What a bounded native-to-canonical comparison can prove

The [short-family sieve note](../../short_families/notes/6_SHORT_FAMILY_SEXTIC_SIEVE_BARRIER_20261008.md)
already records the intended construction. The mixed manuscript correctly
keeps this comparison inside its imported input. The following lemma
makes its precise defining premise and its elementary consequence explicit.

**Comparison lemma.** Fix $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$, a good set outside
a fixed $S$, and compatible residue-symbol data. Require the native
denominator symbol on good primes to be the globally oriented standard
symbol $\chi_p(t)=(t/p)_6$, or its one common conjugate. Fixed native
ray factors are retained separately. Choose canonical construction data
with a global generator $m_E$ of every representative ideal $E$.
Assume the native and canonical expressions are the finite-order inducing
Hecke characters specified by those definitions, with their prescribed
zero extensions. Then for every good sixth-power-free ideal $v$ and a
chosen physical generator $v_0$, there is $\varepsilon_v\in\mu_6$ such
that on every good ideal column $n$,

\[
 \chi_n(v_0)=\chi_n(\varepsilon_v)\chi_v^{\rm DF}(n).       \tag{6}
\]

For fixed physical-unit, bounded bad-part and finite native-ray choices,
the same identity has a fixed additional column factor. It transfers the
canonical positive-square sieve to the native sixth-power-free physical
rows, with only fixed finite splitting costs and no temporary-prime mask.

**Proof.** In the canonical decomposition write

\[
 v=(x)E\mathfrak j^6,\qquad x\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak c}.
\]

Since the Eisenstein ring is a PID, choose a generator $j_0$ of
$\mathfrak j$. Equality of principal ideals gives

\[
 v_0=\varepsilon_v x m_E j_0^6.
\]

Away from the finite primes introduced by this chosen decomposition,
multiplicativity of the compatible residue symbols removes the sixth
power and gives (6). This identifies the inducing Hecke characters:
their quotient is trivial on every prime outside a finite set, hence on
every ray class. For the last assertion, each ray class has an ideal
representative avoiding any prescribed finite set, by the usual
approximation property for ideals; factor that representative into
primes outside the set. Thus equality extends across every additional
unramified good prime. Both original zero extensions vanish precisely
when $(n,v)\ne1$ on good columns, so (6) also holds there. Temporary
decomposition primes never become permanent deletions.

Split the rows into the at most six values of $\varepsilon_v$. On each
class, $b_n\chi_n(\varepsilon_v)$ is a fixed column coefficient vector,
with squared norm at most $\sum|b_n|^2$; enlarge the row class only after
forming its nonnegative square. Fixed physical units, bounded bad parts
and the stated finite ray factors are treated identically. Conjugating
the common orientation conjugates the whole coefficient vector. This
proves the asserted operator transfer. $\square$

This is a comparison proof conditional on the defining-character
identification, not a proof of reciprocity, primitive completion, or the
deep sieve. Directly checked [de Faveri source §§2.3–2.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04045v1#S2.SS3)
provides the canonical decomposition, construction, and fixed-class
reciprocity convention. Its [Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04045v1#S1.SS1)
is an imported positive-square estimate on sixth-power-free indices.

The exact outstanding native dictionary is:

\[
 \psi_t(n)=\nu_\rho(n)
 \left((t/n)_6\right)^{s_\chi}
 \quad\text{on every good column, with the original zeros}.             \tag{7}
\]

Here $\rho$ must range over a fixed finite list, $\nu_\rho$ must be
independent of the moving row within its class, and $s_\chi$ must be one
common orientation. The mixed manuscript states this as imported native
data; it does not specify and verify the complete source-to-symbol
dictionary. Exact order six or equality of conductor norms alone cannot
prove (7): changing the orientation separately at moving good primes
retains those local orders while changing the family. A source-scoped
verification of (7), its fixed exclusions and its zero convention would
retire this bounded transfer assumption. An explicit fixed class-factor
formula is sufficient; one does not need a new large-sieve theorem.

For an additional sixth-power physical factor $r^6$, the actual factor
is $1_{(n,r)=1}$. For each fixed $r$, this is a fixed column mask;
summing the canonical bound at row radius $H/(Nr)^6$ gives exactly the
already proved physical operator

\[
 H+XH^{1/6}+H^{5/6}X^{1/3}+H^{1/3}X^{5/6}.
\]

A growing frozen endpoint enters $b_n$, whose norm is explicitly paid.
It is not a twist that demands a new constant uniform in growing
conductor. This comparison does not allow the row-dependent quadratic
factor in a joint $pq^2$ sum to be frozen as a column coefficient.

## 4. Plain fourth scope and source-choice order

The [selector-preserving source audit](../../../quasi-rh-character-amplification/notes/SELECTOR_PRESERVING_SUBCASES_20261008.md),
§1, records a material distinction:

| Input | Permitted physical coefficient scope |
| --- | --- |
| Marked inverse moment | Bounded row-independent coefficients on disjoint physical prime lists, common character and orientation, and the two fixed strict width margins. |
| Plain fourth and buffered prime envelope | Original smooth full physical prime annuli with the fixed exclusions; ray restrictions represented by fixed finite linear combinations of characters in $\Theta$; permitted physical norm twists and derivative profiles; disjoint row-independent windows and the source mesh. |

Arbitrary bounded coefficients, or finite-ray coefficients restricted to
arbitrary deleted prime subsets, do not license the second row of this
table. Its source proof uses the smooth all-prime logarithmic-derivative
estimate. All whole-slot subsets keep their original coefficient arrays;
no fractional last slot or moving selected prime list is introduced.

For a positive legal subset $J$, the present capacity is

\[
 2m+\frac92z_J<1
\]

with the required support/decrement margins. The primitive row must lie
outside the fixed exceptional group $\Theta$; the current high-conductor
bin ensures this for sufficiently large $U$. The empty subset uses the
separate zero-slot assertion. The sharp selected bin is allowed by
positive-norm restriction, not by inserting its indicator into complete
smooth Poisson.

The local records establish the *existence and order of choice* of a
sufficiently fine source mesh, chosen before the fixed number of slots;
they do not supply an explicit numerical value for every source mesh
parameter. No numerical candidate should claim source-mesh certification
solely from $2m+(9/2)z_J<1$. Record the source admissibility parameters
or keep this as a stipulated analytic input. For an existence theorem,
one may choose the fixed mesh smaller than both the source's permitted
mesh and the explicit rounding budget; a numerical value for the source
mesh is unnecessary if its existence and order of choice are valid.
The profile-aware supply
test uses $\ell/(h+\zeta)>1/5$ with a fixed margin; $7/37$ suffices for
the older scalar crossing and is not silently interchangeable with the
stronger optimized-profile supply condition.

Real losses, capacities, mesh, slots and geometry are chosen before the
target. Allowed target-specific finite characters and excluded sets are
then fixed, followed by internal seminorm/height orders, the eventual
height exponent, and finally external tail orders. All signal and physical
sum exclusions must agree. No uniformity over all target conductors is
claimed by this arrangement.
