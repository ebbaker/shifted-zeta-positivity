## The precise quasi RH input

Assume [the linked October 5 paper, Theorem 1.1, printed page 2](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-October-5-2026/paper2.pdf):
every finite-order Hecke L-function over K=Q(sqrt(-3)), hence every
Dirichlet L-function and zeta, has no zeros in Re s>11/12. This is stronger
than zeta-only quasi-RH. Its reference to 7/8 concerns another result;
that stronger bound is not part of this assumption.

Keep three inputs separate: the zeta strip, the full family strip, and
quantitative moment/reciprocal-growth estimates. A width uniform in
conductor and height does not supply every prescribed conductor loss,
selector or coefficient class needed by a moment argument. For fixed
characters, equation (3.3), printed page 12, supplies a smooth Möbius
bound with exponent 11/12+epsilon. Proposition 3.1, printed page 11,
uses rows up to D^(1+vartheta), 0<vartheta≤1/10. It does not give the
shorter-row estimate proposed below. The paper's internal iteration
reduces a dual row parameter; it is not an iteration of zero-free

## Arithmetic contraction and a concrete first step

For the actual sextic-character preparation over K, write

\[
 A_u(D)=\sum_{(n,S)=1}\mu_K(n)\nu(n)\chi_n(u)W(Nn/D).
\tag{1}
\]

Here S is a fixed finite exclusion set, W is a smooth compact annular
profile, and all physical zero extensions remain. The weakest useful
initial version can fix ν=1 and S: it would improve the Dedekind zeta
function, whose factorization ζK=ζ L(s,χ−3) transfers zero-freeness in
Re s>1/2 to zeta. A family theorem for every ν would be stronger, and
is not necessary just to obtain RH by this route.

The proposed new estimate is

\[
 \sum_{0<Nu\le D^h}|A_u(D)|^2
 \ll D^{1+h+a+\epsilon},\qquad 0<h<1,
\tag{2}
\]

with the required finite derivative norm of W, every permitted detection
profile, every epsilon>0 and all large D. Constants may depend on the
fixed character and the displayed parameters; no moving-conductor
uniformity is silently assumed.

Suppose the prior same-character smooth bound is
|A1(L)|≪L^(theta+epsilon). The physical sixth-power rows u=p6 reproduce
the unit-row response. Exact omission of p is paid using this prior
bound; keeping that payment gives the
[short-family extraction](../../../notes/CODEX_CONTINUATION_20261008.md)

\[
 \theta_{\mathrm{out}}
 =\max\left\{\frac{1+a}{2}+\frac{5h}{12},
              \theta\left(1-\frac h6\right)\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

The second term is the omitted-prime cost, not an optional error.
If a<2theta−1, balancing gives

\[
 h_*=\frac{6(2\theta-1-a)}{5+2\theta},\qquad
 T_a(\theta)=\frac{\theta(6+a)}{5+2\theta}.
\tag{4}
\]

At theta=11/12 and a=0 the first request is h=30/41:

\[
 \boxed{\sum_{0<Nu\le D^{30/41}}|A_u(D)|^2
  \ll \|W\|_{C^k}^2D^{71/41+\epsilon}.}
\tag{5}
\]

If (5) is proved with the complete profiles and masks, and the paper's
(3.3) is granted or its fixed-character consequence established, the
starting bound yields theta_out=33/41. This is a conditional payoff;
(5) has not been proved. Restricting a longer row sum retains its old
upper bound and does not prove (5). If one grants only the zero-free
statement, the fixed-character reciprocal-growth and contour implication
used for the starting smooth bound must also be supplied; the paper's
equation (3.3) offers a direct quantitative starting input.

For e=theta−1/2, the exact contraction is

\[
 T_a(\theta)-\tfrac12
 =\frac{5e+a(e+1/2)}{6+2e}.
\tag{6}
\]

Thus a=0 contracts the excess by less than 5/6. More generally,
0≤a≤lambda(2theta−1), with fixed lambda<1, contracts it by at most
(5+lambda)/6. A fixed positive a instead has fixed point (1+a)/2.
One fixed h also has a positive extraction floor. To reach RH, the
estimate must survive as h* decreases to zero, or supply a strict
improvement at the positive optimal exponent by another argument.
The starting thresholds and constants may vary between stages; what
matters is a valid all-scale theorem at each stage.

The [fixed-scale variance criterion](../../../fixed_scale_descent/notes/1_FIXED_SCALE_DESCENT_CRITERION_20261008.md)
is an alternative formulation. Under the project's imported attained
optimal-exponent theorem, proving
Eδ(X)≤q Eδ(X/b)+C X^(−eta), q<1, at every positive admissible δ
would contradict a positive optimal exponent. No uniform constants as
δ decreases are needed for that contradiction. A recurrence through
X^alpha instead of X/b may supply only a logarithmic gain.

The decisive arithmetic ingredient is cancellation across the complete
actual response. The
[coherent cofactor controls](../../../short_families/notes/28_GROWING_NORM_COMPENSATED_COFACTOR_BARRIER_20261009.md)
show why cancellation of selected pieces or a positive sieve cannot be
promoted to (2). In the short h limit, the unit row itself already has
near-RH-strength consequences. The explicit criterion locates the hard
estimate; it does not bypass it.

## Theta correlations near a possible Newman threshold

The [stationary-sign theorem](5_STATIONARY_SIGN_CRITERION_AND_THETA_TARGETS_20261010.md)
requires, on the relevant open predecessor domain,

\[
 H_x=0\Longrightarrow HH_{xx}\le0,
 \qquad H_{xx}=0\Longrightarrow H_xH_{xxx}\le0.
\tag{7}
\]

It covers every finite multiplicity. The physical conditions cannot
be replaced by stationarity of a normalized finite sum. Their
[complete-source dictionary](7_COMPLETE_LAGUERRE_KERNELS_AND_TRIPLE_ZERO_LIMIT_20261010.md)
uses m_t(u)=e^(tu²)Φe(u), A=2H and

\[
 J_{j,t}(s)=\int_{\mathbb R}r^2(r^2-s^2)^j
 m_t(s+r)m_t(s-r)\,dr,
\]
\[
 \mathscr L_j(H;x)=H_{j+1}^2-H_jH_{j+2}
                 =\widehat J_{j,t}(2x).
\tag{8}
\]

Consequently the target is Fourier nonnegativity of J0 on Hx=0 and
J1 on Hxx=0. It is enough to prove those signs near all possible
positive thresholds and their earlier critical branches; positivity
at every time and height is stronger than the exclusion theorem needs.
Global positive definiteness of J0 and J1 is an attractive alternative
if a natural source decomposition appears, but is not a consequence of
pointwise density positivity.

The [bounded theta certificates](6_PAID_GENUINE_THETA_STATIONARY_SIGNS_20261010.md)
now calibrate each sign on a nonempty stationary set in a different
rectangle. They add no collision coverage because simpler first-jet
facts already exclude there. The high-cell third-jet Cauchy cost of
about 472 is paid and remains an obstacle to nonvacuous huge-height
S1 tests. The genuine J1 kernel is negative at a source coordinate
while its Fourier response is positive on the low-height rectangle.
This focuses the task on signed Fourier correlation, not pointwise
source positivity.

Nor can the first sign replace the second: every analytic exact triple
has locally nonnegative first Laguerre expression. Ordinary collisions
are regular folds, so IFT transversality and passive coordinates do
not eliminate them. These are established limitations from Notes 4–7.

With theta=11/12, the zeta functional equation gives initial heat strip
half-width a=2theta−1=5/6. The
[de Bruijn strip theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438#S3.Thmtheorem2)
gives Λ≤a²/2=25/72. This ceiling is weaker than
[Polymath's established Λ≤0.22](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438#S1.Thmtheorem1),
so the linked strip supplies no new numerical heat bound.
[Rodgers–Tao's Λ≥0](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05914), together with the
threshold property, identifies the RH endpoint as Λ=0.

The parent imports large-height reality and local Hermite splitting:
a positive threshold produces a finite real multiple zero. For every
epsilon>0, possible thresholds epsilon≤T≤0.22 lie in a bounded height
region. The
[parent paid interface](../../newman_collision_reductions.tex)
permits a sufficient rectangle epsilon≤t≤0.22, |x|≤exp(64/epsilon).
A first-jet certificate can cover that closed rectangle directly.
Using (7) instead requires an earlier-time and spatial buffer around
its boundaries. Such exclusion gives Λ<epsilon; RH needs this for
epsilon tending to zero. An increasing collection of disconnected
cells does not supply that limit.

Terminal all-realness gives genuine Laguerre positivity, but descent
subtracts a source:

\[
 \mathscr L_0(t)=P_{T-t}\mathscr L_0(T)
   -2\int_t^T P_{s-t}\mathscr L_1(s)\,ds.
\tag{9}
\]

Thus terminal positivity alone gives no lower bound at earlier times.
Finite attraction floors likewise improve a positive landing time
without contradicting a collision normal form. A new signed theta
estimate is required to reach the endpoint.

## The most concrete next use of the enlarged source

The full theta pair has more arithmetic structure than a generic
positive lift. The product-index identity in the appendix below groups

logarithmic shifts and prime-power moments. This is a complete source
identity, not a Fourier sign theorem.

The proposed next analytic lemma is a **paired Jacobi or Mellin transform
with complete matched endpoint and remainder bounds on one closed
shrinking-time sector**. Start with the actual product-index kernel,
retain J1's signed insertion, and impose stationarity only on the
complete physical readout. The transform must retain its modular
partner and every boundary source. A useful conclusion would be a
paid conditional Fourier bound or a complete first-jet lower bound,
rather than another unsigned energy identity.

The first-jet alternative remains valuable: a full-source residual
R≤1−delta and multiplier bound Y≤C N^alpha, alpha<5/8, would give
eventual exclusion on the manuscript's stated shrinking sector.
But the [sharp residual theorem](2_SHARP_ADJOINT_RESIDUALS_AND_RESTRICTED_PRIME_OBSTRUCTION_20261010.md)
already shows unrestricted pointwise transport equals the scalar
observation defect; retaining only primes 2 and 3 has a large-scale
obstruction. Improving certificate algebra without a new uniform
source correlation supplies no arithmetic gain.

A sensible first promotion test is quantitative control of the paired
representation on the complex contours actually required by its
oscillatory estimate. Then test the resulting signed bound on a genuine
nonempty high-height Hxx stationary set, paying the full third jet.
Leading reflection preserves the old threshold quadratic, so any gain
must come from the matched correction or a new correlation. A contour
shift outside the existing small approximation disk needs its own

## Appendix Exact divisor pairing of the complete theta kernel

For every real v the raw theta series defining Φ extends to Φe(v).
This uses Jacobi gluing, not termwise positivity on the negative half-line.
Indeed let

\[
 k(v)=e^v[\Theta(e^{4v})-1],\qquad
 \Theta(y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\pi n^2y}.
\]

Jacobi gives k(−v)=k(v)+2sinh(v). Direct differentiation identifies
(k''−k)/16 with the defining Φ series. The operator annihilates
2sinh(v), so the result is even. Local uniform convergence licenses
the derivatives for every finite v.

For P≥1, n|P, m=P/n, define

\[
 d_n=\tfrac14\log(P/n^2),\quad z=2\pi P e^{4s},
\]
\[
 B_P(s,\rho)=\pi^2P^2e^{10s}
 [z^2-6z\cosh(4\rho)+9]e^{-z\cosh(4\rho)},
\]
\[
S_j(P;t,\rho)=\sum_{n\mid P}(\rho+d_n)^{2j}
                        e^{2t(\rho+d_n)^2}.
\]

The original kernel is

\[
 K_{j,t}(s)=\int_{\mathbb R}r^{2j}
 e^{t[(s+r)^2+(s-r)^2]}\Phi_e(s+r)\Phi_e(s-r)\,dr.
\]

Then, for fixed j≥0 and s≥0,

\[
 \boxed{K_{j,t}(s)=e^{2ts^2}\sum_{P\ge1}
             \int_{\mathbb R}B_P(s,\rho)S_j(P;t,\rho)\,d\rho.}
\tag{10}
\]

The proof expands the full n,m pair and sets r=rho+d_n. Both exponential
arguments become P exp(±4rho), and the polynomial product becomes
4π4P4e18s−12π3P3e14s cosh(4rho)+9π²P²e10s, giving B_P. Thus J0
uses S1 and J1 uses S2−s²S1, with no cross terms removed.

To justify the rearrangement, sum the absolute positive and negative
polynomial parts of each raw theta summand into Φabs(v). Elementary
Gaussian-sum bounds give Φabs(v)≤C exp(−v) for v≤0 and
Φabs(v)≤C exp(9v−π exp(4v)) for v≥0. On s≥0,

\[
 \Phi_{\rm abs}(s+r)\Phi_{\rm abs}(s-r)
 \le C\exp\{18(s+|r|)-\pi e^{4(s+|r|)}\}.
\tag{11}
\]

The positive argument is s+|r|; its double-exponential decay dominates
any negative-argument growth. The bound is integrable in s≥0 and r,
even after any fixed polynomial insertion, bounded-time heat weight
or bounded-complex-frequency Fourier factor. Tonelli/Fubini therefore
justify (10) and the half-line Fourier expansion. Use evenness only
after Jacobi gluing. Termwise integration over both negative original
arguments is not justified by this absolute envelope.

Finally the finite divisor generating function is

\[
 R_P(\lambda)=\sum_{n\mid P}e^{\lambda d_n}
 =\prod_{p^b\parallel P}
 \frac{\sinh((b+1)\lambda\log p/4)}{\sinh(\lambda\log p/4)}.
\tag{12}
\]

Zero denominators have their removable limits. Pairing n with P/n
makes R_P even. If τ(P) counts divisors and μq(P)=Σd_n^q, then

\[
 S_1(P;0,\rho)=\tau(P)\rho^2+\mu_2(P),
\]
\[
 S_2(P;0,\rho)=\tau(P)\rho^4+6\mu_2(P)\rho^2+\mu_4(P),
 \qquad
 \frac{\mu_2(P)}{\tau(P)}
 =\sum_{p^b\parallel P}\frac{b(b+2)(\log p)^2}{48}.
\tag{13}
\]

These are exact restrictions of the full integer preparation. They do
not imply positivity: B_P is negative for sufficiently large |rho|,
and the oscillatory Fourier phase remains. Their purpose is to supply
explicit arithmetic channels for the next signed estimate.
