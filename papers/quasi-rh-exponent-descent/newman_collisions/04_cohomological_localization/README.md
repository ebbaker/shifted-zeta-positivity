Question: can a protected superspace observation reproduce the genuine
heat function and retain a constraint on its collision jets?

- Enlarged state: a zero-dimensional quartet \(u,b,\psi,\bar\psi\), with
  \(Qu=\psi\), \(Q\bar\psi=ib\), \(Q\psi=Qb=0\).
- Action: \(\lambda Q[\bar\psi(F_t(u)-ib/2)]\), where \(F_t\) is the
  monotone Gaussian quantile map of the genuine normalized theta density.
- Reduction: integrate the fermions and then the real Gaussian \(b\);
  the remaining density at \(\lambda=1\) is exactly \(\rho_t\).
- Physical evolution: \(\partial_t\rho_t=(u^2-\mu_2)\rho_t\), with
  \(H_t=H_t(0)\mathbb E_{\rho_t}\cos(xu)\). The action and observation
  normalization move with time; they are not a fixed supersymmetric
  Hamiltonian semigroup.
- Domain: real bosonic contours, the stated ordered integral, smooth
  genuine theta densities, and compact real time/positive-\(\lambda\)
  intervals.
- Target: a protected completion or Ward relation that fixes a signed
  candidate jet combination.

[Scout note](notes/1_EXACT_THETA_SUPERSPACE_AND_REGULAR_COMPLETION_OBSTRUCTION_20261010.md)
provides the complete finite-dimensional action and reduction. It proves
that the second spatial jet changes strictly under the localization
deformation, and that a regular even completion retaining the cosine as
its bosonic \(b=0\) component cannot be \(Q\)-closed in this quartet.
A protected finite arithmetic spectator-sector embedding is also exact,
but imports the coefficients and supplies no arithmetic relation.
