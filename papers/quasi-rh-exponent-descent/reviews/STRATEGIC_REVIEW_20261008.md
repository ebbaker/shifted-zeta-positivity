**1. Short families offer the best main research target, with a substantial qualification.**

The strongest deduction is that one hypothesis
\[
\sum_{0<Nu\le D^h}|A_u(D)|^2
\ll_\varepsilon D^{1+a+h+\varepsilon}
\]
can be reused finitely to obtain the fixed-character response exponent
\[
\beta_{\rm out}=\frac{1+a}{2}+\frac{5h}{12}.
\]
It needs neither a prior strip nor the previously proposed supremum over
smaller column scales. The exact mask identity and the arbitrary-epsilon
quantifiers support this conclusion. To infer a zero-free boundary, the
moment must cover a profile class with no common Mellin zero in the relevant
half-plane; one arbitrary fixed profile is insufficient.
See the [extraction and detection argument](../short_families/notes/SHORT_FAMILY_DESCENT_REFINEMENT_20261008.md),
Sections 2–4.

The latest reduction does actual work: it removes the original diagonal,
uses the old positive theorem only on its valid complete blocks, and then
eliminates a further sector by a second Poisson sum. The remaining region is
\[
Nb<D^{1-h+\eta},\qquad
(Nf)^2N(m_1,m_2)<D^{h+2\eta},\qquad m_1\ne m_2.
\]
However, at \(b=f=1\) and coprime columns the decay parameter is \(1/H<1\).
The second Poisson estimate gives no saving there. Thus a smaller residual
has been identified, but no mechanism has yet made its difficult core easier.
The actual unit-column obstruction also prevents extending the old positive
dual theorem unchanged.
See the [small-cofactor note](../short_families/notes/SHORT_FAMILY_SMALL_COFACTOR_20261008.md),
Sections 3–6.

The first useful target should be chosen by its achievable loss, not solely
by how short its cofactor range looks. Beating \(7/8\) requires
\(a+5h/6<3/4\):

| Fixed \(h\) | Lossless output | Permitted loss to beat \(7/8\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(8/9\) | \(47/54=7/8-1/216\) | \(a<1/108\) |
| \(4/5\) | \(5/6\) | \(a<1/12\) |
| \(2/3\) | \(7/9\) | \(a<7/36\) |

The \(8/9\) choice has the smallest remaining \(b\)-range among these
examples, but a particularly tight loss budget. Merely extending a
lossless theorem a little below \(h=1\) does not beat \(7/8\): one must
reach \(h<9/10\).

Start with the actual \(b=f=1\), low-overlap signed form, including the
Gauss coefficients, masks, and smooth profiles. A saving in that core
would justify investing in the full remainder, but would not itself
prove its bound. The decisive target is equation (25) of the small-cofactor
note for one admissible pair \((h,a)\). Initially taking the original
character \(\nu=1\) avoids an unnecessary all-character objective while
still addressing \(\zeta_K=\zeta L(\,\cdot\,,\chi_{-3})\).

The qualification is important: fixed \(h>0\) has a floor above \(1/2\).
To reach RH, a new theorem must produce smaller \(h\) with losses tending
to zero, or conditionally produce successively smaller floors. Such
arbitrarily short-family hypotheses already approach RH strength from
the single row \(u=1\). The extraction is therefore a useful reduction,
not an automatic bootstrap from quasi-RH.

**2. The selected mixed energy is the best parallel target for a localized gain.**

The new transposition is legal because the smooth sum runs over complete
plain columns while the exceptional-row selector remains outside.
The bounded primitive-character fibers are meaningful arithmetic
information. Together with the imported growth estimates, they control
every cubic term with a repeated primitive character, with a certified
positive margin. These are stronger accomplishments than merely rewriting
the desired inequality.
See the [selected inverse note](../mixed_character_families/notes/SELECTED_INVERSE_DISTRIBUTION_20261008.md),
Sections 1–2 and 5, and the
[primitive-character audit](PRIMITIVE_CHARACTER_FIBER_REVIEW_20261008.md).

Nevertheless, the remaining signed three-character cycle is unbounded.
An entrywise estimate, even of generic square-root strength, does not
close the displayed absolute-Schur calculation. The finite character
frame explains why retaining phases matters; it is not evidence that
the actual selected Möbius weights have the desired cancellation.

I would prioritize the original energy on its actual all-ones column
vector before demanding the full cubic operator bound. The latter
controls more vectors than the application needs and can make the
research target unnecessarily difficult. A useful next result is a
signed estimate on a nonempty part of the still-uncontrolled region,
with the actual weights and profile/height requirements preserved.
Another diagonal or principal-character calculation would not resolve
the current remainder.

The payoff is presently localized near \(7/8\). The existing amplification
architecture does not support iteration to \(1/2\): its current high
estimate stalls near \(0.8749572\), its displayed low-envelope optimization
has floor \(13/15\), and its imported fourth-moment range cannot simply
be extended by lowering the input boundary.
See [Route C](../notes/CODEX_CONTINUATION_20261008.md#6-route-c-a-boundary-dependent-amplification-theory).
These are limitations of the present estimates, not impossibility results
for stronger arithmetic methods.

**3. Fixed-scale feedback is the cleanest zeta-only formulation, but not yet the most developed method.**

The recurrence
\[
E_\delta(X)\le qE_\delta(X/b)+CX^{-\eta},
\qquad b>1,\quad q<1,\quad \eta>0,
\]
really would improve the exponent. The complete scalar comparison has
remainder \(O(X^{-7/12})\), which leaves at least \(X^{-1/12}\) decay
after the relevant normalization. The error budget is therefore not the
main obstruction.

The missing result is a signed arithmetic estimate. Existing convolution
feedback preserves forbidden-zero residues, and a contraction at
\(X^\vartheta\) scales can yield only logarithmic savings. Neither
repeated substitution nor an improved constant supplies the needed
power. This route deserves priority if the question is specifically the
logical implication from a zeta-only strip, but the current evidence
does not establish a practical way to prove its covariance inequality.
See the [fixed-scale criterion](../fixed_scale_descent/notes/FIXED_SCALE_DESCENT_CRITERION_20261008.md)
and [arithmetic resonance analysis](../fixed_scale_descent/notes/ARITHMETIC_FEEDBACK_AND_RESONANCE_20261008.md).

**4. Heat flow is useful for certification, with the endpoint difficulty intact.**

The normalized construction correctly pays cutoff changes, controls an
analytic error on a full disk, and obtains derivative errors by Cauchy's
formula. Its consequence
\[
t\in[\varepsilon,1/2],\quad |x|\ge e^{64/\varepsilon}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (H_t(x),H_t'(x))\ne(0,0)
\]
is an effective version of known positive-time eventual simplicity,
derived from the [Polymath approximation theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438#S1.Thmtheorem3).
Even at \(\varepsilon=1/2\), the stated cutoff is \(e^{128}\).

No compact rectangle has yet been interval-certified. More fundamentally,
leading-pair dominance requires sufficiently large \(t\log|x|\), leaving
the shrinking-time region uncontrolled. A possible positive transition
can occur arbitrarily close to zero; the present theorem does not rule
that out. Improving the constant 64 would help computation but would not
resolve this quantifier problem.

One modest interval-certified rectangle, including a natural-cutoff
crossing, is a useful bounded next task. Further priority as an RH route
should depend on a new arithmetic lower bound for the normalized value
and derivative in the shrinking-time region.
See the [normalized criterion](../newman_collisions/notes/NORMALIZED_HEAT_COLLISION_CRITERION_20261008.md).

**5. The quadratic lift should receive less effort under the present inputs.**

The exact integer lift is sound and avoids an invalid integer-to-ideal
substitution. But the target implies an improved strip for every fixed
primitive quadratic character of odd conductor, not just zeta.
The actual prime-pair absolute majorant is \(\gg X^2\), above the desired
power. Even granting conductor-uniform individual response bounds,
combining them with the classical quadratic large sieve cannot give
descent. At \(B=7/8,h=7/5\), the missing weighted-energy saving is
\(7/40\), concentrated near conductors \(X^{1/4}\).

Furthermore, the currently justified conductor reduction uses \(1<h<2\),
whose extracted boundaries \(1/2+h/4\) remain above \(3/4\). This is a
limitation of that reduction, not of every possible quadratic-family
approach. Retain the exact lift, but resume sustained work only with a
new signed prime-correlation mechanism.
See the [quadratic barrier analysis](../integer_quadratic_lift/notes/QUADRATIC_MOMENT_STRENGTH_AND_BARRIER_20261008.md)
and the [integer lift](../integer_quadratic_lift/notes/INTEGER_QUADRATIC_RESPONSE_LIFT_20261008.md).
