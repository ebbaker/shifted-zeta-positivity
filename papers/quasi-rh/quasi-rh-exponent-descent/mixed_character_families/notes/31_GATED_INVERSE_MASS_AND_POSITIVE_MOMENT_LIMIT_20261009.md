## 1. The actual gate forces a stronger high-conductor domain

Write \(d=2a-1\), \(D=U^r\), \(N=U^m\), and keep the actual
common-character witnesses and original physical slot product \(Q_I\).
On the simultaneous witness set, assume

\[
 |M_uS_u|^2\ge cU^{d(r+m)-\lambda_1},\qquad
 |Q_I(u)|^2\ge cU^{2G_I-\lambda_2}.
 \tag{1}
\]

The inverse upper bound is
\(|M_u|^2\le C U^{dr+e_M}H^{b_M}\). Set
\(\tau=\lambda_1+e_M\), and retain the gate dictated by this
inequality, rather than assuming an individual witness lower bound:

\[
 \mathcal J=\sum_{u\in\mathcal C}|M_uQ_I(u)|^2
 1_{|S_u|^2\ge(c/C)U^{dm-\tau}H^{-b_M}}.
 \tag{2}
\]

The physical zero extensions, original inverse annulus, whole slot
profiles, and fixed excluded set are unchanged. A row whose polynomial
is forced to zero cannot pass a positive gate; no character is divided by
its value at a nonunit.

The imported conductor-sensitive bound, on this nonprincipal bin, is

\[
 |S_u|^2\le C_P U^{d\min(m,\kappa(u)-m)+e_P}H^{b_P},
 \qquad \kappa(u)=\log_U Q_{\psi_u}.
 \tag{3}
\]

The conductor is the primitive character conductor; it is not the
physical row norm or the norm of its sixth-power-free part. For a gated
row with \(\kappa<2m\), (2)–(3) give exactly

\[
 d(2m-\kappa)
 \le \tau+e_P+(b_P+b_M)\log_U H
             +\log_U(C_PC/c).
 \tag{4}
\]

Fixed native conductor factors and profile constants enter the last
term. The deleted Euler factors remain in the imported estimate; their
power/height costs belong in \(e_P,b_P\), not in a newly enlarged fixed
excluded set.

Let \(\ell=10^{-6}\). If the effective gate loss satisfies

\[
 \tau+e_P+(b_P+b_M)\log_U H\le\ell/4,
 \tag{5}
\]

then, after the eventual threshold for the fixed constants, every gated
row has

\[
 \boxed{\kappa(u)>2m-\ell/(2d)\ge2m-1/720000.}\tag{6}
\]

This holds uniformly for \(9/25\le d\le21/50\). If \(\kappa\ge2m\)
there is nothing to prove. Otherwise a violation of (6) would require
cost at least \(\ell/2\) in (4), while its right side is eventually
less than \(\ell/2\). In particular every row with
\(Q_{\psi_u}\le U^{2m-1/1000}\) is absent from (2) under (5).
This is a necessary condition of the actual detector gate, not an
arbitrary sector removal or an auxiliary smooth comparison.

Condition (5) is a real loss requirement. It is not established by
writing \(U^\varepsilon\), or by reusing a previously fixed height
power. The source permits selecting smaller buffers and a height power
after the fixed internal derivative orders. One must allocate the
available real budget to \(\lambda_1,e_M,e_P\) and then choose the
height exponent so that (5) holds. The statement is conditional on that
allocation, uniformly in the common separating parameters and permitted
derivative/profile family. No evaluated detector manifest is supplied.

\[
 |M_uQ_I(u)|^2\le
 C U^{dr+e_M+\Gamma_K-2dm+2\tau}H^{b'}

Summing first on the physical gate and only then enlarging the positive

## 3. Two nonempty paid marginal strips in the buffered wedge

Use the existing two-rebalance quantities \(R_{\rm new},r_{\rm new},
m_{\rm new}\), put \(a_r=r-r_{\rm new}\), \(b_m=m_{\rm new}-m\),
and retain the operational inequalities

\[
 a_r<\frac{\eta+\ell}{d(1-x)},\quad
 b_m> -\frac{\ell}{dB_x},\quad b_m\le a_r+\ell,
 \qquad\eta=1/5000,\quad B_x=2-8x/9.
 \tag{12}
\]

The ideal marginal counts have the exact differences

\[
 A_I(r)-R_{\rm new}=\eta-d(1-x)a_r,\qquad
 P_{\rm plain}(m)-R_{\rm new}=dB_xb_m.
 \tag{13}
\]

These alone are not whole-slot or loss-paid estimates. For those, use the
source original slot construction with widths at most \(h\le\ell/4\)
and the required moment mesh. Choose \(I\) as the positive decreasing
prefix just below \(z_M-\ell/2\), where \(z_M=(1-r)/2\).
Its actual shortfall is \(t_I\le3\ell/4\), with first inverse margin
at least \(\ell\) and the second margin bounded away from zero. Choose
\(J\subset I\) just below \(z_P-\ell/8\), where
\(z_P=2(1-2m)/9\); it has shortfall \(t_P\le3\ell/8\) and strict
plain capacity margin at least \(9\ell/16\). The existing supply proof
gives enough positive original slots for both choices. No slot is split
or its physical prime support altered.

The decreasing-prefix gain is at least its mean gain \(qz=dxz\).
Thus, including every physical rounding loss, the actual witness counts
satisfy

\[
 \#\mathcal W\ll
 U^{A_I(r)+d t_I+L_M+\varepsilon}H^b,
 \qquad
 \#\mathcal W\ll
 U^{P_{\rm plain}(m)+d t_P+L_P+\varepsilon}H^b.
 \tag{14}
\]

Here \(L_M\) includes the product-witness and slot lower losses, plain
upper cost, source inverse cost, finite witness cover, and any chosen
height conversion. \(L_P\) includes the **twice** charged gate loss,
slot lower loss, fourth cost, finite cover and height conversion.
These are count-level costs, distinct from the gate-localization budget
(5). Suppose separately that \(L_M,L_P\le\ell/4\).

Then each of these nonempty strips of (12) is already paid:

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\frac{\eta+3\ell/4}{d(1-x)}\le a_r
          <\frac{\eta+\ell}{d(1-x)},
 &&\#\mathcal W\ll U^{R_{\rm new}-37\ell/200+\varepsilon}H^b;\\
 &-\frac{\ell}{dB_x}<b_m\le-\frac{3\ell}{4dB_x},
 &&\#\mathcal W\ll U^{R_{\rm new}-137\ell/400+\varepsilon}H^b.
 \end{aligned}\tag{15}
\]

Indeed \(d\le21/50\) gives rounding losses at most
\(63\ell/200\) and \(63\ell/400\); subtracting these and \(\ell/4\)
from clearance \(3\ell/4\) gives exactly the reserves in (15).
All remaining \(U^\varepsilon\) and eventual threshold losses must
fit these small reserves. One can prescribe, for example,
\(\varepsilon<37\ell/800\) before selecting the source mesh and fixed
orders, then choose the later height exponent under the remaining budget.
If an existing loss schedule does not meet these bounds, (15) is only
a conditional budget certificate until it is refined.

These results pay boundary strips of the actual application wedge. They
do not lower the maximal short-witness envelope: the central band remains.
They also explain why a uniform gated mass saving should not be requested
on a part already paid by an original marginal count.

## 4. Optimal positive-moment envelope on flat original amplitude profiles

The source permits a fixed original slot system with a fine equal width
and disjoint physical windows, chosen before \(U\) and the bin. Consider
a flat **amplitude-bin** profile

\[
 g_i=q=dx\quad\hbox{for every original slot},\qquad
 49/100\le x\le1/2.
 \tag{16}
\]

This is admissible profile bookkeeping: \(0\le g_i\le d/2\), its
weighted mean is \(q\), \(G(z)=qz\), and the profile refinement gain
is exactly zero. It lies in the residual profile class. No assertion is
made that an actual native zero bin realizes (16).

To test the *strongest* possible positive inference, grant the endpoint
moments at fractional capacities

\[
 z_M=(1-r)/2,\qquad z_P=2(1-2m)/9.
 \tag{17}
\]

This grant is stronger than the actual legal whole-slot moments with
strict margins; it is used only for the limitation argument, never as a
native theorem. Since \(r>1/2\), the other inverse capacity
\((3-2r)/8\) is larger than \(z_M\). Every legal inverse subset
therefore has gain at most \(qz_M\), and every legal marked fourth
subset has gain at most \(qz_P\).

Grant in addition the plain second moment obtained from source Lemma
18.1 using a unit-scale plain factor, with capacity
\(z_S=2(1-m)/9\). Its count exponent is

\[
 P_1=1-dm-2qz_S,
\]

while the original fourth gives \(P_2=1-2dm-2qz_P\).
For (16),
\(P_1-P_2=dm(1-4x/9)>0\). The second moment and all its Hölder
interpolations with the fourth are weaker than \(P_2\) at this
near-maximal plain response. Higher positive moments obtained by
pointwise multiplication likewise add no new constraint. No new sixth
moment is imported.

Take the available full-bin count \(R=R_*+\ell\). The largest
one-level response-table count compatible with the named constraints is

\[
 \boxed{\rho=\min\{R,A_I(r),P_{\rm plain}(m)\}.}\tag{18}
\]

Here is the exact proof of sharpness for these scalar inputs. Put
\(\lfloor U^\rho\rfloor\) formal rows at

\[
 |M|^2=U^{dr},\quad |S|^2=U^{dm},\quad
 |Q_H|^2=U^{2qz_H}\quad\hbox{for every whole original subset }H.
 \tag{19}
\]

For every legal inverse subset,
\(\rho+dr+2qz_H\le\rho+dr+2qz_M\le1\).
For every legal fourth subset,
\(\rho+2dm+2qz_H\le\rho+2dm+2qz_P\le1\).
The bin count, all pointwise bounds, the unmarked amplified inverse at
\(r<1\), and the granted plain second moments also hold.
All statements concern the same fixed original slot list; the inequality
depends only on the subset's total width, so it covers **every** legal
subset, not merely one chosen \(I,J\). Strict margins only weaken those
constraints. Interpolation cannot rule out data satisfying both endpoints.
At a gate below the pointwise plain maximum, every formal row in (19)
passes it.

If the actual original inverse product has gain \(G_I\), its gated mass
in this table has exponent \(\rho+dr+2G_I\). Consequently the largest
gain that these positive constraints alone force is exactly

\[
 g_{\rm pos}=1-\rho-dr-2G_I.
 \tag{20}
\]

It is achieved by the strongest of the bin-count, inverse-extension and
marked-fourth bounds (9)–(11), in the loss-free endpoint grant. The
required loss-free gain for \(R_{\rm new}\) is
\(g_{\rm need}=1-R_{\rm new}-dr-2G_I\), so

\[
 \boxed{g_{\rm need}-g_{\rm pos}=\rho-R_{\rm new}.}\tag{21}
\]

The inverse whole-slot shortfall cancels from this deficit. Finer slot
rounding, positive interpolation or accounting again for the selected
bin's smaller mass cannot remove it.

This is a positive-information limitation. The table does not assign
native character values, realize the original prime coefficient class,

 \boxed{g_{\rm need}-g_{\rm pos}=F_x\eta+\ell
 \ge\frac{220252413}{2012413000000}
