## Amplification now has a concrete theorem to audit

The [conditional theorem and ledger](../../quasi-rh-character-amplification/notes/GEOMETRY_CONDITIONAL_THEOREM_AND_DEPENDENCY_LEDGER_20261009.md)
consolidate the boundary
\[
\sigma_0=\frac{20999}{24000}=\frac78-\frac1{24000}.
\]
They give direct proofs of the common-signal continuation and target-height
absorption steps. These no longer need to be additional imported black
boxes. The genuine arithmetic proof obligations remain the source
reflection/reflected energy, canonical inverse recursion, centered plain
fourth induction, buffered detector, and exact contour/normalizer package.
The original global family seven-eighths bound is also assumed to make
\(\kappa=3/4\) legal. A zeta-only baseline does not satisfy that premise.

The unspecialized high exponent is checked relative to the hypothetical
family edge, including the subtraction
\(E_{\beta_*}=E_{\sigma_0}-(\beta_*-\sigma_0)\). One practical
qualification is now explicit. The clean geometric margin \(1/400000\)
is not the final common analytic saving. For an even fixed slot count
\(K\), a conservative permitted normalizer saving is
\[
m_P=\frac{87}{200}\frac{\ell}{K},\qquad \ell=\frac{1001}{6000}.
\]
It is smaller than the geometric margin at \(K>29029\). The qualitative
mesh theorem does not give a numerical upper bound for \(K\), but the
fixed choice leaves a strictly positive saving, which is sufficient for
the family contradiction. No claim of a uniform numerical final saving
is needed or supported.

This completes the review's consolidation checkpoint. The next priority
is independent audit of the substantive imported proofs, starting with
the frozen-base reflected-energy argument and the two recursive moment
proofs. Retesting the rational geometry or seeking the tiny remaining
fixed-envelope displacement has lower value.

## Mixed arithmetic now has a weaker application checkpoint

The [mixed continuation](../mixed_character_families/notes/30_SURVIVING_BLOCK_AND_GATED_COUNT_CHECKPOINT_20261009.md)
constructs actual nonzero centered annular columns with opposite local
valuations \((1,2)\) and \((2,1)\). A finite instance has coefficients
\(-4,-4\), gcd norm 91, large exclusive supports and quotient conductor,
and unit sixth-power heads. It survives all current column removals.
Compatible high-conductor auxiliary rows exist, but no row in the
actual zero/profile bin or positive-slot witness population is constructed.
This is a zero-slot compatibility scout, not an estimate for a detector
block.

For each such pair, finite Fourier inversion gives the complete kernel
with the sharp row predicate retained inside its periodization. The
opposite sextic factors leave a nonzero primitive Gauss phase and a
coherent character mode. The transform returns an unknown covariance
between that mode and the actual mask Fourier coefficients. An arbitrary
nonnegative coherent mask can give full correlation; this rejects an
arbitrary-mask cancellation inference but proves no bias or obstruction
for the physical selector. The complementary pair allocations are
explicitly retained and remain unbounded. This does not complete the
original review's proposed varying-block power estimate.

A useful new sufficient target is weaker than a full mixed second moment.
Let the simultaneous product witness and the inverse pointwise upper
bound determine the literal necessary plain threshold
\(\mathfrak s_{\min}\). Request only
\[
\mathcal J=\sum_{u\in\mathcal C}|M_uQ_I(u)|^2
   1_{|S_u|^2\ge\mathfrak s_{\min}}
\ll U^{1-g+\varepsilon}H^b.
\]
The witness count follows by dividing by the actual inverse/slot lower
bound. If \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\) are witness and slot losses and
\(e_S\) the plain pointwise exponent cost, it suffices to take
\[
g\ge\max\{0,1-R_{\rm goal}-\delta r-2G_I
                 +\lambda_1+\lambda_2+e_S\},
\]
with the additional mesh, union, capacity, and height costs paid. For
ideal slots this tapers to
\(\max\{0,1/5000-\delta(1-x)(r-r_{\rm new})\}\).
The existing inverse moment supplies no such positive gain. A full mixed
second-moment estimate implies a version of this gated bound; the converse
fails, as a simple positive-measure example shows. Direct witness counting
is weaker still.

This changes the immediate work order within the leading program, rather
than its rank. Start with the actual selected inverse mass or direct
count on the buffered wedge. Continue the stronger fourth route when a
complete varying-label estimate controls the sharp-mask coherent covariance
beyond the current envelope. Neither finite Gauss algebra nor the number
of surviving-sector descriptions is evidence that such an estimate is
close.

## Short families require broader compensation

The [new short-family note](../short_families/notes/27_COHERENT_COVARIANCE_AND_FINITE_SPAN_BARRIER_20261009.md)
extends the earlier complete-cofactor obstruction to any fixed finite
signed linear combination. Group its actual cofactor coefficients by norm.
Unless every grouped coefficient vanishes, a finite Vandermonde argument
finds a first surviving logarithmic moment. The response still has weighted
energy \(D^{16/15}\), up to logarithms. A two-block subtraction can
cancel the Euler-weighted leading term, but merely moves the first
nonzero logarithmic order. The theorem permits exact zero combinations
and does not cover coefficients or a number of blocks growing with \(D\).

The full moment is not disproved. On the coherent row set, write the
known constant block as \(P_M\), the remaining products as \(R_u\),
their weighted average as \(\bar R\), and their centered variance as
\(V_R\). The complete energy is exactly
\[
m_M|P_M+\bar R|^2+V_R.
\]
To reach \(D^{4/5+\varepsilon}\), the complement must match
\(-P_M\) to relative power \(D^{-2/15+\varepsilon/2}\), up to
logarithms, and separately control \(V_R\) at the target. Its real
normalized correlation must approach \(-1\) with defect of order
\(D^{-4/15+\varepsilon}\), again up to logarithms. A fixed negative
sign or leading-coefficient match does not suffice.

This strengthens the original ranking. A worthwhile alternative scout
must involve growing cofactors, broader product shapes, or the actual
cross-product mean and centered covariance. Its result must also pay
the complementary rows and products. Another fixed finite Euler-product
compensation will not meet the promotion condition.

## Quadratic dispersion has a sharper finishing target

The [quadratic continuation](../integer_quadratic_lift/notes/3_SEPARATED_PRIME_PAIR_CHECKPOINT_20261009.md)
pays prime-power cross terms with the already available quadratic sieve,
without assuming the requested moment. The comparison error is
\(X^{2-h/4+\varepsilon}\), below the target \(X^{1+h/2+\varepsilon}\)
when \(h>4/3\). Elementary counting also controls distinct prime pairs
within distance \(X^{h-1-\delta}\) with reserve \(\delta>0\).

At the useful example \(h=7/5,\delta=1/40\), the full actual moment
has the additive representation
\[
\mathcal W_{7/5}(X)=F_{7/5,1/40}(X)
 +O_\varepsilon(X^{67/40+\varepsilon}),
\]
where \(F\) is the real signed prime-pair sum restricted to
\(|p-q|>X^{3/8}\), with the original coefficients, Jacobi zeros,
squarefree row weights, and principal row. Thus its one-sided
\(X^{17/10+\varepsilon}\) upper bound is the exact finishing task,
with reserve \(1/40\) for the comparison. Positivity gives only a
small negative lower bound for \(F\), not the needed upper bound.

This nonzero controlled sector clarifies where dispersion must act, but
does not improve the old hybrid bottleneck or its \(7/40\) deficit.
The entrywise absolute majorant of the separated residual is still
\(\gg X^2\): the removed near-pair absolute sector is smaller in power
and cannot eliminate the original majorant obstruction.
The strong fixed-quadratic-family consequence persists. The branch
should stay deferred until a concrete signed estimate for these separated
pairs is available, with its complement paid.

## Fixed scale needs a net energy gain

The [complete fixed-delay note](../fixed_scale_descent/notes/4_COMPLETE_FIXED_DELAY_AND_COVARIANCE_OBSTRUCTION_20261009.md)
derives the delay difference on a single common domain. It retains the
bulk kernel difference, centering corrections, the changed upper product
ceiling, both lower-cutoff strips, and the exact scalar remainder at both
scales. None of those correction integrals receives an unproved small
budget.

For the complete normalized response \(B_X\), let
\(D_X=B_X-B_{X/c}\). The useful contraction quantity is
\[
2\Re\langle D_X,B_{X/c}\rangle+\|D_X\|^2,
\]
not the covariance alone. For a persistent boundary-zero mode, the
logarithmic-scale mean covariance is
\(2|b_\rho|^2(\cos(\gamma_\rho\log c)-1)\), and the squared
defect has exactly the opposite mean. Their sum is zero. The covariance
can therefore look strongly favorable while yielding no contraction.
At resonance both terms vanish.

The calculation is conditional on a boundary zero being present and
does not assume that the supremum is attained. If it is unattained,
the earlier spectral-edge difficulty remains. The actual energy
inequality expands to quartic correlations in the two arithmetic error
functions. A bound for an isolated bilinear integrand is not automatically
that inequality. These qualifications narrow the success condition
without supplying a contraction mechanism, so the exploratory ranking
remains appropriate.

## The heat branch has met one bounded checkpoint

The [compact heat certificate](../newman_collisions/notes/4_CERTIFIED_COMPACT_CUTOFF_CROSSING_20261009.md)
proves, under Polymath's effective approximation theorem and the existing
analytic disk-error lemma,
\[
\frac15\le t\le\frac3{10},\qquad451.9\le x\le452.5
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q_t(x)>\frac14.
\]
The nonzero analytic normalizer makes this a no-real-zero result for
\(H_t\), hence also a collision exclusion, on the whole rectangle.
The fixed approximant uses \(N=6\). All 120 closed interval cells pay
the change from natural cutoff five to six, including its reflected term.
The full finite approximant is bounded below by more than \(2.50723\),
and the outer-disk error is less than \(2.247447\), giving a normalized
margin greater than \(0.25978\).

This corrects the original review's statement that no compact rectangle
has been certified. It is deliberately a value-positive rectangle; it
contains no real zero. The derivative error in this cell is too large
to certify simplicity near a zero. The next bounded milestone is a
rectangle containing a genuine simple zero on which the derivative
alternative succeeds, followed by an explicit finite cover at a fixed
positive time floor. Neither this rectangle nor such a finite cover
would itself provide control as that floor tends to zero or improve the
Newman constant.

## Highest priorities moving forward

1. **Audit the consolidated amplification theorem's imported arithmetic
   proofs.** The near-term artifact is now concrete. Resolve the
   reflected-energy, canonical inverse, and centered fourth dependencies,
   including masks and quantifiers, before treating the conditional
   candidate as an established boundary.
2. **Prove an estimate for the weakest sufficient selected mixed target.**
   Focus on the buffered detector wedge and nearly saturated profiles,
   keeping every physical slot, row presentation, zero extension, and
   common height. Use the surviving coupled block to identify and control
   the actual coherent mode and selector covariance. An exact transform
   is preparation; a power bound with the aggregate remainder paid is the
   completion condition. Give the fourth route priority when it supplies
   a quantified advantage over the direct selected target.
3. **Maintain one gated short-family alternative.** Seek actual broader
   cross-product compensation satisfying the coherent mean accuracy and
   centered variance budgets. Promote it only when the complete moment
   exponent improves; fixed finite cofactor subtraction has a proved
   limitation.
4. **Keep the other programs bounded.** Extend heat certification to a
   zero-bearing derivative cell. Resume fixed-scale work against the
   complete net energy inequality, and quadratic work against its
   separated signed residual. These are specific side tasks, not reasons
   to divide arithmetic effort equally among all branches.

The first mixed follow-up has isolated the mask/coherent-mode problem
but has not passed the power-saving checkpoint. If the next genuinely
analytic scout also reproduces only the present envelope or leaves that
mode uncontrolled, pause further fourth-route sector refinements and
reassess the main arithmetic allocation. A direct count theorem, a
selected inverse-mass estimate, or a full short-family covariance gain
would change the comparison; another equivalent finishing form would not.
