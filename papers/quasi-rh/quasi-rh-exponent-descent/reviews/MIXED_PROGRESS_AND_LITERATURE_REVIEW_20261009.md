## The exact remaining theorem

Write X=DN²P_J, B=U^(dr−1/500), and let A_≤ be the complete original
centered coefficient restricted to full-column sixth-power factors of norm
at most U^(1/10). The remaining real signed aggregate is
\[
 \mathcal T_*=X^{-1}
 \sum_{\substack{k,k'\\ Na>B,\ Nb>B\\ Nf(k,k')>B^2}}
 A_\le(k)\overline{A_\le(k')}\mathcal H_{W_*}(k,k').
\]
Here a,b are full exclusive parts, not ordinary gcd quotients; f records
unequal valuations modulo six. The physical row weight W_* retains
nonprincipality, Q>U^κ₁, and failure of the complete physical core frontier.
The required theorem is
\[
 \mathcal T_*\ll U^{1+dr-1/700+\varepsilon}H^b.
\]
It is one-sided because the aggregate is real. Bounding individual absolute
summands is a stronger problem. Note 29 proves an absolute old/new comparison
with saving 11/6250, making this residual sufficient for the preceding
centered endpoint. The residual estimate itself remains open.

Three practical gaps are material. First, a sharp row predicate or signed
pair restriction does not automatically inherit radial Poisson. Second,
native arbitrary-vector finite-ray transfer and derivative uniformity remain
inputs requiring exact domains, phases and zeros. Third, the operational
whole-slot certificate still needs witness losses below 6.85×10^−7 and
fourth-criterion perturbations below approximately 6.54×10^−6. The requested
1/700 is a uniform sufficient saving, not an achieved saving or a necessary
lower bound on the missing estimate.

## Literature and useful adaptations

### Fixed order sieves and joint conductor estimates

De Faveri's Theorem 1.1 is already the source of the sixth-free sieve in this
investigation. Proposition 8.1 is the more useful unexplored model: for cubic
characters it estimates a joint a b² row family with squarefree columns.
Its conjugate-component Poisson argument and the root-number coupling in
Sections 3.2 and 1.3.2–3 offer a way to retain two row factors in one average.
Remark 8.2 does not supply the present full sextic-column extension.
Theorem 1.4's higher-order moment application also assumes 2n-th roots of
unity; the Eisenstein field lacks the twelfth roots needed there for n=6.
[Alexandre de Faveri, Optimal large sieve for fixed order characters, v1,
2 October 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04045v1).

A proposed adaptation is to pair valuations 1 and 5, or 2 and 4, in
u=a₁a₂²a₃³a₄⁴a₅⁵w⁶. On units,
\(\chi(a_1a_5^5)=\chi(a_1)\overline{\chi(a_5)}\); valuations 2 and 4 give a cubic conjugate
pair, while valuation 3 is quadratic. This could improve on freezing all
higher-valuation factors in Note 29. It requires a new joint lemma with
physical zeros, frozen twists, actual column multiplicities, root-number
coupling and derivative costs. The algebraic conjugacy alone proves no such
estimate. An exponent comparison against the existing frontier should precede
an extensive proof attempt.

### Coherent Gauss and theta modes

Theorem 1.1 of de Faveri, Dunn and Hoffstein proves unconditionally that the
cubic arbitrary-coefficient sieve has an extra (AB)^(2/3) term. Its Section
2.1 witness uses conjugate normalized Gauss sums; equation (2.4) isolates a
polar contribution. This obstruction is not an ordinary coefficient
diagonal. Their general-order discussion is prospective, so it is not a
proved sextic lower bound for the current target.
[Non-orthogonality of the cubic and quartic large sieves via Rankin–Selberg,
v1, 8 July 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07911v1).

The useful inference is to calculate the Gauss/theta projection of the
complete centered marked coefficient. Equal-product centering vanishes on
the equality of the two plain Mellin arguments; a metaplectic polar locus
need not lie there. Determine whether the actual pole or coherent projection
is killed, retains an explicit main term, or is small for a different reason.
Removing ordinary diagonals and small quotients does not settle that test.

### Dispersion after subtracting the coherent contribution

Dunn and Radziwiłł's Definition 3.1 and Propositions 9.1–9.2 provide refined
cubic mean-square estimates after subtraction of an explicit Gauss/theta
term. Their coefficient class requires squarefree rough support and uniform
cancellation under character/angular twists and divisibility conditions.
GRH is used to establish the needed class properties for their prime
applications. A divisor bound for A_c and the present family-strip premise
do not imply these hypotheses. The transferable idea is to identify the
coherent term before estimating dispersion, with a proved interface for
the sharp physical predicates.
[Dunn and Radziwiłł, Bias in cubic Gauss sums Patterson's conjecture,
Propositions 9.1–9.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.07463).

### Mollified cubic moments

David, de Faveri, Dunn and Stucky's Proposition 1.6 gives a twisted cubic
second moment with an aggregate remainder over squarefree twists. Sections
6–7 treat polar terms and balanced/unbalanced ranges separately. Remark
1.9's effective second-moment mollifier length is X^(1/6−δ), much shorter
than the present inverse scale U^r with r near 0.7. Their result does not
directly cover mixed sextic rows, the long marked inverse, or the signed
filters. Its organization of aggregate twisted errors is nevertheless a
useful model for retaining Möbius structure before applying a majorant.
[Non-vanishing for cubic Hecke L-functions, v2, 3 March
2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.03048).

### Local trace correlations and factored moduli

Fouvry, Kowalski, Michel and Sawin's Theorem 1.1 gives bilinear cancellation
for suitable higher-rank trace functions over a prime modulus, with
MN≥q^(3/4+δ) and M≥q^δ. Its monodromy assumptions are not met by a raw
rank-one residue character. Section 9.10 treats rank-one correlations by
classifying exceptional algebraic configurations. That classification is
the promising local ingredient after the exact transform produces a
genuinely coupled kernel. The raw kernel χ(mn) factorizes and cannot have
uniform cancellation for arbitrary coefficients. A transfer to composite
ideal moduli and Eisenstein lattice regions would require proof.
[Bilinear forms with trace functions, v3, 11 March
2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09459v3).

Wu and Xi's Section 3 develops the q-analogue of van der Corput through
differencing and Poisson for factored squarefree moduli. This is a secondary
option for suitably factorable quotient-conductor blocks. A large conductor
alone supplies neither an advantageous factorization nor the required trace
correlation properties; arbitrary rough blocks would need another estimate.
Their setting is integer intervals, so the number-field adaptation is
substantive.
[Arithmetic exponent pairs for algebraic trace functions and applications,
v5, with Sawin's appendix](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.07060),
[associated addendum](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14508).

Baier and Young's Theorems 1.4–1.5 and Section 4.4 are older precedents for
primitive-product extraction and cubic/sixth Dirichlet-character mean values.
Their integer coefficient and character setting does not identify the
native operator needed here, and offers no evident improvement over the
already imported sieve.
[Mean values with cubic characters](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0804.2233).

No ready-made theorem matching the complete residual was identified among
these sources. The literature supports structured coefficient and coherent
main-term analysis, rather than another unrestricted positive-norm estimate.
