## 1. The operator channel and its exact square defect

Let \(U_y\), \(y\in\mathbb R^d\), be a strongly continuous unitary
representation on a Hilbert space \(\mathcal H\), and put
\(\alpha_y(B)=U_yBU_y^*\). For \(t>0\), define the normal unital
completely positive map

\[
 P_t(B)=\int K_t(y)\alpha_y(B)\,dy,
 \qquad K_t(y)=(4\pi t)^{-d/2}e^{-|y|^2/(4t)}.                 \tag{1}
\]

For general bounded observables the integral is weak/ultraweak. On the
subalgebra of observables with norm-continuous translation orbit it is
also a norm integral. Gaussian convolution gives \(P_tP_s=P_{t+s}\).
On the norm \(C^2\) translation subalgebra, with
\(\delta_j B=\left.\partial_{y_j}\alpha_y(B)\right|_{y=0}\),

\[
 \mathcal L=\sum_j\delta_j^2,
 \qquad \Gamma(A,B)=\sum_j(\delta_jA)^*\delta_jB .             \tag{2}
\]

The noncommutative product rule is

\[
 \mathcal L(A^*B)-(\mathcal LA)^*B-A^*\mathcal LB
 =2\Gamma(A,B).
\]

Consequently, for such \(A,B\),

\[
 \boxed{P_t(A^*B)-P_t(A)^*P_t(B)
 =2\int_0^t P_{t-s}\bigl(\Gamma(P_sA,P_sB)\bigr)\,ds.}       \tag{3}
\]

Differentiate \(P_{t-s}((P_sA)^*P_sB)\) to prove (3). The endpoint
values are \(P_t(A^*B)\) and \(P_t(A)^*P_t(B)\), and the derivative
is minus the displayed integrand. For \(A=B\) every integrand is
positive. This is a precise operator version of the scalar heat variance
identity, retaining all factors of two.

No smoothness is needed for the alternative bounded-observable identity

\[
 \boxed{P_t(A^*B)-P_t(A)^*P_t(B)
 =\frac12\iint K_t(y)K_t(z)
 [\alpha_y(A)-\alpha_z(A)]^*
 [\alpha_y(B)-\alpha_z(B)]\,dy\,dz.}                         \tag{4}
\]

Expand the four products and use independence. Equivalently, for
\(A=B\), this equals the average of
\((\alpha_y(A)-P_t(A))^*(\alpha_y(A)-P_t(A))\).

**Exact multiplicative-domain obstruction.** For every \(t>0\), equality
in the Schwarz inequality
\(P_t(A^*A)=P_t(A)^*P_t(A)\) holds exactly when
\(\alpha_y(A)=A\) for every \(y\). Indeed, the centered square has
zero expectation against every vector only if each squared vector norm
vanishes for almost every \(y\). Strong continuity of the orbit and
strict positivity of the Gaussian density extend this to all \(y\).
At \(y=0\) one obtains \(A=P_t(A)\). Conversely, invariance makes the
defect zero. Therefore the multiplicative domain is \(\{U_y:y\in
\mathbb R^d\}'\). On the usual scalar multiplication algebra in
\(L^2(\mathbb R^d)\), this leaves only constant functions.

A single invertible similarity preserves every product. It cannot agree
with (1) on a translation-stable algebra containing a nonconstant
multiplication observable and its products. This obstruction is exact
and does not depend on the choice of approximation parameters.

If \(U_y=e^{iy\cdot P}\) with commuting selfadjoint momenta, then
\(\mathcal L(B)=-\sum_j[P_j,[P_j,B]]\). This is heat generated by
squares of *derivations*. It must not be confused with the state-space
heat operator \(e^{-t\sum P_j^2}\), nor with conjugation by that
nonunitary operator.

## 2. Independent density smoothing and the self contraction

Work on a fixed \(N\)-particle antisymmetric position Hilbert space
\(\bigwedge^N L^2(\mathbb R^d)\); spin labels can be included without
changing the particle-index contraction. For \(a>0\), define the
bounded multiplication observable

\[
 \rho_a(x)=\sum_{i=1}^N K_a(x-X_i),\qquad
 :\rho_a(x)^2:
 =\rho_a(x)^2-\sum_i K_a(x-X_i)^2.                          \tag{5}
\]

The second expression removes exactly the terms with the same particle
index. Its spatial integral is the bounded positive pair operator

\[
 \boxed{Q_a=\int :\rho_a(x)^2:\,dx
 =\sum_{i\ne j}K_{2a}(X_i-X_j),
 \quad \int\rho_a(x)^2dx=Q_a+NK_{2a}(0).}                   \tag{6}
\]

The integrals in (6) are positive quadratic-form/weak integrals evaluated
by the displayed multiplication formulas; they are not asserted to be
operator-norm Bochner integrals over \(x\). The norm bound
\(0\le Q_a\le N(N-1)K_{2a}(0)I\) makes the result bounded.

Let \(U_y\) translate all particles by the same vector. Depending on the
translation sign convention, \(\alpha_y(\rho_a(x))=\rho_a(x\pm y)\),
so \(P_t(\rho_a(x))=\rho_{a+t}(x)\). Smoothing each of the two density
factors independently and then applying the pair-index subtraction
produces \(Q_{a+t}\). A common translation of the already formed pair
operator instead gives

\[
 P_t(Q_a)=Q_a,                                             \tag{7}
\]

because every particle separation is unchanged. Before spatial
integration the independent construction contains two independent
translation variables \(Y,Z\); their difference has heat kernel
\(K_{2t}\). The common construction has \(Y=Z\). Equations (6)--(7)
display the missing relative diffusion explicitly.

Normal ordering does not commute with this replacement. For the ordinary
square covariance, spatial integration of (3) yields

\[
 Q_a-Q_{a+t}+N[K_{2a}(0)-K_{2(a+t)}(0)]I\ge0.               \tag{8}
\]

It gives no conclusion \(Q_a\ge Q_{a+t}\). The positive contraction
difference in (8) can pay for a growing pair term. For two distinct
particles separated by \(r>0\),

\[
 Q_a=2(8\pi a)^{-d/2}e^{-r^2/(8a)}
\]

increases up to \(a=r^2/(4d)\), then decreases. This pointwise formula
also gives a quadratic-form counterexample to monotonicity using
wavefunctions supported in a sufficiently small open region of
separations where the strict sign persists. Monotonicity of an integrated
positive Coulomb cutoff does not imply monotonicity of the scale density
\(Q_a\).

All formulas retain \(a>0\). Taking an unsmeared density square first,
or splitting divergent continuum self and pair integrals without their
cancellation, is outside this bounded formulation.

## 3. Exact four-site CAR obstruction

Let \(c_j,c_j^*\), \(j\in\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\), satisfy CAR and
\(n_j=c_j^*c_j\). There is one spin mode per site. Let \(\alpha_1\)
be the fermionic unitary translation, and use the continuous-time
nearest-neighbor channel with generator
\(\mathcal L=\alpha_1+\alpha_{-1}-2I\). At
\(t=(\log2)/2\), its exact translation probabilities and their
semigroup convolution are

\[
 p_t=\frac1{16}(9,3,1,3),\qquad
 p_{2t}=\frac1{64}(25,15,9,15).                            \tag{9}
\]

These follow from Fourier eigenvalues \(0,-2,-4,-2\) of the generator.
For \(\rho_t(x)=\sum_jp_t(j-x)n_j\),

\[
 :\rho_t(x)^2:=\rho_t(x)^2-\sum_jp_t(j-x)^2n_j,
 \quad Q_t=\sum_x:\rho_t(x)^2:
 =\sum_{j\ne k}p_{2t}(j-k)n_jn_k.                         \tag{10}
\]

Every summand on the right is positive. In the declared two-particle
sector, \(Q_t\) has value \(15/32\) for an adjacent occupied pair
and \(9/32\) for an opposite pair. In contrast,
\(:n_x^2:=n_x^2-n_x=0\), so every linear common channel sends the
original contact pair operator to zero. Thus even a bounded CP channel
cannot replace the independently smoothed pair operation by its action
on the normal-ordered contact square.

This example also excludes importing the ordinary covariance sign
through normal ordering. On the fully occupied state,

\[
 \langle P_t(:n_0^2:)-:\rho_t(0)^2:\rangle=-39/64,
 \quad \langle P_t(n_0^2)-\rho_t(0)^2\rangle=0.              \tag{11}
\]

Smoothing annihilation and creation fields is a third operation:

\[
 P_t(c_0)=\sum_jp_t(j)c_j,
 \quad \{P_t(c_0),P_t(c_0)^*\}
 =\Bigl(\sum_jp_t(j)^2\Bigr)I=\frac{25}{64}I.              \tag{12}
\]

A canonical unitary/similarity transformation preserves CAR and cannot
produce (12) while smoothing both fields in this way. Also,
\(P_t(c_0)^*P_t(c_0)\) contains intersite hopping terms and is different
from the heat-smoothed density \(P_t(n_0)\). Consequently, heat-smoothing
fields, densities, and density-factor pairs cannot be interchanged.

## 4. Finite-spin trace and the corrected Dirac averaging

Independently of the source argument, suppose a balanced finite spin
representation has \(D^2=LI\) and \(\operatorname{tr}_{\rm spin}D=0\),
where the spin trace is normalized. The power series gives

\[
 \operatorname{tr}_{\rm spin}e^{sD}
 =\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{s^{2m}L^m}{(2m)!},                     \tag{13}
\]

which is \(\cosh(s\sqrt L)\), interpreted by its entire series.
For a Fourier symbol with \(L=-|k|^2\) it is \(\cos(s|k|)\).
The scalar two-by-two example \(D=k\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) already verifies the obstruction exactly.
The proposed heat \(e^{s^2L}\) has coefficient \(1\), rather than
\(1/2\), at \(s^2L\). Even replacing \(s\) by \(\sqrt2s\)
to match that coefficient leaves \(1/6\), rather than \(1/2\), at
\(s^4L^2\). No constant time rescaling repairs the trace identity.

More generally, a finite positive spin trace of one unitary first-order
propagator with symbol \(kS\), \(S=S^*\), is a finite characteristic
function. It cannot equal \(e^{-\tau k^2}\) for every real \(k\) and
\(\tau>0\): equality of the entire functions would make its value at
\(k=iR\) grow at most exponentially in \(|R|\), while the Gaussian
grows as \(e^{\tau R^2}\).

For a selfadjoint Dirac operator \(\mathsf D\), the valid replacement is
the strong spectral-calculus identity

\[
 e^{-t\mathsf D^2}
 =\frac1{\sqrt{4\pi t}}\int e^{-s^2/(4t)}e^{is\mathsf D}\,ds.\tag{14}
\]

For observables, averaging conjugations by the same propagators gives
\(\exp[-t\operatorname{ad}_{\mathsf D}^2]\), not (14) acting by
left multiplication. This preserves the distinction between state heat,
an observable channel, and a single first-order flow. The checker matches
the Gaussian even moments with the heat coefficients through degree six;
the all-order identity follows from the scalar Fourier transform and
spectral calculus, not from that finite check.

standard Schwarz inequality for unital CP maps and multiplicative-domain

[A Schwarz inequality for positive linear maps on C*-algebras](https://doi.org/10.1215/ijm/1256051007),

uses semigroup interpolation explicitly. No novelty is assigned to
Gaussian averaging, Schwarz positivity, or the semigroup product rule.

**Decision:** the initial corrected-smoothing gate is complete as a
rigorous reconstruction and a precise obstruction to the source's
similarity/common-trace route. Independent density smoothing with its
self contraction is the admissible construction. Ordinary CP covariance
positivity cannot be carried through normal ordering without the extra
terms. This result supplies corrected machinery and a bounded no-go
statement; it gives neither a many-body approximation advantage nor a
stationary Laguerre/theta sign certificate. The next application must
retain its own constrained terms, source oscillations and dynamical
residuals.
