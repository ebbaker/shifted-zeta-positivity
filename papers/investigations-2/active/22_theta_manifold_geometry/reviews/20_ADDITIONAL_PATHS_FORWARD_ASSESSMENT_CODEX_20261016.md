**The numerical frontier needs a corrected premise.** In [Polymath, Theorem 1.2 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438v2), the criterion is

\[
\Lambda\le t_0+\frac{y_0^2}{2},
\]

subject to initial verification, final-time exterior exclusion, and an intermediate barrier. The table row used for the 0.2 result has \(t_0=0.186\), \(y_0\approx0.16733\), and \(X=5\cdot10^{12}+194858\). The strip payment is about 0.014. [Platt–Trudgian, Section 3.4 and Corollary 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.09765) explicitly obtains 0.2 from that row and its verified height. Thus Review 19's attribution of a zero-strip-payment 0.2 bound is incorrect.

The expression \(\theta/\log(X/4\pi)\) can describe a hypothetical fully effective all-real outer theorem joined to adequate finite coverage. It does not supply either ingredient. Numerically, \(\log(6\cdot10^{12}/4\pi)=26.891756\ldots\), and \((61/24)/26.891756\ldots=0.0945147\ldots\); this is a planning calculation, not a bound proved by the current manuscript. A threshold inferred from one published parameter choice cannot be moved to a new height while discarding its other hypotheses. The useful optimization includes \(t_0,y_0,X\), the actual finite-sum margins, exterior onset, and barrier coverage.

Likewise, an improvement is not automatically “for free,” and a new barrier is not automatically cheap. There is a valid reuse argument for a *specific* existing rectangular barrier when only its final time is reduced; the finite-certificate proposal below has that advantage. Failure of one outer implementation would not prove that greater verification height is the only remaining lever.

**The strongest near-term analytic lead is already in the Newman audit.** The [audit README](../../../newman_certificate_audit/README.md) and [Note 3, Section 5](../../../newman_certificate_audit/notes/3_CERTIFIED_ROW_REPLAY_AND_FINITE_LANE_REDUCTIONS_20261012.md) report an unused improved triangle inequality in the pinned package. At the original weakest row its sharp weight gives approximately 0.077 instead of \(7.9\cdot10^{-7}\). Four first-leg rows remain positive in floating arithmetic at \(t_0=0.157\), suggesting the candidate

\[
0.157+\frac{0.0350708}{2}=0.1745354.
\]

Those four rows do not certify a new bound. They do locate a concrete source of slack, which is more actionable than a still-unevaluated asymptotic onset. The gain concerns a lower bound on the actual finite sum, rather than a smaller residual attached to a different approximation.

The [first cross-model review, Section 3.3](../../../newman_certificate_audit/reviews/1_CROSS_MODEL_REVIEW_OF_NOTES_1_2_20261012.md) already proves the sharp algebraic inequality and monotonicity in the bound on \(|\gamma|\). Window freezing therefore has a route. The unresolved first mathematical task is a uniform transfer in \(y\) for the sharper weight, including its branch changes and degenerate cases. The next task is the unbounded tail at the proposed smaller time. Both should precede a large sweep. Lowering the time also enlarges the required final-time interval in \(y\): at \(t_0=0.157\) it reaches \(\sqrt{0.686}\), beyond the previous \(\sqrt{0.6775}\). The transfer, tail, and error suppliers must cover that full interval; any reuse of a previously proved larger hull requires an explicit domain check.

I would use the following bounded sequence:

1. **Compare current public methods and state the goal.** Distinguish an independently checked certificate or cost improvement from a new record. A fresh primary-source check finds [Gordon's public package](https://github.com/stefangordon/dbn-upper-bound) claiming \(\Lambda<0.158\); its README explicitly says external peer review is absent and its Lean theorem has analytic assumptions. This review does not validate that claim. Its existence nevertheless makes an automatic “new record” campaign toward 0.1745 inappropriate without a method and evidence comparison.
2. **Prove the sharp-weight transfer and screen the tail.** Keep the exact source, mollifier, correction terms, normalization, cutoff seams, and all stated parameter ranges. A successful result is a fully specified lower-bound mechanism with a positive complete budget at the proposed parameters. If its tail or transfer fails, stop that parameter choice or lemma and record the obstruction.
3. **Certify the first rows and transitions under the new suppliers.** Include every prime-leg seam and the new uniform approximation allowance. Selected rows are a feasibility test only.
4. **Expand only after these gates pass.** A complete result requires all finite windows, transfer, infinite tail, and coverage. At fixed \(X,y_0\), the pinned initial verification and rectangular barrier can be reused by restriction to the smaller time interval, as Note 3 explains, subject to those imported certificates' stated validity.

**The outer theorem is a structural redesign problem.** The [low-index budget note](../notes/06_effective_outer_bridge/01_LOW_INDEX_BUDGET_REFINEMENT_20261015.md) improves its leading elementary constant by more than 3974, yet its retained majorant alone still needs \(L-1\gtrsim31{,}424{,}688\). Its analytic setup already requires \(L\ge100002\). These are restrictions of that proof budget, not lower bounds on where the true sign begins.

Consequently, extracting the remaining exponent-pair constant or reallocating the current error shares cannot bridge to \(L\approx27\). If this direction continues, first select one attainable \((t_0,X)\), identify the exact exterior statement needed by the global argument, and derive a new nonasymptotic remainder budget there. Stop a proposed budget as soon as an unavoidable nonnegative term consumes the entire margin. Four-jet strict Laguerre estimates may be stronger than the chosen global certificate needs; determine whether fewer derivatives or direct complex nonvanishing removes a major loss before retaining the factor 1728 differentiation payment.

This is my preferred ambitious analytic alternative, but its present evidence does not justify placing it ahead of the concrete finite-sum feasibility test. If an explicit asymptotic theorem is itself the objective, evaluating its imported constants can be worthwhile with that narrower deliverable.

This is a premise audit, not a fresh replay of collision certificates. A universal algebraic inequality can hold for every control: the distinguishing step is proving a favorable complete numerical margin from actual theta data. A valid finite certificate may also succeed for a control on a region where that control has no collision. Failure is required on a domain containing the controlled collision, or for an upper bound contradicted by its certified witness, not indiscriminately on every test box. Different controls may fail different premises. Arithmetic rigidity identifies the source; it does not supply the transform's sign.
