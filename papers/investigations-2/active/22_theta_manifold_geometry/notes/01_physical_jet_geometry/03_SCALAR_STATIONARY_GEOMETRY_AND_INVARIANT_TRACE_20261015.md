## 1. Positive even heat germs

Let a real analytic field \(G\) satisfying \(G_t=-G_{xx}\) have exact spatial order \(2k\) at \((T,a)\), with leading spatial coefficient \(c \ne  0\); allow \(k=0\). Put \(s=T-t\ge 0\), \(y=x-a\), and \(R=\sqrt{s}+|y|\). The heat Taylor expansion gives

\[
G(T-s,a+y)=cP_{2k}(s,y)+O(R^{2k+1}),
\qquad
P_{2k}(s,y)=\sum_{p=0}^k\frac{(2k)!}{p!(2k-2p)!}s^py^{2k-2p}.
\]

All coefficients are positive, and

\[
P_{2k}(s,y)\ge(s+y^2)^k\ge2^{-k}R^{2k}.
\]

Consequently \(G\) has the fixed sign of \(c\) throughout a sufficiently small backward half-neighborhood, away from \((s,y)=(0,0)\). For \(k=0\), ordinary continuity proves the same assertion including the center. The remainder estimate follows by applying the ordinary analytic Taylor theorem to \(G(T-\eta^2,a+y)\); its first nonzero homogeneous part has degree \(2k\).

## 2. Complete local classification of both stationary sets

Suppose \(H\) has exact spatial multiplicity \(m=2r+j\), where \(r\ge 1\) and \(j\in\{0,1\}\). Set \(W=\partial_x^j H\). There are \(s_0,d>0\) such that, on

\[
0<s<s_0,\qquad |x-a|<d,
\]

the following hold:

1. \(W\) is nonzero and has the sign of its leading spatial coefficient at the event.
2. \(W_{xx}\) is nonzero with the same sign, since its event order is \(2r-2\).
3. The full stationary equation \(W_x=0\) has exactly one solution in \(x\), namely the manuscript's analytic graph \(x=\chi(s)\).
4. The opposite parity stationary equation has no solution there: for even \(m\), \(H_{xx}=W_{xx}\) never vanishes; for odd \(m\), \(H_x=W\) never vanishes.

Proof: the preceding even-germ lemma gives (1), (2), and (4). Since \(W_{xx}\) has fixed nonzero sign, \(W_x\) is strictly monotone in \(x\). The existing analytic predecessor theorem gives a root \(\chi(s)=a+O(s)\) in the rectangle for all sufficiently small positive \(s\); monotonicity makes it unique. No claim is made about the forward side \(t>T\), larger neighborhoods, or other collision centers.

Thus the union of the two full stationary sets in this punctured backward neighborhood consists of a single analytic curve. The parity-matched branch is the whole local stationary geometry, not just a selected necessary test curve. Both branches remain necessary in an event-independent theorem, because the parity of a hypothetical event is unknown.

## 3. One analytic factor for the physical first jet

Write all following fields at \((T-s,\chi(s))\). Analyticity and the predecessor expansion give

\[
W=s^r u(s),\qquad u(0)=\frac{\partial_x^{2r}W(T,a)}{r!}\ne0.
\]

On the even branch, \(H=W\) and \(H_x=0\). On the odd branch,

\[
H_x=s^r u(s),\qquad H=s^{r+1}v(s),
\]

with analytic \(v\). Therefore the analytic ideal generated by the two physical jet coordinates, restricted to the curve, is exactly \((s^r)\) on either branch. In particular,

\[
E=W^2Q(s),\qquad
Q(s)=\begin{cases}1,&j=0,\\1+s^2\lambda(s)^2,&j=1,\end{cases}
\qquad\lambda=v/u
\]

is analytic and bounded, with \(Q(0)=1\). This is a unit factor, not a change that removes the radial zero: \(\rho=|W|\sqrt{Q}\) still has exact order \(s^r\).

For the odd branch there is also an exact geometric integral identity. Put \(h(s)=H(T-s,\chi(s))\) and \(w(s)=H_x(T-s,\chi(s))\). Stationarity gives

\[
h'(s)=\chi'(s)w(s),\qquad
h(s)=\int_0^s\chi'(u)w(u)\,du.
\]

Meanwhile \(w'(s)=W_{xx}(T-s,\chi(s))\) has the same sign as \(w(s)\). Thus \(|w|\) increases with \(s\), and

\[
\left|\frac{H}{H_x}\right|\le s\sup_{0\le u\le s}|\chi'(u)|.
\]

If \(\alpha=\partial_x^{2r+1}H(T,a)\) and \(\beta=\partial_x^{2r+2}H(T,a)\), then

\[
\lambda(0)=-\frac{\beta}{r(r+1)\alpha}.
\]

Indeed \(\chi'(0)=-\beta/(r \alpha)\), \(w(s)=(\alpha/r!)s^r+O(s^{r+1})\), and integration gives \(h/w=\chi'(0)s/(r+1)+O(s^2)\).

## 4. Scalar signed observation, with mixed errors retained

Let \(B_\partial\) be a complete boundary trace on this curve, and let \(\sigma=\operatorname{sgn}(W)\), constant for small positive \(s\). The exact signed target is

\[
\frac{W}{\rho}B_\partial\le C\rho
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{B_\partial}{W}\le CQ(s).
\]

Since \(Q\) is bounded above and below by positive constants, this holds with a locally bounded nonnegative \(C\) exactly when

\[
\left(\frac{B_\partial}{W}\right)_+
\]

is locally bounded. This reduction now covers the entire punctured backward stationary locus around the event. The finite-order diagnostic in physical-jet Note 02 therefore becomes a complete local analytic criterion there: if \(B_\partial\) is analytic on the curve with first nonzero term \(b_qs^q\), where \(b_q\ne 0\), then the exact signed bound holds precisely when \(\sigma b_q<0\) or \(q\ge r\), with the identically zero case included.

The reduction does not automatically supply uniformity in endpoint length, cutoff, or additional source parameters. Those remain part of the original estimate.

For a nonnegative mixed error \(\epsilon_0\), the exact formula is

\[
\frac{W}{\rho}B_\partial\le C\rho+\epsilon_0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sigma B_\partial\le C|W|Q(s)+\epsilon_0\sqrt{Q(s)}.
\]

For nonnegative \(M,\eta\), the simpler scalar estimate \(\sigma B_\partial \le  M|W|+\eta\) directly implies \(W B_\partial \le  M E+\eta \rho\). Conversely the original estimate implies a scalar estimate with the bounded comparison factors displayed above. A fixed positive mixed error cannot be dropped: after division by \(W\), its contribution has order \(\epsilon_0/|W|\), which is singular at the event. The prescribed order of fixing length, making cutoff errors small, and then approaching the event remains necessary.

## 5. Actual-curve reconstruction can be simpler than a full-space bound

Consider the complete identity

\[
V=A H+B H_x+C h_{j+1}+B_\partial+R.
\]

On the unique stationary curve its reconstructed first-jet term is \(\kappa_j W\), where

\[
\kappa_0=A,\qquad\kappa_1=B+s\lambda(s)A.
\]

The exact nonnegative coefficient required for its contribution to \(WV\) relative to physical energy is

\[
q_{\rm actual}=\frac{(\kappa_j)_+}{Q(s)}.
\]

This can be strictly smaller than the signed norm computed over every first-jet direction in a two-dimensional relaxation. For example, on the odd curve \(A=1/s\), \(B=0\) has bounded \(q_{\mathrm{actual}}\), since \(\kappa_1=\lambda(s)\), while the full-plane signed constant is \(1/(2s)\) and diverges. More generally, independently bounded \(|sA|\) and \(B_+\) suffice when \(\lambda\) is bounded. Favorable cancellation can allow still larger coefficients.

This is a valid new allowance for an independently prescribed reconstruction, not an arithmetic certificate by itself. Defining coefficients using the unknown quotient \(V/W\) remains circular. Singular coefficients can also destroy source or cutoff remainder estimates; each such estimate must still be proved independently. The local curve is constructed conditional on a putative event; an implementable source theorem must establish its bounds on an event-independent class covering that curve, or prove them uniformly under the hypothetical-event assumptions without using the desired conclusion.


## 6. Prescribed cones and a scalar certificate

Let \(W=h_j(t,x)\), \(F=(H,H_x)\), \(E=|F|^2\), and retain full stationarity \(h_{j+1}(t,x)=0\). Fix any **strictly positive constant** \(\kappa\), before considering a candidate event. Let \(Z_j\) denote the other physical first-jet coordinate (\(Z_0=H_x\), \(Z_1=H\)) and restrict starting points to

\[
\mathcal D_{j,\kappa}=\{h_{j+1}=0,\quad W\ne0,\quad |Z_j|\le\kappa|W|\}.
\]

This is a restriction on the starting observation only. The complete field, stationarity, full starting factor, and entire endpoint intervals are unchanged. On this set,

\[
W^2\le E\le (1+\kappa^2)W^2.\tag{S1}
\]

Branch zero lies in every such cone automatically. At a multiplicity \(2r+1\) event, the complete branch-one predecessor has \(H/H_x=O(s)\), so it lies in every fixed positive cone for all sufficiently small positive \(s\). Thus these **prescribed** cone domains retain all finite-event predecessor coverage. The threshold can depend on the event and on the fixed indices. Taking \(\kappa=0\) would in general discard asymmetric odd predecessors and is not an equivalent universal restriction.

### Distance from the favorable half-line

For \(\sigma=\operatorname{sgn}(W)\), let \(\mathcal C_\sigma=\{b:\sigma b\le0\}\) be the opposite closed ray. Its Euclidean distance is

\[
\operatorname{dist}(B,C_\sigma)=(\sigma B)_+.
\]

The scalar condition

\[
\operatorname{dist}(B_\partial,C_\sigma)\le C|W|+\epsilon_0\tag{S2}
\]

is exactly equivalent to the division-free product inequality

\[
WB_\partial\le C W^2+\epsilon_0|W|.\tag{S3}
\]

It implies Note 01's physical target with the same nonnegative constants:

\[
\frac W{\sqrt E}B_\partial
\le C\sqrt E+\epsilon_0.
\]

Conversely that physical target implies (S2) on the prescribed cone with constants \(C(1+\kappa^2)\) and \(\epsilon_0 \sqrt{1+\kappa^2}\). Therefore local boundedness and a family of errors tending to zero are preserved in both directions. No new arithmetic assertion follows from this equivalence.

A usable source-prescribed certificate for (S3) would be

\[
B_\partial=\lambda W-\sigma P+R,\qquad
\lambda\le C,\quad P\ge0,\quad |R|\le\epsilon_0.\tag{S4}
\]

Multiplication by \(W\) proves (S3) directly. There is no lower bound required on \(\lambda\), and no upper bound required on \(P\). The independent content would be the source construction and the inequalities in (S4). Defining \(\lambda\) or \(P\) afterward by division by \(W\) merely restates the desired estimate.

### A simpler coefficient bound on the cone

Suppose the independently prescribed reconstruction is

\[
V=A H+B H_x+C h_{j+1}+B_\partial+R,
\qquad |R|\le\epsilon.
\]

For \(j=0\), the reconstruction term contributes at most \(A_+ W^2\). For \(j=1\), on \(\mathcal D_{1,\kappa}\) it contributes at most

\[
H_x(AH+BH_x)\le q_\kappa(A,B)W^2,
\qquad q_\kappa(A,B)=(B+\kappa|A|)_+.\tag{S5}
\]

This is the **exact least nonnegative constant relative to \(W^2\) over the unrestricted algebraic cone of jet pairs**, because that cone permits either endpoint of \([-\kappa,\kappa]\). The actual theta image may be smaller, so this is not a claim of sharpness on that image. It is a valid, though not generally optimal, constant relative to \(E\). Together with (S3),

\[
WV\le(q_{j,\kappa}+C)W^2+(\epsilon+\epsilon_0)|W|.
\tag{S6}
\]

Here \(q_{0,\kappa}=A_+\). If desired, the exact cone constant relative to \(E\) is the maximum over \(|z|\le \kappa\) of \((B+A z)_+/(1+z^2)\); (S5) avoids that unnecessary optimization.

The cone genuinely enlarges the allowed coefficient class. For \(\tau>0\), take

\[
A=\tau^{-2},\qquad B=-\kappa\tau^{-2}.
\]

Then \(q_\kappa=0\), whereas the full-plane constant from Note 02 is

\[
\frac{B+\sqrt{A^2+B^2}}2
=\frac{\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}-\kappa}{2\tau^2}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

The gain comes from retaining the actually needed cone of starting jets. It is not a claim that these particular coefficients occur in an admissible theta identity. Uniform residual estimates must still be proved for the chosen singular coefficient rule; the cone does not bound a source remainder that depends separately on \(|A|+|B|\).

### Collision coverage with mixed errors

For the endpoint cutoff \(V=W_N(t,x+\delta)\), an estimate

\[
WV\le M W^2+\epsilon|W|
\]

with locally bounded nonnegative \(M\) gives, for \(\alpha=\exp(-\delta)\),

\[
W^2-\alpha WV\ge -\alpha M E-\alpha\epsilon\sqrt E.
\]

Hence the manuscript's fixed-length mixed-error theorem remains valid when its start estimates are assumed only on the fixed positive cones \(\mathcal D_{j,\kappa}\): every putative finite event has its matching predecessor inside that cone eventually. All original interval coverage, complete starting and stationary factors, uniform cutoff errors, and the order **fixed length, fixed accurate cutoff, then event limit** remain essential. This reduction is an equivalence of useful targets near an event, not a proof of the theta sign needed to satisfy them.

## 7. The two arithmetic boundaries form one invariant trace

The [joint source current calculation](../03_theta_bundles_and_connections/09_JOINT_SOURCE_CURRENT_AND_POLYNOMIAL_OBSTRUCTION_20261015.md) has two lower fluxes. Their bookkeeping admits an exact simplification. It does not provide a new estimate for the physical signed observation. Write

\[
r_n=\Delta J_n+\partial_uK_n+e_n,\qquad
\Delta J_n=J_{n-1}-2J_n+J_{n+1}.
\]

Here \(J_n\) is defined for \(n\ge0\), \(K_n\) for \(n\ge1\), and \(u\ge0\). On a finite rectangle, smoothness sufficient for the displayed derivatives is enough. For the infinite-rectangle statements below, assume that every weighted index supremum of \(J(u)\) and \(r(u)-e(u)\) has an integrable source majorant, that \(K(0)\) is rapidly decreasing in the index, and that \(\sum_n|K_n(U)|\to0\). The integrated upper index flux then vanishes. These hypotheses justify the sums, integrals, and their exchanges; locally uniform parameter versions require locally uniform majorants and boundary limits. Admissible gauges retain these hypotheses.

For \(M\ge1\), the complete rectangular trace is

\[
\mathcal B_{M,U}(J,K)
=\int_0^U\bigl(J_0-J_1+J_{M+1}-J_M\bigr)\,du
+\sum_{n=1}^M\bigl(K_n(U)-K_n(0)\bigr).
\tag{G1}
\]

For any admissible \(L_n(u)\), the transformation

\[
\widetilde J=J+\partial_uL,\qquad
\widetilde K=K-\Delta L
\tag{G2}
\]

preserves both \(r_n-e_n\) and \(\mathcal B_{M,U}\). Indeed, \(\Delta\partial_uL=\partial_u\Delta L\); the variation of the first term of (G1) is the upper-minus-lower source evaluation of \(L_0-L_1+L_{M+1}-L_M\), and the variation of its second term is precisely its negative, since

\[
\sum_{n=1}^M\Delta L_n=L_0-L_1+L_{M+1}-L_M.
\]

Thus all four sides, including their corner cancellations, are essential to the invariance. The same residual \(e\) is retained.

There is also a canonical one-current description after source integration. Define

\[
a_n=\int_0^\infty(r_n-e_n)\,du,\quad
\overline J_n=\int_0^\infty J_n\,du,\quad k_n=K_n(0),
\qquad
(\mathcal Gk)_n=\sum_{m>n}(m-n)k_m.
\]

The rapidly decreasing inverse satisfies \(\Delta\mathcal Gk=k\). Consequently

\[
\widehat J=\overline J-\mathcal Gk,
\qquad \Delta\widehat J=a,
\qquad
\boxed{\widehat J_0-\widehat J_1
=\int_0^\infty(J_0-J_1)\,du-\sum_{n\ge1}K_n(0)
=\sum_{n\ge1}a_n.}
\tag{G3}
\]

Moreover \(\widehat J=\mathcal Ga\), by uniqueness of the rapidly decreasing inverse. Hence the source-integrated effective current, and not merely its trace, is independent of an admissible gauge. The two lower boundaries contain one scalar obstruction, exactly the total-sum obstruction of the original arithmetic recurrence.

One can move the entire source lower flux into the index lower flux explicitly. Choose a smooth source collar \(\chi\), with \(\chi(0)=1\) and compact support, and set

\[
L_n(u)=\chi(u)(\mathcal Gk)_n.
\]

Then \(\widetilde K_n(0)=k_n-\Delta(\mathcal Gk)_n=0\), while

\[
\int_0^\infty(\widetilde J_0-\widetilde J_1)\,du
=\int_0^\infty(J_0-J_1)\,du-\sum_{n\ge1}k_n.
\tag{G4}
\]

The minus sign follows from \(\int_0^\infty\chi'=-1\). This gauge is local in the source coordinate but generally nonlocal in the mode index. It need not preserve the finite-range polynomial ansatz, and its derivatives need not improve any norm. It provides no escape from the existing obstruction: it transfers an unchanged trace to a single boundary. The substantive remaining problem is still a source-derived one-sided bound for that invariant trace paired with the complete starting observation.

## 8. Assessment and recommended simplification


The useful geometric simplification is one-dimensionality of the entire backward stationary locus and the analytic principal factor \(s^r\) of its physical first jet. It permits scalar signed boundary estimates and actual-curve reconstruction constants while retaining the full vanishing order. Further frame changes or blow-ups do not by themselves decide the sign or order of the complete arithmetic boundary trace. The source-prescribed theta estimate remains open.

The practical target can therefore be stated with one total arithmetic trace and one physical scalar:

\[
W B_\partial\le C W^2+\varepsilon_0|W|
\]

on complete stationary starts in any prescribed cone with fixed \(\kappa>0\), together with the original uniform error and endpoint-interval requirements. Start with this scalar target before introducing another metric, pseudoinverse, or boundary normalization. The source-derived favorable sign or sufficient vanishing order remains the independent mathematical task.

Two exact non-theta controls show why the simplification alone cannot finish that task. In backward time \(s=T-t>0\), take \(H=y^2+2s\) on branch zero, or \(H=y^3+6sy\) on branch one. Both have stationary center \(y=0\), while at any fixed \(\delta>0\),

\[
\frac{W(T-s,\delta)}{W(T-s,0)}=1+\frac{\delta^2}{2s}\longrightarrow+\infty.
\]

They have exactly the scalar geometry proved here and still violate the desired bounded positive endpoint ratio. These are heat-polynomial controls, not theta sources.
