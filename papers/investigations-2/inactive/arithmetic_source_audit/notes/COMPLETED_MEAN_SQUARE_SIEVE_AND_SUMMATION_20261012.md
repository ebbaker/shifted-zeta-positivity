## An explicit family satisfying the imported interface

Here is an independent verification of the source's family in
`K=Q(omega)`. For squarefree primary `k` prime to 2 and 3, put

\[
e_k=\begin{cases}0,&N(k)\equiv1\pmod4,\\
                 1,&N(k)\equiv3\pmod4,\end{cases}
\qquad
\Xi_k(x)=(x/k)_2\kappa_\lambda(x)^{e_k},
\]

where `kappa_lambda` is the nontrivial quadratic character modulo
`lambda`. For `e_k=0`, this supplementary factor is omitted.

The units are generated by `-1` and `omega`.
The unit `omega` is itself a square, so `(omega/k)_2=1`.
Also

\[
(-1/k)_2=(-1)^{(N(k)-1)/2}=(-1)^{e_k},
\quad
\kappa_\lambda(-1)=-1,
\quad\kappa_\lambda(\omega)=1.
\]

Thus `Xi_k` is trivial on all units. Since the Eisenstein field
has class number one, evaluating it on a generator of a principal
ideal gives a well-defined finite Hecke character. Its infinite type
is trivial.

At each prime dividing the squarefree odd ideal `k`, the local
quadratic character is nontrivial. It therefore has primitive finite
conductor exactly `k`. If `e_k=1`, the nontrivial factor at
`lambda` has exact conductor `lambda`; it is coprime to
`k`. The full primitive conductor is therefore

\[
\mathfrak f_k=(k)\lambda^{e_k}.
\]

There is **no conductor at 2** in this denominator-character
realization. The ideal defining the finite class group may include 2,
as `24 O` does, without requiring the individual character to
ramify at 2.

For a primary column `n equiv1 mod3`, the supplement satisfies
`kappa_lambda(n)=1`, so
`Xi_k((n))=chi_k(n)^3`. Restricting the row and column indices
to ideals coprime to a fixed modulus supported on `S` is therefore
compatible with the source's matrix.

The primary generator of a product is the product of the primary
generators, so reduction modulo `24 O` gives a multiplicative
finite-class map. The quadratic reciprocity quotient is the fixed
bicharacter `R(k,n)`, obtained by cubing sextic reciprocity.
If `k_1,k_2` are coprime and in the same such class, their
`e_k` are equal. The lambda supplements cancel; every prime
in `k_1 k_2` retains its nontrivial local quadratic character.
The primitive inducing character of their product consequently has
conductor `k_1 k_2`, as required.

When both `e_k=1`, their zero-extended finite-character product
still vanishes at `lambda`, whereas its primitive inducing character
need not. This does not obstruct the family interface: all queried
ideals are prime to the fixed class modulus, and the external paper
explicitly uses the primitive inducing product in this condition.

The imported theorem is stated with `Xi_n(k)`, while the desired
matrix has `Xi_k(n)`. Partitioning both indices into the finite
reciprocity classes makes their quotient a constant sign. This
transposes the theorem into the stated bound with only a fixed
class-count factor. Equivalently, the symmetric large-sieve matrix
norm can be used by duality.

## Columns that meet the fixed excluded primes

The source's quadratic lemma does not explicitly exclude `S`
from its squarefree column `n`. That is acceptable, but must not
be achieved by simply dropping this column mask from the imported
theorem.

Write `n=s n_0`, where `s` is the product of the prime
factors of `n` lying in `S`. There are finitely many choices of
`s`, because `n` is squarefree and `S` is fixed.
Primary `n` is automatically prime to `lambda`, so the
lambda prime contributes no choice. For rows prime to `S`,

\[
\chi_k(n)^3=\chi_k(s)^3\chi_k(n_0)^3,
\qquad |\chi_k(s)^3|=1.
\]

For each fixed `s`, the first factor is a row multiplier of
unit modulus. The coefficient sequence
`beta(s n_0)` is arbitrary, and `n_0` is squarefree and
prime to the fixed modulus. Its norm range is scaled by the fixed
number `N(s)`. Applying Cauchy over the finitely many choices
of `s` and the imported estimate to each produces the source's
same bound, with a constant depending on `S`.

If the scaled range is below one, it is either empty or contains only
a bounded set of ideals. The theorem can be applied with length at
least one; because the unscaled `U>=1` and `s` is fixed,
this does not change the claimed `H+U` bound.

## Independence from the moving row

The row is `u_0 t k_0`, with `t` and `g` fixed and
`k_0` squarefree, primary, and prime to `S g`. Empty
ranges are discarded, leaving
`H_0=H/N(t)>=1`. After fixing the ray class of `k_0`,
`lem:reflection-uniformity` makes the cusp sequence `d`,
additive character `psi`, and `c_0` independent of
`k_0`. The active fixed primes are those dividing `t g`
outside `S`; every prime dividing `k_0` has exponent one
and supplies the quadratic character.

The resulting bare coefficient

\[
A_{u,m}(n,b)=
{d(u\lambda^mnb^3)\alpha(u\lambda^mnb^3)
 \psi(u\lambda^{m+4}nb^3)
 \over 3^{m/6}\sqrt{N(b)}}
\]

has magnitude at most 27 and is independent of `k_0` in
the fixed class. Fixed-prime reindexing modifies it only by zero
extensions, fixed local characters, or substitutions
`n -> p n`, `b -> p b`; all remain independent of
`k_0`.

Extracted phases `chi_{k_0}(p)^3`,
`chi_{k_0}(u lambda^{m+4})^3`, and the original
reflection scalar may vary with `k_0`, but remain bounded and
are put into `c_{iota,m}(k_0)`. The later triangle inequality
allows these row-dependent scalars. It does not allow the coefficient
`A_{iota,m}(n,b)` itself to depend arbitrarily on the moving
row; that stronger assertion is neither needed nor used.

The residual restrictions `(k_0,gS)=1` and the fixed ray class
are retained during transformation. Once the fixed coefficients have
been obtained, their mean squares may be bounded by the larger set
of all squarefree rows in the quadratic lemma. This enlargement is
valid even though the transformed identity was only needed on the
original restricted row set.

## Common Mellin majorant and the coefficient norm

For a fixed block `m,U,B`, let

\[
z_*={3^m U B^3\over Y}.
\]

The smooth weight in the column variable `x=N(n)/U` is
`V(x) V_*^sharp(Rx)`, where

\[
R={3^m U N(b)^3 H_0^2\over YN(k_0)^2}\ge z_*.
\]

Here `N(k_0)<=H_0` and `N(b)>=B`. Its logarithmic
derivatives up to order two are bounded by
`C_{I,A} (1+R)^(-A)`, hence by
`C_{I,A} (1+z_*)^(-A)`. Mellin inversion in this one
column variable gives coefficients satisfying the **common pointwise**
majorant

\[
|c_{b,k_0}(s)|\ll
(1+z_*)^{-A}(1+|s|)^{-2}.
\]

The weaker statement that each row's coefficients have a bounded
integral would not be sufficient to put the row sum inside an
arbitrary row-dependent Mellin integral. The pointwise common bound
in the source is exactly what makes the recombination valid.

For each `b,s`, the sieve column coefficients are

\[
\beta_{b,s}(n)={A_m(n,b)\over\sqrt{N(n)}}(N(n)/U)^{is}.
\]

They are independent of `k_0`, and their squared norm is

\[
\sum_{N(n)\asymp U}|\beta_{b,s}(n)|^2
\ll a^2\sum_{N(n)\asymp U}N(n)^{-1}\ll a^2.
\]

This bound is uniform in the Mellin variable, since the final factor
has modulus one. Ideal counting, rather than a cancellation claim,
gives the last inequality.

Weighted Cauchy for the Mellin integral has total majorant mass
`O((1+z_*)^(-A))`. Using that same majorant in the second
factor yields `(1+z_*)^(-2A)`, not merely one copy of
this decay. The subsequent Cauchy in `b` uses weights
`N(b)^(-1)` and

\[
\sum_{B\le N(b)<2B}N(b)^{-1}\ll1.
\]

There is no factor `B` left over. Thus the displayed result

\[
\sum_{k_0}|\mathscr S_{m;U,B}(k_0)|^2
\ll a^2 3^{-2m/3}(H_0U)^\sigma(H_0+U)
      (1+3^mUB^3/Y)^{-2A}
\]

has the correct coefficient norm, row/column lengths, and both
Cauchy factors.

## Infinite dyadic sums, including Y below one

For `Z>0`, define the dyadic sums

\[
S_0(Z)=\sum_{U,B\ge1}(1+UB^3/Z)^{-2},
\quad
S_1(Z)=\sum_{U,B\ge1}\sqrt U(1+UB^3/Z)^{-2}.
\]

For `0<Z<=1`, direct domination by the large-argument bound gives

\[
S_0(Z)\ll Z^2\sum_{U,B}U^{-2}B^{-6}\ll Z^2,
\quad
S_1(Z)\ll Z^2\sum_{U,B}U^{-3/2}B^{-6}\ll Z^2
\le\sqrt Z.
\]

For `Z>=1`, the region `UB^3<=Z` contains
`O(log^2(2+Z))` pairs. For a fixed `B`, summing
`sqrt U` up to `U<=Z/B^3` costs
`O(sqrt Z B^(-3/2))`; the dyadic `B` sum converges.
Outside that region, geometric decay gives the same orders.
Consequently the two bounds used in the source hold for every
positive `Z`:

\[
S_0(Z)\ll\log^2(2+Z),\qquad S_1(Z)\ll\sqrt Z.
\]

Taking `Z=Y/3^m`, the lambda-adic contribution to the first
part is bounded by

\[
\sum_{m\ge-4}3^{-m/3}\sqrt{H_0}\,
\log^2(2+Y/3^m)
\ll\sqrt{H_0}\log^2(2+Y).
\]

The second part is bounded by

\[
\sum_{m\ge-4}3^{-m/3}\sqrt{Y/3^m}
=\sqrt Y\sum_{m\ge-4}3^{-5m/6}\ll\sqrt Y.
\]

The lower bound `m>=-4` is essential, and is supplied by the
theta-support lemma. No logarithm of `1/Y` is introduced for
small `Y`.

These summable bounds justify the triangle inequality in
`ell^2(k_0)` over all blocks and all `m`. The row set is
finite. One may first take finite truncations and then pass to the
limit; summability of the norms gives convergence, while absolute
convergence of the transformed series identifies the limit with the
original transformed expression. Row-dependent bounded scalars
`c_{iota,m}(k_0)` do not change these estimates.

Absolute convergence before regrouping can also be seen directly:
for fixed transformed data, coefficients are bounded by a fixed
multiple of
`3^(-m/3)/(sqrt(N(n)) N(b))`, while the transformed weight
decays faster than any power of
`3^m N(n)N(b)^3`. Dyadic ideal counting therefore gives
geometrically summable tails in each of the three variables.
There are only finitely many terms whose weight argument is below
one for each fixed data set.

## Ambient powers and derivative budget

The local updates satisfy `Y<<D^{C_1}` for fixed
`C_1=C_1(C_0)` with fixed arithmetic constants. An independent
bound follows from `Y_0=N(c_0)^2 H_0^2/X` and the fact that
no local update exceeds a factor `N(p)^2`:

\[
Y\ll {H_0^2N(\operatorname{rad}(tg))^2\over X}
\le {H_0^2N(t)^2N(g)^2\over X}
={H^2N(g)^2\over X}\le D^{4C_0}.
\]

Thus one can use `C_1=4C_0`, with a fixed implied constant.

Because `m>=-4` and `B>=1`,
`U<=81Yz_*`, so

\[
(H_0 U)^{\sigma/2}
\ll D^{(C_0+C_1)\sigma/2}(1+z_*)^{\sigma/2}.
\]

Choose `0<sigma<=1` and
`(C_0+C_1)sigma/2<=epsilon_1`. With `A=3`,
the post-square-root decay is at least
`(1+z_*)^(-2)`, giving the source's summable norm bound.
This choice is valid independently of whether `Y>=1`.

The theta-kernel decay requires only `A=3` and logarithmic
derivatives up to order two for the one-variable Mellin majorant.
In `eq:ray-kernel`, these choices give a Mellin integrand weight
at most `(1+|u|)^16`. Eighteen integrations by parts yield an
integrable majorant, so `J>=18` is a sufficient fixed order for
`V_*`, and hence for `W`, on the fixed support interval.
The dyadic cutoff itself needs only its uniformly bounded first
two derivatives. No derivative order increases with `m,U,B` or
with the moving row. The source's statement
`J=J(epsilon,C_0)` is more permissive than necessary here
and is consistent with this uniform bound.

The number of fixed-prime branches, fixed choices of `t`,
and local reindexing choices is bounded by a fixed power of the
divisor function of polynomial-size ideals. Triangle/Cauchy may
square this number, but it remains `D^eta` for any chosen
`eta>0`. Likewise `log^4(2+Y)` is absorbed in a reserved
small power of `D`, uniformly when `Y<<D^{C_1}`.
One can choose `epsilon_1`, the sieve exponent, and each
divisor/logarithm loss after fixing the final `epsilon`.
This is a finite budget; the infinite block sums have already
converged absolutely at the norm level.

Finally, the established conductor ledger gives

\[
a^2\le1,\qquad
a^2Y\ll H_0^2N(t)^2N(g)/X.
\]

Thus a prepared branch contributes at most a constant times
`D^epsilon a^2(H_0+Y)` for a derivative-normalized weight.
After summing the fixed branches for a given `t`, and reserving their
divisor losses in the final small-power budget, this gives

\[
\sum_{k_0}|T(X;u_0t k_0,g)|^2
\ll D^\varepsilon\bigl(H+H^2N(g)/X\bigr).
\]

This step uses `H_0=H/N(t)`, and summing the divisor-bounded
choices of `t` is covered by the same reserved losses. The
weight was normalized to have derivative norm at most one;
homogeneity restores its squared norm.
