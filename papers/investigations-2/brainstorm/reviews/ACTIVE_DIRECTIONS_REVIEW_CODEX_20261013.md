## Concrete sharpening of angular gluing

Write
\[
\Theta(r,y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\pi n^2r+2\pi iny},\qquad
F(u,z)=e^{u+\pi z^2/2}\Theta(e^{4u},e^{2u}z).
\]
Classical Jacobi inversion gives the exact identity
\[
F(u,z)=F(-u,-iz).
\]
At fixed \(z\), define \(M=(\partial_u^2-1)F/16\). The zero mode is
annihilated, both real \(u\) tails decay on compact complex \(z\) sets,
and \(M(u,0)=\Phi_e(u)\). Consequently
\[
K_t(x,z)=\tfrac12\int_{\mathbb R}e^{tu^2+ixu}M(u,z)\,du
\]
is an exact heat-compatible angular lift with
\(K_t(x,0)=H_t(x)\) and \(K_t(x,z)=K_t(-x,-iz)\).
This removes a particular half-line/zero-mode bookkeeping difficulty;
it does not supply a sign. The derivation and domain are in the
[angular review seeds](../../active/02_angular_jacobi_gluing/notes/ANGULAR_GLUING_AND_RIGIDITY_REVIEW_SEEDS_20261013.md).
The imported transformation is [DLMF 20.7.32](https://dlmf.nist.gov/20.7.E32);
the lift and its implications here are our explicit application.

A useful negative check is
\[
[z^2]K_t(x,z)=\frac{i\pi}{2}(2tH_x-xH).
\]
It vanishes automatically whenever \(H=H_x=0\). Thus the first angular
coefficient cannot be used as an independently nonzero anchor. The remaining
opportunity is global elliptic/lattice information or a signed relation
among complete angular channels. This is why I favor a **specific elliptic
transport calculation**, rather than another derivation of scalar modular
reflection.

The distinction is sharp. In the family
\(\Theta_c(r,y)=\sum c_ne^{-\pi n^2r+2\pi iny}\), the full elliptic
quasiperiod forces adjacent coefficients to agree. With \(c_0=1\), it
selects genuine theta. Approximate quasiperiodicity at one scale has
exponentially weak control of high coefficients. The note states the exact
defect energy and also shows how arbitrarily small positive relative
perturbations of the genuine density can create a distant positive-time
multiple zero while retaining scalar Green reflection. These controls
explain which constraints matter; uniqueness of the selected source is
still not a sign theorem for its Fourier transform.

## A distinct addition from collision geometry

Let \(V(t,x)=(H,H_x)\), with coordinate order \((t,x)\). At an ordinary
collision,
\[
\det DV=-H_{xx}^2<0.
\]
The sign cannot cancel against another ordinary heat collision. The
[new program](../../active/17_spacetime_collision_degree/notes/INITIAL_RESEARCH_PROGRAM_20261013.md)
extends the local calculation: an analytic heat zero of spatial multiplicity
\(m\ge2\) has index \(-\lfloor m/2\rfloor\). Hence, on a compact
rectangle with a nonzero boundary pair,
\[
\operatorname{wind}_{\partial R}(H+iH_x)
=-\sum_{p\in R:\,H=H_x=0}\lfloor m_p/2\rfloor.
\]
A certified zero winding therefore excludes every finite multiplicity
inside, without enclosing every interior stationary branch individually.
The note gives a heat-polynomial proof, and exact rational boundary checks
for multiplicities 2 through 10 confirm the seed and orientation.

This is new to the searched portfolio, not a claim of a new general theorem
in the literature. It is closely related to classical parabolic zero-number
theory, and to endpoint real-zero counts when no zeros cross the spatial
sides. The existing complex argument-principle certificates count zeros of
one spatial holomorphic function; this one counts joint real zeros of a
spacetime pair. The potential gain is a different boundary computation or
a theta-specific homotopy giving its winding. That gain must be measured.

First benchmark one genuine bounded rectangle against the existing joint-jet
certificate, including precision, derivative bounds, cutoff crossings and
all tail errors. A faster certificate or a new boundary identity justifies
continuation. A polynomial check alone, or an equivalent endpoint count
with no cost advantage, does not. No finite collection of rectangles
settles the unbounded-height and small-positive-time obligations for RH.
