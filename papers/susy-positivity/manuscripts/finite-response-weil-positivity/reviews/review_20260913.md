## 1. Points to revise or clarify

### 1.1 Distinguish the canonical minimum from an effectively certified cutoff

**Priority: moderate; constructive specification, not a failure of the analytic theorem.** Locations: [M, lines 123–131, equation (2.8)](/Users/ebbaker/Documents/shifted-zeta-positivity/papers/finite-response-weil-positivity/manuscript.tex:123), [M, lines 213–215](/Users/ebbaker/Documents/shifted-zeta-positivity/papers/finite-response-weil-positivity/manuscript.tex:213), and [D, lines 607–619](/Users/ebbaker/Documents/shifted-zeta-positivity/papers/finite-response-weil-positivity/derivations.tex:607).

The definition

\[
N(n)=\min\{j\ge1:b_j\ge\beta_n+\delta_0\}
\]

is mathematically valid, and it gives the required high-sector inequality. However, convergent rational enclosures of the quantities being compared do not by themselves decide an equality such as

\[
b_j=\beta_n+\delta_0.
\]

The text does not exclude this equality. Its observation that integer lengths cannot coincide with prime-power thresholds settles prime enumeration, not the separate cutoff comparison. I am not claiming that an equality occurs or that the canonical sequence is uncomputable; the missing point is a justification of the proposed uniform constructive interpretation.

The easy repair is already permitted by M: use a deterministic search for **any cutoff with a certified strict margin**. For example, at stage \(r\), enclose \(\beta_n\) and \(b_j\), \(1\le j\le r\), to accuracy \(2^{-r}\). Stop at the first stage with some certified

\[
\underline b_j>\overline\beta_n+\delta_0,
\]

and choose the least such \(j\) at that stage. This terminates because \(b_j\to\infty\). It avoids stalling while resolving an equality at a smaller index. State that the computational family uses this cutoff; retain the exact minimum as an optional mathematical convention. Every positivity equivalence remains valid, although the actual matrix dimensions and entries can differ between cutoff conventions.

### 1.2 Display the spatial-to-Fourier gamma identity

**Priority: minor proof-completeness improvement.** Location: [D, lines 71–78 and 111–145](/Users/ebbaker/Documents/shifted-zeta-positivity/papers/finite-response-weil-positivity/derivations.tex:71), between equations (2.2) and (3.1).

D identifies the spatial gamma energy \(K[F]\), then constructs a closed source with multiplier \(b(\tau^2)\). The equality of these two energies is correct but should be explicitly written, since it is the bridge from the cited Weil normalization to the operator used in every later theorem:

\[
K[F]=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
\left[2\int_0^\infty(1-\cos(\tau r))n_\gamma(r)\,dr\right]
|\widehat F(\tau)|^2\,d\tau,
\]

\[
2\int_0^\infty(1-\cos(\tau r))e^{-ar}\,dr
=\frac2a\frac{\tau^2}{a^2+\tau^2}.
\]

Plancherel and Tonelli then give \(K[E_Lf]=t_L[f]\). The nonnegative integrands justify the interchange, including infinite energy before restricting to the form domain. This addition makes the normalization chain directly auditable without asking the reader to supply it.

### 1.3 Define the projected source output and the defect output

**Priority: minor, confined to the optional interpretation.** Location: [D, lines 477–480](/Users/ebbaker/Documents/shifted-zeta-positivity/papers/finite-response-weil-positivity/derivations.tex:477).

The statement that a positive source “has a low output orthogonal to its high range” needs the projection/minimization operation to be specified. The raw low output \(\mathcal A p\) is generally not orthogonal to high outputs when \(B\ne0\). The defect output is also not defined in this companion.

For a closed source with \(\mathcal A^*\mathcal A=T=W_L+\alpha I\), define

\[
C p=\mathcal A\bigl(p-(H+\alpha I)^{-1}Bp\bigr).
\]

Then \(C^*C=G\), and pairing with \(\mathcal A h\) for high form-domain \(h\) proves the asserted orthogonality. One can take the abstract defect output to be \(D^{1/2}p\). The map \(Cp\mapsto D^{1/2}p\) is a contraction exactly when \(D\preceq G\), and extends by zero on the orthogonal complement. This supplies a precise argument without importing undefined objects from the physical investigation. Alternatively, delete the paragraph; the matrix result preceding it is sufficient.

### 2.1 Weil normalization and the negative constant — checks out

Locations: [D §2, equations (2.1)–(2.4)](/Users/ebbaker/Documents/shifted-zeta-positivity/papers/finite-response-weil-positivity/derivations.tex:51); M (2.1)–(2.4).

Directly,

\[
g(r)=\int F(x)\overline{F(x-r)}\,dx
=\overline{\langle F,U_rF\rangle},
\]

so the equality of real parts in D is correct. The spatial energy is
\(\int_0^\infty(2g(0)-g(r)-g(-r))n_\gamma(r)\,dr\), with no missing factor of two. Rewriting the subtracted gamma integral leaves

\[
w_0=-\log(4\pi)-\gamma_E
-2\sum_{k\ge0}\left(\frac1{2k+1/2}-\frac1{2k+1}\right)
=\psi(1/4)-\log\pi.
\]

The sign is negative, and the quoted special-value formula gives exactly
\(-\gamma_E-\pi/2-3\log2-\log\pi\).

The pole kernel is \(2\cosh((x-y)/2)\), hence \(2cc^*-2ss^*\), rather than a positive rank-two operator. The prime coefficients are \((\log p)p^{-m/2}\), not \(m\log p\). The strict activity condition \(m\log p<L\) is correct: at equality the overlap has measure zero. The extension to the closed form is justified once the gamma identity in §1.2 above is made explicit.

### 2.2 Gamma source, closed domain, and cores — checks out

Locations: [D §3](/Users/ebbaker/Documents/shifted-zeta-positivity/papers/finite-response-weil-positivity/derivations.tex:111), equations (3.1)–(3.3); M lines 77–82.

Substituting \(z=1/4\), \(t=\sqrt s/2\) into the digamma partial fractions gives

\[
\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac1{k+z}-\frac1{k+z+it}\right)
=\frac2{a_k}\frac{s}{a_k^2+s}.
\]

The series is positive and locally uniformly convergent. The stated asymptotic has the correct \(-\log2\) term and remainder \(O(\tau^{-2})\): taking real parts removes the apparent first-order imaginary correction. The formulas agree with [DLMF 5.7.6](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.7#E6), [5.11.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11#E2), and [5.4.13 and 5.4.19](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.4).

The one-mass minimizer and both source components are correct. Their squared multipliers add to

\[
\frac{2\tau^4/a+2a\tau^2}{(a^2+\tau^2)^2}
=\frac2a\frac{\tau^2}{a^2+\tau^2}.
\]

The direct-sum source is closed on its maximal square-summability domain: convergence of the total output identifies each bounded component, and its limiting square norm is precisely the form energy. Pullback by zero extension preserves closedness. The equivalence of \(1+b(\tau^2)\) with the logarithmic weight gives the stated domain.

The smooth-core proof is sound. Dilation is uniformly bounded near one in the weighted Fourier norm; density proves strong continuity; contraction leaves a support gap; mollification within that gap converges in the same norm. No endpoint trace is being imposed accidentally. Boundedness of the finitely many prime/pole terms gives the claimed semibounded self-adjoint perturbation.

The Fourier-band proof of compact embedding is valid: the omitted part is uniformly small on a form-norm unit ball, and reconstruction from a fixed band onto a finite interval is Hilbert–Schmidt. Thus the associated operators have compact resolvent and only finitely many negative eigenvalues. The scalar tail

\[
\sum_{k\ge J}\frac2{a_k}\frac{s}{a_k^2+s}
\le s\left(\frac2{a_J^3}+\frac1{2a_J^2}\right)
\]

has the correct integral-test constant for masses spaced by two.

Chain lengths are at most \(\lceil L/\log p\rceil\) almost everywhere, including when the ratio is an integer. The absolute row-sum estimate controls the self-adjoint chain norm and gives the negative-prime lower bound in the correct direction.

The pole norms are

Consequently \(R_L\ge-\beta_LI\), and the high compression is at least \(\delta_0I\). There is no assumption of RH in this step. ### 2.4 Boundary sign, boundedness, and equality of domains — checks out

Locations: [D §5.1](/Users/ebbaker/Documents/shifted-zeta-positivity/papers/finite-response-weil-positivity/derivations.tex:241); M Theorem 3.1 and (3.1).

The free and Neumann Green kernels have derivative jump \(-1\), and the Neumann kernel has zero derivative at both endpoints. Their difference satisfies

\[
K_a=2a(R_{N,a}-R_{f,a})\succeq0.
\]

Since a mass contributes \((2/a)I-2aR\), the identity is
\(T_{L,J}=b_J(H_N)+K^{(J)}\), with a **plus** sign. The coefficient eigenvalues \((1-r_a)^{-1}\) and \((1+r_a)^{-1}\) are both positive.

In the kernel sum, the two singular images are \(n(x+y)\) and \(n(2L-x-y)\). The other images have path lengths at least \(L\), and the bound
\(2[n(2L)+n(L)]/(1-e^{-L})\) is valid for each fixed \(L>0\). Furthermore,

\[
n(t)=\frac1{2t}+\frac14+O(t)
\]

near zero. Thus the difference from half of each endpoint Carleman kernel is bounded on the finite square and hence Hilbert–Schmidt. The weighted Schur estimate supplies a uniform norm bound on all partial sums; their positivity gives a bounded strong limit.

Passing to increasing energy limits is legitimate even for inputs with infinite energy. The bounded correction changes neither which inputs have finite form energy nor the operator domain under bounded perturbation. Thus both domain equalities in (5.3) follow, and finite cosine sums form an **operator** core. This establishes the needed membership of all low columns in \(\operatorname{Dom}W_L\); a merely formal matrix decomposition would not have sufficed.

### 2.5 Essential norm and finite-input tails — checks out

The normalized logarithmic-window test functions have the displayed triangular-weight convolution quotient. In particular, finite-rank mass sums cannot converge in full operator norm. The proof given in D is self-contained; the [Yafaev reference](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.5709v2) is appropriate background.

The integrated cosine column correctly cancels the denominator \(1-r_a^2\):

\[
K_ae_j=\frac{\nu_ja}{a^2+\omega_j^2}(u_a+(-1)^jv_a).
\]

The \(L^2\) estimate \(\sqrt2\nu_ja^{-3/2}\) is valid without assuming \(aL\) is large. Summation with spacing two and
\(\sum_{j<h}\nu_j^2=(2h-1)/L\) gives exactly the stated \(\eta_{h,J}\). Opposite-parity boundary entries vanish; the numerator \(1-(-1)^je^{-a_kL}\) and the factor \(2\nu_i\nu_j\) in equal-parity entries are correct. Fixed finite-input convergence is used consistently and is never silently replaced by convergence on the entire Hilbert space.

## 3. Proposed simplifications and useful refinements

### 3.1 Shorten the enclosure convergence proof

Location: [M, lines 290–297](/Users/ebbaker/Documents/shifted-zeta-positivity/papers/finite-response-weil-positivity/manuscript.tex:290); D lines 525–538.

The theorem only needs the enclosure gap to vanish. It does not need the stronger conclusion \(R_K\to0\). With the bounded \(E\) above, coercivity gives

\[
\|Y_K\|\le\delta_0^{-1}\|B\|,
\qquad
\|R_K\|\le\|B\|+\|E\|\|Y_K\|
\le C_L:=\|B\|(1+\|E\|/\delta_0).
\]

Therefore

\[
\|U_{L,K}-L_{L,K}\|
\le\frac{C_L^2}{d_K}\longrightarrow0.
\]

Since \(L_{L,K}\preceq S_L\preceq U_{L,K}\), both matrices converge in norm to \(S_L\), with each error bounded by the same gap. This proof uses only boundedness of the perturbation, finite-system coercivity, and \(d_K\to\infty\). For fixed \(L\) it also gives a crude \(O_L(1/\log K)\) bound. It is not a practical complexity estimate. Keep the current stronger residual-convergence proof in D if desired for §10, and use this shorter argument in M.

### 3.2 Split the complete response into even and odd blocks

Location: [D, lines 344–346](/Users/ebbaker/Documents/shifted-zeta-positivity/papers/finite-response-weil-positivity/derivations.tex:344); M after (3.4).

Parity separation applies to **the full operator**, not just its boundary correction. Let \((Jf)(x)=f(L-x)\). Then

\[
JV_dJ=V_d^*,\quad Jc=c,\quad Js=-s,
\quad Je_j=(-1)^je_j.
\]

Hence \(J\) commutes with \(W_L\), \(P\), and \(Q\), and therefore with the high inverse and the Schur matrix in the appropriate spaces. Thus

\[
S_L=S_L^{\rm even}\oplus S_L^{\rm odd}.
\]

This roughly halves each block dimension and removes zero couplings from the finite-trial calculations. Choose the trials to respect parity to retain the same split in the enclosures. Both blocks must be tested; no restricted version of Weil's criterion is needed. A further optional improvement is to use the smaller remainder bound \(-w_0+\text{prime bound}\) on the even high sector, where the negative odd pole vanishes.

### 3.3 Use a faster tail for finite matrix entries

Location: [D, equation (6.2)](/Users/ebbaker/Documents/shifted-zeta-positivity/papers/finite-response-weil-positivity/derivations.tex:339).

For equal-parity entries, direct estimation of that series gives

\[
\left|\langle e_i,(K_L-K^{(J)})e_j\rangle\right|
\le2\nu_i\nu_j(1+e^{-a_JL})
\left(a_J^{-2}+\frac1{2a_J}\right).
\]

Indeed, the rational part of each summand is at most \(a_k^{-2}\), and its exponential factor is at most \(1+e^{-a_JL}\). The integral test supplies the parenthesis. Opposite-parity errors are zero. Consequently,

\[
\|P_h(K_L-K^{(J)})P_h\|
\le\frac{2(2h-1)}L(1+e^{-a_JL})
\left(a_J^{-2}+\frac1{2a_J}\right).
\]

This is an \(O(J^{-1})\) bound for a fixed finite compression, compared with the \(O(J^{-1/2})\) column bound. Use it for low matrix entries and finite Galerkin matrices. Retain the original column bound for full residual functions; the faster compressed estimate does not replace that bound.

### 3.4 Put the residual identity in variational form once

Locations: M §§4–5; [D, start of §10](/Users/ebbaker/Documents/shifted-zeta-positivity/papers/finite-response-weil-positivity/derivations.tex:557).

Introduce

\[
C(Y)=(P-Y)^*W_L(P-Y),\qquad R(Y)=B-HY.
\]

Then

\[
S_L=C(Y)-R(Y)^*H^{-1}R(Y).
\]

This one identity explains the energy minimization, arbitrary-trial lower bounds, Galerkin upper bounds, and the finite-mass allowance. It reduces repeated expansions and makes it immediately clear why the residual term must be subtracted. The current algebra is correct; this is a presentation improvement.

### 3.5 Remove the maximum from the prime row-sum formula

Location: [M, lines 106–114](/Users/ebbaker/Documents/shifted-zeta-positivity/papers/finite-response-weil-positivity/manuscript.tex:106).

The maximum occurs in the central row(s). For \(h\ge1\),

\[
r_h(q)=\frac{q}{1-q}
\left(2-q^{\lfloor(h-1)/2\rfloor}-q^{\lceil(h-1)/2\rceil}\right).
\]

To see this, the difference between consecutive row sums is \(q^i-q^{h-i}\). This gives an elementary closed formula, including \(r_1=0\), and simplifies both the definition and evaluation of \(\beta_L\).
