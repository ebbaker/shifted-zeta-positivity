\begin{abstract}
This research note gives an explicit Neumann boundary representation for
the gamma part of the localized Weil form. The correction is bounded and
noncompact, has essential norm $\pi/2$, and admits controlled tails on finite
input spaces. An image expansion retains the endpoint singularities and
gives an exponentially decreasing remainder in operator norm at fixed interval
length. An unconditional high-frequency lower bound allows exact Schur
elimination; standard residual identities then give lower and upper response
enclosures. We record the resulting localized positivity criterion and its
RH-equivalent formulation over unbounded interval lengths, using the classical
Weil criterion. The arithmetic sign of the response remains unresolved.
No new positivity interval or practical complexity bound is established.
\end{abstract}

\section{Explicit boundary formulas and their scope}
The localized gamma operator differs from its diagonal Neumann comparison
by a bounded boundary correction. Its two endpoint singularities prevent
approximation by finite mass sums in operator norm, while its action on
any fixed finite input space has a controlled tail. This note records the
kernel, its essential norm, and two complementary truncation estimates:
finite mass sums control the input columns, and grouped images retain the
singularities while controlling the remainder in operator norm.

An unconditional positive high sector then permits exact Schur elimination,
retaining every coupling to the low modes. Standard residual identities
give lower and upper bounds for that response. These calculations are
developed from \cite{Investigation}; they provide explicit formulas and
error bounds for this operator, without a general priority claim.

Localized Weil positivity, finite-codimension positivity and variational
formulations have substantial antecedents; see \cite[Section 1]{Suzuki}.
Connes--Consani--Moscovici give a localized form core, explicit matrices and
finite restrictions, whose smallest eigenvalues recover the localized
lower bound \cite[Proposition 3.4 and Sections 4--5]{CCM}.
Their Sections 7--8 also discuss the prolate approximation to the lowest
eigenfunction. The comparison principles, localization and Schur machinery
are established tools; the particular boundary and remainder formulas are
the focus here. The later matrix conjecture records the remaining arithmetic
sign through the classical Weil criterion. Neither that reformulation nor
the numerical illustration establishes a new positivity interval or a
computational advantage over existing restrictions.

\section{The arithmetic operator and an unconditional cutoff}
Use $X_L=L^2(0,L)$, zero extension $E_L$, and
$\widehat F(\tau)=\int_\R F(x)e^{-i\tau x}\,dx$. Inner products are
antilinear in the first slot. Set
\begin{align}
a_k&=2k+\tfrac12,&
b(s)&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac2{a_k}\frac{s}{a_k^2+s}
=\re\psi(\tfrac14+i\sqrt{s}/2)-\psi(\tfrac14),\label{eq:b}\\
w_0&=\psi(\tfrac14)-\log\pi,&
\mathcal D_L&=\left\{f\in X_L:
\int_\R\log(2+|\tau|)|\widehat{E_Lf}(\tau)|^2\,d\tau<\infty\right\}.
\end{align}
Let $T_L$ be the nonnegative operator associated with the closed form
\[
t_L[f]=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\R b(\tau^2)|\widehat{E_Lf}(\tau)|^2\,d\tau.
\]
For $d>0$, $U_dF(x)=F(x-d)$ and $V_d=E_L^*U_dE_L$. Put
$c_L(x)=\cosh((x-L/2)/2)$ and $s_L(x)=\sinh((x-L/2)/2)$.
The bounded remainder and the full operator are
\begin{align}
R_L={}&w_0I+2c_Lc_L^*-2s_Ls_L^*
-\sum_{\substack{p\ \mathrm{prime},\ m\ge1\\m\log p<L}}
 (\log p)p^{-m/2}(V_{m\log p}+V_{m\log p}^*),\label{eq:remainder}\\
W_L={}&T_L+R_L,\qquad Q_L[f]=t_L[f]+\ip f{R_Lf}.\label{eq:weil}
\end{align}
Here $vv^*f=v\ip v f$. The sum includes every active prime power.
All sums in $R_L$ are finite. In the additive normalization of the Weil
functional in \cite[Section 1.1]{Suzuki}, $Q_L$ is exactly the Weil form of
$E_Lf$. The normalization and its spatial-to-Fourier bridge are detailed in
\cite[Sections 2--3]{Companion}. The constant, both poles and all prime
powers are essential.

The digamma expansion \cite{DLMF} gives
$b(\tau^2)=\log|\tau|-\log2-\psi(1/4)+O(\tau^{-2})$.
Consequently $\mathcal D_L$ is the form domain and $C_c^\infty(0,L)$ is
a form core. The support contraction followed by mollification proves the
core assertion in this logarithmic Fourier norm \cite[Section 3]{Companion}.
Bounded perturbation then
defines the self-adjoint, semibounded $W_L$ on $\Dom T_L$.

Let $H_{\rm N}$ and $H_{\rm D}$ denote the Neumann and Dirichlet interval
Laplacians. Minimizing
\[
\frac2a\left(\norm{E_Lf-u}_{L^2(\R)}^2+a^{-2}\norm{u'}_{L^2(\R)}^2\right)
\]
over $u\in H^1(\R)$ gives $(2/a)s/(a^2+s)$ in Fourier space.
Restricting $u$ to the interval lowers this minimum; allowing only zero
extensions of $H_0^1(0,L)$ competitors raises it. Summation proves the
closed-form bracket
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bracket}
b(H_{\rm N})\le T_L\le b(H_{\rm D}).
\end{equation}
The positive resolvent representation belongs to the complete Bernstein
function framework of \cite{KM}. For fractional powers of the Laplacian,
the corresponding comparisons of the restricted form with the spectral
Dirichlet and Neumann forms are established in
\cite[Theorem 2]{MN} and \cite[Theorem 3]{Nazarov}, respectively.
Those theorems concern fractional powers; the variational argument above
proves \eqref{eq:bracket} for this digamma multiplier. The comparison does
not assign Neumann boundary conditions to the actual whole-line fields.

Write
\[
e_j(x)=\nu_j\cos(j\pi x/L),\quad
\nu_0=L^{-1/2},\quad\nu_j=\sqrt{2/L}\ (j>0),\quad
b_j=b((j\pi/L)^2).
\]
Let $P_h=\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}e_je_j^*$. For $q\in(0,1)$ define
\[
r_h(q)=\frac{q}{1-q}
\left(2-q^{\lfloor(h-1)/2\rfloor}-q^{\lceil(h-1)/2\rceil}\right),
\]
and specify
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
\beta_L=-w_0+2\sinh(L/2)-L+
\sum_{\log p<L}(\log p)r_{\lceil L/\log p\rceil}(p^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
The prime bound follows by decomposing translation by $\log p$ into finite
chains and bounding each symmetric correlation matrix by its largest row
sum $r_h$: successive row sums differ by $q^i-q^{h-i}$, so the
maximum occurs centrally. The odd pole costs at most $2\norm{s_L}^2=2\sinh(L/2)-L$;
the even pole is nonnegative. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
W_L\ge b(H_{\rm N})-\beta_LI.
\end{equation}
Fix $\delta_0=1/16$. For any $L>0$, call an integer $N\ge1$ admissible if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:N}
b_N>\beta_L+\delta_0,\qquad P=P_N,\quad Q=I-P.
\end{equation}
Such cutoffs exist because $b_j\to\infty$. At integer lengths, a terminating
rational-enclosure search can select an admissible $N(n)$ without deciding
equality at a smaller index \cite[Section 4]{Companion}. This is an optional
way to implement the construction. The mathematical family retains $N$
as an explicit parameter.

Let $H$ be the self-adjoint operator associated with the high form
$Q_L$ restricted to $Q\mathcal D_L$. Then $H\ge\delta_0I$ unconditionally,
so $H^{-1}$ is well defined with norm at most $\delta_0^{-1}$.
No invariance of $QX_L$ under $W_L$ is assumed.

\section{An explicit boundary operator}
The following representation makes that coupling accessible. For $a>0$ set
\[
u_a(x)=e^{-ax},\quad v_a(x)=e^{-a(L-x)},\quad r_a=e^{-aL},\qquad
K_a=\frac{u_au_a^*+v_av_a^*+r_a(u_av_a^*+v_au_a^*)}{1-r_a^2}.
\]
\begin{theorem}[Boundary representation]\label{thm:boundary}
The positive finite sums $K^{(J)}=\sum_{k<J}K_{a_k}$ converge strongly to
a bounded positive operator $K_L$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary}
T_L=b(H_{\rm N})+K_L,\qquad
\Dom T_L=\Dom b(H_{\rm N}),\quad
\mathcal D_L=\Dom b(H_{\rm N})^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Finite cosine sums are an operator core. The essential norm is
$\norm{K_L}_{\rm ess}=\pi/2$, so finite mass sums do not converge in
operator norm. Nevertheless, for the first $h$ cosine modes,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
\norm{(K_L-K^{(J)})P_h}\le
\eta_{h,J}:=\sqrt{\frac{2(2h-1)}L}
 (a_J^{-1/2}+a_J^{-3/2})\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The compressed free Green function is $e^{-a|x-y|}/(2a)$; the Neumann
Green function is
\[
\frac{\cosh(a\min(x,y))\cosh(a(L-\max(x,y)))}{a\sinh(aL)}.
\]
Their difference multiplied by $2a$ is $K_a$. Subtracting the corresponding
resolvent energies proves the finite-tower version of \eqref{eq:boundary}.
The coefficient matrix of $K_a$ is positive.

Let $n(t)=e^{-t/2}/(1-e^{-2t})$. Summing the kernels gives
$n(x+y)+n(2L-x-y)$ plus a bounded kernel. Since
$n(t)=(2t)^{-1}+O_L(1)$ on $(0,2L]$, this is one half of each endpoint
Carleman kernel, modulo a bounded-kernel, hence compact, operator. The
weighted Schur test bounds the Carleman operator by $\pi$. The partial
towers have the same uniform kernel bound, so monotone convergence yields
a bounded strong limit and the asserted domains.

Splitting the interval in half makes the two endpoint operators a direct
sum modulo compact operators. Each has essential norm $\pi$ before the
factor $1/2$: normalized functions proportional to $t^{-1/2}$ on $[1,R]$,
then dilated toward zero, have limiting Rayleigh quotient $\pi$ and tend
weakly to zero. This also proves the lower essential-norm bound; see the
Carleman background in \cite{Yafaev}.

Direct integration, with $\omega_j=j\pi/L$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:column}
K_ae_j=\frac{\nu_j a}{a^2+\omega_j^2}(u_a+(-1)^jv_a).
\end{equation}
Its norm is at most $\sqrt2\nu_j a^{-3/2}$. Sum the decreasing series
using $\sum_{k\ge J}a_k^{-3/2}\le a_J^{-3/2}+a_J^{-1/2}$, then sum squared
column bounds over $j<h$ to obtain \eqref{eq:tail}.
\end{proof}

In particular all low basis vectors lie in $\Dom W_L$. The boundary entries
vanish for opposite parities; for equal parities they are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entries}
\ip{e_i}{K_Le_j}=2\nu_i\nu_j\sum_{k\ge0}
\frac{a_k^2(1-(-1)^j e^{-a_kL})}
{(a_k^2+\omega_i^2)(a_k^2+\omega_j^2)}.
\end{equation}
The remaining entries are elementary integrals of the rank-two pole
kernel and of translated cosines on their overlap intervals. These formulas
specify the operator using gamma and prime data, independently of RH.
For a fixed finite compression the entry series has a sharper $O(J^{-1})$
mass tail; for residual norms, errors from a mass approximation are
controlled by the full-column tail \eqref{eq:tail}
\cite[Section 6]{Companion}.
An alternative image expansion sums the gamma masses exactly before
truncation. After retaining the singular endpoint images, its remaining
kernel has an explicit exponentially decreasing operator-norm bound
\cite[Section 5]{Companion}. Evaluating the resulting columns and residual
Grams still requires control of the endpoint singularities and integration
errors.

\section{Exact elimination and localized positivity}
For each $L$ and admissible $N$, use $P=P_N$, $Q=I-P$ and define
\[
A=PW_LP,\qquad B=QW_LP,\qquad
H=QW_LQ,\quad \Dom H=Q\Dom b(H_{\rm N}).
\]
Here $A$ is an $N$ by $N$ Hermitian matrix, $B:PX_L\to QX_L$ is
bounded, and $H$ is the operator associated with the high form above;
the displayed compression and domain follow from \eqref{eq:boundary}. Define the finite matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schur}
S_{L,N}=A-B^*H^{-1}B,\qquad S_{n,N}:=S_{L,N}\big|_{L=n}.
\end{equation}
For a fixed admissible pair $(L,N)$, abbreviate $S_L=S_{L,N}$.
Its entries involve an infinite-dimensional inverse. It is a finite matrix
with explicitly specified, approximable entries, not a formula involving
only finitely many elementary operations.

The operators defining $S_{n,N}$ use no zeta zeros or assumption on their
location. Their remaining sign is expressed by the following conjecture.
\begin{conjecture}[Finite response]\label{conj:main}
For every positive integer $n$ and every admissible integer $N$, the matrix
$S_{n,N}$ defined in \eqref{eq:schur} is positive semidefinite.
\end{conjecture}
Equivalently, for each $n$ it suffices to use one admissible $N$, as the
next theorem shows. No enclosure routine is part of the conjecture.
In energy language, allowing the positive high modes to respond to a low-mode
input never removes more than the low input's original energy.

\begin{theorem}[Localized positivity and the Weil criterion]\label{thm:equiv}
For each $L>0$ and each admissible $N$, $Q_L\ge0$ on $\mathcal D_L$
if and only if $S_{L,N}\succeq0$.
Consequently Conjecture \ref{conj:main} is equivalent to RH.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $f=p+h$, $p=Pf$, $h=Qf$. Completing the high square gives the
identity of closed forms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square}
Q_L[p+h]=\norm{H^{1/2}(h+H^{-1}Bp)}^2+\ip p{S_Lp}.
\end{equation}
Every term is defined: $p\in\Dom W_L$, $h\in\Dom H^{1/2}$, and
$H^{-1}Bp\in\Dom H$. If $S_L\succeq0$, this proves positivity. Conversely,
choose $h=-H^{-1}Bp$ to recover exactly $\ip p{S_Lp}$.

RH implies Weil positivity on smooth compactly supported functions.
The form core extends it to $\mathcal D_L$, so every $S_{n,N}\succeq0$.
For the converse, every compact support can be translated into $(0,n)$
for some integer $n$. The Weil form is translation invariant: its gamma
multiplier and prime correlations are invariant, and the pole kernel is
$2\cosh((x-y)/2)$. Zero extension to a larger interval preserves all these
terms. Thus positivity of $S_{n,N}$ for one admissible $N$ at each $n$
implies Weil positivity on all required
test functions, and hence RH by the classical criterion \cite{Suzuki}.
\end{proof}
No uniform gap in $n$, common source on all intervals, or compatible limit
of the matrices is required. The dimensions can grow without bound.

Reflection $\mathcal Jf(x)=f(L-x)$ preserves the form and the cosine
cutoff: $\mathcal Je_j=(-1)^je_j$ and
$\mathcal JV_d\mathcal J=V_d^*$. Thus $H^{-1}$ and the response respect
parity, and $S_L=S_L^{\rm even}\oplus S_L^{\rm odd}$. Both blocks must be
nonnegative; their dimensions are $\lceil N/2\rceil$ and $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$.
Trials respecting parity retain this split in the enclosures below
\cite[Sections 6--7]{Companion}.

\section{Lower and upper response enclosures}
Fix $L$ and an admissible $N$ throughout the construction below.
Put $d_j=b_j-\beta_L$ for $j\ge N$, and let $\Lambda e_j=d_je_j$ on
$QX_L$. Then $H\ge\Lambda\ge\delta_0I$. For any finite-input trial map
$Y:PX_L\to\Dom H$, set $C(Y)=(P-Y)^*W_L(P-Y)$ and $R_Y=B-HY$.
Expansion gives the variational identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
S_L=C(Y)-R_Y^*H^{-1}R_Y,\qquad
0\preceq R_Y^*H^{-1}R_Y\preceq R_Y^*\Lambda^{-1}R_Y.
\end{equation}
It explains why the energy of a trial is an upper bound until its
remaining response is subtracted.
For $K>N$, let $P_{N,K}$ project onto $e_N,\ldots,e_{K-1}$ and define
the Galerkin trial by the positive finite system
\[
(P_{N,K}HP_{N,K})Y_K=P_{N,K}B,\qquad
\operatorname{ran}Y_K\subset P_{N,K}X_L.
\]
Its residual $R_K=B-HY_K$ is supported in cosine modes $j\ge K$.
\begin{theorem}[Convergent enclosures]\label{thm:enclosure}
The finite Hermitian matrices
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-matrix}
\begin{aligned}
L_{L,N,K}&=A-B^*Y_K-d_K^{-1}R_K^*R_K,\\
U_{L,N,K}&=A-B^*Y_K
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
satisfy $L_{L,N,K}\preceq S_L\preceq U_{L,N,K}$ and converge to $S_L$ in norm
as $K\to\infty$. Their gap is exactly $d_K^{-1}R_K^*R_K$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Galerkin orthogonality gives
$Y_K^*HY_K=B^*Y_K=Y_K^*B$ and $P_{N,K}R_K=0$.
Apply \eqref{eq:residual} and
$\Lambda^{-1}\le d_K^{-1}I$ on the remaining modes to obtain the bounds.
Write $H=\Lambda+E$, where $E=Q(K_L+R_L+\beta_LI)Q$ is bounded.
Coercivity gives $\norm{Y_K}\le\delta_0^{-1}\norm B$, while
$R_K=(I-P_K)(B-EY_K)$. Consequently
\[
\norm{U_{L,N,K}-L_{L,N,K}}
\le\frac{\norm B^2(1+\norm E/\delta_0)^2}{d_K}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Each enclosure's distance from $S_L$ is at most this gap.
Since $d_K=\log K+O_L(1)$, this is a crude $O_L(1/\log K)$ bound.
\end{proof}
The column tail \eqref{eq:tail} controls finite mass approximations to $B$
and $HY_K$. The residual Gram must include their interference with the
pole and prime columns. Positive eigenvalues of an upper enclosure alone
are not a positivity certificate.

\begin{corollary}[An equivalent tolerance formulation]\label{cor:epsilon}
Conjecture \ref{conj:main}, and hence RH, is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epsilon}
\begin{gathered}
\forall n,m\in\N,\quad\forall N\text{ admissible at }n,\quad\exists K>N:\\
L_{n,N,K}+2^{-m}I\succ0,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where $L_{n,N,K}$ is \eqref{eq:lower-matrix} with $L=n$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $S_{n,N}\succeq0$, choose $K$ with
$\norm{L_{n,N,K}-S_{n,N}}<2^{-m}$, using Theorem \ref{thm:enclosure}.
Conversely $S_{n,N}\succeq L_{n,N,K}\succ-2^{-m}I$ for every $m$ implies
$S_{n,N}\succeq0$. This avoids assuming that a semidefinite limit is detected
by an unshifted, finite strict certificate.
\end{proof}
The strict margin makes each true instance amenable to sufficiently
accurate enclosures. Arbitrary finite trials and explicit column-error
allowances are treated in \cite[Section 10]{Companion}; its Section 9 proves
the stronger residual convergence needed there. An optional equivalent
comparison of two positive matrices is given in its Section 8.
For the same reason as in Theorem \ref{thm:equiv}, one may instead choose
one admissible $N$ at each $n$. A finite collection of successful intervals
or tolerances does not by itself establish
\eqref{eq:epsilon}.

\section{Arithmetic sign and limits of the construction}
The reduction works for many semibounded operators. For example
$W=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1/2&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has
a positive high block $H=1$ and $W+I>0$, but its low Schur complement is
$-1/2$. A positive shifted source and a positive high sector alone therefore
do not imply Conjecture \ref{conj:main}.

There is a more relevant negative control. Define the Chebyshev function
$\psi_{\rm Ch}(x)=\sum_{p^m\le x}\log p$ and
$E(r)=\psi_{\rm Ch}(e^r)-e^r+1$. For
$h_f(r)=\re\ip{E_Lf}{U_rE_Lf}$, define the gamma form with its lowest
mass removed by
\[
t_{\ge1,L}[f]=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\R
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac2{a_k}\frac{\tau^2}{a_k^2+\tau^2}
|\widehat{E_Lf}(\tau)|^2\,d\tau.
\]
Cancellation of the smooth prime density against the poles and the
lowest gamma mass gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discrepancy}
Q_L[f]=t_{\ge1,L}[f]+(4+w_0)\norm f^2
-2\int_0^L e^{-r/2}h_f(r)\,dE(r).
\end{equation}
The Stieltjes form holds on $\mathcal D_L$; derivatives of $h_f$ are not
required. If the prime measure is replaced by $e^r\,dr$, the last term
vanishes. On the unit input $f(x)=\sin(\pi x/2)$ on $(0,2)$,
\[
Q_2^{\rm dens}[f]<-1+\frac{26}{125}\frac{(22/7)^2}{4}
=-\frac{2979}{6125}<0.
\]
Here $w_0<-5$ and
$\sum_{k\ge1}2/a_k^3\le26/125$; smooth approximation preserves negativity.
The actual prime discrepancy carries information that the leading density
and the positive response architecture cannot replace.

At $L=1$, an explicit rational certificate permits the alternative cutoff
$N=4$ with $\delta_0=1/16$ \cite[Section 13]{Companion}.
This certifies only the high sector. Numerical finite-response observations
in that section illustrate small eigenvalues but supply no sign certificate.
The smallest admissible cutoff $N_{\min}(L)$ already grows doubly
exponentially: $\log\log N_{\min}(L)=L/2+O(\log L)$ as $L\to\infty$
\cite[Section 4]{Companion}. This concerns the present conservative bound,
not an intrinsic minimum dimension for testing the Weil form.
Moreover the response estimates supply no positive lower bound on the
smallest eigenvalue and hence no uniform working precision for a sign test.
No new positivity interval or practical complexity estimate is established.

The boundary and response estimates isolate the arithmetic ordering that
remains to be proved. Positive auxiliary constructions alone do not settle
it; the exact prime contribution remains essential.
