\begin{abstract}
This file expands the chain of arguments needed for the condensed paper.
It distinguishes the classical Weil criterion, unconditional operator
calculations, and the unproved arithmetic matrix conjecture. It also gives
finite-input error bounds suitable for rigorous computation. The source
investigation is repository revision \texttt{efb908a}; this companion is a
new exposition, not an independently refereed validation of that manuscript.
The human review should cover the hypotheses, domains, constants, quantifiers
and citations as well as the displayed algebra. No verification of RH is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{What must be reviewed}
The proposed short paper does not use the interacting vacuum metrics,
physical residue normalization, the conditional $tt^*$ equation, the remote
delay construction, or the graph realization as premises. Those results
remain in the longer investigation. The short paper needs only the following
chain:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{Weil criterion and its exact arithmetic normalization}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{closed logarithmic form and explicit boundary correction}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{unconditional high-mode positivity and well-defined mixed block}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{Schur identity on the full form domain}\\
\Downarrow\\
\mathrm{RH}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ S_n\succeq0\text{ for every }n\ge1.
\end{gathered}
\]
The remainder estimates provide a constructive way to approximate $S_n$;
they do not prove its sign. The positive shifted reference supplies an
equivalent contraction statement but is not needed for the Schur identity.

Throughout, $X_L=L^2(0,L)$ over $\C$, inner products are antilinear first,
$F=E_Lf$ is the zero extension, and
$\widehat F(\tau)=\int F(x)e^{-i\tau x}\,dx$.
The full-line shift is $U_dF(x)=F(x-d)$.
Set $a_k=2k+1/2$ and write $b$ for the scalar gamma multiplier, reserving
$B$ for the mixed operator. These differ from some letter choices in the
long manuscript.

\section{The arithmetic normalization, including the contact}
For $g=F*\widetilde F$, $\widetilde F(x)=\overline{F(-x)}$, one has
\[
g(0)=\norm F^2,\quad g(-r)=\overline{g(r)},\quad
\re g(r)=\re\ip F{U_rF}=:h_f(r).
\]
With $n_\gamma(r)=e^{-r/2}/(1-e^{-2r})$ for $r>0$, the additive
Weil functional, in the normalization of \cite[Section 1.1]{Suzuki}, is
\begin{align}
\mathcal W(g)={}&\int_\R 2\cosh(x/2)g(x)\,dx
-\sum_{p,m}(\log p)p^{-m/2}\{g(m\log p)+g(-m\log p)\}\notag\\
&-(\log(4\pi)+\gamma_E)g(0)
-\int_0^\infty[g(r)+g(-r)-2e^{-r/2}g(0)]n_\gamma(r)\,dr.
\label{d:weil-functional}
\end{align}
This equality is initially used on smooth compact support. The published
criterion states that RH is equivalent to $\mathcal W(F*\widetilde F)\ge0$
for every complex $F\in C_c^\infty(\R)$. This is the one external
number-theoretic equivalence on which the short paper rests.

The gamma energy is
\begin{align}
K[F]&=\frac12\int_\R\int_\R
 |F(x)-F(y)|^2n_\gamma(|x-y|)\,dx\,dy\notag\\
&=\int_0^\infty[2g(0)-g(r)-g(-r)]n_\gamma(r)\,dr.
\end{align}
Consequently the last line of \eqref{d:weil-functional} equals
$K[F]+w_0\norm F^2$, where
\[
w_0=-\log(4\pi)-\gamma_E
-2\int_0^\infty(1-e^{-r/2})n_\gamma(r)\,dr.
\]
The integral is convergent at zero and infinity. Expanding the denominator
and integrating positive terms gives
\begin{align*}
\int_0^\infty(1-e^{-r/2})n_\gamma(r)\,dr
&=\sum_{k\ge0}\left(\frac1{2k+1/2}-\frac1{2k+1}\right)\\
&=\tfrac12[\psi(1/2)-\psi(1/4)].
\end{align*}
Using $\psi(1/2)=-\gamma_E-2\log2$ proves
\begin{equation}\label{d:contact}
w_0=\psi(1/4)-\log\pi
=-\gamma_E-\pi/2-3\log2-\log\pi.
\end{equation}
This checks the negative contact without assigning it by a normalization
of a positive state.

For the poles, convolution gives the kernel $2\cosh((x-y)/2)$.
On $(0,L)$, with $c(x)=\cosh((x-L/2)/2)$ and
$s(x)=\sinh((x-L/2)/2)$, the identity
$\cosh(u-v)=\cosh u\cosh v-\sinh u\sinh v$ gives
$2cc^*-2ss^*$. In particular the pole term has one positive and one
negative direction, despite its pointwise positive kernel.
The prime correlations vanish for $m\log p\ge L$. Thus the precise
form is
\begin{equation}\label{d:full-form}
Q_L[f]=K[E_Lf]+w_0\norm f^2+2|\ip c f|^2-2|\ip s f|^2
-2\sum_{m\log p<L}(\log p)p^{-m/2}\re\ip F{U_{m\log p}F}.
\end{equation}

\section{Gamma expansion, source domain and cores}
The digamma partial fractions \cite{DLMF} give
\[
\psi(z+it)-\psi(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}
\left[\frac1{k+z}-\frac1{k+z+it}\right].
\]
At $z=1/4$, taking real parts and substituting $t=\sqrt{s}/2$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{d:gamma-series}
b(s)=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac2{a_k}\frac{s}{a_k^2+s}.
\end{equation}
The series converges locally uniformly in $s\ge0$. Its summands are
nonnegative and increasing in $s$, and at least one is strictly increasing.
Digamma asymptotics give
$b(\tau^2)=\log|\tau|-\log2-\psi(1/4)+O(\tau^{-2})$.
Thus $1+b(\tau^2)$ is comparable above and below to
$1+\log(2+|\tau|)$.

For $a>0$, minimizing
\[
\mathcal E_a(F,u)=\frac2a(\norm{F-u}^2+a^{-2}\norm{u'}^2)
\]
gives $\widehat u=a^2\widehat F/(a^2+\tau^2)$ and minimum
$\int (2/a)\tau^2/(a^2+\tau^2)|\widehat F|^2\,d\tau/(2\pi)$.
This can alternatively be represented by the output column
\[
\left(\sqrt{2/a}\,H_\R(a^2+H_\R)^{-1}F,
\sqrt{2a}\,\partial_x(a^2+H_\R)^{-1}F\right),\quad H_\R=-\partial_x^2.
\]
Squaring the two components gives exactly the same multiplier. The
orthogonal sum of these columns is a closed source: convergence of input
and output identifies every bounded component, and the square-summability
of the limiting output is exactly membership in its maximal domain.
That domain after zero extension is
\begin{equation}\label{d:domain}
\mathcal D_L=\{f:\int_\R\log(2+|\tau|)|\widehat{E_Lf}(\tau)|^2d\tau<\infty\}.
\end{equation}
The resulting form $t_L$ is closed. Every prime shift is a contraction
after compression, and the two pole functions are in $L^2(0,L)$, so their
finite sum $R_L$ is a bounded self-adjoint operator. Bounded perturbation
gives a closed semibounded form $t_L+R_L$ and the operator
$W_L=T_L+R_L$ on $\Dom T_L$.

\subsection{Smooth compact support is a form core}
Translate to $(-L/2,L/2)$ for this proof. Given the zero extension $F$,
let $D_rF(x)=r^{-1/2}F(x/r)$, $r<1$. The Fourier transform is
$r^{1/2}\widehat F(r\tau)$. Changing variable $\sigma=r\tau$ shows that
the weighted norm uses $1+\log(2+|\sigma|/r)$. For $r$ in, say,
$[1/2,1]$, this is uniformly comparable to the original weight.
Dilations converge strongly as $r\uparrow1$: first prove it on Schwartz
functions by dominated convergence, then use their density in the weighted
Fourier space and uniform boundedness. The support of $D_rF$ lies strictly
inside the interval. Convolution with a smooth approximate identity of
radius smaller than the support gap stays inside the interval, and its
bounded Fourier multiplier converges to one in the same weighted norm.
This proves the required core assertion. No pointwise trace at the
interval endpoints is imposed on $\mathcal D_L$.

\subsection{Compactness and the scalar truncation error}
For a bounded set in the form norm, its Fourier mass outside $[-R,R]$ is
bounded by a constant divided by $\log(2+R)$. The map from a fixed Fourier
band to $L^2(0,L)$ has a square-integrable kernel and is compact. Approximating
the embedding by these compact maps shows that $\mathcal D_L\hookrightarrow X_L$
is compact. The associated operators therefore have compact resolvent.
They can have finitely many negative eigenvalues; this is not their absence.

For numerical enclosure of $b(s)$, positivity and an integral test give
\begin{equation}\label{d:b-tail}
0\le b(s)-\sum_{k<J}\frac2{a_k}\frac{s}{a_k^2+s}
\le s\left(\frac2{a_J^3}+\frac1{2a_J^2}\right).
\end{equation}
Indeed $a_k=a_J+2(k-J)$, and
$\int_J^\infty 2(2x+1/2)^{-3}dx=1/(2a_J^2)$.

\section{Neumann--Dirichlet comparison and the cutoff}
For one mass, restricting a whole-line competitor $u$ to $(0,L)$ drops
the nonnegative exterior energy. Minimizing over all $H^1(0,L)$
competitors yields the Neumann resolvent. In the other direction, a
Dirichlet competitor extends by zero to an admissible whole-line
competitor. Thus, for every finite tower,
\[
b_J(H_{\rm N})\le T_{L,J}\le b_J(H_{\rm D}).
\]
Monotone limits of the nonnegative forms preserve this order and its
domain inclusions. In particular
\begin{equation}\label{d:ND}
b(H_{\rm N})\le T_L\le b(H_{\rm D}).
\end{equation}
Min--max, indexing eigenvalues from one, gives
\[
b(((j-1)\pi/L)^2)\le\lambda_j(T_L)\le b((j\pi/L)^2).
\]
The logarithmic asymptotic then yields
$\lambda_j(T_L)=\log(\pi j/(2L))-\psi(1/4)+O_L(j^{-1})$.

For one prime $p$, put $d=\log p$ and $q=p^{-1/2}$. Express $x=t+kd$
with $0<t<d$ and retain those integers $k$ for which $0<x<L$.
This decomposes $X_L$ into a direct integral of finite chain spaces.
Their lengths are at most $h=\lceil L/d\rceil$. The complete off-diagonal
correlation on a chain is the matrix with entries
$q^{|i-j|}$ for $i\ne j$, zero on the diagonal. Its operator norm is
bounded by the maximum absolute row sum $r_h(q)$. Therefore the negative
prime contribution is bounded below by $-d\,r_h(q)I$. The assertion also
holds when $L/d$ is an integer; endpoint chains of exceptional length
occur only on null sets.

Direct integration gives
\[
\norm c^2=L/2+\sinh(L/2),\quad
\norm s^2=-L/2+\sinh(L/2),\quad\ip c s=0.
\]
Dropping $2cc^*$ and bounding $-2ss^*$ proves
\begin{equation}\label{d:beta}
W_L\ge b(H_{\rm N})-\beta_LI,
\quad\beta_L=-w_0+2\sinh(L/2)-L+
\sum_{\log p<L}(\log p)r_{\lceil L/\log p\rceil}(p^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
An optional coarser prime term is
$2\sum_{m\log p<L}(\log p)p^{-m/2}$.

For $\delta_0=1/16$, define $N(L)$ as in the short paper and put
$P=P_{N(L)}$, $Q=I-P$. The high form on $Q\mathcal D_L$ has the lower
bound
\[
H\ge\Lambda,\qquad\Lambda e_j=d_je_j,\quad
d_j=b((j\pi/L)^2)-\beta_L\ge\delta_0\quad(j\ge N).
\]
Its inverse is legitimate before RH is assumed. The comparison uses the
gamma operator and a crude bound on the signed remainder; it does not
select low modes by assuming the spectrum of $W_L$ is positive.

\section{Deriving and controlling the exact boundary correction}
For a mass $a>0$, let $R_{f,a}$ be the compressed free resolvent and
$R_{N,a}$ the Neumann resolvent. Their kernels are
\[
R_{f,a}(x,y)=\frac{e^{-a|x-y|}}{2a},\qquad
R_{N,a}(x,y)=\frac{\cosh(a\min(x,y))\cosh(a(L-\max(x,y)))}{a\sinh(aL)}.
\]
Both solve $(-\partial_x^2+a^2)R=\delta_y$ in the open interval;
the derivative jump is $-1$. The second kernel has zero normal derivative
at both endpoints. Expand its hyperbolic factors separately on $x\le y$
and $x\ge y$. Subtraction gives
\begin{equation}\label{d:mass-boundary}
2a(R_{N,a}-R_{f,a})=K_a
=\frac{u_au_a^*+v_av_a^*+r_a(u_av_a^*+v_au_a^*)}{1-r_a^2},
\end{equation}
where $u_a=e^{-ax}$, $v_a=e^{-a(L-x)}$, $r_a=e^{-aL}$.
The coefficient matrix is $(1-r_a^2)^{-1}
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&r_a\\r_a&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, with
positive eigenvalues $1/(1-r_a)$ and $1/(1+r_a)$.
The energy summand is $(2/a)I-2aR$, so the free compressed energy is
the Neumann energy plus $K_a$, with the positive sign.

\subsection{Uniform boundedness of the infinite sum}
Let $n(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}e^{-a_kt}=e^{-t/2}/(1-e^{-2t})$. In the kernel
of $K_a$, the first $u_au_a^*$ and $v_av_a^*$ image terms sum to
$n(x+y)+n(2L-x-y)$. Every additional diagonal image has an extra
factor $e^{-2aL}$; the mixed images have path length at least $L$.
Thus the remainder is bounded, for example by
\[
\frac{2[n(2L)+n(L)]}{1-e^{-L}}.
\]
As $n(t)-(2t)^{-1}$ is bounded on $(0,2L]$, the sum is
\begin{equation}\label{d:Carleman}
K_L=\tfrac12(C_0+C_L)+\text{a bounded-kernel operator},
\end{equation}
where $C_0(x,y)=(x+y)^{-1}$ and $C_L(x,y)=(2L-x-y)^{-1}$.
Using weight $x^{-1/2}$, the identity
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{y^{-1/2}}{x+y}\,dy=\pi x^{-1/2}
\]
proves $\norm{C_0}\le\pi$ by the weighted Schur test; reflection treats
$C_L$. The same kernel majorant bounds every positive partial sum.
The monotone bounded sequence $K^{(J)}=\sum_{k<J}K_{a_k}$ converges strongly
to a bounded positive $K_L$.

For any $f\in X_L$, finite-tower energies satisfy
$t_{L,J}[f]=\ip f{b_J(H_N)f}+\ip f{K^{(J)}f}$.
The last term has a bounded limit for every $f$. Hence the two increasing
energy limits are finite for exactly the same $f$, and their closed forms
differ by $K_L$. This proves
\begin{equation}\label{d:domains}
T_L=b(H_N)+K_L,\quad\Dom T_L=\Dom b(H_N),\quad
\mathcal D_L=\Dom b(H_N)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Spectral partial sums of $b(H_N)$ are an operator core; bounded perturbation
preserves that fact. In particular all finite cosine sums belong to
$\Dom W_L$, not merely to its form domain.

\subsection{Why the boundary operator is not compact}
Split $(0,L)$ at $L/2$. In \eqref{d:Carleman}, all off-diagonal blocks and
the opposite endpoint kernel within each diagonal block are bounded kernels.
They are Hilbert--Schmidt. Modulo compact operators, $K_L$ is the direct
sum of two half-Carleman operators. Thus its essential norm is at most
$\pi/2$.

For the reverse bound take
$\phi_R(t)=t^{-1/2}1_{[1,R]}(t)/\sqrt{\log R}$ on $(0,\infty)$.
Changing to $t=e^u$ transforms the Carleman kernel to convolution by
$k(u-v)=[2\cosh((u-v)/2)]^{-1}$. Hence its Rayleigh quotient is
\[
\int_{-\log R}^{\log R}
\left(1-\frac{|v|}{\log R}\right)k(v)\,dv\longrightarrow
\int_\R k(v)\,dv=\pi.
\]
For fixed $R$, dilate into $(\epsilon,\epsilon R)\subset(0,L/2)$.
The unit vectors tend weakly to zero and keep their Carleman quotient,
while every compact quadratic term tends to zero. First let
$\epsilon\downarrow0$, then $R\to\infty$. This gives the reverse
essential-norm bound and proves
\[
\norm{K_L}_{\rm ess}=\pi/2,\qquad
\norm{K_L-K^{(J)}}\ge\pi/2\quad\text{for every finite }J.
\]
This does not conflict with compact resolvent of the unbounded $T_L$.
The needed Carleman facts are proved here; \cite{Yafaev} gives broader context.

\section{Matrix entries and finite-input tails}
Let $\omega_j=j\pi/L$ and $e_j=\nu_j\cos(\omega_jx)$. Integration gives
\[
\ip{u_a}{e_j}=\frac{\nu_ja(1-(-1)^je^{-aL})}{a^2+\omega_j^2},\qquad
\ip{v_a}{e_j}=(-1)^j\ip{u_a}{e_j}.
\]
Inserting this into \eqref{d:mass-boundary} cancels the denominator and yields
\begin{equation}\label{d:column}
K_ae_j=\frac{\nu_ja}{a^2+\omega_j^2}(u_a+(-1)^jv_a).
\end{equation}
Taking a second inner product gives zero for opposite parities, and for
equal parities gives
\begin{equation}\label{d:entries}
\ip{e_i}{K_Le_j}=2\nu_i\nu_j\sum_{k\ge0}
\frac{a_k^2(1-(-1)^je^{-a_kL})}
{(a_k^2+\omega_i^2)(a_k^2+\omega_j^2)}.
\end{equation}
The parity separation follows also from reflection about $L/2$.
It is useful for calculation, but the short paper does not rely on an
odd-only variant of the Weil criterion.

Since $\norm{u_a},\norm{v_a}\le(2a)^{-1/2}$, equation \eqref{d:column}
gives $\norm{K_ae_j}\le\sqrt2\nu_ja^{-3/2}$.
The integral test yields
$\sum_{k\ge J}a_k^{-3/2}\le a_J^{-3/2}+a_J^{-1/2}$.
The operator norm on the first $h$ columns is at most their Hilbert--Schmidt
norm. Using $\sum_{j<h}\nu_j^2=(2h-1)/L$ proves
\begin{equation}\label{d:column-tail}
\norm{(K_L-K^{(J)})P_h}\le
\sqrt{\frac{2(2h-1)}L}(a_J^{-1/2}+a_J^{-3/2})=:\eta_{h,J}.
\end{equation}
Only the input dimension $h$ is fixed in this limit. It does not imply
operator-norm convergence on the full space.

\subsection{Prime and pole entries without an unspecified kernel}
Define
\[
\mathcal I(\lambda,\phi;a,b)=
\begin{cases}
[\sin(\lambda b+\phi)-\sin(\lambda a+\phi)]/\lambda,&\lambda\ne0,\\
(b-a)\cos\phi,&\lambda=0.
\end{cases}
\]
For $0<d<L$, product-to-sum gives
\begin{align}
t_{ij}(d):=\ip{e_i}{V_de_j}
=\frac{\nu_i\nu_j}{2}\bigl[&\mathcal I(\omega_i-\omega_j,\omega_jd;d,L)\notag\\
&+\mathcal I(\omega_i+\omega_j,-\omega_jd;d,L)\bigr].
\label{d:shift-entries}
\end{align}
The adjoint entry is $t_{ji}(d)$. This handles the zero-frequency cases
by the second branch of $\mathcal I$.

For $\sigma\ne0$ set
\[
J_j(\sigma)=\int_0^Le^{\sigma x}\cos(\omega_jx)\,dx
=\frac{\sigma[(-1)^je^{\sigma L}-1]}{\sigma^2+\omega_j^2}.
\]
Then
\begin{align*}
c_j:=\ip{e_j}c&=\tfrac{\nu_j}{2}
[e^{-L/4}J_j(1/2)+e^{L/4}J_j(-1/2)],\\
s_j:=\ip{e_j}s&=\tfrac{\nu_j}{2}
[e^{-L/4}J_j(1/2)-e^{L/4}J_j(-1/2)].
\end{align*}
Thus the entire cosine matrix is
\begin{equation}\label{d:W-entries}
w_{ij}=(b_j+w_0)\delta_{ij}+k_{ij}+2c_ic_j-2s_is_j
-\sum_{m\log p<L}(\log p)p^{-m/2}
[t_{ij}(m\log p)+t_{ji}(m\log p)].
\end{equation}
All entries are real, but the quadratic-form assertions apply to complex
inputs. For residual norms, the full columns are also explicit:
\begin{align}
W_Le_j={}&(b_j+w_0)e_j+K_Le_j+2c_jc-2s_js\notag\\
&-\sum_{d=m\log p<L}(\log p)p^{-m/2}
\left[1_{x>d}e_j(x-d)+1_{x<L-d}e_j(x+d)\right].
\label{d:W-columns}
\end{align}
Finite mass sums in \eqref{d:column} turn these into finite sums of
exponentials, trigonometric functions, and their interval restrictions.
Products and squared norms are elementary integrals on the finitely many
overlap intervals. Equations \eqref{d:b-tail} and \eqref{d:column-tail}
supply the missing infinite tails.

\section{The finite response and both directions of the equivalence}
Because $PX_L\subset\Dom W_L$, both $A=PW_LP$ and $B=QW_LP$ are bounded
on the finite low space. Restriction of the closed form defines
$H\ge\delta_0I$ on $QX_L$. Its operator domain is
$Q\Dom b(H_N)$, by \eqref{d:domains}. For $p=Pf$ and $h=Qf$ the form is
\[
Q_L[p+h]=\norm{H^{1/2}h}^2+2\re\ip h{Bp}+\ip p{Ap}.
\]
As $H^{-1}Bp\in\Dom H$, expand the square to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{d:Schur}
Q_L[p+h]=\norm{H^{1/2}(h+H^{-1}Bp)}^2+\ip p{S_Lp},
\quad S_L=A-B^*H^{-1}B.
\end{equation}
The expansion uses $H^{1/2}H^{-1}=H^{-1/2}$ and the closed form pairing.
It does not require every $h$ to be in $\Dom H$.

If $S_L\succeq0$, equation \eqref{d:Schur} gives $Q_L\ge0$ on its full
form domain. Conversely choose $h=-H^{-1}Bp$. The high square vanishes
and $Q_L[p+h]=\ip p{S_Lp}$, so positivity of $Q_L$ forces positivity of
$S_L$. A negative vector of $S_L$ therefore produces a negative form-domain
input. Approximation by the smooth core produces a smooth negative input.

Under RH, the classical Weil criterion gives positivity on the smooth
core; continuity in the shifted form norm extends it to $\mathcal D_L$.
Thus $S_n\succeq0$ for every integer $n\ge1$.
Conversely, every compactly supported test function can be translated into
$(0,n)$ for an integer $n$. Translation changes its Fourier transform by
a phase and preserves every translated correlation. The pole kernel
$2\cosh((x-y)/2)$ also depends only on the difference, so the entire form
is invariant. Zero extension to a larger interval preserves the same
form and merely adds inactive translations with zero overlap. Positivity
of every $S_n$ now implies the global Weil criterion, hence RH.

This argument uses only a cofinal sequence of supports. It requires
neither source compatibility nor a uniform full-form gap. It also shows
that an arbitrary finite list of successful lengths cannot finish the proof.

\section{The positive matrix comparison and the source interpretation}
For $\alpha=\beta_L+1$, the operator $T=W_L+\alpha I\ge I$ is positive
independently of RH. Its block matrix is
\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}A+\alpha I&B^*\\B&H+\alpha I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let $V=(H+\alpha I)^{-1}$. Completing the shifted high square yields
\[
G=A+\alpha I-B^*VB\ge I.
\]
To check the bound, minimize $\ip{p+h}{T(p+h)}\ge\norm p^2+\norm h^2$
over $h$ at fixed $p$. Define
\[
D=\alpha I+B^*(H^{-1}-V)B
=\alpha[I+B^*VH^{-1}B]\ge\alpha I.
\]
The product $VH^{-1}$ is positive because both factors are functions of
the same $H$; arbitrary products of positive operators would not justify
this step. Direct subtraction gives $G-D=S_L$. Congruence by $G^{-1/2}$
therefore proves
\begin{equation}\label{d:contraction}
S_L\succeq0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
G^{-1/2}DG^{-1/2}\preceq I.
\end{equation}
Both $G$ and $D$ can change with the permissible positive shift; their
difference cannot. This rules out tuning a source normalization to improve
the arithmetic sign.

Abstractly, any positive source $\mathcal A$ for $T$ has a low output
orthogonal to its high range, with Gram $G$. The defect output has Gram
$D$. A contraction taking the former output to the latter exists precisely
when $D\preceq G$. Existence alone is therefore the same conjecture.
A microscopic physical law constructing such a contraction from specified
interactions would be additional structure, whose necessity under RH
is not established by this equivalence.

For a simple control, take
$W=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1/2&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and
$\alpha=1$. Then $H=1$, $V=1/2$, $G=1$, $D=3/2$, and $S=-1/2$.
The positive shifted reference and the positive high block coexist with
negative full-form energy. The conjectural ordering is an essential step.

\section{Residual identity, inverse order and Galerkin convergence}
For a trial map $Y:PX_L\to\Dom H$, let $R=B-HY$. Expand without
reordering any noncommuting factors:
\[
R^*H^{-1}R=B^*H^{-1}B-B^*Y-Y^*B+Y^*HY.
\]
This proves
\begin{equation}\label{d:residual}
B^*H^{-1}B=B^*Y+Y^*B-Y^*HY+R^*H^{-1}R.
\end{equation}
The form inequality $H\ge\Lambda>0$ reverses for inverses:
$H^{-1}\le\Lambda^{-1}$. One proof uses
\[
\ip z{H^{-1}z}=\sup_v\{2\re\ip z v-\norm{H^{1/2}v}^2\}
\le\sup_v\{2\re\ip z v-\norm{\Lambda^{1/2}v}^2\}.
\]
The form-domain inclusion makes the supremum comparison valid.
Thus $R^*H^{-1}R\le R^*\Lambda^{-1}R$.

Let $E_K=P_{N,K}$ and solve $E_KHE_KY_K=E_KB$ in $E_KX_L$.
The finite coefficient matrix is at least $\delta_0I$ and hence invertible.
Multiplying by $Y_K^*$ gives
\[
Y_K^*HY_K=Y_K^*B=B^*Y_K,
\quad E_K(B-HY_K)=0.
\]
The residual $R_K$ has no modes below $K$, so
\begin{equation}\label{d:enclosures}
A-B^*Y_K-d_K^{-1}R_K^*R_K
\preceq S_L\preceq A-B^*Y_K.
\end{equation}
The lower expression, denoted $L_{L,K}$, includes the entire residual
Gram. Omitting it leaves an upper bound.

For convergence, each exact solution column $u=H^{-1}Be_j$ minimizes
the coercive energy with that source. The Galerkin solution $u_K$ is its
orthogonal projection in the $H$-form inner product onto the trial space.
The cosine core gives $\norm{H^{1/2}(u-u_K)}\to0$; in particular
$u_K\to u$ in $L^2$. There are finitely many source columns, so this is
operator-norm convergence on the input space.
Write $H=\Lambda+E$ with $E$ bounded, using \eqref{d:domains}. Then
\[
R_K=(I-P_K)(B-EY_K).
\]
Here $(I-P_K)\Lambda Y_K=0$ and the other residual rows vanish by
Galerkin orthogonality. Since $EY_K\to EH^{-1}B$ in norm and the input
space is finite dimensional, $R_K\to0$ in norm. Consequently the gap
between the two matrices in \eqref{d:enclosures} tends to zero.

For ordered inverse approximations the direction of the bound is easy
to reverse accidentally. If $F_\ell\preceq H^{-1}$, then
$A-B^*F_\ell B\succeq S_L$.
For example with $H=1$, $\alpha=1$, $V=1/2$,
$F_\ell=\sum_{j=0}^\ell 2^{-j-1}=1-2^{-\ell-1}$ and
$A=1-2^{-\ell-2}$, one has
\[
A-F_\ell=2^{-\ell-2}>0,\qquad A-H^{-1}=-2^{-\ell-2}<0.
\]
Positive approximate upper matrices cannot certify the target.

\section{A fully specified finite-trial error allowance}
This section makes the word ``approximable'' operational. It is a
consequence of the boundary column estimate and the residual identity,
with an explicit allowance for a finite mass approximation. It is not
an executed positivity certificate for any arithmetic matrix.

Choose $K>N$ and any matrix $Y:PX_L\to P_{N,K}X_L$, for example one
with rational coefficients. No exact inverse or exact Galerkin solution
is required. Set $V_0=P-Y$ as a map from the low coefficient space to
$P_KX_L$. Define
\[
W^{(J)}=b(H_N)+K^{(J)}+R_L,\qquad
C_J=V_0^*W^{(J)}V_0,\qquad\widetilde R=QW^{(J)}V_0.
\]
The true residual is $R=QW_LV_0=B-HY$. Positivity of $K_L-K^{(J)}$
gives $V_0^*W_LV_0\succeq C_J$, while \eqref{d:column-tail} gives
\begin{equation}\label{d:residual-error}
\norm{\Lambda^{-1/2}(R-\widetilde R)}
\le\frac{\eta_{K,J}\norm{V_0}}{\sqrt{\delta_0}}
=:\epsilon_{K,J,Y},\qquad\norm{V_0}^2=1+\norm Y^2.
\end{equation}
The last equality uses orthogonality of the low and trial high spaces.

For a chosen $M>N$, let $r_j=e_j^*\widetilde R$ and define the finite
matrix
\begin{equation}\label{d:weighted-tail}
T_M=\sum_{j=N}^{M-1}\frac{r_j^*r_j}{d_j}
+\frac1{d_M}\left(\widetilde R^*\widetilde R-
\sum_{j=N}^{M-1}r_j^*r_j\right).
\end{equation}
It is positive and bounds $\widetilde R^*\Lambda^{-1}\widetilde R$ from
above, by Parseval and monotonicity of $d_j$. All its quantities are finite
integrals of the explicitly given functions in \eqref{d:W-columns}, with
$K^{(J)}$ in place of $K_L$. In particular the full residual norm is kept
even if only finitely many cosine coefficients are individually computed.

For operators $X$ and $Z$ with $\norm Z\le\epsilon$,
\[
(X+Z)^*(X+Z)\preceq X^*X+(2\norm X\epsilon+\epsilon^2)I.
\]
Apply this with $X=\Lambda^{-1/2}\widetilde R$ and use
$\norm X\le\sqrt{\norm{T_M}}$. Equations \eqref{d:residual} and
\eqref{d:residual-error} then prove the rigorous lower bound
\begin{equation}\label{d:finite-certificate}
S_L\succeq C_J-T_M-
\left(2\sqrt{\norm{T_M}}\,\epsilon_{K,J,Y}
+\epsilon_{K,J,Y}^2\right)I.
\end{equation}
This uses only finite matrices, integrals of finite sums, and explicit
scalar error bounds. Extra numerical rounding or integration uncertainty
must be subtracted as an additional scalar operator-norm allowance.
If entrywise Hermitian errors are bounded by $e_{ij}\ge0$, the maximal
row sum of the symmetric error majorant bounds their operator norm.
One may use rational upper bounds for all norms and lower bounds for
the positive $d_j$ and $\delta_0$.

The special-function values $b_j$ can be enclosed by \eqref{d:b-tail}.
Exponentials, logarithms and trigonometric values in the finite entries
admit convergent elementary series with rational remainder estimates.
At integer $L=n$, the active prime set is determined by $p^m<e^n$;
there is no equality because $e^n$ is transcendental. Finite enumeration
is therefore well-defined, though potentially expensive.

For fixed trial $Y$ and $K$, let $J\to\infty$ and refine scalar enclosures;
then $\epsilon_{K,J,Y}\to0$, $C_J\to V_0^*W_LV_0$, and the residual
approximations converge in $L^2$. Exact Galerkin trials with $K\to\infty$
have vanishing true residual by the preceding section. Approximating their
finite coefficients by rationals and taking sufficiently many masses
therefore makes \eqref{d:finite-certificate} as close to $S_L$ as needed.
This proves availability of finite error control, not a bound on the cost
or a sign for the resulting matrix.

\section{The tolerance conjecture and its quantifiers}
Define $L_{n,K}$ by \eqref{d:enclosures}. The alternate conjecture is
\begin{equation}\label{d:tolerance}
\forall n,m\in\N\quad\exists K>N(n):\quad
L_{n,K}+2^{-m}I\succ0.
\end{equation}
If $S_n\succeq0$, norm convergence gives a $K$ such that
$\norm{L_{n,K}-S_n}<2^{-m}$. Thus the displayed matrix is strictly
positive. Conversely a witness $K$ gives
$S_n\succeq L_{n,K}\succ-2^{-m}I$. Since this holds for every $m$,
each eigenvalue of $S_n$ is nonnegative. Combining with Section 7 proves
that \eqref{d:tolerance} is equivalent to RH.

The order of quantifiers matters. Neither a single $K$ for all $m$ nor a
uniform $K$ or $N$ for all $n$ is asserted. Replacing ``every $m$'' by one
small tolerance would be weaker than positivity. Requiring an unshifted
strict finite certificate for each $n$ is stronger on its face and would
need a separate strict-positivity argument to claim equivalence.
The tolerance formulation avoids this unnecessary extra premise.

The strict margin in \eqref{d:tolerance} also permits replacing the exact
matrix by a sufficiently accurate certified lower enclosure such as
\eqref{d:finite-certificate}. Under $S_n\succeq0$, choose all approximation
errors less than a fixed fraction of $2^{-m}$. The existence argument
then has slack. It remains necessary to prove the arithmetic inequalities
for all $n,m$; the generic convergence theorem is not that proof.

\section{The exact prime discrepancy and a negative control}
Let $h_f(r)=\re\ip F{U_rF}$ and
$E(r)=\psi_{\rm Ch}(e^r)-e^r+1$. The prime term is
$-2\int_0^L e^{-r/2}h_f(r)\,d\psi_{\rm Ch}(e^r)$.
The pole term is $4\int_0^L\cosh(r/2)h_f(r)dr$.
Subtracting the smooth prime density $e^rdr$ from this pole expression
leaves $2\int_0^Le^{-r/2}h_f(r)dr$, the form of the kernel
$e^{-|x-y|/2}$. The $a_0=1/2$ gamma summand is $4I$ minus precisely
that kernel, since $2a_0R_{f,a_0}$ has kernel $e^{-|x-y|/2}$.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{d:discrepancy}
Q_L[f]=t_{\ge1,L}[f]+(4+w_0)\norm f^2
-2\int_0^Le^{-r/2}h_f(r)\,dE(r).
\end{equation}
Autocorrelations of $L^2$ functions are continuous, and the finite-interval
measure has finite variation. This is a bounded form contribution, so
the identity extends from the smooth core to $\mathcal D_L$.

For smooth compact support, $E(0)=0$ and $h_f(L)=0$ remove both boundary
terms in Stieltjes integration by parts. The last term becomes
$2\int_0^L E(r)e^{-r/2}(h_f'(r)-h_f(r)/2)dr$. No such derivative is
assumed to be an ordinary function on the whole logarithmic domain.

If the prime measure is replaced by its leading density, then $E=0$.
For a zero extension in $H^1(\R)$,
\[
t_{\ge1,L}[f]\le\sum_{k\ge1}\frac2{a_k^3}\norm{F'}^2
\le\frac{26}{125}\norm{F'}^2.
\]
The last fraction is $2/(5/2)^3+1/[2(5/2)^2]$ by an integral tail.
Equation \eqref{d:contact} implies $w_0<-5$. To check the elementary
inequality, use $\gamma_E>1/2$, $\pi>3$, $\log2>2/3$ and $\log\pi>1$.
For example
$\gamma_E>H_8-\log9>H_8-11/5=29/56>1/2$;
$e^{11/10}>3$ follows from its first six positive series terms,
and $e<3$ gives $\log\pi>1$.

On $(0,2)$, $f(x)=\sin(\pi x/2)$ has norm one, vanishes at the endpoints,
and $\norm{F'}^2=\pi^2/4$. Hence
\[
Q_2^{\rm dens}[f]<-1+\frac{26}{125}\frac{(22/7)^2}{4}
=-\frac{2979}{6125}.
\]
Approximation in $H_0^1(0,2)$ preserves negativity and gives smooth compact
support. This shows why simply discarding the signed prime discrepancy
does not provide a route to the matrix ordering. It does not rule out
methods that use stronger, signed prime information.

\section{The length-one high-mode example}
At $L=1$ only $p=2,m=1$ is active. The chain has two sites, so
\[
\beta_1=-w_0+2\sinh(1/2)-1+\frac{\log2}{\sqrt2}.
\]
The longer manuscript supplies the rational bounds
\[
\begin{gathered}
\gamma_E<0.58,\quad\pi<22/7,\quad\log2<0.7,\\
\log\pi<23/20,\quad2\sinh(1/2)-1<43/1000,\quad
\log2/\sqrt2<1/2.
\end{gathered}
\]
They give
\[
\beta_1<\frac{58}{100}+\frac{11}{7}+\frac{21}{10}
+\frac{23}{20}+\frac{43}{1000}+\frac12=\frac{41611}{7000}.
\]
These constants may be checked using rational series: the atanh series
$\log2=2\sum_{j\ge0}3^{-(2j+1)}/(2j+1)$ gives its upper and lower bounds;
$\gamma_E<H_{256}-\log256$ gives the displayed Euler-constant bound
with $\log2>6931/10000$; positive exponential sums and geometric tail
bounds give the remaining logarithm and sinh bounds. For $\pi<22/7$ use
$22/7-\pi=\int_0^1x^4(1-x)^4/(1+x^2)dx>0$.

Since $\pi>3$ and $b$ is increasing,
\[
b((4\pi)^2)>\sum_{k=0}^{63}\frac2{a_k}\frac{144}{a_k^2+144}
>\frac{3007}{500}>\frac{41611}{7000}+\frac1{16}.
\]
The two final comparisons use exact fractions. Thus $N=4$ is an
admissible cutoff. This proves $N(1)\le4$ for the canonical minimum;
it is not necessary to prove minimality. When using this certificate,
one may deliberately use the four-dimensional low space.
No arithmetic Schur-matrix entries or sign certificate are inferred
from these scalar inequalities.
