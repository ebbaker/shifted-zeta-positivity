\begin{center}
{\LARGE\bfseries Arithmetic ground-state geometry\par}
\vspace{6pt}
{\Large Supersymmetric local factors and the positive-pairing problem\par}
\vspace{10pt}
{Working draft, September 2026\par}
\end{center}
\begin{abstract}
We investigate positive supersymmetric quantum theories as possible sources of an exact Hilbert-norm representation of the finite-interval Weil form. The complete positivity identity remains open. We first explain how a positive gamma kinetic energy and a finite-prime Morse model isolate the missing contact and pole contributions. We then construct a four-supercharge theory on a cylinder, retain the gamma function as a holomorphic period, and obtain a physical Euler-factor metric from quadratic prime fields. A cross-coupled quartic sector has nine physical vacua. At fixed quartic coupling, an exact differential equation excludes the Euler identity period on every admissible flat rapid-decay cycle. Scaling the quartic interaction with the square of the prime mass restores both the Euler period and its modulus-squared dependence in the physical identity-class metric. This interacting mass direction is nevertheless trivial in the Jacobi ring. Finite-rank curvature, scalar ground-transform, and normalization tests further restrict prospective completions. The results identify a concrete next problem: a physical pairing with nontrivial interaction geometry that fixes the source normalization, contact and poles together.
\end{abstract}
