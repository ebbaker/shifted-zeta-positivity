\begin{abstract}
The localized Weil quadratic form on an interval $I_L$ is usually written as an
archimedean energy, a contact constant, a rank-two pole form and a sum of prime
translations. We give a presentation in which the first, second and fourth of
these are a single object. Adding and subtracting the value of the prime symbol
at the origin puts everything but the pole term in L\'evy--Khinchine form: the
target is a jump Dirichlet form whose L\'evy measure has the archimedean density
as its continuous part and an atom of mass $2(\log p)p^{-m/2}$ at each prime
period $m\log p$, together with one explicit constant. Two identities organize
the account. The archimedean symbol together with the contact is exactly twice
the derivative of the Riemann--Siegel theta function, so it is integration
against the smooth zero-counting measure; the contact constant is not an
independent object but the $-\log\pi$ inside that derivative, and the negativity
of the archimedean part is confined to a band that shrinks with $L$ and is empty
above $\log13$. Splitting the form by the sign of its symbol rather than by that
constant then gives a compression presentation that moves strictly less to the
subtracted side. The jump presentation carries a Perron--Frobenius structure:
its resolvent is positivity preserving, as Beurling--Deny requires, and the
rank-two pole form destroys that structure at every $L$. We explain what a cone
can and cannot supply here --- Krein--Rutman adds nothing to the spectral theorem
for a self-adjoint form, Birkhoff bounds a spectral gap and never a spectral
radius, and Collatz--Wielandt is the only cone theorem that bounds what must be
bounded --- and we use the part of the form that does lie in a cone to prove an
elementary necessary condition: if the Weil form is positive on $I_L$ then
$\alpha_L\le2\sinh(L/2)+L$, where $\alpha_L$ is a Perron-root deficit of an
explicitly nonnegative kernel. The condition is sharp, it is bounded below by a
single positive trial function, and a trial function violating it would show
that the Riemann hypothesis fails; a scan of $7713$ intervals finds no
violation. We close with eight necessary conditions that any source for the
localized form must satisfy. Nothing here establishes Weil positivity, and no
statement below assumes the Riemann hypothesis.
\end{abstract}
