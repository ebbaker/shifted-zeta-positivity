\begin{abstract}
We study the problem of realizing the complete localized Weil quadratic
form as the norm of a linearly prepared state in an independently positive
quantum or boundary system. The intended difficulty is an exact matching
identity, potentially conditional on construction of a specified field
theory. We give explicit calculations in sphere and Schur quantization.
The real Gaussian boundary module supplies the quarter-shift gamma factor
and the positive oscillator tower, while its natural dilation pairing
differs from the Weil form. In an elementary Schur representation, an
electric dressing followed by one magnetic insertion realizes the full
positive single-prime reference, including every negative prime-power
coefficient and its compulsory positive contact. A single quantization
parameter works for all primes, and the insertion is justified by a
graph-domain approximation. We derive restrictions on higher magnetic
powers and exclude a constant-vector coherent gluing of two primes through
its unwanted mixed translation atoms. We then prove that the resulting
architecture cannot be completed at all: an elementary interference bound,
applied with explicit test functions, shows that no coherent source has the
positive gamma energy and the summed positive prime references as its two
channel norms, at the first intervals where primes act and at every
sufficiently long interval. Recording the target in its spectral form
explains why, and identifies the mechanism that remains. We then exhibit it:
a mirror of the positive prime reference, with the same prime atoms of the
opposite sign and a strictly smaller contact, presents the target as a
difference in which the remaining source carries no prime translation, and
turns the criterion on each interval into a single domination, equivalently a
compression. We then record what the two signed poles are: in the coordinates
given by the evaluations at $s=0$ and $s=1$ the pole form is off-diagonal, a
hyperbolic pairing of signature $(1,1)$ whose null lines are the two
evaluations, and every source for the localized form carries a unitary
involution exchanging them, which for a compatible realization is the
functional equation acting on the zeros. We then show that the pole term needs
no mechanism of its own: splitting it by parity puts its positive half on the
source side and its rank-one negative half on the subtracted side, and presents
the target as a compression of the gamma energy in which both sides are
positive term by term and the dominating side has an explicit source. That
presentation assumes no unproved inequality and reduces the construction
problem to exhibiting one critical contraction. We also settle the prime-free
inequality outside a compact window, which is no longer a prerequisite for that
problem, and characterise the source states reachable by one magnetic
insertion: they are exactly the functions analytic past the unit circle, and
the mirror prime reference is among them. Further exact tests
cover interacting sphere states, a conformal electric sector, gauge transfer,
and finite rational feedback. These results select and exclude source
constructions; they do not establish Weil positivity or a field-theoretic
reduction of the Riemann hypothesis.
\end{abstract}
