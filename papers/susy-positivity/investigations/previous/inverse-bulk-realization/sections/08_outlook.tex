\section{The next complete matching problem}\label{sec:outlook}
\subsection{Relation to spectral realizations of the zeros}
Corollary~\ref{cor:spectral} places this objective inside a long line of work
on spectral interpretations of the zeros: the Hilbert--P\'olya expectation as
developed by Berry and Keating \cite{BerryKeating} and surveyed by Sierra
\cite{Sierra}, the Hamiltonian proposal of Bender, Brody and M\"uller
\cite{BBM}, and the trace formula of Connes on the adele class space
\cite{ConnesTrace,CCM}. What distinguishes the present objective is not its
conclusion but its class of candidate pairings: protected line and defect
algebras whose positive twisted traces are supplied in advance by a field
theory, rather than an operator constructed to have the required spectrum.
We are not aware of an attempt to realize the Weil form in that class, and
the representation-theoretic results used above
\cite{Sphere,Schur,RG,EKRS,Klyuev} do not appear to have been applied to it.
That is a reason to continue; it is not evidence that the matching exists.

Two features of the existing literature bear directly on
Section~\ref{subsec:interference}. Connes and Consani obtain archimedean Weil
positivity from the trace of the scaling action compressed to the orthogonal
complement of the range of cutoff projections \cite{CCWeil}. That is exactly
a mechanism of the kind Proposition~\ref{prop:no-augment} demands: a negative
contact relative to a positive reference arises as
$\norm{(1-\Pi)v}^2=\norm v^2-\norm{\Pi v}^2$, never as an added channel.
Their functional is, however, the archimedean local term with the two pole
contributions removed, and their hypothesis $\widehat g(i/2)=0$ is by
Lemma~\ref{lem:polepair} the statement that the source vector lies on a null
line of the hyperbolic pole pairing. The mechanism is therefore a precedent
for the shape of a subtraction and not a tool for \eqref{eq:prime-free-target},
which asks for a norm on all of $C_c^\infty(I_L)$ and so cannot discard the
pole directions. With Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-compression} the comparison
changes shape: that inequality is no longer a prerequisite for anything here,
and what would be wanted from this direction is the mechanism rather than the
inequality --- a compression of a natural positive operator by projections
whose rank grows, which is what the spectrum of $\Pi$ recorded there looks
like. Whether it transfers, with the pole directions retained and the primes
carried by the compression, is not settled in this manuscript.
Bombieri's analysis of the quadratic functional
\cite{Bombieri} likewise treats contact, poles and primes together rather than
as separate positive pieces. Both are precedents for the structure that
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-channel} shows to be unavoidable.

\subsection{What the exclusion leaves}\label{subsec:leaves}
\paragraph{The contact must arise by projection, and now does.}
A positive norm produces a constant more negative than any of its channels
only through a subtraction that is itself a norm, that is through
$\norm{(1-\Pi)v}^2$ for a projection $\Pi$, or more generally through a
compression. Both the gamma and prime data must then live in one space in
which the projection acts, rather than in separate positive summands.
Theorem~\ref{thm:subtraction} supplies the arithmetic side of such a
presentation: the target is the prime-free form $A_L$ of
\eqref{eq:prime-free} minus a sum of mirror references, and by
Corollary~\ref{cor:domination} the Weil criterion on $I_L$ is the single
domination $\sum_p\widetilde B_{r_p,d_p}\preceq A_L$. What remains is one
compression, and by Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-compression} its dominating
side may be taken to be the gamma energy together with two elementary terms,
which is a form one can write down. Everything else --- the contact, the odd
half of the pole term, and every prime --- is then carried by the compression
alone.

\paragraph{A prime-free archimedean inequality is the first target.}
Positivity of \eqref{eq:prime-free},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-free-target}
 K[E_Lf]+\Big(w_0+\sum_{p<e^L}\widetilde\kappa_{r_p,d_p}\Big)\norm f^2
 +P_L[f]\geq0,
\end{equation}
is implied by $Q_L\geq0$, so it cannot fail unless RH does; it is the
statement a source for $A_L$ must satisfy, and primes enter it only through
the constant. It is \emph{not} needed by
Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-compression}, whose two sides are positive term by
term, so it is no longer a prerequisite for the construction problem. We record
its status because it is a natural question about $A_L$, and because it is not
uniform in $L$.

For $L\leq\log2$ no prime is active, the constant is $w_0$, and
\eqref{eq:prime-free-target} \emph{is} $Q_L\geq0$; there is no slack to be had
from the mirror contact. That case is a theorem of Yoshida
\cite[Theorem 1]{Yoshida}, whose Hermitian form is \eqref{eq:target} including
both pole terms, who imposes no side condition, and whose test space contains
$C_c^\infty(I_L)$; the proof is a computer-assisted numerical argument. The
restatement of that theorem in \cite{CCWeil} carries the additional hypothesis
$\widehat f(\pm i/2)=0$, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:polepair} is exactly
$P_L[f]=0$, and is the form the result takes once the pole terms are moved out
of the functional.

For large $L$ an elementary argument suffices.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:prime-free-large-L}
Set $c_L=w_0+\sum_{p<e^L}\widetilde\kappa_{r_p,d_p}$ and
$N(a)=\int_a^\infty\gammak(r)\dd r=\sum_{n\geq0}e^{-a_na}/a_n$. If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:large-L-criterion}
 c_L+2N(L/2)\;\geq\;2\sinh(L/2)-L,
\end{equation}
then \eqref{eq:prime-free-target} holds on $C_c^\infty(I_L)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Splitting \eqref{eq:gamma-energy} according to whether the second variable lies
in $I_L$, and using $E_Lf=0$ outside,
$K[E_Lf]=D_L[f]+\int_{I_L}|f|^2h$ with $D_L\geq0$ and
$h(x)=N(L/2-x)+N(L/2+x)$. Since $N$ is convex and decreasing,
$h\geq2N(L/2)$. By \eqref{eq:parity-split} the pole term is at least
$-2|\ip{\sinh(x/2)}f|^2$, and
$\norm{\sinh(x/2)}_{L^2(I_L)}^2=\sinh(L/2)-L/2$, so Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$P_L[f]\geq-(2\sinh(L/2)-L)\norm f^2$. Adding the three bounds gives
\eqref{eq:large-L-criterion}.
\end{proof}

On each interval between consecutive primes $c_L$ is constant, $N(L/2)$
decreases and $2\sinh(L/2)-L$ increases, so \eqref{eq:large-L-criterion} need
only be checked at the right endpoints. It first holds on the interval of
primes through $11$ and holds at every later endpoint below $10^6$, with least
margin $0.556$ at $L=\log13$; beyond $10^6$ partial summation with
$\theta(t)\geq t(1-1/\log t)$ gives
$\sum_{p<X}\widetilde\kappa_{r_p,d_p}\geq1.855\sqrt X+62.9$ against a
requirement of $\sqrt X+O(\log X)$. Hence \eqref{eq:prime-free-target} holds
for every $L\geq\log11$, and a weighted form of the same estimate, keeping the
blow-up of $h$ at the endpoints, extends this to $L\geq\log7$. The remaining
range is the compact window $\log2<L<\log7$, where the least Galerkin
eigenvalue of $A_L$ is between $0.25$ and $1$. The supporting computations and
the two estimates are recorded in the investigation's note on this inequality
and in \texttt{check\_prime\_free\_archimedean.py}.

\paragraph{The pole term needs no separate mechanism.}
$P_L$ is the contribution of the two poles of $\zeta$, at the non-real points
$\tau=\pm i/2$, and every pairing used in this manuscript is positive by
construction, so no positive channel adjoined to the others supplies its
negative direction. That is Proposition~\ref{prop:no-augment}, and it is a
statement about channels. It does not upgrade into a structural requirement on
a realization: a realization realizes $Q_L$, not $P_L$ as a separate summand,
and by Corollary~\ref{cor:spectral} one compatible in $L$ is unitarily
equivalent to the evaluation map on the zeros, in a positive $\ell^2$ which has
no null vectors at all. The two poles are not vectors of that space. They are
the two points the functional equation adds to the zero set, and in
\eqref{eq:explicit-formula} the term $P_L$ is exactly what makes the right-hand
side a sum over zeros only.

What absorbs the pole term is the same domination that absorbs the negative
contact. By \eqref{eq:parity-split} its indefiniteness is an odd-sector,
rank-one phenomenon of size at most
$2\norm{\sinh(x/2)}_{L^2(I_L)}^2=2\sinh(L/2)-L$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-compression} places it on the source side, inside
$K_+$. It is not close to binding. In the Galerkin computation of
\texttt{check\_prime\_free\_archimedean.py} the least eigenvalue of $A_L$ lies
in the \emph{even} sector for $L\leq\log2$, where by \eqref{eq:parity-split}
the pole form is positive; above $\log2$ the minimum moves to the odd sector,
and admitting the negative direction there moves the least eigenvalue from
$0.2654$ to $0.2567$ at $L=1$ and from $2.31$ to $2.12$ at $L=2$.

What Lemma~\ref{lem:polepair} and Proposition~\ref{prop:involution} give a
candidate is therefore not a sector to look for but a symmetry to check. Every
source carries the reflection involution $J$ with $J\Phi(f)=\Phi(Rf)$ and
$J^2=\id$, the parities decouple, and a pairing that does not commute with
$x\mapsto-x$ is excluded before any arithmetic is computed. The hyperbolic
description of $P_L$ remains the right way to see which combination of the two
pole evaluations carries the negative direction; it is not a demand for an
indefinite metric.

\paragraph{What to compute in a candidate model.}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:spectral} a realization compatible in $L$ is
translation covariant with spectral measure the zero counting measure. The
object to extract from a candidate theory is therefore a spectral measure or
density of states, and the question to ask of it is whether it is that
measure. Reproducing the terms of \eqref{eq:target} one at a time is not a
weaker version of the same question: Theorem~\ref{thm:two-channel} shows that
every term can be matched exactly while no realization exists.

\subsection{A finite-prime phase identity is useful background}
For comparison with earlier local-factor approaches, define
$S_p=J_p/\overline{J_p}$ using \eqref{eq:prime-phase}, and set
$S_{\mathcal P}=S_\infty\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}S_p$ for a finite prime
set. Logarithmic differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-phase}
 -i\overline{S_{\mathcal P}}S_{\mathcal P}'
 =b(\tau^2)+w_0
 -2\sum_{p\in\mathcal P,\,m\geq1}(\log p)p^{-m/2}
                         \cos(m\tau\log p).
\end{equation}
When $\mathcal P$ contains the active primes of $I_L$, integration
against $|\widehat{E_Lf}|^2/(2\pi)$ and addition of $P_L[f]$ gives
$Q_L[f]$. Inactive repetitions vanish by support. This argument uses
no infinite Euler product on the critical line.

The identity is classical local-factor algebra. Its phase derivative
is negative at $\tau=0$, and the signed poles are outside it. It is
neither a positive norm nor an identification of a canonical semilocal
positive pairing with the Weil form. The present inverse problem is
to find a physical source for the whole form, not merely for this phase.

\subsection{A precise open problem}
\begin{problem}[Joint arithmetic preparation]\label{prob:joint}
Specify a positive quantum, boundary or defect model, with a physically
defined source $\Phi_L(f)$ on smooth compact support, for which the
entire pairing is \eqref{eq:target}. Derive the source's adjoint,
domain and normalization, including the fixed contact, both signed
poles, every active prime repetition, and absence or exact cancellation
of mixed translations. For an all-support realization, give one
compatible rule as $L$ increases.
\end{problem}

A candidate preparation must in addition pass
\eqref{eq:interference-bound} for every decomposition into channels whose
norms it computes separately, and must produce the pole term and the
contact from one mechanism rather than from independent positive pieces.
Theorem~\ref{thm:subtraction} and Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-compression}
reduce Problem~\ref{prob:joint} to a single compression, which is worth
stating on its own.

\begin{problem}[The compression]\label{prob:subtraction}
Exhibit the contraction of Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-compression} from a
specified model: realize $K_+$ of \eqref{eq:source-side} as the norm of a
physically defined source, in place of \eqref{eq:explicit-source}, and $T_L$ of
\eqref{eq:subtracted-side} as $\ip{G\,\cdot}{\Pi\,G\,\cdot}$ for a positive
contraction $\Pi$ derived from that model's own structure. By
\eqref{eq:gamma-compression} this realizes $Q_L$ as a compression, and
$\norm\Pi\leq1$ is the Weil criterion on $I_L$, so the contraction produced
must be critical rather than strict. The presentation of
Corollary~\ref{cor:domination}, with $A_L$ on the dominating side, is an
alternative form of the same problem; it additionally requires
\eqref{eq:prime-free-target}.
\end{problem}

This is an open problem, not an existence conjecture established for
a named field theory. A worthwhile intermediate theorem could be
conditional on construction of a credible specified theory, provided
the construction assumptions do not prescribe the desired covariance.
Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional} would then give the forward
implication to RH. Neither a reverse implication nor an equivalence
with Yang--Mills is proposed here.

The two leading directions have complementary roles. Sphere boundary
modules give the clearest real Gaussian and oscillator normalization
control. The next sphere object should be a boundary or vortex
correspondence acting on that control with a derived infinite source.
An arbitrary choice of coefficients in an orthogonal basis would not
establish the required physical correspondence. The Schur direction
now has a specified positive prime source, and its next object should
be a charge-neutral gauge or RG/interface preparation.

In the conformal $SU(2)$ benchmark, the magnetic generator has two
shifted contributions and a neutral coefficient, schematically
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neutral-word}
 H=uC_+(v)u+C_0(v)+u^{-1}C_-(v)u^{-1},
\end{equation}
and the representation includes a Weyl constraint
\cite[Sections 5.1.2--5.1.3]{Schur}. The notation in
\eqref{eq:neutral-word} describes the structure to retain; it is not
yet a specified arithmetic source. The exact coefficients and projection
must be carried through a chosen neutral line or interface word. The
neutral term and projection are possible mechanisms for joint
interference, not terms whose desired effect can be assumed. This
calculation must also implement the real-input lift or replace it by
another derived boundary map.

The immediate target is the complete identity at $L=1$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-target}
 \norm{\Phi_1(f)}^2=K[E_1f]+w_0\norm f^2+P_1[f]
 -\frac{2\log2}{\sqrt2}\re\ip{E_1f}{U_{\log2}E_1f}.
\end{equation}
The gamma response, contact, pole terms and prime atom must be computed
together, and by Theorem~\ref{thm:two-channel} they cannot be computed
apart. In the form of Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-compression} the same test
reads: realize $K[E_1f]+2|\ip{\cosh(x/2)}f|^2$ as a physically defined source
norm, and
$(-w_0-\widetilde\kappa_{r_2,d_2})\norm f^2+2|\ip{\sinh(x/2)}f|^2
+\widetilde B_{r_2,d_2}[E_1f]$ as a compression of it. At $L=5/4$ the atom at $\log(3/2)$ must be absent; at $L=3/2$
the second return of $2$ must have primitive coefficient $(\log2)/2$
for each oriented translation, while the prime $3$ term remains active.
These are complete identity tests. Failure calls for a different source
or model rather than a growing sequence of residual estimates.

\subsection{Scope of the working manuscript}
The positive gamma action, its integer refinement, and the positive
prime reference are established ingredients. The dressed calculation of
Section~\ref{sec:dressed} places the last of these in an explicit elementary
Schur preparation. Section~\ref{subsec:interference} then shows that this
preparation cannot be completed in the way \eqref{eq:full-comparison}
suggests: no coherent source has the gamma energy and the summed prime
references as its two channel norms. Section~\ref{subsec:subtraction}
replaces that architecture by a subtraction in which the source is prime-free
and every prime translation sits in a compression. The source tests in this manuscript are
sufficiently explicit to be checked and reused, but they have not supplied
the full matching identity. The main remaining difficulty has therefore not
been reduced to a numerical sign check.

Other constructions remain available: interacting boundary gluing,
integrable defects, gauge networks and arithmetic graph models may
provide different source rules. Their broader literature comparison
is retained in the investigation's research notes, while this working
manuscript focuses on the calculations above. The evidence for sphere
and Schur systems is the availability of explicit protected
representations and positive pairings, not an assertion that their
arithmetic matching is inevitable. Further drafts should strengthen a
specific joint construction or replace it when its complete pairing fails.
