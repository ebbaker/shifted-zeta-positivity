\section{Research agenda and present status}\label{sec:outlook}

The current physical result is a quantum insertion hierarchy for a genuine
Loewner trace in one fixed interacting theory. The return chord specifies
its color contraction, the moving-tip terms cancel, and a linear driver
has a short-time response with an explicit smooth-field remainder bound.
The quantum identities assume the stated regularity and moments at fixed
field resolution. They do not yet close on the scalar expectation.

\subsection{The next discriminating tasks}

The immediate task is to apply finite-regulator Schwinger--Dyson equations
to the derivative moment in \eqref{eq:main-trace-second}. Retain the
transverse field components, finite-$N$ product expectations and, at
positive field-flow time, the derivative of $B_\tau[A]$. The concrete
question is whether these terms form a useful closed observable family,
or leave a residual that can be bounded. Deriving that equation does not
require first computing the expectation value explicitly. A change of
return path, endpoint contraction or insertion should be assessed within
the fixed theory before proposing a different bulk action.

The unflowed limit is a separate task. The present short-time theorem
holds at fixed positive resolution and cannot be extrapolated past
uncontrolled cusp or contact terms. Likewise an $\mathcal N=4$ extension
must include its scalar couplings; the pure-YM backtracking control is
not automatically preserved. Loop equations organize this question but
do not identify an arbitrary planar Loewner map with a symmetry of the
four-dimensional theory.

The earlier free and Gaussian tests remain scoped controls. The regular
readout fails the arithmetic singularity and compactness comparison;
the free localized hemisphere fails direct spatial descent, and its
canonical Hodge projection loses higher elementary modes. Those results
do not exclude other observables in an interacting theory. A counting
character, a scalar expectation and an operator on arbitrary boundary
inputs still require distinct comparisons.

If a useful quantum evolution closes, a later boundary construction can
be tested against the complete arithmetic generator and its fixed
normalization. A separate positive-norm identity would still be required.
The arithmetic anchor and bounded append certificate are contextual
results in the critical-path investigation \cite{EMAAnchor,CumulativeCoupling};
they do not provide that physical identification or an all-depth theorem.
The new hierarchy makes progress on the physical relation without making
an arithmetic match an entrance condition for every calculation.
