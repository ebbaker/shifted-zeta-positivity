\section{Continuation and the arithmetic operator norm}
\label{sec:limits}

\subsection{Four parameters with different meanings}

The boundary capacity $t$ in \eqref{eq:chordal}, the generalized Loewner
time $\tau$ in \eqref{eq:radial_flow} or \eqref{eq:koenigs_flow}, the
source translation $\eta$, and the arithmetic shift $\omega$ are distinct.
No equality between them, or between them and the spatial window length,
has been derived.

Differentiating the normalized source gives
\begin{equation}
 \partial_\eta P_\eta(w)
 =\frac{(1-w)^2}{2}\partial_wP_\eta(w)
 -\kappa_\eta P_\eta(w),\qquad
 \kappa_\eta=\frac{m'(1+\eta)}{m(1+\eta)}.
 \label{eq:source_transport}
\end{equation}
For $h\ge0$, it integrates exactly to
\begin{equation}
 P_{\eta+h}(\chi(p))=
 \frac{c_\eta}{c_{\eta+h}}P_\eta(\chi(p+h)).
 \label{eq:translation_composition}
\end{equation}
Right translation preserves the half-plane and the positive-real property.
On safe lines, $u_\eta(y)=\Ree m(\eta+\ii y)$ obeys Poisson smoothing,
\begin{equation}
 u_{\eta+h}(y)=\frac1\pi\int_\R
 \frac{h}{h^2+(y-v)^2}\,u_\eta(v)\dd v,\qquad h>0.
\end{equation}
There is no extra linear harmonic term at infinity, by the xi asymptotic.
Reversing this smoothing need not preserve positivity.

For a concrete control, the even real entire polynomial
\begin{equation}
 H_\delta(p)=((p-\delta)^2+\gamma^2)((p+\delta)^2+\gamma^2)
\end{equation}
has a positive-real shifted logarithmic derivative when $\eta>\delta$,
but interior poles when $\eta<\delta$. At $\delta=1/4,\gamma=2$,
the shift $1/2$ is safe while at $\eta=1/10$ a disk pole has modulus
approximately $0.941951$. This excludes a general continuation argument
based only on functional symmetries and positivity at a safe shift; it is
not a claim about the actual xi zeros. Even $H(p)=p^2+\gamma^2$ has
$m'(x)=2(\gamma^2-x^2)/(x^2+\gamma^2)^2$ of both signs, so simple
pointwise monotonicity cannot replace the missing argument.

\subsection{Endpoint paths and operator actions do not yet match}

The endpoints used in \eqref{eq:source_recovery} satisfy
\begin{equation}
 w_\pm(\omega)=\chi(p\pm\omega-\eta),\qquad
 \partial_\omega w_\pm=\pm\tfrac12(1-w_\pm)^2.
\end{equation}
These are opposite half-plane translations. At $w=0$ their velocities
are $\pm1/2$, while the radial field $-wP_\eta(w)$ vanishes.
A scalar change of clock cannot identify these vector fields on the whole
domain. The reciprocal-source flow has a position-dependent velocity
instead of either fixed translation.

Likewise, the arithmetic action in Laplace coordinates is multiplication,
$F\mapsto K_\omega F$, whereas a self-map acts by composition.
If $W(p)F(\psi(p))=K_\omega(p)F(p)$ for every analytic $F$, testing
$F=1,p$ forces $W=K_\omega$ and $\psi(p)=p$ where $K_\omega\ne0$.
This algebraic test does not presume that $1,p$ are half-plane Hardy
vectors. It excludes the direct identification on all analytic functions,
not a further boundary projection, integral transform or intertwiner.

\subsection{Cumulative positivity is the appropriate target}

Even under RH, $m(p-\omega)$ has poles at $p=\omega+\ii\gamma$
inside $\HR$ for every $\omega>0$. The other source term does not
cancel them. Thus $a_\omega$ is not a holomorphic positive-real driver
on that half-plane. Zeros of $K_\omega$ cancel those poles in
\eqref{eq:arithmetic_shift}.

The failure is also visible away from the poles. For $H(p)=p^2+4$,
$K_\omega$ is a finite half-plane Blaschke product, but at
$p=1/4+2\ii$, $\omega=1/2$, exact arithmetic gives
\begin{equation}
 |K_\omega(p)|^2=\frac{257}{2385}<1,\qquad
 \Ree a_\omega(p)=-\frac{538768}{204315}<0.
 \label{eq:increment_counterexample}
\end{equation}
Hence the squared modulus is locally increasing in $\omega$ despite
remaining below one. Cumulative contraction does not require contractive
multiplicative increments.

The cumulative positive-real candidate is the Cayley transform
\begin{equation}
 Z_\omega=\frac{1-K_\omega}{1+K_\omega},\qquad
 \Ree Z_\omega=\frac{1-|K_\omega|^2}{|1+K_\omega|^2},\qquad
 \partial_\omega Z_\omega=\frac{a_\omega}{2}(1-Z_\omega^2).
 \label{eq:cumulative_cayley}
\end{equation}
The Riccati identity holds on regular regions and through removable
singularities. It is not an independent proof of positivity.

On a finite causal window, the corresponding target is
$V_{0,L}=I$, $\partial_\omega V_{\omega,L}=-G_{\omega,L}V_{\omega,L}$.
Its accumulated defect obeys, on admissible inputs,
\begin{equation}
 I-V_{\omega,L}^*V_{\omega,L}
 =\int_0^\omega
 V_{s,L}^*(G_{s,L}^*+G_{s,L})V_{s,L}\dd s.
 \label{eq:accumulated_defect}
\end{equation}
Generator pairings are understood as forms when separate operator domains
are unavailable. The initial limit is strong, with differentiation on
smooth tests; no operator-norm derivative at zero is assumed. A physical
construction must account for the full accumulated quantity, not impose
the false pointwise condition excluded by \eqref{eq:increment_counterexample}.

\subsection{The unconditional weighted operator control}

Fix $0\le\omega\le\Omega$ and translate the transfer by
$b\ge\Omega+1/2$. Both terms in $a_\omega(p+b)$ are then positive-real
on $\HR$. Integrating \eqref{eq:arithmetic_shift} from $K_0=1$ gives
\begin{equation}
 |K_\omega(p+b)|\le1,\qquad \Ree p>0.
\end{equation}
It is an analytic contractive multiplier on the causal Hardy/Laplace
space. On $L^2(0,L)$, define $(E_bf)(x)=e^{bx}f(x)$. The Laplace
translation rule and causal compression give
\begin{equation}
 V_{\omega,b,L}=E_{-b}V_{\omega,L}E_b,\qquad
 \norm{V_{\omega,b,L}}\le1,\qquad
 \boxed{\norm{V_{\omega,L}}\le e^{bL}.}
 \label{eq:weighted_bound}
\end{equation}
This also defines the bounded unshifted finite-window operator consistently
with its causal inverse Laplace kernel. Pointwise multiplier convergence
and dominated convergence give the strong identity limit at zero shift.

The last estimate is an upper bound, not a claim of actual exponential
growth. Its length-dependent cost is precisely why safe-line positivity
does not give the unweighted contraction. The unconditional $b$ remains
at least $1/2$, so the bound does not tend to one as $L\to\infty$.
