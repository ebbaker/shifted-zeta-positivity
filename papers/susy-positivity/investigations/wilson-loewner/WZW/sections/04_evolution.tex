\section{Complete deterministic evolution and the tensor norm}
\label{sec:evolution}

The relative coordinates and Jacobians satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \dot a=2/a-\dot u,\quad \dot b=2/b-\dot u,\qquad
 \partial_t\log J_x=-2/a^2,\quad
 \partial_t\log J_y=-2/b^2.
 \label{eq:geometry_rates}
\end{equation}
The two finite-coordinate KZ connections are
\begin{equation}
 A_a=\nu\left(\frac Da+\frac B{a-b}\right),\qquad
 A_b=\nu\left(\frac Cb+\frac B{b-a}\right).
\end{equation}
Translation invariance includes the moving tip:
$\partial_uF=-(\partial_a+\partial_b)F=-\nu(D/a+C/b)F$.
Differentiating \eqref{eq:observable} therefore yields $\dot M=GM$, with
\begin{equation}
 G=\nu\left[
 \left(\frac2{a^2}-\frac{\dot u}a\right)D+
 \left(\frac2{b^2}-\frac{\dot u}b\right)C-
 \frac{2B}{ab}\right]
 -2h\left(\frac1{a^2}+\frac1{b^2}\right)I.
 \label{eq:ward_generator}
\end{equation}
The $B$ coefficient follows by subtracting the two spectator velocities;
their common $-\dot u$ cancels in that coefficient, but not in the $D,C$
terms. The final scalar comes from the primary Jacobians.

\begin{proposition}[Deterministic square reduction]
On every compact interval before collision or swallowing, the normalized
observable satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\dot M_t=-\nu(2Q_t^2+\dot u_tQ_t)M_t,\qquad
 Q_t=\frac D{a_t}+\frac C{b_t},\quad \nu=\tfrac14.}
 \label{eq:square_reduction}
\end{equation}
The associated two-dimensional initial-value problem has a unique
invertible propagator.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The matrices in \eqref{eq:matrices} obey
\begin{equation}
 D^2=\tfrac34I-D,\qquad C^2=\tfrac34I-C,\qquad DC+CD=B.
 \label{eq:spin_identities}
\end{equation}
Expanding $-2\nu(D/a+C/b)^2$ reproduces the terms in
\eqref{eq:ward_generator} independent of $\dot u$, since
$2h=3\nu/2$. The remaining term is $-\nu\dot uQ$.
The coefficients are continuous and bounded on the stated compact time
interval, giving the usual matrix-ODE existence and uniqueness.
\end{proof}

The same algebraic reduction extends to a spin-$1/2$ tip with finitely
many real boundary primary spectators, using
$Q=\sum_iT_{0i}/w_i$, $h_i=\nu C_i/2$,
$T_{0i}^2=C_iI/2-T_{0i}$ and
$\{T_{0i},T_{0j}\}=T_{ij}$ for $i\ne j$.
Boundary conditions and integrable channels still require specification.
Complex bulk or mirror coordinates need not give Hermitian $Q$.

\subsection{An exact balance for a constant driver}

For the real boundary coordinates, $Q=Q^\dagger$ in the fixed tensor
metric of $W$. If $\dot u=0$, differentiation of the norm and integration
give
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\norm{M_T}_W^2+4\nu\int_0^T\norm{Q_tM_t}_W^2\dd t
 =\norm{M_0}_W^2.}
 \label{eq:tensor_balance}
\end{equation}
This holds for every initial evaluated vector in $W$, without assuming
that matrices at different times commute. For $a=1,b=2$,
\begin{equation}
 G_{\dot u=0}=\begin{pmatrix}
 -39/32&-3\sqrt3/16\\-3\sqrt3/16&-3/32
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
More generally $\det Q=-3(1/a-1/b)^2/4$, so distinct spectators give
an invertible $Q$. The constant-driver evolution is nontrivial.

\subsection{Moving drivers can increase the same norm}

The general identity is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\norm{M}_W^2
 =-4\nu\norm{QM}_W^2-2\nu\dot u\ip{M}{QM}_W.
 \label{eq:moving_balance}
\end{equation}
At $a=1,b=2$, $\dot u=4$,
\begin{equation}
 G=\begin{pmatrix}9/32&\sqrt3/16\\\sqrt3/16&-3/32\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For the selected state \eqref{eq:selected_initial}, the initial derivative
of its squared norm is approximately $0.436388495438834>0$.
This disproves an all-driver contraction claim even for the chosen Cardy
preparation. It does not imply monotone growth at later times.

Completing the square gives the general bound
\begin{equation}
 G=-2\nu(Q+\dot uI/4)^2+\nu\dot u^2I/8,
 \qquad
 \norm{U(T,0)}_W\le
 \exp\left(\frac\nu8\int_0^T\dot u^2\dd t\right).
 \label{eq:driver_bound}
\end{equation}
Multiplying $M$ by the inverse scalar on the right would make this
pairing contractive for every $C^1$ driver. That factor is additional to
\eqref{eq:observable}; no physical partition function has been shown to
supply it. It therefore cannot stand in for a fixed arithmetic normalization.
