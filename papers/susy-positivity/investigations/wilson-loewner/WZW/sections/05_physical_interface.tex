\section{The next test: a finite-place physical interface}
\label{sec:interface}

The coefficient calculation selects a concrete next candidate: the
positive sector \eqref{eq:finite_hamiltonian} for $S=\{2,3\}$,
coupled to an archimedean radiation channel. It has native observables
$P_2$, $P_3$ and $P_6$, and its equilibrium state exists at every
relevant temperature. The mathematical task is now to specify and
derive the coupling, not to invent another list of arithmetic weights.

The first comparison should include $n=2,3,4,6$. The first two test
the primitive amplitudes, $n=4$ tests repetition, and $n=6$ tests the
combination of distinct primes. A successful model must meet four
linked requirements:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An explicit Hamiltonian or boundary coupling, together with its
state preparation and measured output, must convert the native
arithmetic labels into physical propagation delays $\log n$.
\item The same construction must derive the centered amplitudes
$n^{(\beta-1)/2}\varphi_\beta(P_n)$ and their parameter derivative,
including $c_2'(1/2)=(3/2)\log2$. A temperature-dependent detector
chosen to equal $B_{n,\beta}$ is not by itself that derivation.
\item The archimedean sector must produce
$A_\omega$ in \eqref{eq:archimedean} with $\beta=2\omega$, including
the rational completion as well as the gamma factor.
\item The full assembly must have an ordinary radiation-energy
balance that accounts for internal storage, later output and any
additional channels. It must not define its positive norm by
pulling back the desired arithmetic transfer.
\end{enumerate}
Assigning a filter equal to $A_\omega$ times a finite Euler product
would merely restate the target. A finite-place model need not be
lossless if omitted channels carry explicitly accounted flux; an
unexplained gain or deficit cannot be treated as the desired scalar
response.

Only after this finite interface is derived should one seek an
infinite-place common-space limit. Proposition~\ref{prop:product_obstruction}
identifies a specific embedding that cannot supply it. The full
KMS construction remains available, but an intertwiner from that
construction to the required fixed radiation norm is an additional
obligation.

The zero-shift endpoint requires a separate check as well. For each
fixed $n>1$, $c_n(\omega)\to0$ as $\omega\downarrow0$, while
$c_1=1$. This is the correct coefficientwise identity limit.
Meanwhile, the geometric thermal distribution at each prime spreads
to arbitrarily high occupation as $\beta\downarrow0$. Coefficientwise
convergence is therefore not yet a strong state limit or a strong
scattering limit. The current result supplies an independently
motivated arithmetic probability law and precisely locates the
physical work that remains.
