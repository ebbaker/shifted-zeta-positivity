## Mathematical checks

1. **Test domain and powers of R.** $f_R=R^{-2}T[R^{1/2}h(Rx)]$ lies exactly in the global pole-neutral space. Its Fourier transform is $R^{-5/2}(\tau^2+1/4)\widehat h(\tau/R)$, not $R^{-1/2}\widehat h(\tau/R)$. Its squared norm is $\|h''\|^2+\|h'\|^2/(2R^2)+\|h\|^2/(16R^4)$. Rescaling the archimedean integral gives the leading $\|h''\|^2\log R$ and the finite constant displayed in the note. The region $|u|<1/R$ contributes a vanishing error. No pole constraint or contact is approximated.
2. **Nonzero translations include prime contacts.** A fixed nonzero prime-power separation produces $-\Lambda(a)\|f_R\|^2/\sqrt a$, not zero. All other prime overlaps eventually vanish. The smooth archimedean cross term is $O(R^{-5})$, after moving both copies of T onto the separated kernel. Therefore all these pairings are $o(\log R)$, which is all the compact-return proof needs.
3. **Weak-null step.** Pairing with each fixed $Jg$ gives $O(R^{-5/2}/\sqrt{\log R})$. Boundedness and density extend this to every vector of the source closure, and orthogonality handles its complement. The proof does not assume compact source balls, a zero spectrum, an ordinary-input-norm bound, or a distributional seed of finite order.
4. **Essential spectrum argument.** The normalized spectral moments at a nonzero step converge to Haar moments on the circle. Consequently a nonzero polynomial, and then any continuous function, has the stated positive limiting squared norm. A continuous function supported outside essential spectrum would be compact by normal functional calculus modulo compacts. This proves the full-circle result, not just nonperiodicity. The group must be unitary on the physical Hilbert space and implement the source translations. A merely formal insertion does not meet the assumptions.
5. **State and compression.** The actual full electric form is evaluated on $F_0I+F_1\Sigma$. Trace/2 removes cross terms, the angular adjoint term is $2|F_1|^2$, and four distinct links give the common factor four. The derived operators are form compressions. No unproved invariance under the full electric operator is asserted. The density potential $v''/v$ is retained, not subtracted to obtain an arithmetic expression.
6. **Both polarizations and endpoint domains.** The scalar comparison spectrum is $n^2$, $n\ge1$; the adjoint comparison spectrum is $n^2$, $n\ge2$, using the Friedrichs realization of $2\csc^2\theta$. The factorization by $\partial-\cot\theta$ identifies the missing $n=1$ mode and the non-square-integrable adjoint kernel. A bounded retained potential shifts squared eigenvalues by at most its supremum norm, hence frequencies by $O(1/n)$. Both signs therefore have a compact return at $2\pi$. Keeping only the scalar branch would make the exclusion much less informative because its spectrum is already one-sided.
7. **Actual source completion.** Finite-order distributional seeds have a negative electric Sobolev norm. Rapid Fourier decay of the compact smooth input makes the full source series converge strongly, with its positive state-dependent mixed sum. Increasing seed order includes derivatives; an ordinary smearing bound is not silently imposed. No discarded winding channel, positive-matrix embedding, or subtraction supplies that norm. All sources under this group, including seeds outside this explicitly constructed class, are excluded by the compact-return theorem if they claim the complete pairing.
8. **Continuum model declaration.** The second candidate changes to an infinite-volume positive-energy Poincaré vacuum representation. This is allowed as a conditional model change and has no identified limiting relationship to the prepared finite slab. Separability and vacuum uniqueness are explicit. A physical gap is permitted but not used. Ordinary spacetime covariance of the proposed source is an extra relation with independent physical meaning, not a requirement of all Weil sources.
9. **Lorentz averaging.** The control momentum measure and its average over a compactly supported Lorentz-group density have the same null sets by covariance, continuity and nonnegativity. For each nonzero forward momentum, the Lorentz-orbit projection onto any nonzero spacetime direction is real analytic and nonconstant; its critical set is null and its regular charts give absolutely continuous pushforward. Tonelli transfers the conclusion to every vector measure. No assumption about stable particles, a mass-shell list, or asymptotic completeness is needed.
10. **Vacuum term and probe dependence.** A continuum coefficient is a vacuum constant plus a $C_0$ function. Equality with the detecting response first implies RH by the previously proved boundedness criterion; then zero Cesàro mean removes the constant and positive mean square contradicts decay. This is a contradiction proof, not an assumption of RH. The probe product's nonvanishing strip, its absolute convergence at zeros, and the Laplace-residue argument were rechecked in Section 10 and the original single-probe note.
