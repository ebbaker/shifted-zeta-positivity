## 1. Judgment

The central displayed identities survive this review under their stated finite-slab hypotheses. I found no substantive coefficient, sign, or weighted-adjoint error requiring retraction of the complete **signed mixed limit**. The archimedean calculation really does recover the local constant as well as the separated kernel; it is more than a kernel match.

It does not provide a positive YM source, and the remaining gap is structural. The state-dependent compression/compensation and normalized derivative define an admissible signed insertion. No identity identifies that insertion, together with the prime contribution, as the Gram form of physical sources. OS positivity applies to an actual reflected norm; it does not make this insertion positive.

The most useful next direction is therefore **an independently positive source pairing in the fixed physical representation**, with source relations weaker than occurrence of the entire character winding module. Further special-function matching or repair of individual phase divergences is lower priority unless it comes with that positive source construction.

There is a new reason for this recommendation. The accompanying [bounded winding-occurrence obstruction](../notes/BOUNDED_WINDING_OCCURRENCE_OBSTRUCTION_20260925.md) proves that the whole limiting winding module has no nonzero bounded intertwiner into the original class space preserving all original windings. This already holds on the module generated by the identity phase, and with pole-neutral starting profiles. Thus bounded, winding-preserving recovery of these packets is not merely an unverified compactness hypothesis: it is impossible in that specified class.

## 2. Weighted adjoints, branching, and the infinite prime insertion

The four-dimensional state remains the fixed SU(2) Wilson slab with spatial size \(6^3\), five time slices, beta \(8/5\), and boundary measure \(Z^{-1}\Omega^2dm\). The marginal proof is valid: changing one distinct plaquette link to the holonomy is Haar preserving, and integrating the remaining compact variables gives a smooth strictly positive central \(\rho\). Neither Haar independence nor a simulated marginal is used.

With normalized Haar radial measure, direct product-to-sum gives

\[
\langle\chi_n,\chi_m\rangle_\nu=t_{n-m}-t_{n+m},\qquad
t_k=\pi^{-1}\int_0^\pi\rho(\theta)\cos(k\theta)d\theta.
\]

The correct normalization is \(t_0-t_2=1\). On the odd-circle representation, winding is composition \(C_a u(\theta)=u(a\theta)\). Splitting the circle into inverse branches gives exactly

\[
C_a^{*\rho}=\rho^{-1}L_a\rho,\qquad
C_a^{*\rho}C_a=M_{\rho_a/\rho},\qquad\rho_a=L_a\rho.
\tag{1}
\]

Thus the actual winding is generally not an isometry. The audit correctly refuses to use the Haar divisibility adjoint in its place.

For the normalized phase packets, the root-of-unity filter proves the branching formula before taking any limit. The Toeplitz part of the Gram matrix supplies \(\rho(\alpha)\) on equal phases and vanishes on distinct fixed phases; the Hankel part is rapidly small. In particular opposite phases are distinct except at zero and pi. These facts give

\[
\lim_R\langle S_Rf,V_aS_Rg\rangle_\nu
=a^{-1/2}\langle f,U_{\log a}g\rangle.
\tag{2}
\]

This is a fixed-test, fixed-winding statement. It is not a norm limit of operators on ordinary input L2, where the finite-R sampling maps are not bounded.

For the infinite insertion \(\mathcal L=\sum_a\Lambda(a)V_a\), the following distinctions in the audit are necessary and correct:

- On smooth character observables, the series is a distribution in \(H^{-s}\), \(s>1/2\). At label \(k\), the coefficient is \(\sum_{n\mid k,n<k}\Lambda(k/n)b_n\), bounded by \(\log k\sum_n|b_n|\). This proves the stated negative-Sobolev convergence on that core.
- It is not Hilbert valued on any nonzero finite character combination. If \(m\) is the smallest nonzero label and \(p\) exceeds all labels, the coefficient at \(mp\) is exactly \(b_m\log p\). Those coefficients are not square summable. Actual norm equivalence transfers the conclusion to \(\nu\).
- For raw packets the separated-index estimate is \(O(N^{1-M}a^{-M})\), where \(N=e^R\). It is summable with \(\Lambda(a)\leq\log a\) and establishes the distributional prime mixed limit, including cofinal prime cutoffs, for these packets.
- None of this constructs an infinite Hilbert adjoint or an OS source represented by \(\mathcal LS_Rf\). The electric-parity note correctly uses fixed finite cutoffs before the R limit for its modified packets. Mere norm closeness of modified and raw packets would not transfer an unbounded-insertion limit.

No further correction to (1), (2), or this distributional argument was found.

## 3. The electric current and phase failure

The full weighted electric commutator is the appropriate operator. For \(X=\cos\theta\), \(\ell=4\),

\[
A_\nu=\frac{i}{2\ell}[E_\nu,X]
=-i(Y+\tfrac12\operatorname{div}_\nu Y),\qquad
Y=\ell^{-1}\nabla X.
\]

The actual drift correction is \(-i\ell^{-1}\nabla\log\Omega\cdot\nabla X\). It is bounded and need not preserve class functions. The note properly distinguishes this full operator from the subsequent compressed radial construction.

The Haar coefficient calculation gives, at a fixed phase beta,

\[
A_0c_n=N\sin\beta\,e^{i\beta n}n^{-1/2}(e^xf)(\log(n/N))
-i\cos\beta\,e^{i\beta n}n^{-1/2}f'(\log(n/N))
+\text{remainder}.
\]

At zero and pi the principal term vanishes. The smooth drift also vanishes in norm on those packets because \(|\nabla X|=\sqrt\ell|\sin\theta|\). This checks the two signs:

\[
A_\nu S_R^0f=S_R^0(-if')+o(1),\qquad
A_\nu S_R^\pi f=S_R^\pi(+if')+o(1).
\tag{3}
\]

The winding-two defect in the audit consequently has squared limit
\(2\rho(\pi)\rho(0)^{-1}\|f'\|_2^2\), as claimed. Other roots with nonzero sine have an order-N current and order-N-squared energy. Bounded drift cannot cancel it.

For a smooth radial Wilson current generated by \(h(X)\), the principal coefficient is \(h'(\cos\beta)\sin\beta\). Bounded response on every rational phase forces this continuous function to vanish on a dense set, and hence forces h to be constant. The scoped smooth-current obstruction is sound. It does not exclude singular or scale-dependent operations, or source laws that do not retain the full winding action.

## 4. Archimedean response: what is established

Write \(\mathscr W_\rho F=\sqrt{2/\pi}\sqrt\rho\sin\theta F\), \(D=\sqrt{-\partial_\theta^2{}_D}\), \(B=\partial_\theta D^{-1}\), and \(q=\theta/(2\pi)\). The proposed insertion is

\[
C_\rho=\mathscr W_\rho^{-1}
\bigl(\log D+B^*\log q\,B\bigr)\mathscr W_\rho.
\tag{4}
\]

The adjoint and domain points are correct. B is an isometry from the sine basis into the mean-zero cosine subspace, rather than a unitary onto all interval L2. Finite sine polynomials lie in the domains of both terms in (4), because a cosine polynomial times \(\log q\) is square integrable. Their sum is densely defined symmetric and hence closable. No self-adjointness or positive square root follows merely from these facts, and the note does not claim either.

The compressed electric form transforms to \(-\partial_\theta^2+v''/v\), with \(v=\sqrt\rho\sin\theta\). The compensating potential is smooth at both endpoints. It is legitimate to specify its subtraction as part of an insertion ansatz. It is not a restriction of the full weighted electric operator, nor a proved dynamical Ward identity.

For the packets \(F_Rf=\sum n^{-1/2}f(\log(n/N))\rho^{-1/2}\chi_n\), the two R terms cancel exactly because \(B^*B=I\). Rescaling the cosine sum gives a periodized half-line cosine transform of \(H_f(t)=t^{-1/2}f(\log t)\). The zero-frequency singularity is integrable with the logarithmic weight, and the compact smooth input away from t=0 gives Schwartz decay of the transform. These facts justify weighted Poisson convergence, not only ordinary norm convergence.

Mellin transformation gives

\[
\gamma_+(\tau)=\pi^{i\tau}
\frac{\Gamma(1/4-i\tau/2)}{\Gamma(1/4+i\tau/2)},\qquad
i\gamma_+'(\tau)/\gamma_+(\tau)
=\operatorname{Re}\psi(1/4+i\tau/2)-\log\pi.
\tag{5}
\]

The derivative sign and normalization in (5) check out. The starting Mellin integrals and gamma identities were checked against [DLMF 5.9.6–7](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.9#E6) and [5.5](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.5). Omitting B gives the sine channel and 3/4 instead of 1/4; that is a substantive parity distinction correctly handled by the note.

The digamma integral yields the requested off-diagonal kernel and contact value; its asymptotic gives logarithmic growth. The normalization is

\[
m_+(0)=-\gamma-\frac\pi2-3\log2-\log\pi,
\qquad m_+(\tau)=\log(|\tau|/(2\pi))+O(|\tau|^{-2}).
\tag{6}
\]

The primary formulas are [DLMF 5.9.16](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.9#E16) and [5.11.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11#E2). Their use here establishes a limit for each fixed input; it gives no simultaneous uniformity in packet scale and arbitrarily growing arithmetic modulation.

Combining this response with the original finite windings, first taking R to infinity and then the prime cutoff to infinity, therefore gives the complete signed Q on the global pole-neutral domain. This is the strongest affirmative conclusion of the reviewed work.

**Normalization versus physical selection.** The contact in (6) is derived from the stated integer/turn Fourier convention, not fitted after calculating a kernel. However, this convention and the compensated parity prescription are part of the chosen observable. No YM principle is proved to select their sum as an OS norm. For example, adding \(B^*M_hB\) with a smooth real h adds \(h(0)\langle f,g\rangle\) in the packet limit. This does not invalidate (4) or (5); it explains why a successful insertion calculation is distinct from a source-occurrence or positivity theorem. The original note already acknowledges this distinction, and it must be retained in subsequent summaries.

## 5. Positivity and occurrence are separate unsolved obligations

The prime zero-domain theorem is valid. For every nonzero fixed class vector F,

\[
V_aF\rightharpoonup0,\qquad
\|(I-V_a)F\|_\nu^2\longrightarrow
\|F\|_\nu^2+t_0\|F\|_{\rm Haar}^2>0.
\]

Since \(\sum_a\Lambda(a)=\infty\), the unrenormalized positive prime-difference form is finite only at zero. Pole neutrality of an input cannot remove the nonzero output norm in this argument. Formal subtraction of the divergent diagonal changes the form and loses the prior positivity argument.

The archimedean response is itself negative on some exactly pole-neutral broad profiles. Thus it cannot simply be given its own positive square root. This is compatible with possible positivity of the completed arithmetic form, whose terms have to be treated together. The negative full-core example in the response note uses the transported winding \(\widetilde V_a\); it is correctly labeled and must not be quoted as a counterexample for the original \(V_a\), or for the limiting pole-neutral source image.

The raw and compensated packets retain norm while tending weakly to zero. Their positive-order electric Sobolev norms grow like \(N^r\), invalidating the uniform compact-source balls. The fixed-closable-insertion lemma is correct: testing against the dense adjoint domain proves weak convergence of bounded inserted outputs to zero. A nonzero strong limit cannot follow.

The accompanying new obstruction further rules out uniformly bounded, scale-dependent recovery into the class space if it preserves every original winding and converges strongly on the whole winding orbit. Its proof uses the fact that ordinary character labels have finitely many prime divisors, whereas almost every label in the derived profinite limit has divisors outside every finite prime set. It does not assume that physical windings are isometries; bounded similarities account explicitly for \(\rho\).

This is a sharper scope statement, not a contradiction of the earlier note's example of a scale-dependent unitary restoring one vector. Such a unitary need not preserve the winding module. Nor is the new obstruction a theorem against arbitrary smooth-test source maps into the full YM Hilbert space.

## 6. Recommended next investigation

The next project should begin with an independently defined **positive physical source pairing** and test whether the already derived signed identity is its evaluation. The existing work approached from the other direction: it assembled correct signed terms, leaving positivity and occurrence untouched. Another gamma calculation would not address either obligation.

A concrete success criterion is one law \(J:\mathcal D^0\to\mathcal H_{\rm YM}\), or finite feasible source relations with compact control, for which the actual reflected Gram pairing is derived from the model. Then establish the separated-support gamma-plus-prime identity, symmetries, logarithmic growth, and the three local anchors from the [determination theorem](../notes/OFF_DIAGONAL_RIGIDITY_AND_COMPACT_SOURCE_EXISTENCE_20260924.md). The relations selecting J must precede matching its arithmetic moments. Assuming positivity of the completed signed form and taking its abstract square root would assume the outstanding result.

For the present character approach, the first discriminating question is whether an independently defined physical constraint or compression can supply such a pairing **without requiring the entire limiting winding module to occur with its original windings**. Any proposal should first exhibit its positive finite expression and its source domain, and explain how the prime local divergence is absent or physically cancelled with a proved positive limit. Only then is phase engineering useful. No such constraint or positive completion is supplied by the reviewed files, and this review does not invent one.

There are two practical decisions:

1. Do not invest further in bounded winding-preserving recovery of the full character packet module; the accompanying theorem excludes it. An unbounded relation, loss of exact winding covariance, or a genuinely different physical observable sector would need an explicit new argument. None is ruled out by this review.
2. Do not make a common finite response on every rational phase a universal prerequisite for the original source goal. It is necessary only for a proposed source module that retains those operations. The bare goal asks for the correct reflected mixed pairing, and a separately native translation generator is optional. A narrower source law can be investigated if its prime mixed identity is derived directly.

The strongest remaining use of the present calculations is as a stringent verification target: exact coefficients, contact, domain requirements, phase losses, and failed positive constructions are now known. A proposed source relation should be rejected early if it merely reproduces these signed matrix coefficients or remains locally L2 bounded. The reviewed work does not yet justify preferring a particular new matter theory, two-dimensional model, or extra arithmetic sector; changing the physical model is not the recommended default.
