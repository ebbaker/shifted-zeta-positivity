## 0. Summary

The flipped-return loop is the prefix of the Loewner trace with scalar direction n₀, closed by the straight chord back to the base with direction −n₀. It is a well-behaved observable in every regime examined, and it carries genuine shape information at first order in s = a√t. The main results:

1. **Exact evolution equations.** For smooth fields, the first two evolution equations are exact. The generator is 𝒳 = ik(J + i𝒢) + γΦ̂(1). The coefficient γ of the scalar tip insertion is exactly d(L_trace − L_chord)/dt, the growth rate of the perimeter mismatch. The second equation, Ẅ = ⟨N⁻¹tr((𝒳̇ + 𝒳²)Q)⟩, follows from one transported-derivative rule. Both hold for any driver with a C² trace, and both are verified by finite differences on a noncommuting background.
2. **One loop in the continuum** (cusp neighbourhoods of size ε excised):
\[
\log W=B_1\Big[-A(s)\log\frac{\ell}{\varepsilon}+F(s)\Big]+O(\varepsilon),\qquad
A(s)=\sum_{\rm cusps}2(\pi-\alpha_c)\tan\frac{\alpha_c}{2}=\frac{2\pi}{3}s+A_2s^2+\dots,\qquad
F(s)=\frac{4\pi}{3}s+O\big(s^2\log\tfrac1s\big).
\]
Here A₂ ≈ −0.224. So W → 1 like 1 − (2πB₁/3)|s|(log(ℓ/ε) − 2).
3. **One loop with gradient-flow smearing: three regimes.**
   - Regime I (τ ≫ t): log W = B₁[−a²t³/(1296τ²) + a⁴t³/(5832τ)] (1 + O(s², t/τ)). This independently confirms the interacting smooth-field short-time formula of the first note, including its scalar-gradient and length-mismatch terms.
   - Regime II (s²t ≪ τ ≪ t): log W ≈ −B₁(√(2π)/27)s²√(t/τ).
   - Regime III (τ ≪ s²t): log W ≈ −B₁(2πs/3)[log(8κ) + γ_E/2 − 2], with κ = s√t/(6√(8τ)). Point-splitting schemes cut the near-BPS cusp where the sliver width reaches √τ, which produces a |s| log|s| term.
4. **Open all-orders question.** Every regime is regular, and the order of the limits t → 0 and τ → 0 changes the exponent (a²t³ versus |a|√t log) but never produces a divergence. The all-orders continuum coefficient of |s| log is the near-BPS cusp limit H(λ,N) = lim Γ_cusp(π − α, π)/α. At one loop H = λ(1 − 1/N²)/(16π). The known near-BPS formula does not apply uniformly as φ → π (it is off by a factor of 2 at one loop), so H is open. *Update (same day): resolved in the [corner note](NEAR_BPS_CORNER_ROUNDING_AND_CLOSURE_20260924.md). H = λ(1−1/N²)/(16π) is one-loop exact order by order. The effective coupling is λ|s|, and at strong coupling there is a √(λ|s|) window. The factor-2 mismatch came from linearizing at x = 0 instead of at the BPS line x = 1, where the near-BPS formula is exact.*
5. **The second equation is not closed.** It introduces irreducible transverse-derivative, ordered-pair and tip-gradient insertions; Section 6 lists them. The field equations reduce only the Laplacian parts.

## 1. The observable

Take the Loewner trace q[0,t], generated by a driver u, with the Maldacena–Wilson connection iA·dx + |dx|n₀·Φ. Close it with the straight chord from q(t) back to the base, carrying the connection iA·dx − |dx|n₀·Φ. Traversed in reverse, that chord transport is the inverse of the base-to-tip transport R_t with connection iA·dx + |dx|Φ. Hence
\[
Q_t=R_t(1)^{-1}U_t,\qquad W(t)=\big\langle N^{-1}\operatorname{tr}Q_t\big\rangle .
\]

**Basic properties** (proofs are elementary):

- For u ≡ 0 the trace is vertical and Q_t = I for every configuration: backtracking along a one-form transport.
- The reflection x₁ → −x₁, combined with orientation reversal and charge conjugation, maps a to −a. So W is even in a, and in the continuum it depends on |s|.
- The loop is not supersymmetric for s ≠ 0: the trace has constant n₀ but nonzero curvature. As s → 0 it approaches the backtracked one-form configuration, and its opposite legs become antiparallel with opposite scalar charge (the no-force pairing). Both cusps have deflection π − α_c and internal angle π, hence are nearly BPS.
- By conformal collapse (first note, Proposition 6), for the linear driver the renormalized W depends on t only through s = a√t, the cusp scalings, and the ratio of any regulator scale to √t.

## 2. Exact evolution equations for smooth fields

Write k = q₁q̇₂ − q₂q̇₁, n̂ = (−q₂, q₁)/|q|, ρ = |q| and γ = |q̇| − ρ̇. Hats denote conjugation by R_t(r). Put J(r) = ∫₀^r r′F̂₁₂ dr′, 𝒢(r) = ∫₀^r r′(n̂·DΦ)^ dr′, J = J(1) and 𝒢 = 𝒢(1).

**Proposition 1 (first equation; first note, Proposition 7).**
\[
\dot Q_t=\mathcal X_tQ_t,\qquad \mathcal X_t=ik\,(J+i\mathcal G)+\gamma\,\hat\Phi(1).
\]
Since ∫₀^t γ = L_trace(t) − |q(t)|, the scalar tip coefficient is exactly the growth rate of the perimeter mismatch between the two legs. For the constant-n₀ completion the corresponding coefficient is d(L_trace + L_chord)/dt, the growth rate of the total perimeter. That is why the constant-n₀ family measures perimeter while the flipped family measures shape.

**Proposition 2 (transported-derivative rule).** For a local adjoint field X evaluated on the chord,
\[
\partial_t\hat X(r)=r\,\widehat{(\dot q\cdot DX)}(r)+\big[\hat X(r),\mathcal K(r)\big]+\widehat{(\partial_tX)}_{\rm explicit},
\qquad
\mathcal K(r)=-ik\big(J(r)+i\mathcal G(r)\big)+\dot\rho\,r\,\hat\Phi(r).
\]

*Proof.* The parent ordered-transport variation, applied to the sub-chord [0,r] with η = r′q̇, gives R(r)⁻¹∂_tR(r) = R(r)⁻¹(iA·rq̇)R(r) + ∫₀^r 𝓘̂. The gauge part combines with ∂_t[X(rq)] into the covariant derivative. The integral ∫₀^r 𝓘̂ reduces to 𝒦(r) by the same radial integration by parts as in Proposition 1, using ∂_rΦ̂ = (q·DΦ)^. ∎

**Proposition 3 (second equation).** Ẅ = ⟨N⁻¹tr((𝒳̇ + 𝒳²)Q)⟩, with
\[
\dot{\mathcal X}=i\dot k\,(J+i\mathcal G)+\dot\gamma\,\hat\Phi(1)+ik\,(\dot J+i\dot{\mathcal G})+\gamma\Big[\widehat{(\dot q\cdot D\Phi)}(1)+[\hat\Phi(1),\mathcal K(1)]\Big],
\]
\[
\dot J=\int_0^1 r\Big(r\,\widehat{(\dot q\cdot DF_{12})}+[\hat F_{12},\mathcal K(r)]\Big)dr,
\]
\[
\dot{\mathcal G}=\int_0^1 r\Big(r\,\widehat{(\dot q^\mu\hat n^\nu D_\mu D_\nu\Phi)}-\frac{k}{|q|^2}\widehat{(\hat q\cdot D\Phi)}+[\widehat{(\hat n\cdot D\Phi)},\mathcal K(r)]\Big)dr .
\]
The middle term of 𝒢̇ comes from the rotation of n̂, dn̂/dt = −(k/|q|²)q̂. For the linear driver q̈ = 2q̇/q². For a general driver, q̈ is needed, so the trace must be C² for t > 0.

**Checks (F3, T5).** On the SU(2) background of `check_trace_completion.py`, with nonconstant noncommuting A and Φ:

- 𝒳̇ from Proposition 3 matches central finite differences of 𝒳 to ≤ 1.2×10⁻⁹ relative, at four (a,t) points.
- d²trQ/dt², computed from directly integrated transports, matches tr((𝒳̇ + 𝒳²)Q) to ≤ 8×10⁻⁷.
- At a = 0, 𝒳 and 𝒳̇ vanish identically.

**Ordered structure.** The terms ik·ik[F̂, J(r)] from J̇ and (ikJ)² from 𝒳² combine into −2k²𝒫, exactly as in the parent YM hierarchy, where 𝒫 is the ordered F̂F̂ moment. The new terms are ordered pairs mixing F̂, (n̂·DΦ)^ and Φ̂(r) on the chord, together with chord–tip pairs through [Φ̂(1), 𝒦(1)] and the γ·ik cross terms in 𝒳². These do not combine into a single ordered moment, so Proposition 3 is stated in commutator form.

## 3. Short time at fixed resolution, and an independent one-loop confirmation

The first note gave, configuration by configuration, (1/N)trQ − 1 = (1/2N)tr(𝒜(iF₁₂ + D₁Φ)(0) + (L_trace − L_chord)Φ(0))² + O(t^{7/2}), with 𝒜 = −(2a/9)t^{3/2} and L_trace − L_chord = a²t^{3/2}/27 (1 + O(s²)). In an SO(6)-invariant, parity-invariant flowed state this becomes
\[
W_\tau=1+\frac{2a^2t^3}{81}\big(C^\Phi_\tau-C_\tau\big)+\frac{a^4t^3}{1458}S_\tau+O(t^{7/2}).
\]

**One-loop test.** In the free theory with the gradient-flow propagator g²(1 − e^{−x²/8τ})/(4π²x²), applied to gauge fields and scalars alike, the condensates are C_τ = B₁/(16τ²), C^Φ_τ = B₁/(32τ²) and S_τ = B₁/(4τ). The prediction is therefore
\[
\log W_\tau^{(1)}=B_1\Big[-\frac{a^2t^3}{1296\,\tau^2}+\frac{a^4t^3}{5832\,\tau}\Big]+\dots
\]

Independently, expanding the flowed one-loop Maldacena–Wilson integral for τ ≫ t gives B₁[M₀/(8τ) − M₂/(128τ²) + M₄/(3072τ³) − …], where M_n = ∬N(x,y)|x−y|ⁿ and N = |ẋ||ẏ|n·n′ − ẋ·ẏ. Two exact computations follow. First, M₀ = (∮σ|dx|)² = (L_trace − L_chord)², where σ = ±1 on the two legs. Second, M₂ = 2Σ_{μν}(∮x_νdx_μ)² − 2|∮σx|dx||² = 4𝒜² − 2𝒜² = 2𝒜² to leading order, since the first-moment mismatch of the legs is |∮σx|dx|| = |𝒜| + O(s³). The two computations agree term by term.

Numerically (F2), M₀ equals (L_trace − L_chord)² to the floating floor, and −M₂/128 → −s²/1296 with relative error falling like s². The full flowed integral at τ/t = 200 and 2000 equals the moment series to 3×10⁻⁹ of the individual pieces.

The interacting formula was derived from the exact first equation, whereas this test is a separate Feynman-integral evaluation, so the agreement is not circular. It confirms the scalar pieces: the positive S-term from the length mismatch, and the C^Φ − C competition between scalar-gradient and gauge-curvature insertions.

## 4. Continuum one loop: cusp structure and finite part

With the flow removed, log W⁽¹⁾ = B₁I and
\[
I=\underbrace{2\!\int_{\rm trace}\!\int_{\rm chord}\frac{-|\dot x||\dot y|-\dot x\cdot\dot y}{|x-y|^2}}_{J_f}+\underbrace{\iint_{\rm trace^2}\frac{|\dot x||\dot y|-\dot x\cdot\dot y}{|x-y|^2}}_{\rm tt},
\]
while the chord–chord part vanishes. The trace–trace part is finite and → (2/9)s² (F1; a parabola of curvature s/3 on [0,2]). The exchange J_f has logarithmic cusp divergences. They are regulated by excising points within Euclidean distance ε of each vertex on both legs, in units where the trace has Loewner time 1 and the chord length ℓ ≈ 2.

**Cusp coefficient.** The one-loop cusp formula at deflection φ = π − α and internal angle π gives Γ⁽¹⁾/B₁ = 2(π − α)tan(α/2) per cusp. So A(s) = Σ_c 2(π − α_c)tan(α_c/2), with the exact angles α_b (base) and α_t (tip) taken from the trace. Both angles are s/3 at leading order, so A = (2π/3)s + A₂s² with A₂ ≈ −0.224.

F1 tests the exactness of A without fitting it. With the exact A subtracted, the successive ε-differences of I + A log(ℓ/ε) halve (ratios 0.501 to 0.509), so the remainder is O(ε). A wrong coefficient would leave a constant.

**Finite part.** F(s) = lim_{ε→0}[I + A log(ℓ/ε)]. The sliver computation of the first note, taken at fixed ε and then ε → 0, gives F = (4π/3)s at leading order. The two limits commute here because the narrow wedge is self-similar. Numerically, a fit F/s = f₁ + s(c log(1/s) + c′) on s = 0.05, 0.025 and 0.0125 gives f₁ = 4.18864, against 4π/3 = 4.18879, and c ≈ 0.23. The s² log(1/s) correction is a labelled numerical observation; its origin was not derived. So, in this scheme,
\[
W^{\rm flip}=1-B_1\,\frac{2\pi}{3}|s|\Big(\log\frac{\ell}{\varepsilon}-2\Big)+O\big(s^2\log\tfrac1s\big).
\]

## 5. The flow regulator and three regimes

Keep the flowed propagator and send τ/t through all scales, in rescaled units (Loewner time 1, τ̂ = τ/t). The trace–chord interaction is controlled by the sliver width w(y) = (s/6)y(2−y) compared with √(8τ̂). When √τ̂ is small compared with the chord, a thin-sliver evaluation gives
\[
I\simeq-\frac{2\pi s}{3}\int_0^2\Big(\frac1{z}-1\Big)\operatorname{erf}(\kappa z)\,dy,\qquad z=y(2-y),\quad \kappa=\frac{s}{6\sqrt{8\hat\tau}} .
\]

This formula has two limits:

- **Regime II (κ ≪ 1, i.e. s²t ≪ τ ≪ t).** erf(κz) ≈ 2κz/√π gives I ≈ −(√(2π)/27)s²(t/τ)^{1/2}.
- **Regime III (κ ≫ 1, i.e. τ ≪ s²t).** Using ∫₀^X erf(v)/v dv = log 2X + γ_E/2 + o(1) (checked), the integral is log 8κ + γ_E/2 − 2 + O(1/κ). Hence
\[
I\simeq-\frac{2\pi s}{3}\Big[\log\Big(\frac{\sqrt2}{3}\,s\sqrt{t/\tau}\Big)+\frac{\gamma_E}{2}-2\Big].
\]

**Checks (F2).**

- The erf law reproduces the regime-III asymptotic to 1.1×10⁻³ at κ = 59 and 7×10⁻⁵ at κ = 589.
- The full flowed one-loop integral equals the erf law plus s²D(τ̂), where D is independent of s to ≤ 0.7% (s = 0.1 against 0.05, τ̂ = 10⁻³, 10⁻⁵, 10⁻⁷).
- The log(1/τ̂) slope of D, 0.1124, equals −A₂/2 = 0.1120. So even the s² part of the flowed result is the O(s²) cusp coefficient.

**Interpretation.** Separation-based regulators, such as flow or point splitting in |x − y|, remove the near-BPS cusp interaction where the opposite legs come within √τ of each other. That happens at distance ≈ √τ/α from the vertex, not √τ. The effective cutoff ε_eff ∝ √τ/s produces a |s| log|s| term absent from vertex excision. The two schemes differ by Σ_c(Γ_c/B₁)log α_c. That is the usual angle-dependent scheme freedom of finite parts at a cusp, made large here by the small angle. The scheme-independent content at O(s) is the coefficient A(s) of log(ℓμ).

**Summary of limits.** At fixed a and τ, t → 0 passes through regime I (W − 1 ∝ a²t³). At fixed a and t, τ → 0 passes through regime III (W − 1 ∝ |a|√t log). The crossovers sit at t ~ τ and a²t² ~ τ. No regime diverges. This contrasts with the constant-n₀ family, whose continuum limit diverges like 1/s.

## 6. Closure inventory for the second equation

The insertions in Proposition 3 are to be averaged in the interacting theory. At finite regulator, the Schwinger–Dyson identities reduce only these combinations:

- **Gauge current.** E_ν = Σ_μ D_μF_{μν}, which the N=4 field equation equates to a scalar current ∝ i[Φ_I, D_νΦ_I] plus a fermion current. The planar derivative q̇·DF₁₂ splits exactly as in parent eq. (22). It leaves the transverse terms q̇¹(D₃F₃₂ + D₄F₄₂) − q̇²(D₃F₃₁ + D₄F₄₁), which a planar contour does not remove.
- **Scalar Laplacian.** D_μD_μΦ equals the scalar-potential commutator plus the Yukawa term. The planar mixed derivative q̇^μn̂^νD_μD_νΦ is not a trace. Write q̇ = ρ̇q̂ + (k/|q|)n̂. The ρ̇ part is a radial derivative of (n̂·DΦ)^, which integrates by parts, but along this chord ∂_rX̂ = (q·DX)^ − |q|[Φ̂, X̂]: the extra commutator appears because the chord connection contains Φ. The (k/|q|)n̂n̂ part equals the Laplacian minus q̂q̂ (radial) minus the transverse (D₃² + D₄²)Φ, so the transverse part is irreducible.
- **Tip gradient.** (q̇·DΦ)^(1) at the tip is irreducible at this level. In the continuum it is an insertion at a cusp and needs its own renormalization.
- **Ordered pairs.** The chord–chord pairs of {F̂, (n̂·DΦ)^, Φ̂} and the chord–tip pairs are new correlators, not fixed by lower moments.

So the flipped hierarchy is not closed at second order either. Its residual differs from pure YM by scalar-current, Yukawa and scalar-potential terms, transverse scalar curvature, and tip insertions. This is an inventory of uncontrolled terms, not an obstruction theorem.

## 7. Assessment

The flipped-return completion does what the first note hoped:

- It restores the backtracking identity control.
- It removes the leading trace–chord attraction.
- It gives a family whose short-time behaviour is regular in every regime and whose leading s-dependence is shape (area, curvature, normal scalar gradient, length mismatch) rather than perimeter.
- Its first two evolution equations are exact for smooth fields and verified.

It does not give:

- a closed equation;
- an all-orders continuum coefficient: the near-BPS cusp limit H(λ,N) is open;
- a causal input/output channel. It is a Euclidean expectation value on a one-parameter shape family, with W(s) regular and even in a.

Nothing in its structure (analytic in s up to logarithms, no delays, no variable exponent) resembles the shifted-zeta transfer, and no arithmetic comparison is warranted at this stage.

**Most useful next steps:**

1. **H(λ,N).** Obtain it at two loops, or at strong coupling from a near-degenerate string worldsheet between nearly antiparallel legs with opposite S⁵ charge. *(Done in the [corner note](NEAR_BPS_CORNER_ROUNDING_AND_CLOSURE_20260924.md): H is one-loop exact order by order as s → 0; the effective coupling is λ|s|, with a √(λ|s|) window at strong coupling. The near-BPS formula applies at the BPS line and fixes the corner slope exactly; the factor-2 remark above concerns its linearization away from x = 1.)* This decides whether the continuum |s| law carries coupling dependence. The near-BPS formula Γ ≈ (θ − φ)(2φ/(1 − φ²/π²))B(λ(1 − φ²/π²)) suggests, but does not show, that the effective coupling λ(1 − φ²/π²) → 0 in this corner.
2. **Rounded family.** Use a rounding at scale δ = δ̂√t with a stated scalar profile on the rounding arcs, so that W(s, δ̂) is finite and scheme-free.
3. **Closure audit.** Carry out the finite-regulator Schwinger–Dyson audit of Section 6 for the first derivative moment, as proposed for pure YM, keeping the transverse and Yukawa terms.
