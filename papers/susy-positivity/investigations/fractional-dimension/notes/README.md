| [WHICH_DIMENSION_AND_THE_VOLUME_20260918.md](WHICH_DIMENSION_AND_THE_VOLUME_20260918.md) | Second note; current | Items 1 and 3 of the opening note's plan, both answered. **The dimension is $2\omega$** and the factor of two was a coordinate artifact: $\tau=\log\lvert t+i\rvert$ is a *Busemann* function on the horocycle, not a distance, and $\tau\sim r^2/2$ is a square, so a measure with local exponent $r^{2\omega-1}dr$ has local exponent $\tau^{\omega-1}d\tau$ and the two normalized limits differ by exactly $2^{1-\omega}$ (Prop. 1.1). In the true half-distance $u=d_H/2$, $r=2\sinh u$, Lebesgue on $\mathbb R^{2\omega}$ is the radial measure of **complex hyperbolic space of complex dimension $\omega$, real dimension $2\omega$** --- Jacobi parameters $(\omega-1,0)$ with $\rho=\omega>0$, against $(\frac\omega2-1,-\frac12)$ with $\rho<0$ in the Busemann coordinate; only the first has positive $\rho$ and only it accounts for $e^{\tau/2}$ (Prop. 2.1). The comb, having no radial coordinate, fixes $d=2\omega$ independently (§2.3), and §2.4 records the caveat that $CH^\omega$ is a space only at integer $\omega$, never in the family. **The correction is a volume term:** $\operatorname{Res}_{p=b}\widetilde K_\omega=\lvert S^{d}\rvert/2\zeta(d+1)$, sphere of $\mathbb R^{d+1}$ times primitive density in $\mathbb Z^{d+1}$ --- the volume term of a **lattice of rank $d+1$** whose cusp has a $d$-dimensional horocycle, giving $6/\pi=2/\mathrm{vol}$ at $d=1$ (Prop. 3.1). §4 closes the dictionary on that one object; §5 re-ranks what is open, headed by **the rank**, which is a cardinality and cannot simply be continued. Written by Claude Opus 5. |
| [THE_RANK_AND_THE_LATTICE_20260918.md](THE_RANK_AND_THE_LATTICE_20260918.md) | Third note; current | The head item of the second note's plan, answered. **The object at integer $d$ is a lattice of rank $d+1$, at every integer and not only at $d=1$.** For a covolume-one $L\subset\mathbb R^m$ the primitive Epstein zeta $E^{*}_L(s)=\sum_{v\ \mathrm{prim}}\lvert v\rvert^{-2s}$ satisfies $\xi(2s)E^{*}_L(s)=\xi(m-2s)E^{*}_{L^{*}}(m/2-s)$, so its reflection ratio is independent of the lattice and is an invariant of the **rank alone** --- and under the dictionary that ratio is exactly $\widetilde K_\omega$ (Props. 1.1--1.2). The transfer's reflection $p\mapsto-p$ *is* the Epstein functional equation $s\mapsto m/2-s$, which is why $\lvert\widetilde K_\omega\rvert=1$ on $\Re p=0$. **The family's cap $\omega\le\frac12$ is the rank-two wall:** $\xi$'s poles put a second pole of the numerator at $p=\omega-\frac12$, in the closed left half-plane iff $m\le2$, and in $s$ it sits where $\zeta$ has its own pole (Prop. 2.1) --- so the family occupies the first gap $m\in(1,2]$ with the modular surface at its right endpoint. **And inside that gap the lattice does not exist:** the point count's unique interpolation is the $q$-coefficients of $\theta^{m}$, polynomial in $m$, and $r_{d+1}(3)=\frac43(d^{3}-d)<0$ for every $d\in(0,1)$, vanishing only at the endpoints --- equivalently $\theta(it)^{d+1}$ is not completely monotone anywhere strictly inside (Props. 3.1--3.2). What continues is all the cusp data ($J_d>0$, $1/\zeta(d+1)\in(0,1)$, the horocycle integral, the unimodularity on the critical line); what fails is the interior. At fractional $d$ the object is a **scattering matrix without a space**. Written by Claude Opus 5. |
| [PAST_THE_WALL_20260918.md](PAST_THE_WALL_20260918.md) | Fourth note; current | Item 1 of the third note's plan, both halves, pointing opposite ways. **The decomposition survives past the wall and needs no repair:** $R_\omega=B_b\cdot\frac{p+a}{p-a}$ at every $\omega$, and $\frac{p+a}{p-a}=B_c$ with $c=\omega-\frac12$ is a genuine Blaschke factor exactly when $\omega\ge\frac12$, so $R_\omega=B_bB_c$ is **inner** past the wall where before it was a ratio $B_b/B_a$ with a right-half-plane pole (Prop. 1). **And the positivity survives, once the Gamma factors are regrouped:** $\widehat K^\Gamma_\omega=\pi^\omega\frac{\Gamma(z+c)}{\Gamma(z+\omega)}\frac{\Gamma(z+1)}{\Gamma(z+c+1)}$ with $z=\frac{p+a}2$, two Gamma ratios of parameter gap $\frac12$ and $c$, both completely monotone exactly when $\omega\ge\frac12$ --- so $\widehat K_\omega$ is completely monotone for **every** $\omega>0$ and the parent's Prop. 3.6 was uncapped, only its proof was (Prop. 2). The two poles have different origins: $p=b$ is the comb's, $p=c$ is the *archimedean* factor's, so past the wall the correction removes one pole from each half. The instrument needed no second factor at all, only a removable $0/0$ at $\omega=1$. **But the test cannot fail:** for every $\omega\ge\frac12$ no zero has $\lvert\Re\rho-\frac12\rvert>\omega$, so by the parent's dichotomy every $V_{\omega,L}$ is a contraction **unconditionally** --- by the Euler product alone for $\omega>\frac12$, with the classical zero-free region needed only at $\omega=\frac12$ (Prop. 3) --- the criterion is vacuous there, and has arithmetic content on exactly $\omega\in(0,\frac12)$, i.e. $d\in(0,1)$, i.e. **exactly the open interval on which the third note proved the lattice point count negative** (Obs. 4.1). Section 5 corrects the reading of the endpoints: RH is the criterion's *derivative at rank one*, not its value at rank two, where it is the Euler product. Section 6 re-ranks. Written by Claude Opus 5. |
| [THE_TRADE_IS_NOT_IT_20260918.md](THE_TRADE_IS_NOT_IT_20260918.md) | Fifth note; current | Item 1 of the fourth note's plan, answered in the negative. **The trade is not the explanation:** the point count fails on $(2,3)$ as well as on $(1,2)$ while the criterion has content only on $(1,2)$, and at $m=3$ the point count is completely monotone and the rational factor is not --- so no equivalence can hold (Cor. 3.1), and the coincidence of intervals is the first gap and nothing more, forced by both of the criterion's endpoints sitting at the two smallest ranks. On the way, **the theta side's mechanism becomes a theorem:** on the gap $(k,k+1)$, $\operatorname{sign}\binom mj=(-1)^{\max(0,j-k-1)}$ (Prop. 1.1), so $r_m(n)>0$ free for $n\le k+1$ (Prop. 1.2) and the first forced negative coefficient sits at $N_k$, the least integer needing $k+2$ **positive** squares --- $N_0=2$, $N_1=3$, $N_2=7$, **and no $N_k$ for $k\ge3$, because Lagrange's four-square theorem gives $s(n)\le4$** (Props. 1.3--1.4). That is the third note's Lagrange threshold, proved. On the transfer side there is **exactly one positivity threshold, at $m=2$** (Prop. 2.1); the only other integer feature, at $m=3$, is a regularity threshold and points the wrong way. **What does explain the split** (Prop. 4.1): in the transfer the rank enters every positive object as an *exponent* --- $(2\sinh\tau)^{\omega-1}$, $(1-e^{-t})^{\beta-\alpha-1}$, $p^{-d}$ --- where positivity is a local inequality that survives continuation at every real value; in the point count it enters as a *binomial index*, where it does not. So item 3 of the third note, as posed, has no solution: no factor of the transfer loses positivity at fractional $d$. Section 5 judges the investigation's question answered --- $d$ is a parameter --- and says what a manuscript would contain. Written by Claude Opus 5. |

**A convention warning.** The parent manuscript writes $\Lambda$ for the completed
zeta with poles at $0$ and $1$ and reserves $\xi$ for the entire
$\frac12u(u-1)\Lambda(u)$. The first three notes here write $\xi$ for the parent's
$\Lambda$. The two differ by exactly the rational factor $R_\omega$, so the clash is
harmless inside each document and lethal across them; the fourth note uses the
parent's convention and says so at its head.
