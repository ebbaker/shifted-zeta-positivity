\begin{abstract}
The shifted Weil family turns the Riemann hypothesis into a statement about
causal all-pass filters: for each shift $\omega>0$ the transfer
$K_\omega(p)=\xi(\frac12+p-\omega)/\xi(\frac12+p+\omega)$ is unimodular on the
critical line, and its compressions to windows of every length are contractions
if and only if no zero lies further than $\omega$ from that line. Its Markov
part $\Kt_\omega=\Lambda(\frac12+p-\omega)/\Lambda(\frac12+p+\omega)$ is
completely monotone, and every one of its factors is $d$-dimensional as a
formula for $d=2\omega$. This manuscript asks what that $d$ is. We show first
that it is a \emph{rank}: for a covolume-one lattice $L\subset\R^{m}$ the
primitive Epstein zeta $\Est_L(s)=\sum_{v\ \mathrm{prim}}\lvert v\rvert^{-2s}$
satisfies $\Lambda(2s)\Est_L(s)=\Lambda(m-2s)\Est_{L^{*}}(m/2-s)$, so its
reflection ratio is independent of $L$ and is an invariant of the rank alone ---
and with $m=d+1$ that ratio is exactly $\Kt_\omega$, whose own reflection
$p\mapsto-p$ is the Epstein functional equation. Every integer $d\ge1$ is
realized, as rank $d+1$; the family's range $\omega\le\frac12$ is the first gap
$m\in(1,2]$ of that sequence. Everything then turns on the sign of
$a=\frac12-\omega=\frac{2-m}2$. We show that at $a=0$ three things happen at
once: the second pole of $\Kt_\omega$ --- which is the \emph{archimedean}
factor's, while the first is the comb's --- crosses into the right half-plane;
the rational correction $R_\omega=B_b\cdot\frac{p+a}{p-a}$ turns from a ratio of
Blaschke factors with a right half-plane pole into the inner product $B_bB_c$,
$c=\omega-\frac12$; and the poles of $K_\omega$ leave the closed right
half-plane, which makes every compression a contraction \emph{unconditionally,
on the strength of the Euler product alone}. Complete monotonicity survives the
crossing, by two groupings of the Gamma factors that are exchanged there, so the
decomposition continues past the wall with its positivity intact --- but the
criterion does not: it is vacuous for $\omega\ge\frac12$ and has arithmetic
content on exactly $\omega\in(0,\frac12)$, that is $m\in(1,2)$, where it is
equivalent to a zero-free strip of half-width $\omega$. The Euler product is
therefore the criterion's \emph{value} at rank two and the Riemann hypothesis is
its \emph{derivative} at rank one, where the transfer degenerates to the
identity and the first-order defect is the localized Weil form. Inside the gap
no lattice exists: the point count's unique interpolation is the
$q$-expansion of $\theta^{m}$, and on the gap $(k,k+1)$ the binomial
$\binom mj$ is positive through $j=k+1$ and first negative at $j=k+2$, so the
first coefficient that is \emph{forced} negative sits at the least integer
needing $k+2$ positive squares --- $2$, $3$, $7$, and none at all once $k\ge3$,
because Lagrange's four-square theorem bounds that least number by four. The
two failures share the interval $m\in(1,2)$ and nothing else: the point count
fails on $(2,3)$ as well, where the criterion is already vacuous, and at $m=3$
the point count is completely monotone while the transfer's rational factor is
not. The agreement is the first gap, forced by both of the criterion's endpoints
lying at the two smallest ranks. What separates them is where the rank sits: in
the transfer it is always an \emph{exponent} --- $(2\sinh\tau)^{\omega-1}$,
$(1-e^{-t})^{\beta-\alpha-1}$, $p^{-d}$ --- so each positivity is a local
inequality, at one delay or at one prime, that continuation cannot break; in the
point count it is a \emph{binomial index}, where positivity is a global
cancellation. The transfer's positivity was never inherited from a lattice, and
the lattice's failure leaves no trace in it. At fractional $d$ there is a
scattering matrix without a space, and $d$ is a parameter. Nothing here
constructs a source, proves positivity of the Weil form, or assumes the Riemann
hypothesis.
\end{abstract}
