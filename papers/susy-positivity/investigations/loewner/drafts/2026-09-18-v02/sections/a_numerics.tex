\section{Programmes, records, and numerical lessons}\label{app:numerics}

Two programmes are \emph{registered} in the sense of the program's conventions:
standard library only, JSON to standard output, a preserved record, and
registration in \texttt{validation/drafts.py}, so that
\texttt{python3 validation/drafts.py check -{}-replay} re-runs it and requires
byte-identical output. The rest live in \texttt{numerics/exploratory/}, use
\texttt{mpmath}, and are replayed by nobody; every value in them is either an
identity checked to working precision or a labelled quadrature. Nothing here is
a positivity certificate, and counts refer to finite test cases rather than to
independent theorems.

\subsection*{Registered}

\paragraph{\texttt{check\_contraction\_margin.py}} (double precision, record
\texttt{records/\allowbreak contraction-\allowbreak margin-\allowbreak checks.json}, 53 cases in six
groups, about one second). It is self-contained: complex digamma and trigamma
by the Bernoulli asymptotic series with the argument shifted by recurrence
(reproducing \texttt{mpmath} to $10^{-15}$ relative), Gauss--Jacobi nodes by
Golub--Welsch on the closed-form Jacobi recurrence, Gauss--Legendre by Newton on
the Legendre polynomials, a cyclic Jacobi symmetric eigensolver, Gaussian
elimination, and the Weil-form assembler of \cite{FrameBound} ported from
\texttt{mpmath}. \emph{(A)} the Gauss--Jacobi moments against $1/(\omega+m)$ for
$m<64$ at four shifts, the Gauss--Legendre moments against $1/(m+1)$ for
$m<128$, and \eqref{eq:S} against direct quadrature at $24$ triples $(j,k,t)$.
\emph{(B)} $\Gh$ of \eqref{eq:atom} by the $\Psi$ route against an independent
split rule, and the closed Laplace transform \eqref{eq:laplace-atom} at
$p\in\{2,3,5,8\}$ and $\omega\in\{0.01,0.1\}$, agreeing to $1.4\times10^{-13}$.
\emph{(C)} the mass at $L=\log3,\log5,\log7$ against the 40-digit assembly to
$10^{-13}$, and its two-point extrapolation against \eqref{eq:tail}.
\emph{(D)} $\norm{V_{\omega,\log3}}<1$ at five shifts; $m_L^{(24)}$ against its
40-digit value to $10^{-8}$ relative; $\lambda_{\min}(D_N)/2\omega=m_L^{(24)}(1+\delta)$
with $|\delta|<1\%$ at $\omega=0.01$ (measured $+0.536\%$); and the first-order
law on $e_1$ at every shift. \emph{(E)} the first-order law on $e_1$ at
$L=\log5,\log7$. \emph{(F)} the congruence of
Proposition~\ref{prop:congruence} to $10^{-16}$, accretivity of $Z$ at five
shifts, and the quadratic gap \eqref{eq:cayleygap}.

Two limits of double precision are recorded in the output itself.
$\lambda_{\min}(D)\approx2\omega m_L$ is $10^{-9}$ at $L=\log3$ and $10^{-17}$,
$10^{-22}$ at $\log5,\log7$, read off matrices of norm one, so about four digits
of it survive at $\log3$ and none at the longer horizons: the margin assertion
is made at $\log3$, and the longer horizons are carried here by the mass and by
the first-order law on a fixed vector, both of size $10^{-4}$. And the mass
extrapolation is two-point, so it is asserted at $10^{-5}$ while the $10^{-13}$
agreement with the 40-digit assembly is the sharp check.

\paragraph{\texttt{check\_beta\_realization.py}} (double precision, 43 cases in
five groups, under a second; record
\texttt{records/beta-\allowbreak realization-\allowbreak checks.json}). \emph{(A)} the archimedean delay
identified pointwise as $\tau=-\frac12\log U$, $U\sim\mathrm{Beta}(\frac a2,\omega)$,
its Mellin transform against the Gamma ratio, the zero-delay exponent of
Proposition~\ref{prop:singular} and its dual power law. \emph{(B)} the exact
factorization $\E[U^qS^r]=\E[U^q]\E[S^r]$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:indep}.
\emph{(C)} the chord diffusion of Proposition~\ref{prop:antipode}: the algebraic
identity $\frac12(\sigma_x^2m)'=\mu_xm$ for $m=x^{d/2-1}(1-x)^{-1/2}$ at seven
$\kappa$ and five points, and the two endpoint limits \eqref{eq:chordexp} for
five drivings, two of them with $x$-dependent $\sigma$. \emph{(D)} the parameter
exclusion of Corollary~\ref{cor:nobeta} at seven shifts, and the three Bessel
dimensions against the SLE range $d=1+4/\kappa>1$. \emph{(E)}
Proposition~\ref{prop:horocycle}: \eqref{eq:horocycle} at five $p$ and eight
shifts, the prefactor against $|S^{2\omega-1}|$, the Riesz integral by honest
quadrature at integer $d\in\{1,2,3\}$, and the push-forward. $\Gamma$, $\sinh$
and elementary algebra only.

\subsection*{Exploratory}

$\omega=0.3,0.4,0.49$ at $L=\log3$ for Proposition~\ref{prop:endpointfree} and

\paragraph{\texttt{gram\_control\_vs\_transfer.py}} (50 digits, record
\texttt{gram\_control\_\allowbreak T3000\_N24.json}): the three lattice

from $n\geq0$, and the half-shifted lattice --- as even-block $\lambda_{\min}$ in

\paragraph{\texttt{contraction\_margin.py}} (40 digits): the first version of the
assembly, which used the substitution $\tau=u^{1/\omega}$ and a tanh--sinh rule
on each inter-atom interval, and its records \texttt{cm\_om\{omega\}\_log3\_N\{N\}.json}.
Superseded by \texttt{contraction\_margin\_gj.py}; kept, with its records,
because its quadrature floor is the reason two rows of \cite{ContractionNote}
were wrong, and because the lessons below were learnt on it.

\subsection*{Lessons}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Put the singularity in the weight.} The spike
$k^\Gamma_\omega(\tau)\sim\omega\pi^\omega2^\omega\tau^{\omega-1}$ puts half its
mass below $\tau=2^{-1/\omega}$. Removing it by a substitution and integrating
what is left with a general-purpose rule works, but only at high precision and
with a floor; carrying it in a Gauss--Jacobi weight
(Proposition~\ref{prop:analytic}) removes the difficulty instead of managing
it, converges geometrically, and runs in double precision.
\item \emph{Local coordinates, either way.} Any formulation that forms the
global time $t=\log n_0+\tau$ and recovers $\tau$ by subtraction loses the spike
at every finite precision. Symptom: $\norm V>1$ by $10^{-3}$ and a first-order
check off by a factor $100$. With one Gauss--Jacobi rule per atom over its whole
support this is automatic.
\item \emph{Know the floor before reading the signal.} At $1/h=16$ the
tanh--sinh rule resolved $\lambda_{\min}(D)$ to about $6\times10^{-12}$, which is
half a percent of the signal at $\omega=0.01$ and more below it; two rows of
\cite[Table~3.1]{ContractionNote} were read from beneath that floor and are
corrected in Table~\ref{tab:sweep}. The mass, being non-oscillatory, was
unaffected, which is why it agreed to ten digits while the margin did not.
\item \emph{Atoms at the horizon.} With $L$ entered as a decimal slightly above
$\log3$, or formed at default precision before the working precision is set, the
atom $n=3$ enters with a sliver $[\log3,L)$ of length $10^{-17}$ and carries
$(10^{-17})^\omega$ of its spike mass --- $68\%$ of it at $\omega=0.01$. This
changes $\int_0^Lk_\omega$ (to $1.0027$ from $0.9937$) but not the operator,
because $S_{jk}(L)=0$ for all $j,k$. Parse $L=\log n$ exactly, set the precision
first, take $\log n<L$ strictly.
\item \emph{Sign and size of the Galerkin bias.} $D_N\geq P_ND_{\omega,L}P_N$:
the plain Galerkin defect overestimates at first order in $\omega$ by the energy
of $Af_N$ outside the basis. Compare within a basis, never a Galerkin
$\lambda_{\min}(D_N)$ against a converged $m_L$ --- and past $L=\log7$ do not
compare a plain Galerkin $\lambda_{\min}(D_N)$ against $m_L^{(N)}$ in its own
basis either, since the bias stops being a correction and becomes the whole
number (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}
