## Lead question: P at microscopic resolution

Assume RH and select a complete endpoint population. At width \(y=\sqrt t\),
a multiple endpoint forces Poisson excess \(\mathscr D\ge2/3\).
The goal is a fully paid upper bound below \(2/3\) for every sufficiently
high endpoint core and a certified bounded-height remainder. A suitable
positive time for each covered population is allowed; a universal clock
is an additional theorem.

The [initial note](../notes/01_WHOLE_CLUSTER_POISSON_TRANSFER_AND_ACQUISITION_20261026.md)
proves an elementary finite-polynomial transfer. It accepts monic cluster
coefficient enclosures, a complete real population, a finite-window
\(L^2\) bound for a rational model density and a paid tail. It divides
by the complex-height floor \(y^n\), never by individual root slopes.
Arbitrary internal multiplicities are allowed, but resolution/degree
loss can be severe. Acquisition from the canonical field remains unpaid.

The next work must distinguish:

1. Acquiring enclosing contours, counts, monic coefficients and exterior-unit
   data from the completed arithmetic source, with explicit height, time,
   population, margin and cancellation cost.
2. Estimating the second moment or its transfer sharply enough to leave
   an absolute margin below one collision. Keep arithmetic cross terms
   and the subtraction \(H'/H-U'/U\) before squaring.

An \(n+o(n)\) second-moment asymptotic can hide a single collision. An
internal-gap assumption cannot exclude an endpoint collision. The Book's
fixed-filter density/covariance is a different observable; nonholomorphic
spatial joins require their own correction before any holomorphic use.

## Benchmarks and secondary directions

**J.** Seek a normalized joint first-jet lower bound \(cX^{-\beta}\),
\(\beta<3/4\); squared norm \(cX^{-1}\) would beat the current error.
Compare every more elaborate representation against the same input
information. A polynomial margin is stronger than qualitative simplicity.
Positive diagonal sums cannot replace mixed terms, and averaging over
height can miss an exceptional zero.

**RT.** The completed [two-root pilot](../../02_heat_flow_rh_applications/rodgers_tao/notes/03_CANONICAL_TWO_ROOT_MARGINS_AND_FORCE_BUDGET_20261026.md)
has negligible field error; its direct force budget is about 309 times
smaller than the contour budget. These are certified allowances, not runtime
or true-error ratios. New work should concern a microscopic/growing block
with poorly resolved internal gaps and a usable outer wall. The model's
full exterior contribution, reference baseline and corrected boundaries
remain. HCB may supply a comparison to RT-05 without taking over its ID
or reopening the completed pilot.

**V.** Before extending the strong recurrence from \(s\ge1/L\) toward
\(1/L^2\), specify the needed regulator/field accuracy. The microscopic
condition \(\delta/\eta=o(sL^2)\), multiplicity resolution and signed
cutoff terms must be paid. A joint high-height path is not automatically
a fixed-core \(s\downarrow0\) limit.

**E.** Ordered energy has a multiple-event coefficient at least \(1/4\)
in \(tE\). A strict improvement needs arithmetic input, not stronger
universal repulsion: the free double attains that coefficient. Retain
all exterior, cutoff and reference payments.

**RH versus simplicity.** A positive-threshold contradiction needs
canonical inputs at an unknown shifted time and all possible endpoint
cores. Time-zero simplicity under RH does not provide those hypotheses.
For a numerical milestone choose a specified \(b>0\) below a documented
complete baseline and satisfy every global upper-bound condition.
An isolated block improvement or later regular high zeros is insufficient.
