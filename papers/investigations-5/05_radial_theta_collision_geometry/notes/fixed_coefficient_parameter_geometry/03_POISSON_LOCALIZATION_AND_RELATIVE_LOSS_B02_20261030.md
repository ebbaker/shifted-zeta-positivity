## 2. Exact consumer and a valid localized source identity

Put u=z+w, v=z−w, Ω=z²+w², and \(\mathcal C=\{z\ge |w|\}\). The half-line Jacobian is du dv=2 dz dw. Use

\[
 \Psi_C(z,w;A)=\Gamma_A+C\eta_A,
 \quad\Gamma_A=z^2\cos(2Aw)+w^2\cos(2Az),
\]
\[
 \eta_A=\tfrac12\{(1+z^2-w^2)\cos(2Aw)
                  +(1-z^2+w^2)\cos(2Az)\}.
\]

Write B_s(z,w)=B_s^1(z+w,z−w) for the B01 overlap kernel. The same original drift, unit preparation and full spatial domain are retained. Then

\[
 F_{1,C}(s;p)=2\int_{\mathcal C}e^{2T\Omega}B_s(z,w)
 \Phi(z+w)\Phi(z-w)\Psi_C(z,w;A)\,dz\,dw. \tag{1}
\]

All required source moments are finite. The B01/Nash bound supplies a finite source-independent M with 0≤B_s≤M for every s,z,w. The two endpoints used here are B₀, which is independent of c at preparation, and B∞=Q(1)>0. Pointwise convergence B_s→Q(1) passes through all complete-source integrals below by domination, uniformly in p∈K* for fixed C and window. This uses no signed physical-mass lower bound.

For the exact [parity–Poisson identity][old38], set d(w)=2cosh(4w), f=d^{-1/2}, τ=tanh(4w), and

\[
 a_{mn}(z,w)=f\exp[-\pi(e^{4z}m^2+e^{-4z}n^2)/d]
 (-1)^{mn}\cos(\pi mn\tau),
\]
\[
 \Pi=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb Z}a_{mn},\qquad
 \mathcal D=(\partial_z^2-\partial_w^2)^2
             -8(\partial_z^2+\partial_w^2)+16,
 \quad \mathcal D\Pi=4096\Phi(z+w)\Phi(z-w). \tag{2}
\]

Choose χ_ε=e^{−εΩ²}, ε>0, applied **after** the source differential operator. Define

\[
 M_{\varepsilon,s}=\frac2{4096}\sum_{m,n}
 \int_{\mathcal C}\chi_\varepsilon e^{2T\Omega}B_s\Psi_C
 \mathcal D a_{mn}\,dz\,dw,
\]
\[
 R_{\varepsilon,s}=2\int_{\mathcal C}(1-\chi_\varepsilon)e^{2T\Omega}
 B_s\Phi(z+w)\Phi(z-w)\Psi_C\,dz\,dw.
\]

The identity is

\[
 \boxed{F_{1,C}=M_{\varepsilon,s}+R_{\varepsilon,s}.} \tag{3}
\]

Here the sum and integral in M may genuinely be interchanged. [Old Note42 §2][old42] proves, by differentiated Gaussian sums,

\[
 \sum_{m,n}|\mathcal D a_{mn}(z,w)|
 \le C_0(e^{2|z|}+e^{18|w|}). \tag{4}
\]

This input applies on the cone as a restriction of its full-plane bound. Since |Ψ_C|≤(1+C)Ω+C and B_s≤M, the integrable majorant is a constant times

\[
 (1+\Omega)e^{-\varepsilon\Omega^2+2T\Omega}
 (e^{2z}+e^{18|w|}).
\]

It is uniform in s and p∈K*, and for ε bounded below by a positive number. All joint escape directions are included. There is no integration by parts here, hence no missing cone boundary terms. Moving χ, B, or Ψ inside \(\mathcal D\) would be a different identity with additional derivatives and boundary terms.

Let

\[
 \mathcal E_{\varepsilon,s}=2\int_{\mathcal C}(1-\chi_\varepsilon)e^{2T\Omega}
 B_s\Phi(z+w)\Phi(z-w)|\Psi_C|\,dz\,dw.
\]

For half-line moments m_j(T)=∫₀∞u^j e^{Tu²}Φ(u)du and

\[
 \mu_j^+(T)=2^{-j}\sum_{\ell=0}^j{j\choose\ell}
 m_{2\ell}(T)m_{2j-2\ell}(T),
\]

one obtains the explicit **absolute** allowance

\[
 |R_{\varepsilon,s}|\le\mathcal E_{\varepsilon,s}
 \le M\varepsilon\{(1+C)\mu_3^+(T)+C\mu_2^+(T)\}. \tag{5}
\]

Indeed 1−χ≤εΩ² and Ω=(u²+v²)/2. Only moments through order six are used, uniformly bounded by those at T=3/8. No division by E_c has occurred. Equation (5) does not establish a relative error or a favorable sign for (3).

## 3. Unit preparation does not regularize the zero Poisson mode

This is the new domain/weight check needed before importing the older full-plane obstruction. In the present coordinates, r=e^{2(z+w)}, t=e^{2(z−w)}, and G=(8π)^{-3/2}. The prepared overlap is exactly

\[
 B_0=2G e^{-4z}
 \left\{e^{-(r-t)^2/8}-e^{-(r+t)^2/8}\right\}. \tag{6}
\]

For a fixed sufficiently small δ>0, restrict to the narrowing cone strip

\[
 \mathcal T_\delta=\{z\ge z_0,\ |w|\le\delta e^{-2z}\}.
\]

Here r−t=2e^{2z}sinh(2w) stays bounded, while r+t≥2e^{2z}. Consequently there exist positive constants b₋,b₊, independent of z,w, with

\[
 b_-e^{-4z}\le B_0\le b_+e^{-4z}\quad\text{on }\mathcal T_\delta. \tag{7}
\]

The zero Poisson mode is a₀₀=f(w). Direct differentiation, or the exact f identities in [Note38 §3][old38], gives

\[
 \mathcal D f=48f q(35q-24),\quad q=\operatorname{sech}^2(4w),
 \qquad \mathcal D f(0)=528/\sqrt2>0. \tag{8}
\]

Also η_A(z,0)=cos²(Az)+z²sin²(Az)≥0, and

\[
 \eta_A(z,w)-\eta_A(z,0)
 \ge-[A^2(1+z^2)+1]w^2.
\]

Together with Γ=z²cos(2Aw)+w²cos(2Az), this implies Ψ_C≥z²/2 throughout \(\mathcal T_\delta\) after increasing z₀. The bound is uniform for p∈K* and C in any fixed bounded interval; for any fixed finite C it therefore applies. No physical jet is involved in choosing z₀.

The **positive part** of the undamped zero-mode integrand in (3), restricted to this strip, is bounded below by a positive multiple of

\[
 \int_{z_0}^\infty z^2 e^{2Tz^2-6z}\,dz=\infty. \tag{9}
\]

The −4z comes from preparation and −2z from the strip width. With B∞=Q(1), the same argument has −2z instead of −6z and also diverges. Thus the zero mode has no finite absolute integral at either endpoint. We do **not** call its whole signed integral +∞: other regions can have negative contributions, so an undefined cancellation is possible. This proof rules out undamped termwise Lebesgue integration; it does not rule out exact resummation before integration or another justified summation procedure.

## 4. The exact exponential rate of the absolute mode budget

For either B=B₀ or B=Q(1), define the actual triangle-inequality budget

\[
 \mathcal A_\varepsilon^B=\frac2{4096}\sum_{m,n}
 \int_{\mathcal C}\chi_\varepsilon e^{2T\Omega}B
 |\Psi_C\mathcal D a_{mn}|\,dz\,dw. \tag{10}
\]

For fixed (T,A)∈K* and fixed finite C≥0,

\[
 \boxed{\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
 \varepsilon\log\mathcal A_\varepsilon^B=T^2.} \tag{11}
\]

**Lower bound.** Keep only a₀₀ and \(\mathcal T_\delta\), now with Z_ε=√(T/ε)≤z≤Z_ε+1. Complete the square:

\[
 2T\Omega-\varepsilon\Omega^2
 =T^2/\varepsilon-\varepsilon(\Omega-T/\varepsilon)^2.
\]

The last term is bounded on this strip as ε↓0; w is exponentially small there. Equations (7)–(8) and Ψ≥z²/2 yield, for sufficiently small ε,

\[
 \mathcal A_\varepsilon^{B_0}
 \ge k(T/\varepsilon)\exp\{T^2/\varepsilon-6\sqrt{T/\varepsilon}-K_0\}. \tag{12}
\]

For B=Q(1), replace 6 by 2, with different finite positive constants. These prove liminf≥T². The constants may depend on the fixed parameters, but do not contain a physical-mass floor.

**Upper bound.** Use (4), B≤M and |Ψ|≤(1+C)(1+Ω). For any fixed α>0,

\[
 e^{2z}+e^{18|w|}\le2e^{18\sqrt\Omega}
 \le2e^{\alpha\Omega+81/\alpha}.
\]

Polar integration on the cone is therefore bounded by a constant depending on α times

\[
 \int_0^\infty(1+\Omega)
 e^{-\varepsilon\Omega^2+(2T+\alpha)\Omega}\,d\Omega.
\]

Completing the square bounds this by a polynomial in ε^{-1} times exp((T+α/2)²/ε). Hence limsup≤(T+α/2)² for every α>0; sending α↓0 proves (11).

In contrast, the complete-source absolute integral

\[
 2\int_{\mathcal C}\chi_\varepsilon e^{2T\Omega}B
 \Phi(z+w)\Phi(z-w)|\Psi_C|\,dz\,dw
\]

has a finite limit as ε↓0, by the complete theta tail. Thus this exponential modewise cost is discarded arithmetic cancellation, not growth of the physical observable. In particular, at actual prepared theta data E₁(0;p)>0 and finite, \(\mathcal A_\varepsilon^{B_0}/E_1(0;p)\to\infty\). Positivity of E₁(0;p) follows from nonzero raw data and injectivity of Gaussian preparation. No unknown collision is assumed in that conclusion.

Equation (11) rejects a **uniform-in-window, finite-coefficient bound for this termwise absolute budget** at either tested endpoint. It does not reject a signed sum, an exactly regrouped infinite block, or certification at one fixed positive ε. It also does not establish this lower rate at every intermediate s; only the common upper majorant and localized identity were proved uniformly in s.

## 5. Exact loss from paying the exterior in absolute value

There is a separate issue even if the entire signed M_{ε,s} is available exactly. Introduce the positive complete-source measure

\[
 d\mu_s=2e^{2T\Omega}B_s\Phi(z+w)\Phi(z-w)\,dz\,dw.
\]

Then the strongest lower bound using only |R|≤\(\mathcal E\) is

\[
 M_{\varepsilon,s}-\mathcal E_{\varepsilon,s}
 =F_{1,C}(s;p)-\Delta_{\varepsilon,s},
 \quad
 \Delta_{\varepsilon,s}
 =2\int_{\mathcal C}(1-\chi_\varepsilon)(\Psi_C)_+\,d\mu_s. \tag{13}
\]

This is just |x|+x=2x₊, with every factor retained. It identifies the lost positive exterior, not an unspecified quadrature error. The cruder moment allowance (5) gives a lower bound no better than (13).

At B∞=Q(1), \(\Delta_{\varepsilon,\infty}>0\) for every fixed ε>0, p∈K* and C≥0. Indeed on w=0 and z>0,

\[
 \Psi_C(z,0)=z^2+C\{\cos^2(Az)+z^2\sin^2(Az)\}>0.
\]

Continuity supplies an open positive patch, the complete source is strictly positive, and 1−χ>0 there. This proof is independent of J. For fixed ε,C the positive patch and compact parameter bounds also give a positive uniform floor over K*. This floor is for an explicitly positive **loss integral**, not the unproved physical mass.

If a hypothetical theta double event p₀ in K* existed, then F_{1,C}(s;p₀)→0, while Δ_{ε,s}(p₀)→Δ_{ε,∞}(p₀)>0. Consequently the fixed-window lower bound in (13) is strictly negative at all sufficiently large s. The same statement applies **unconditionally** at the two admitted control events, using their own positive complete source measures.

This is a conditional error-scaling obstruction, not a proof that no fixed-window theta certificate can work. One could still prove M_{ε,s}≥\(\mathcal E_{\varepsilon,s}\) for the actual theta family from additional information; that would itself exclude the hypothetical event. No such inequality has been obtained here. Likewise an energy-dependent or time-dependent ε might be useful with new estimates, but it cannot be justified by assuming the unknown energy has a positive floor. Letting ε↓0 while using the uniform modewise budget already fails by (11).

Even the unsplit integrand does not have a pointwise positivity remedy: at u=2π/A and v=π/A, sin(Au)=sin(Av)=0 and cos(Au)cos(Av)=−1, so

\[
 \Gamma_A(u,v)+C\eta_A(u,v)=-(u^2+v^2)/2-C<0. \tag{14}
\]

At u=v>0 it is u²+C(cos²(Au)+u²sin²(Au))>0. These are open sign patches by continuity for every A∈[4,45] and finite C. Both B₀ and Q(1) are positive there. Thus a pointwise-nonnegative-integrand proof fails for the actual theta source at the tested endpoints; an integrated signed argument remains possible.
