### 1. The question behind the change of viewpoint

The existing construction studies a separate auxiliary trajectory at each physical mark \(p=(T,A)\). It deliberately preserves a positive squared spatial norm. Within its stated class, a collision of the physical transform forces that normalized trajectory to break down at a finite auxiliary time. The hoped-for contradiction would come from a theta-specific estimate preventing that breakdown without already assuming the physical jet is nonzero.

The author's alternative is to fix a finite coefficient \(c\), use the same auxiliary operator at every physical mark, and examine the resulting family over the \((T,A)\)-plane. This shifts attention from the singular response of an individual normalized trajectory to the zeros and geometry of a family that remains regular. A collision might then be visible in a stationary projection, a winding number, or a logarithmic derivative, even though the new auxiliary equation itself does not blow up.

These viewpoints are related, but freezing the coefficient changes the equation and removes the energy constraint responsible for the original blow-up theorem. The fixed-coefficient flow is regular even for the admitted colliding controls. Its potential value is therefore the common parameter-family structure and the possibility of extracting additional information from it, rather than a direct transfer of the old blow-up contradiction.

The type of information is itself unsettled. Boundary winding is one available example, not a selected direction for this branch. Local curvature, degeneracy of a parameter-dependent geometric object, sensitivity of the fields to their parameters, and changes in the surviving asymptotic modes are also within scope. Part of the conceptual question is which behavior a collision actually forces, and which independently accessible theta property could rule that behavior out. No particular geometric quantity or exclusion mechanism is chosen in this overview.

The broader RH application also has a domain issue: excluding collisions in a specified physical region is not automatically a proof about all zeros or all heat times. The relevant physical range, boundary behavior, and transfer to the target statement remain distinct from constructing an auxiliary equation. This overview does not select a new physical domain.

### 2. Physical variables, auxiliary variables, and the conserved mass

Use the complete positive theta source \(\Phi\) in the physical transform

\[
H(T,A)=\int_0^\infty e^{Tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(Au)\,du.
\]

Here \(T\) is the physical Newman heat parameter and \(A\) is the physical frequency. Differentiating under the integral gives

\[
H_T(T,A)=\int_0^\infty u^2e^{Tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(Au)\,du
        =-H_{AA}(T,A).
\tag{1}
\]

This physical backward heat equation is separate from every auxiliary evolution below. The auxiliary variables are \(s\ge0\) and \(X\in\mathbb R^3\). The radial realization uses

\[
r=|X|,\qquad u=\tfrac12\log r,\qquad
\rho_T(X)=\mathbf1_{r\ge1}\frac{e^{Tu^2}\Phi(u)}{8\pi r^3}.
\]

Indeed, \(4\pi r^2\rho_T\,dr=e^{Tu^2}\Phi(u)\,du\). The two raw fields and their unit heat preparation are

\[
W^{\mathrm{raw}}=(U^{\mathrm{raw}},V^{\mathrm{raw}})
 =\rho_T\bigl(\cos(Au),-u\sin(Au)\bigr),
\qquad W_0=K_1*W^{\mathrm{raw}},
\]
\[
K_1(X)=(4\pi)^{-3/2}e^{-|X|^2/4}.
\tag{2}
\]

The preparation smooths the raw jump at \(r=1\), preserves the complete integrals, and supplies the regularity needed for the auxiliary equations. The oscillatory multipliers in (2) are applied in the raw source variable before convolution. After preparation, the fields occupy all of \(\mathbb R^3\), including the unit ball.

The **physical vector mass** and its magnitude are

\[
m(T,A)=\int_{\mathbb R^3}W_0(X;T,A)\,dX
      =\bigl(H(T,A),H_A(T,A)\bigr),
\qquad \mu(T,A)=|m(T,A)|.
\tag{3}
\]

“Mass” here means the signed spatial integral of each field. It is not a positive material density. A simple zero has \(H=0\) but \(H_A\ne0\), hence \(\mu>0\). A real collision, in the sense relevant here, satisfies \(H=H_A=0\), hence \(m=0\). The prepared field can be nonzero and have positive squared norm even when its entire vector integral cancels.

The positive scalar carrier is a different object:

\[
h_0=K_1*\bigl[\rho_T(1+u^2)\bigr].
\tag{4}
\]

The elementary inequality

\[
\sqrt{\cos^2(Au)+u^2\sin^2(Au)}\le1+u^2
\]

implies \(|W_0|\le h_0\). Evolving \(h\) with the same positive conservative propagator as \(W\) preserves this domination and its positive scalar mass

\[
|W_s|\le h_s,\qquad
M=\int h_s=\int_0^\infty e^{Tu^2}\Phi(u)(1+u^2)\,du>0.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus \(M\), \(\mu\), and \(\|W\|_2^2\) measure different things. At a collision, \(\mu=0\) while \(M\) and the initial squared norm remain positive. The carrier bounds amplitudes; its positivity does not prevent cancellation of the signed physical jet. The construction and its normalization are inherited from [Note28](../notes/28_ENERGY_NORMALIZED_CONSERVATIVE_COLLISION_MAGNIFIER_20261028.md) and [Note29](../notes/29_POSITIVE_CARRIER_COMPARISON_AND_QUANTITATIVE_REGULARITY_20261028.md).

### 3. Why the original flow preserves energy

The chosen confining drift is

\[
v(X)=X+\frac{X}{1+|X|^2}=\nabla\mathcal V(X),\qquad
\mathcal V(X)=\tfrac12\bigl(|X|^2+\log(1+|X|^2)\bigr),
\]
\[
d(X)=\operatorname{div}v(X)
 =3+\frac{3+|X|^2}{(1+|X|^2)^2},\qquad 3\le d\le6.
\]

At a fixed physical mark, the normalized pair solves

\[
\partial_sW=\Delta W+\alpha(s)\operatorname{div}(vW).
\tag{6}
\]

Both components and the comparison carrier use this same coefficient. Define

\[
E=\int|W|^2,\qquad G=\int|\nabla W|^2,
\qquad D_W=\int d|W|^2.
\]

Integration by parts gives

\[
E'=2\int W\cdot\Delta W
  +2\alpha\int W\cdot\operatorname{div}(vW)
  =-2G+\alpha D_W.
\tag{7}
\]

Consequently, the rule

\[
\alpha(s)=\frac{2G(s)}{D_W(s)}
\tag{8}
\]

preserves \(E(s)=E_0>0\). The diffusion loss is \(2G\); the compression gain is \(\alpha D_W\). The quantity \(D_W\) measures the field's coupling to the divergence of the drift, while \(\alpha\) sets the strength of the inward velocity \(-\alpha v\).

This is a deliberately chosen diagnostic normalization. It is not a physical conservation law of the theta function, and the drift profile is not forced by theta arithmetic. The non-affine term in \(v\) avoids the immediate reduction of a purely affine drift to ordinary heat plus a scalar dilation. Conservation of the squared norm prevents a zero-mass field from simply disappearing by diffusion; maintaining that norm can instead require increasingly strong compression and gradients.

Crucially,

\[
3E_0\le D_W\le6E_0.
\tag{9}
\]

The equation does not divide by the unknown physical mass \(\mu\). Its feedback denominator stays positive along every regular real trajectory, including a trajectory based at a collision. Divergence of \(\alpha\) comes from growing gradient energy, not from inserting \(1/H\) or \(1/\mu\) into the definition.

The Diffusion Monte Carlo analogy captures the use of a scalar adjustment to impose normalization. For imaginary-time evolution

\[
\partial_\tau\psi=-(\widehat H-E_{\mathrm{ref}})\psi,
\]

one obtains

\[
\frac{d}{d\tau}\|\psi\|_2^2
=-2\langle\psi,\widehat H\psi\rangle
 +2E_{\mathrm{ref}}\|\psi\|_2^2.
\]

An exact continuous squared-norm constraint would therefore choose the evolving Rayleigh quotient as \(E_{\mathrm{ref}}\). The true ground-state eigenvalue of a fixed Hamiltonian is fixed, while practical population-control reference energies may be updated. Here the adjustment is a conservative spatial drift, rather than scalar multiplication of the state. Also, the project's “energy” \(E\) is a squared spatial norm, not a Hamiltonian expectation. The [fixed-compression comparison](../notes/CHAT0042_FIXED_COMPRESSION_AND_NORMALIZATION_COMPARISON_20261030.md) records the analogy and its checked DMC reference.

### 4. Collision breakdown and the positive-mass dependency

For the nonzero smooth prepared data with the stated decay and moment hypotheses, Note28 proves the equivalence

\[
S_{\max}<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m=0,
\qquad
S_{\max}=\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\ne0.
\tag{10}
\]

This concerns the normalized equation (6)–(8). It does not assert that arbitrary auxiliary equations break down at collisions. Nor is the field initially singular at a collision: the initial preparation is smooth, and breakdown occurs later in auxiliary time.

A useful sketch of the zero-mass direction uses the accumulated compression

\[
B(s)=\int_0^s\alpha(q)\,dq.
\]

The drift satisfies \(Dv\ge\kappa I\), \(\kappa=7/8\). Contraction of the common diffusion, combined with \(\int W=0\), yields the Fourier estimate

\[
|\widehat W_s(\xi)|\le |\xi|P_0e^{-\kappa B(s)},
\qquad P_0=\int |X|\,|W_0(X)|\,dX.
\tag{11}
\]

Splitting Fourier space into a low-frequency part controlled by (11) and a high-frequency part controlled by \(G\), while retaining \(E=E_0\), forces an inequality of the form

\[
B'(s)=\alpha(s)\ge C_0e^{\gamma B(s)},
\qquad \gamma=7/10,\quad C_0>0.
\]

Thus \(e^{-\gamma B(s)}\le1-\gamma C_0s\), giving a finite upper deadline. At the finite maximal endpoint, \(B\), \(\alpha\), and \(G\) diverge; the source notes also prove amplitude growth. These are properties of the specified normalized construction.

The converse exposes exactly why positive mass helps. Let

\[
R(s)=\int |X|^2h_s(X)\,dX.
\]

The carrier moment equation gives

\[
R'=6M-2\alpha\int X\cdot v(X)h_s(X)\,dX
\le6M-2\alpha R.
\tag{12}
\]

For any spatial radius \(\ell>0\), splitting the signed mass into a ball and its complement gives the elementary estimate

\[
\mu\le\int|W|
\le\left(\frac{4\pi}{3}\right)^{1/2}E_0^{1/2}\ell^{3/2}
   +\frac{R}{\ell^2}.
\]

Optimizing the radius explains the scaling \(R\gtrsim\mu^{7/3}E_0^{-2/3}\). Note29 proves the sharp form

\[
R\ge c_3\|W\|_1^{7/3}E_0^{-2/3}
 \ge r_\mu:=c_3\mu^{7/3}E_0^{-2/3},
\qquad c_3=\frac37\left(\frac{15}{14\pi}\right)^{2/3}.
\tag{13}
\]

If \(\mu>0\), division of (12) by \(R\ge r_\mu\), followed by integration, gives

\[
B(s)\le\tfrac12\log\frac{R(0)}{r_\mu}
          +\frac{3M}{r_\mu}s.
\tag{14}
\]

This stays finite on every finite auxiliary interval and rules out the breakdown required in (10). Subsequent estimates strengthen the bounds, but the positive physical mass supplies the lower moment bound in this argument.

At \(\mu=0\), that lower bound vanishes. The carrier can concentrate so that \(R\to0\), while the signed field keeps its positive squared norm through growing amplitude and gradients. Its \(L^1\) norm need not be conserved and can tend to zero. Positivity of \(M\) does not repair the missing lower bound on \(R\).

A “mass-free” estimate therefore means an estimate that does not assume a positive lower bound on \(\mu\). It need not ignore the source. It must use some independent theta information strong enough to distinguish the genuine source from the colliding controls. The accepted sequence from positive-mass regularity through carrier synchronization, feedback-defect reduction, and attraction cannot be used backwards to establish the mass-free estimate on which its own applicability would depend. The [strategy assessment](../reviews/CHAT0042_AUXILIARY_EVOLUTION_STRATEGY_ASSESSMENT_20261029.md) records that dependency in detail.

### 5. What the initial-time work and R17 do establish

At preparation time \(s=0\), the theta integral is explicit, so proposed estimates can be tested directly. A failed initial inequality rejects a proposed invariant inequality immediately. A successful initial inequality needs a separate propagation argument before it controls the evolving field.

This distinction is particularly important for the carrier decomposition

\[
a=\frac mM,\qquad e=W-ah,\qquad\int e=0,
\qquad\beta=\frac{2\|\nabla h\|_2^2}{\int dh^2},
\]
\[
Q_\beta(f)=2\|\nabla f\|_2^2-\beta\int d|f|^2.
\]

Expanding the quadratic form, using \(Q_\beta(h)=0\), and integrating the mixed gradient term by parts gives

\[
(\alpha-\beta)D_W
=Q_\beta(e)+\int C_\beta(X)\,dX,
\]
\[
C_\beta=-2(a\cdot e)(2\Delta h+\beta dh).
\tag{15}
\]

Proving \(\alpha\le\beta\) would be sufficient for some proposed barriers, but it is stronger than proving \(\alpha\le L\). If an independently established comparison gives \(\beta\le L\), the actual allowance is

\[
Q_\beta(e)+\int C_\beta\le(L-\beta)D_W.
\tag{16}
\]

A positive defect could be harmless if the independently proved gap on the right absorbs it. That observation does not itself provide the gap or the defect estimate.

At a collision, \(a=0\) and \(e=W\), so the mixed term vanishes:

\[
(\alpha-\beta)D_W=Q_\beta(W).
\tag{17}
\]

Improving only the mixed term cannot settle this case. The full integrated numerator and its propagation are the relevant quantities.

[R16](../notes/101_MASS_FREE_FEEDBACK_DEFECT_AND_SLOPE_CLOSURE_TEST_R16_20261029.md) supplies a valid mass-free identity and envelope, but its specified unsigned substitution does not close the proposed feedback ceiling. At the literal mark \((T,A)=(1/4,2)\),

\[
\frac{C_{\beta_0}(r)}{r^2h_0(r)^2}
\longrightarrow16|a|^2\left(\frac{\pi}{1+4\pi}\right)^2>0.
\tag{18}
\]

This is a large-spatial-radius statement at \(s=0\). It proves that the selected density cannot be nonpositive everywhere. It does not determine the sign of its full spatial integral, the full numerator, or \(\alpha-\beta\), and it does not prove anything about later auxiliary times. The same tail mechanism operates at frequency zero, so it is a limitation of this decomposition rather than a specifically arithmetic obstruction.

The original brainstorm suggested retaining the joint two-source-point cosine and jet products until after spatial integration, preserving cancellation that separate absolute values discard. Such a representation still requires an exact theta property that bounds the resulting integral and a justified connection to the evolved quantity. ### 6. Fixed compression: a regular flow with a stationary projection

Fix one finite \(c>0\), independent of \(s,T,A\), and retain the same spatial drift:

\[
\partial_sW_c=\mathcal L_cW_c,
\qquad\mathcal L_c=\Delta+c\operatorname{div}(v\,\cdot),
\qquad W_c(0)=W_0.
\tag{19}
\]

This is a linear conservative equation. Its energy identity is

\[
E_c'=-2G_c+cD_c\le6cE_c.
\tag{20}
\]

Smooth prepared data therefore have a global regular evolution; there is no transfer of the normalized collision blow-up theorem. Choosing \(c=2G_c(0)/D_c(0)\) merely sets the initial energy derivative to zero. It does not preserve energy at subsequent times.

The normalized stationary density is explicit:

\[
p_c(X)=\frac{e^{-c\mathcal V(X)}}{Z(c)},
\qquad Z(c)=\int_{\mathbb R^3}e^{-c\mathcal V(X)}\,dX,
\qquad\int p_c=1.
\tag{21}
\]

Since \(\nabla p_c+cvp_c=0\), one has \(\mathcal L_cp_c=0\). Conservation of vector mass identifies the stationary projection as \(m p_c\). The [fixed-compression note](../notes/CHAT0042_FIXED_COMPRESSION_AND_NORMALIZATION_COMPARISON_20261030.md) derives, using the inherited estimates,

\[
W_c(s;T,A)\longrightarrow m(T,A)p_c
\quad\text{in }L^2(\mathbb R^3).
\tag{22}
\]

The elementary proof subtracts the stationary projection, \(f=W_c-mp_c\). This gives a zero-mass solution. Nash and gradient inequalities give a finite whole-time bound \(\|\nabla f_s\|_2^2\le\overline G\). Contraction gives

\[
|\widehat f_s(\xi)|\le|\xi|P e^{-\kappa cs},
\qquad P=\int|X||f_0(X)|\,dX.
\]

Splitting Fourier space at radius \(R\) yields

\[
\|f_s\|_2^2\le\frac{P^2}{10\pi^2}e^{-2\kappa cs}R^5
                   +\frac{\overline G}{R^2}.
\]

With \(R=e^{2\kappa cs/7}\) and \(\kappa=7/8\), this becomes

\[
\|W_c(s)-mp_c\|_2^2\le C e^{-cs/2}.
\tag{23}
\]

The constant depends on \(c\) and finite initial budgets, with no positive lower bound on \(\mu\). This is a sufficient rate, not an optimal one, and the derivation remains a same-assistant result of this chat.

At a collision, (22) says \(W_c(s)\to0\). At a noncollision, it tends to the nonzero profile \(mp_c\). Thus the collision survives as a missing stationary component. It does not become a finite-time singularity of the fixed-coefficient equation.

There is also an exact stationary energy comparison. Let

\[
Q(c)=\|p_c\|_2^2=\frac{Z(2c)}{Z(c)^2}.
\]

Matching the original normalized energy would require

\[
\mu^2Q(c_*)=E_0.
\tag{24}
\]

[Note90](../notes/90_CARRIER_FEEDBACK_DEFECT_AND_STATIONARY_CANDIDATE_R13_20261029.md) proves that \(Q\) increases continuously from zero to infinity. A unique finite \(c_*\) therefore exists exactly when \(\mu>0\), with

\[
\pi\left(\frac{E_0}{\mu^2}\right)^{2/3}
\le c_*\le
8\pi\left(\frac{E_0}{\mu^2}\right)^{2/3}.
\tag{25}
\]

This is the previously identified stationary candidate for the normalized flow; [R15](../notes/98_RADIAL_LOG_SLOPE_ATTRACTION_B02_R15_20261029.md) establishes actual attraction under its accepted positive-mass inputs. A stationary matching coefficient is not a proof that the transient feedback is constant. Its existence is also equivalent to nonzero mass, so it cannot supply that fact independently.

Three claims consequently remain distinct: a fixed-coefficient equation exists; the original evolving feedback has a finite upper bound; the original feedback is identically constant. The first holds even at collisions, the second would prevent normalized finite-time breakdown, and the third imposes a much stronger constraint.

### 7. Why a common coefficient helps with physical parameter families

The normalized coefficient varies with the mark, \(\alpha=\alpha^p(s)\). This does not make the family discontinuous by definition. Note28 supplies local smooth dependence while a common regular lifespan exists. Differentiating the marked pair introduces an additional response term:

\[
\partial_s(\partial_AW^p)
=\mathcal L_{\alpha^p}(\partial_AW^p)
 +(\partial_A\alpha^p)\operatorname{div}(vW^p).
\tag{26}
\]

In the original construction, the transported spectral jet differentiates the running frequency while holding the mark and its coefficient path fixed. Differentiating along the diagonal family of different marks also differentiates that path. These are different operations.

At an isolated physical collision, the normalized trajectory has a finite lifespan while neighboring noncolliding trajectories are global. Smooth dependence before the center trajectory's endpoint gives neither a common continuation through that endpoint nor uniform long-time control near the event. The difficulty is this loss of a common regular time range, rather than parameter dependence alone.

For fixed \(c\), let \(P_s^c\) be the same spatial propagator at every mark. Then

\[
U_c(s;T,A)=P_s^cK_1*[\rho_T\cos(Au)],
\]
\[
V_c(s;T,A)=\partial_AU_c(s;T,A)
         =P_s^cK_1*[-\rho_Tu\sin(Au)].
\tag{27}
\]

Parameter derivatives commute with this common operator, so

\[
\partial_TU_c=-\partial_A^2U_c,
\qquad\int(U_c,V_c)=(H,H_A).
\tag{28}
\]

The complete theta tail dominates every fixed parameter derivative on compact parameter sets, including bounded complex parameter sets. Polynomial factors in \(u\), factors \(e^{C|u|}\) from complex frequency, and factors \(e^{Cu^2}\) from complex heat time remain integrable. Spatial moment factors are also dominated. This supplies smooth real parameter families and holomorphic complex extensions of the prepared fields and their fixed linear evolutions.

Applying the proof of (23) to each parameter derivative gives the compact-uniform absolute estimate recorded in the [parameter-family note](../notes/CHAT0042_FIXED_FLOW_PARAMETER_FAMILY_AND_COLLISION_INDEX_20261030.md):

\[
\sup_{p\in K}
\left\|\partial_p^\nu\bigl(W_c(s;p)-m(p)p_c\bigr)\right\|_2^2
\le C_{K,\nu,c}e^{-cs/2}.
\tag{29}
\]

This does not divide by the mass and remains meaningful when \(K\) contains a collision. It is absolute control, not relative error control near a vanishing physical jet.

The distinction between time limits is now explicit. For the normalized equation, one cannot assume an \(s\to\infty\) asymptotic before excluding a finite maximal time. A bound that stays finite on every finite lifespan interval would suffice; a constant bound for all time is stronger than necessary. Alternatively, the normalized compression clock obeys

\[
\frac{ds}{dB}=\frac1{\widetilde\alpha(B)},
\qquad S_{\max}=\int_0^\infty\frac{dB}{\widetilde\alpha(B)}
\]

when \(B\to\infty\) at the endpoint. Divergence of that integral would prevent a finite endpoint, but requires a new estimate on \(\widetilde\alpha\). For the fixed equation, global existence is already available, so an \(s\to\infty\) projection is legitimate. Neither auxiliary limit is the spatial \(r\to\infty\) limit used in R17.

### 8. What kind of collision behavior is being sought?

The branch is broader than a boundary-counting argument. A collision could be studied through a local quantity, a geometric structure over the parameter plane, the asymptotics of the field family, or an integral invariant. These choices need not produce the same kind of signal. The underlying fields can remain smooth while a projection vanishes, a normalized direction becomes undefined, or a derived quantity becomes singular.

The fixed-flow limit already gives an example of a local second-order signature. Its limiting squared norm is

\[
\mathscr E_\infty(p)=\|m(p)p_c\|_2^2=Q(c)|J(p)|^2.
\]

Here \(c\) is fixed, so \(Q(c)\) is independent of the physical mark. At a collision \(p_0\), elementary differentiation gives

\[
D_p^2\mathscr E_\infty(p_0)
 =2Q(c)\,[D_pJ(p_0)]^{\mathsf T}D_pJ(p_0).
\]

For a nondegenerate double collision, the determinant calculation in (30) shows that \(D_pJ(p_0)\) is invertible. The limiting energy therefore has a positive definite quadratic minimum there. This is a curvature of a scalar landscape in the chosen physical coordinates; it is regular, rather than infinite. Higher-order collisions can have a degenerate quadratic term. This observation follows directly from the stationary projection and is not a new theta-specific restriction.

Other meanings of curvature would require a different object. For example, the normalized jet direction \(n=J/|J|\) is defined only away from collisions. Its derivatives can become large near a zero, even though the derivatives of \(J\) remain finite. A metric formed from parameter derivatives of the full spatial fields would be another construction, with its own possible degeneracies and curvature. A Hessian of a scalar, curvature of a curve, and curvature of a metric are not interchangeable. A conclusion about one does not establish a singularity of the others.

Even the angular form underlying winding illustrates this distinction. Away from a jet zero, locally write \(J=|J|(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\). Then

\[
\omega=\frac{H\,dH_A-H_A\,dH}{H^2+H_A^2}=d\theta,
\qquad d\omega=0
\]

on that neighborhood. It can still have a nonzero integral around a puncture. Thus a topological charge need not appear as a nonzero ordinary curvature density away from the event. This is a feature of this particular angular form, not a conclusion about all possible geometries on the family.

Asymptotic mode behavior is a further possibility. The fixed operator is the same at every mark, while the coefficient of its stationary mode is \(m(p)\). At a collision that coefficient vanishes, leaving only decaying components. This may change which component dominates a normalized late-time profile, but the estimates above do not identify a complete spectral expansion or the leading surviving mode. They also do not imply a collision-specific slowing of relaxation: the absolute decay bound uses the same fixed \(c\) through the event.

There are consequently two separate logical ingredients in any eventual contradiction: a behavior forced by a collision, and a theta-derived restriction incompatible with that behavior. The first could be local and geometric without being a boundary estimate. It could also be a vanishing, a loss of rank, or a change of asymptotic profile rather than a blow-up. The second cannot follow solely from generic properties shared by colliding sources. The energy minimum, normalized-jet issue, and angular form above illustrate the range of possibilities; they do not select a curvature quantity or supply the required restriction.

### 9. Topology and residues as possible collision signatures

The physical transform is analytic at a collision. The event is a simultaneous zero of the jet

\[
J(T,A)=(H(T,A),H_A(T,A)),
\]

rather than a singularity of \(H\) itself. Singular behavior can appear after normalization or division, and the normalized auxiliary trajectory can break down. These are separate constructions.

For a nondegenerate double collision \(p_0=(T_0,A_0)\), put \(b=H_{AA}(p_0)\ne0\). Equation (1) gives

\[
\det D_{(T,A)}J(p_0)
=H_T(p_0)H_{AA}(p_0)-H_A(p_0)H_{AT}(p_0)
=-b^2<0.
\tag{30}
\]

With orientation \(dT\wedge dA\), a small positively oriented loop around the event is therefore mapped to a jet loop of winding \(-1\). The leading double-collision model

\[
H\approx b\left(\frac{(A-A_0)^2}{2}-(T-T_0)\right),
\qquad H_A\approx b(A-A_0)
\]

shows the same orientation reversal. The inherited finite-multiplicity law is \(-\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) for spatial multiplicity \(k\ge2\), under its isolated finite-order collision hypotheses. See [Note04](../notes/04_GAUSSIAN_SOURCE_DEFORMATION_AND_COLLISION_INDEX_TRANSPORT_20261027.md) and the [local geometry result summary](../../../../result-summaries/13_local_heat_flow_geometry_and_rh_equivalent_criteria.md).

For a closed physical loop \(\Gamma\) avoiding jet zeros, the winding is

\[
\operatorname{wind}_\Gamma J
=\frac1{2\pi}\oint_\Gamma
 \frac{H\,dH_A-H_A\,dH}{H^2+H_A^2}.
\tag{31}
\]

Writing \(H+iH_A\) is convenient for this real-plane map. It does not make it a holomorphic function of \(T+iA\).

A different, ordinary complex residue appears on a fixed-time slice. If

\[
H(T_0,z)=(z-A_0)^k g(z),\qquad g(A_0)\ne0,
\]

then

\[
\frac{H_A(T_0,z)}{H(T_0,z)}
=\frac{k}{z-A_0}+\frac{g'(z)}{g(z)}.
\tag{32}
\]

The residue is \(k\). This counts zeros of \(H\), including simple ones; it is not the real heat-collision index in (30)–(31), and it is not a residue of \(\alpha\).

The real feedback is built from squared norms. Its extension to complex parameters is not automatically meromorphic: literal Hermitian norms involve conjugation, while a bilinear complexification would be a different construction whose positivity and denominator protection require justification. Analytic continuation around a physical zero therefore cannot simply be identified with continuation around the normalized PDE breakdown. The fixed-coefficient family avoids needing such a continuation of the nonlinear feedback.

### 10. Boundary winding as one concrete interface

One concrete spatial observation of the fixed flow is

\[
F_{c,s}(T,A)=\frac{\int U_c(s;T,A;X)p_c(X)\,dX}{\|p_c\|_2^2}.
\tag{33}
\]

This bounded \(L^2\) pairing avoids an unproved pointwise-in-\(X\) limit. Its complex extension uses the same real test \(p_c\), so it is linear in the field. Equations (28)–(29) give

\[
\partial_TF_{c,s}=-\partial_A^2F_{c,s},
\qquad J_s:=(F_{c,s},\partial_AF_{c,s})\longrightarrow J
\tag{34}
\]

uniformly on compact physical parameter sets, with an absolute error bounded by a constant times \(e^{-cs/4}\). The rate follows by taking the square root in (29) and applying Cauchy–Schwarz to (33).

Suppose \(\Omega\) is a bounded physical region with a suitable closed boundary \(\Gamma\), and the physical jet does not vanish on that boundary. Then

\[
\delta_\Gamma=\min_{p\in\Gamma}|J(p)|>0.
\]

If

\[
\sup_{p\in\Gamma}|J_s(p)-J(p)|<\delta_\Gamma,
\tag{35}
\]

the straight-line homotopy between \(J_s\) and \(J\) cannot cross zero on \(\Gamma\). Hence

\[
\operatorname{wind}_\Gamma J_s=\operatorname{wind}_\Gamma J.
\tag{36}
\]

This is the precise sense in which asymptotic information from a regular common flow can retain collision topology. It requires uniform boundary control, not merely convergence at each individual mark. An independently established boundary margin is compatible with interior collisions; it is not an assumption that the whole interior is collision-free.

Under the inherited degree hypotheses, boundary winding equals the sum of the interior collision indices, all of which are negative. Independently establishing zero winding would therefore exclude interior collisions. The fixed-coefficient construction supplies the family and the conditional transfer, but no independent zero-winding estimate has been obtained here.

There are two further distinctions. First, the full mass observation \(\int W_c\) equals \(J\) at every time, so applying topology only to that integral repeats the original invariant exactly. Second, smoothing does not by itself keep an observed jet nonzero on a chosen boundary throughout a deformation. Zeros may move through the boundary. A simple endpoint or a positive terminal profile is insufficient without accounting for those crossings. The exact Gaussian-deformation control in Note04 illustrates this failure through the physical time boundary. It does not rule out every fixed-coefficient argument.

### 11. What this branch currently adds, and what remains unresolved

The new viewpoint organizes the physical parameters with one global linear auxiliary operator. It preserves the actual frequency derivative and the physical heat identity, allows compact-uniform asymptotic estimates through zero mass, and places collisions in the zero set of a stationary projection. This gives a precise setting for the author's intuition that nearby regular flows may carry geometric evidence of a collision.

The difficulty remains quantitative and source-specific. Generic positivity, regularity, contraction, and stationary projection are also available to admitted colliding controls. A prepared weighted-square source at its certified event and the prepared Gaussian-polynomial collision control have zero vector mass, regular fixed-coefficient flows, and a vanishing stationary projection. At their nondegenerate collisions, their real jet index is likewise \(-1\). They therefore do not fail any of those generic premises. Their fixed flows fail the proposed premise of retaining a positive constant energy, because they decay to zero. No new separating theta premise is supplied merely by fixing \(c\).

Likewise, the existence of a finite stationary energy-matching coefficient, universal normalized regularity, and zero collision weight on a prescribed region are reformulations or criteria until their needed theta input is proved independently. The author's proposal may lead to a useful way of obtaining that input; the present background establishes neither its existence nor its impossibility. An estimate on a transformed family would have to add information about cancellation, local geometry, parameter sensitivity, or global topology beyond the conserved physical jet and the generic common-flow estimates. Boundary information is one possible form of that input, not a necessary form. The behavior to be detected and excluded at a collision remains an open part of the investigation.

The scope of the inherited failures matters. R16 rejects its specified unsigned closure. R17 rejects its specified everywhere-nonpositive density at preparation. The earlier Gaussian deformation rejects an unpaid boundary-homotopy inference. None is a universal obstruction to integrated source correlations, fixed-coefficient families, or topology.

### Purpose and the iterative structure

The first objective is to identify what behavior a collision forces in the fixed-coefficient parameter family, and to narrow that question to a few credible leads. The next objective is to determine whether independent information from the complete theta source is incompatible with one of those behaviors. These objectives interact: failure of a theta estimate may show that the observable needs to change, while a more useful source identity may suggest a different collision signature.

The intended cycle is therefore

\[
\text{propose a collision signature}
\longrightarrow\text{connect it to a theta-specific input}
\longrightarrow\text{test the proposed incompatibility}
\longrightarrow\text{assess and refine, retain, or retire}.
\]

This is a short cycle for each candidate, not a requirement to finish a general geometric classification before consulting theta. The initial shortlist contains at most three leads, and one candidate receives substantive development at a time. Each lead includes a preliminary source connection from the outset. A successful structural calculation without a theta connection is recorded as such; it does not automatically justify a larger campaign.

For a chosen collision class, the logic has two distinct implications. Schematically,

\[
\mathcal C_k(p)\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathscr O[W_c;p]\in\mathcal B_k,
\qquad
\mathcal I_\theta\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathscr O[W_c;p]\notin\mathcal B_k.
\]

Here \(\mathcal C_k\) specifies a physical zero of exact order \(k\ge2\), \(\mathscr O\) is the chosen observable or geometric object, \(\mathcal B_k\) is its collision-forced behavior, and \(\mathcal I_\theta\) is an independently available theta input. The behavior may be a zero, loss of rank, curvature pattern, singular normalization, asymptotic mode change, or topological charge. It need not be a scalar blow-up or a boundary condition. The second implication is the missing source restriction, not something supplied by naming the first.

For a scalar candidate, a usable comparison might place the collision value below a threshold and the theta value strictly above it, with all errors included. For a degenerating or asymptotic candidate, the comparison may instead require a leading coefficient, a scale, and a remainder smaller than that scale. A fixed positive margin is not imposed where the geometry requires a vanishing scale. A finite diagnostic may test feasibility, but only a proof or appropriate certificate establishes the declared exclusion.

### Potential path: one finite coefficient on each compact region

The author's proposed regional formulation gives one possible structure for the contradiction. Let

\[
\mathcal D_+=\{(T,A)\in\mathbb R^2:T>0\},\qquad
J(T,A)=(H(T,A),H_A(T,A)),
\]

and let \(K\) be a compact convex region with nonempty interior contained in \(\mathcal D_+\), for example a closed disk lying entirely at positive physical time. Seek a property \(\mathsf X(K,c)\) of the fixed-coefficient auxiliary family such that

\[
J(p)\ne0\ \text{for every }p\in K
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists\,0<c_K<\infty:\ \mathsf X(K,c_K).
\]

The coefficient may depend on the region, but the same \(c_K\) is used at every physical mark in that region and remains constant throughout auxiliary time. The property must specify its own time range or limiting regime. Pointwise choices \(c=c(p)\) do not establish this regional statement. Nor is one finite constant required to work over the entire positive-time plane.

For the contradiction, the essential direction is the collision obstruction

\[
\bigl[\exists p\in K:\ J(p)=0\bigr]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\bigl[\forall\,0<c<\infty:\ \neg\mathsf X(K,c)\bigr].
\]

The converse would make the property an exact characterization; a stronger sufficient property could also work without that converse. The separate, substantive theta target would be

\[
\boxed{\text{For every compact convex }K\subset\mathcal D_+,
\quad\exists\,0<c_K<\infty:\ \mathsf X_\theta(K,c_K).}
\]

Here \(\mathsf X_\theta\) is the same property applied to the complete theta initial data, not a different property chosen after seeing the region's zeros. Its existence proof must use independently established theta information. Combining it with the collision obstruction would exclude the covered collision class at every positive-time point: a hypothetical event lies in the interior of a suitable small closed disk. If the obstruction covers only nondegenerate double events, the conclusion retains that restriction. No endpoint assertion at \(T=0\) or further RH transfer follows without its separate argument.

Compactness is useful because continuous nonvanishing on the whole of \(K\), including its boundary, gives \(\min_K|J|>0\). Boundedness of an open region alone does not: its jet may approach zero at a boundary event. Convexity supplies a simple regional geometry and may help a particular argument, but it does not itself control the jet. Compactness also does not prove the existence of a common coefficient for an arbitrary property; that implication must be established for the chosen \(\mathsf X\). Constants may deteriorate as regions approach \(T=0\) or move to large frequency while remaining finite on each fixed region.

The stationary projection gives a useful illustration of the quantifiers. From (21)–(24),

\[
\lim_{s\to\infty}\|W_c(s;p)\|_2^2=|J(p)|^2Q(c),
\qquad Q(c)=\|p_c\|_2^2\longrightarrow\infty
\quad(c\to\infty).
\]

Consider the illustrative stationary property

\[
\mathsf X_{\mathrm{stat}}(K,c):\qquad
|J(p)|^2Q(c)\ge E_0(p)\quad\text{for every }p\in K.
\]

If \(J\) is nonzero on \(K\), then \(\delta_K=\min_K|J|>0\) and \(\overline E_K=\max_K E_0<\infty\). Choosing \(c\) with

\[
Q(c)\ge\frac{\overline E_K}{\delta_K^2}
\]

establishes the property uniformly on \(K\). At a collision its left side is zero, while \(E_0(p)>0\), so every finite coefficient fails. This uses an inequality in the stationary limit; it neither preserves transient energy nor demands exact energy matching at every mark with one coefficient.

**This example is a reformulation, not a new theta estimate or a selected candidate.** Proving its theta side by assuming \(\delta_K>0\) would assume the desired conclusion. An independent proof would have to establish the implied bound \(|J(p)|^2\ge E_0(p)/Q(c_K)\) from other source information. The admitted prepared Gaussian-polynomial and weighted-square controls fail this stationary property on any region containing their event, because their stationary vector projection vanishes there. They still have regular fixed-coefficient flows; existence or regularity of the flow alone cannot serve as \(\mathsf X\).

FCG-B01 can use this regional formulation to sharpen candidate selection: define a property whose collision obstruction is clear and whose finite-coefficient existence might be accessible from theta. Its content could be curvature, degeneracy, parameter response, asymptotic mode structure, or topology. The regional quantifiers do not require boundary information. The subsequent cycle tests the proposed theta existence argument early, preserving the distinction between a valid characterization and a new way of proving its premise.
