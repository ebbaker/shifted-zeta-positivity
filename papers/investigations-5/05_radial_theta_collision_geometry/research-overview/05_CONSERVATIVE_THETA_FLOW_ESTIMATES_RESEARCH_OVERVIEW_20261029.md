## Why integration by parts is the selected direction

Write A_theta(r)=(Theta(r)-1)/2 and r=e^(2u). Dimension three gives
\[
\Phi(u)=\tfrac12r^{5/2}\Delta_3A_\theta(r).
\]
The half-line primitive psi_theta(u)=e^u A_theta(e^(2u)) satisfies
\[
\psi_\theta''-\psi_\theta=8\Phi,\qquad
\psi_\theta(0)=A_\theta(1),\qquad\psi_\theta'(0)=-\tfrac12.
\]
Consequently, for the test class stated in the manuscript,
\[
\int_0^\infty\Phi f\,du
=\frac{f(0)}{16}+\frac{A_\theta(1)f'(0)}8
 +\frac18\int_0^\infty\psi_\theta(f''-f)\,du.
\]
This keeps the complete arithmetic primitive inside a signed pairing
before separate absolute estimates. Its double-exponential tail supports
positive Newman time. Directly multiplying the smooth potential's
exponential-tail generator by e^(tu²) does not.

For the common feedback propagator and a terminal test eta, duality gives
\[
\langle J_j(s),\eta\rangle
=\langle F_j^{\rm raw},\chi_s\rangle,\qquad
\chi_s=K_1*(P_{s,0}^*\eta).
\]
The scalar test is
\(f=e^{Tu^2}u^j\cos^{(j)}(Au)\chi_s(e^{2u})\).
For j=0 its boundary contribution is
\(\chi_s(1)/16+A_\theta(1)\chi_{s,r}(1)/4\); for j>=1 it vanishes.
All preparation and evolution, including flow into the initially empty
ball, are carried by chi_s. These are initial-support traces, not an
imposed boundary condition on the evolved sphere.
