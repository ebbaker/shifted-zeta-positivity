## 1. Fixed consumer, inputs and classification

Keep the complete theta field, physical Newman equation
\(\partial_\lambda H=-H_{zz}\), the upper-half-plane carrier \(C_-\)
and \(V_\lambda=H_\lambda/C_-\) of [E01, (E1)–(E3)][e01]. Set
\[
 L_X=\log(X/(4\pi)),\quad \lambda=c/L_X,\quad \tfrac12\le c\le1,
 \quad R_X=(32/\pi)\log X,\quad
 K(t)=\frac1{16\cosh(\pi t/8)} .
 \tag{B1}
\]
The time is fixed across each spatial integral. The strip is \(0<y<8\),
the observation point is \(X+4i\), and each boundary has mass \(1/2\).
For the exact critical integral and prime series retain
\[
 I_\lambda(X)=\int_{\mathbb R}K(x-X)\log|V_\lambda(x+i0)|\,dx,
\]
\[
 P_{c,\lambda}(X)=\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda_{\rm vM}(n)}{\log n}
 \frac{n^{-5/2-c/4}}{1+n^{-4}}
 \cos[(X/2+\pi\lambda/8)\log n].
 \tag{B2}
\]
E01 gives \(|J_\lambda-I_\lambda+P_{c,\lambda}|\le C_*/L_X\),
uniformly for every sufficiently large \(X,c\) in (B1), with finite
unevaluated constants/onset. The desired upper value \(U\) for
\(S_\lambda=I_\lambda-P_{c,\lambda}\) must satisfy
\[
 U(X,c)+C_*/L_X<m_*,
 \qquad m_*=\frac{\sin(\pi/8)}{2\cosh(\pi/8)}
 \quad(d=1/2,\ r=1).
 \tag{B3}
\]
An average upper bound would not satisfy this every-center consumer.
An average *lower* bound on a proposed allowance can, however, disprove
its eventual every-center success. That is the direction used in §3.

The [result index][results], [heat/RS cluster11][r11] and
[avoidance index][avoid], particularly [target classification01][a01],
[control premises02][a02], [scope07][a07] and [separate bounds12][a12],
govern the following classification. Jensen is an **acquisition
inequality**. The lower bound on its specified allowance is a **scoped
quantitative obstruction**. The shifted-zeta identity is a
**representation**, with no acquired sign. The proposed moment law is
an **intermediate analytical estimate**, not an RH reformulation.

The [parent signed-log assembly][assembly] and [OG RS/log transfer][og]
already distinguish approximation accuracy from the signed value.
They do not acquire (B3). The earlier [OC signed-Jensen note][oc] treats
a compact two-dimensional Laplacian test and harmonic-distance costs,
at time zero. Its obstruction is not transferred to this strip-boundary
observable or positive shrinking-time band. No literature-priority claim
is made. The two formulations below exhaust this B02 comparison.

## 2. Formulation A: localized coherent quadratic mean

Define the exact compact mass and moment
\[
 q_X=\int_{-R_X}^{R_X}K(t)\,dt,\qquad
 M(X,c)=\int_{|x-X|\le R_X}K(x-X)|V_{c/L_X}(x)|^2\,dx.
 \tag{B4}
\]
Here \(q_X\to1/2\), with \(1/2-q_X=O(X^{-4})\).
The field is analytic and not identically zero, so \(M>0\).
E01's unit-log estimate covers the critical boundary, including zeros
and the carrier singularity at zero; its (E20) gives
\[
 \int_{|x-X|>R_X}K(x-X)|\log|V_{c/L_X}(x)||\,dx
 \le T_X:=D_VX^{-3}\log^2X.
 \tag{B5}
\]
The same finite onset gates and constant \(D_V\) are used.

**Localized Jensen bound.** Concavity of the logarithm for the probability
measure \(K\,dx/q_X\) on the window gives
\[
 I_{c/L_X}(X)\le\frac{q_X}{2}\log\frac{M(X,c)}{q_X}+T_X.
 \tag{B6}
\]
Apply it first to \(|V|^2+\epsilon\), then let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\);
the inherited logarithmic integrability justifies the limit.
No positive real-line field floor, simplicity or separation is assumed.
Consequently
\[
 \mathcal B_J(X,c)=
 \frac{q_X}{2}\log\frac{M(X,c)}{q_X}
       -P_{c,c/L_X}(X)+T_X+\frac{C_*}{L_X}
 \tag{B7}
\]
is an upper allowance for \(J\). Replacing \(M\) by a genuine upper
bound \(M^\#\ge M\) increases this allowance. The complete moment
retains all phases before integration. Jensen itself may still lose
too much information about small values of the field.

### A proved obstruction to paying all cross terms nonnegatively

For positive \(x\), write the complete real HDA approximant in E01's
normalization as
\[
 V_\lambda(x)=
 \sum_{n\le N_x}w_n(\lambda,x)e^{i\nu_\lambda(x)\log n}
 +e^{2i\Theta(\lambda,x)}
       \sum_{n\le N_x}w_n(\lambda,x)e^{-i\nu_\lambda(x)\log n}
 +r_\lambda(x),
 \tag{B8}
\]
\[
 N_x=\lfloor\sqrt{x/(4\pi)}\rfloor,\quad
 w_n=n^{-1/2}\exp\{\lambda[(\log n)^2-L_x\log n]/4\},\quad
 \nu_\lambda(x)=x/2+\pi\lambda/8.
\]
The complete correction and provider remainder are in \(r_\lambda\);
they are not discarded. The [HDA fixed-buffer theorem][hda] supplies
this at \(y=0\) as well as the outer lines, with its original
[internal assessment][hdar]. At \(y=0\), \(|e^{2i\Theta}|=1\).
The diagonal of the two finite arms is
\[
 D(X,c)=2\int_{|x-X|\le R_X}K(x-X)
              \sum_{n\le N_x}w_n(c/L_X,x)^2\,dx.
 \tag{B9}
\]

Consider only moment bounds \(M^\#\ge M\) which retain this diagonal
and add nonnegative payments for every same-arm cross term, cross-arm
term, correction and error. Thus, as part of this recipe,
\(M^\#\ge D\). This condition is **not asserted for the exact \(M\)**.
Already the modes \(1,2\), with \(L_x/L_X=1+o(1)\) uniformly in the
window, give
\[
 \frac{M^\#(X,c)}{q_X}\ge
 2+2^{-c/2}+o(1),
 \qquad
 \mathcal B^\#_J(X,c)\ge
 \tfrac14\log(2+2^{-c/2})-P_{c,c/L_X}(X)+o(1).
 \tag{B10}
\]
All other payments are nonnegative and (B5) tends to zero.

At \(c=1\), put \(j_1=\tfrac14\log(2+1/\sqrt2)\).
The elementary inequality
\(\log u\ge2(u-1)/(u+1)\) for \(u\ge1\), and
\(2+1/\sqrt2>5/2\), give \(j_1>3/14\).
Also \(m_*<1/5\), since
\(\sin(\pi/8)=\sqrt{2-\sqrt2}/2<2/5\) and \(\cosh(\pi/8)>1\).
Hence \(j_1-m_*>1/70\).

For completeness, this fixed gap cannot be paid by assuming \(P\)
always favorable. The absolutely convergent cosine series \(P_{1,0}\)
has mean tending to zero on \([T,2T]\): integrate each fixed frequency,
then use the uniformly summable tail. Further,
\[
 |P_{1,1/L_X}(X)-P_{1,0}(X)|
 \le\frac{\pi}{8L_X}\sum_{n\ge2}n^{-11/4}\log n=O(L_X^{-1}).
 \tag{B11}
\]
For each \(\epsilon>0\) there are therefore arbitrarily large \(X\)
with \(P_{1,1/L_X}(X)\le\epsilon\); otherwise its dyadic means would
eventually be at least \(\epsilon\). Equations (B10)–(B11) prove:

**The nonnegative-cross-payment Jensen recipe cannot satisfy (B3) at
every sufficiently large center.** This is a lower bound on an
allowance, not on \(I\), \(J\) or the exact coherent moment. It is
distinct from B01's divergent separate-magnitude allowance: the present
failure already has a fixed positive margin using two modes after
quadratic integration. It excludes neither signed cross-term estimates
nor direct logarithmic methods.

## 3. Stronger coherent-Jensen question: fixed E03 target

To decide whether even the exact \(M\) makes (B7) unsuitable, the
following single quantitative statement suffices:
\[
 \boxed{\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_T^{2T}M(X,1)\,dX
                  =\zeta(3/2).}
 \tag{B12, proposed}
\]
**B02 does not report (B12) as proved.** Its proof and the ensuing
stronger obstruction are assigned to the separate [frozen E03 r1][request].
Taking \(c=1\) tests a subfamily of the unchanged band; it does not
alter the physical clock or imply a theorem for a new time regime.

### Conditional consequence and explicit comparison margin

Here are elementary constants available to that execution.
\[
 m_*<\frac9{50},\qquad |P_{1,\lambda}(X)|<\frac{23}{100},
 \qquad \zeta(3/2)>\frac{13}{5},\qquad
 e^{41/25}<\frac{26}{5}.
 \tag{B13}
\]
They are analytical inequalities, not floating-point certificates:

- From \(\sqrt2>141/100\), \(\sqrt{2-\sqrt2}<77/100\);
  from \(\pi>3\), \(\cosh(\pi/8)>137/128\).
  Thus \(m_*<9856/54800<9/50\).
- For the prime-power sum, the coefficient is \(1/k\) at \(n=p^k\)
  and zero elsewhere. For \(n\le16\), upper bounds for
  \((1/k)n^{-11/4}/(1+n^{-4})\) are the following exact rationals,
  written as terminating decimals:

  | \(n\) | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 11 | 13 | 16 |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Upper bound | .14 | .049 | .012 | .012 | .005 | .0014 | .0013 | .0014 | .001 | .00013 |

  For the first five use \(2^{1/4}<119/100\),
  \(3^{1/4}<4/3\), \(\sqrt2<3/2\),
  \(5^{1/4}<3/2\), \(7^{1/4}<5/3\).
  For the rest, drop the denominator and use respectively
  \(8^{3/4}>4\), \(\sqrt3>5/3\), \(11^{3/4}>6\),
  \(13^{3/4}>6\), and \(16^{11/4}=2048\).
  The remaining integer tail is at most
  \(\int_{16}^{\infty}u^{-11/4}\,du=1/224<.0045\).
  The displayed budgets plus this tail sum to .22773, below .23.
- Convexity and the trapezoidal bound on \(u^{-3/2}\) give
  \[
  \zeta(3/2)\ge1+\frac1{2\sqrt2}+\frac{13}{6\sqrt3}
       >1+\frac6{17}+\frac54=\frac{177}{68}>\frac{13}{5};
  \]
  use \(\sqrt2<17/12,\ \sqrt3<26/15\).
- For \(0\le x<3\),
  \(e^x\le1+x+x^2/[2(1-x/3)]\), by
  \(k!\ge2\cdot3^{k-2}\) for \(k\ge2\).
  At \(x=41/100\) this is less than \(151/100\). Raising to the
  fourth power gives
  \(e^{41/25}<(151/100)^4=519885601/10^8<26/5\).

If \(\mathcal B_J(X,1)<m_*\) at all sufficiently large centers, then
nonnegativity of \(T_X+C_*/L_X\) and (B13) imply
\[
 M(X,1)<q_X\exp\{2(m_*+P_{1,1/L_X}(X))/q_X\}
 \le q_X\exp\{(41/50)/q_X\}.
 \tag{B14}
\]
As \(q_X\to1/2\), the right side tends to
\(\tfrac12e^{41/25}<13/5\), contradicting (B12).
Thus **conditional on (B12)** there are arbitrarily high centers
where even the exact coherent quadratic-Jensen allowance fails.
No assertion is made about the sign of the true logarithm there.
The missing Jensen gap is precisely information lost by (B6).

### Why the proof request is tractable and bounded

Let \(v(u)=V_{1/L_u}(u)\). On its real chart the two-arm weights are
\[
 w_n(u)=n^{-3/4}\exp\{(\log n)^2/(4L_u)\}\le n^{-5/8},
 \quad n\le N_u,
\]
\[
 \nu(u)=u/2+\pi/(8L_u),\quad
 \Theta(u)=u(L_u-1)/4+\pi/16+7\pi/8 .
 \tag{B15}
\]
The proposed route has four fixed groups, fully stated in E03:

1. Use the same HDA theorem to retain both arms, the correction and
   its uniform fractional-cutoff bound, and the field residual.
   The diagonal tends to \(2\zeta(3/2)\), using domination by
   \(n^{-5/4}\) for \(w_n^2\).
2. Bound the same-arm off-diagonal integrals by their nonzero
   phase derivative \(\nu'(u)\log(n/m)\). For cross-arm phases
   \(2\Theta(u)-\nu(u)\log(mn)\), use their second derivative of
   order \(1/T\) on \([T,2T]\). Sum over the full growing population,
   with amplitudes controlled by \(n^{-5/8}\). Each pair is integrated
   on its actual active interval \(u\ge4\pi\max(m,n)^2\);
   endpoints are paid, and no global floor is differentiated.
3. Compare \(V_{1/L_X}(x)\) with \(v(x)\) on
   \(|x-X|\le R_X\). The time difference is
   \(O(R_X/(XL_X^2))\); compare the *normalized complete formulas*.
   Pay both corrections by their normalized bounds, or compare their
   carrier dependence if needed. Time remains fixed
   in the original definition of \(M\). Use Fubini and pay the
   convolution edges to obtain the factor \(1/2\).
4. Check (B13)–(B14) and give the exact scoped consequence.

This is a proof plan, not a completed execution. The elementary
first/second derivative integral method is documented, for example,
in Joel H. Shapiro's [Notes on Oscillatory Integrals, §2][osc].
E03 must derive bounds uniform in the growing mode pairs and moving
endpoints; an asymptotic with an unspecified phase-dependent constant
is not an acceptable import. No new numerical campaign is needed.

## 4. Formulation B: complete shifted-zeta comparison

For each fixed \(X,c,\lambda\) from (B1), set
\[
 \delta=\pi\lambda/4,\qquad
 s_c(z)=\frac{1-iz}{2}+\frac c4-\frac{i\pi\lambda}{8},
 \qquad Z_{c,\lambda}(z)=\zeta(s_c(z)).
 \tag{B16}
\]
This is a reference field, not a replacement for physical \(H_\lambda\).
The exact completed identity is
\[
 M_\Gamma(s_c(z))Z_{c,\lambda}(z)
       =H_0(z+\delta+ic/2),\quad
 M_\Gamma(s)=\frac{s(s-1)}{16}\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2).
 \tag{B17}
\]
Indeed \(s_c(z)=s_-(z+\delta+ic/2)\) and xi has its exact reflection
symmetry. This uses the inherited complete time-zero normalization,
not an Euler product on the critical boundary.

There is one simple zeta pole in the strip,
\[
 p=-\delta+i(1-c/2).
 \tag{B18}
\]
The factor \(M_\Gamma(s_c)\) is holomorphic there with a single
simple zero at \(p\); its other factors have no zeros or poles because
\(\Re s_c\ge1/2+c/4>0\). After dividing by \(z-p\) it is nonvanishing.
Its logarithm has at most linear plus logarithmic growth on the
fixed strip, by the same gamma calibration used in E01. Poisson
representation therefore gives
\(\mathcal J[M_\Gamma(s_c)](X)=G(X+\delta,1-c/2)\), with no other
divisor or harmonic residue.

Let \(x_j(0)+iy_j(0)\) run over the complete time-zero zeros with
multiplicity. Define
\[
 \mathcal Z_{c,\lambda}(X)=
 \sum_{y_j(0)>c/2}
 G(X+\delta-x_j(0),\,y_j(0)-c/2).
 \tag{B19}
\]
All their original depths satisfy \(|y_j(0)|\le1\).
The affine translate in (B17) thus has precisely these zeros inside
the strip; a zero at \(y_j=c/2\) lies on the boundary and contributes
zero, with no half atom. Its center is nonzero.

The complete [parent paired-product/Green proof][green] applies to
this fixed affine translation. Here is the required extension check:
on a real unit interval \([v,v+1]\), \(0\le y\le8\),
the translated point has modulus at most
\(|v|+1+\pi/8+8+1/2<|v|+12\) when \(\lambda\le1/2\).
The parent unit-log proof only needs this radius, the same paired
zero count and the lower bound on the original zero moduli.
Its near-zero negative distance logarithm bound is invariant under
real translation. The original paired factors
\(1-(z+\delta+ic/2)^2/\rho^2\) therefore obey the same absolute
local \(L^1\) bound and the same exponentially summable boundary
tail. Their finite Green identities pass to the limit. This proves
\[
 \mathcal J[H_0(\,\cdot+\delta+ic/2)](X)=\mathcal Z_{c,\lambda}(X),
 \qquad
 \mathcal J[Z_{c,\lambda}](X)
       =\mathcal Z_{c,\lambda}(X)-G(X+\delta,1-c/2).
 \tag{B20}
\]
The second formula is a meromorphic Green identity: the pole has a
negative sign. It is not an application of a zero-free logarithm of
zeta across the strip. Boundary zero logarithms are locally integrable.

Write
\[
 I^\zeta_{c,\lambda}(X)=
       \int_{\mathbb R}K(x-X)\log|Z_{c,\lambda}(x)|\,dx,\qquad
 \Delta_{c,\lambda}(X)=I_\lambda(X)-I^\zeta_{c,\lambda}(X).
 \tag{B21}
\]
These are finite signed integrals by (B17), the gamma bound and the
translated unit-log argument. The difference is interpreted as a
difference of integrable logarithms, not as a globally holomorphic
logarithm of \(V/Z\). At common or separate real zeros this definition
still makes sense almost everywhere.

On \(y=4,8\), (B16) is precisely E01's comparison \(s_y^0(x)\).
Its exact outer Euler-log combination (E22) is \(-P_{c,\lambda}\).
Subtracting that combination from (B20) proves the exact identity
\[
 \boxed{S_\lambda(X)=
 \Delta_{c,\lambda}(X)+\mathcal Z_{c,\lambda}(X)
                         -G(X+\delta,1-c/2).}
 \tag{B22}
\]
The first missing estimate is an independent quantitative bound on
the **coupled** \(\Delta+\mathcal Z\), sufficiently below
\(m_*+G-C_*/L_X\). Neither term has a favorable acquired bound here.
Simply postulating that inequality would restate the consumer.
Discarding the zero potential, or assuming a holomorphic logarithm
of the quotient on the whole strip, would assume away the unknown
divisors. The isolated pole correction tends exponentially to zero
as \(X\to\infty\) and is not the missing uniform gain.

**Disposition B:** stop this bare shifted-zeta contour reduction at
the unpaid coupled logarithmic estimate. This is not a theorem against
all coupled contours or all source-dependent comparisons.
Parent GNP-B01 retains the separate time-zero signed-value problem;
(B19) does not transfer ownership or prove that potential small.

## 5. Collision-control screen and evidence limits

This is an analytical premise check against [controls12][controls]
and the earlier controls in [cluster11][r11], not a rerun of certificates.
The generic Jensen/meromorphic identities are not exclusion premises.
The theta-specific moments and zeta prime subtraction cannot be assigned
unchanged to a different source.

| Control family | First absent premise and consequence for these two routes |
| --- | --- |
| Euler-factor/translated-source controls | The exact unchanged theta coefficients and completed xi identity (B17) fail. Their own arithmetic completion, pole terms and zero residues must be retained. Jensen remains valid; no small allowance follows from it. |
| Weighted squares, finite-prefix or finite-tail changes | Full unchanged coefficients together with canonical reflection are absent. The particular diagonal in (B9) and moment target (B12) are not their moment laws. |
| Finite-prime-orbit controls | Matching selected primes does not supply full reflection or all coefficients in (B8). Their arbitrarily high gap witnesses rule out dismissing this screen on the basis of large height alone. |
| Reflected super-Gaussian, smooth-shape and positive-primitive controls | Their retained positivity/reflection/endpoint properties do not supply ordinary theta/zeta arithmetic in (B8), (B17). Generic contour identities still retain their actual zeros. |
| Atomic fixed-event and exact finite-profile controls, including continuous extensions | Selected anchors and moment bounds do not identify the complete theta profile or its eventual simplicity; no literal (B8)/(B17) input is supplied. The present argument assumes neither a finite profile nor a trajectory floor. |
| RH-conditional normalized endpoint controls | They fail ordinary Dirichlet-series arithmetic. Their added real double zeros are compatible with Jensen/log integrability, not evidence for an off-line exclusion. |
| Earlier \(q,q_{T,a}\) and related counter-kernels | Heat evolution and selected realization properties do not supply the complete canonical coefficients or xi completion. Apply a generic Green formula only with their own divisor and growth hypotheses. |
| Fixed theta amplitude with variable readout | The actual cosine readout/Newman equation is absent; it is explicitly used in the inherited full RS formula. |
| Actual-theta three-profile relaxation | A relaxed positive measure need not be the exact integer zero measure. Finite observation data neither identify (B19) nor estimate \(\Delta\); its feasible atom cannot be read as a physical event. |

On a control's witness domain an actual off-line zero forces its own
positive Green contribution. A valid bound cannot pass a contradictory
exclusion there. A real collision point alone has zero boundary
contribution, and an isolated finite witness need not lie in (B1);
neither is falsely assigned our \(d=1/2\) consumer. No new favorable
inequality is claimed to reject such witnesses. The failed Jensen
recipe is tested more strongly on the genuine theta field itself,
within its explicitly stated payment class.
