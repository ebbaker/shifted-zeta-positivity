## 2. What an ideal cosine correction actually recovers

On the real line the complete retained arms have the form
\[
 S_\lambda(x)=A_\lambda(x)+e^{2i\Theta(\lambda,x)}
 \overline{A_\lambda(x)},\qquad
 A_\lambda(x)=\sum_{n\le N_x}w_n(\lambda,x)
 e^{i\nu(\lambda,x)\log n},
 \tag{4}
\]
with exactly B02's weights, cutoff and phases. The correction and
provider residual belong to \(V-S\), not to zero.

For an independently uniform phase \(\vartheta\),
\(Y=2|A|\cos\vartheta\) has
\[
 \mathbb E_\vartheta\log|Y|=\log|A|,
 \qquad \mathbb E_\vartheta|Y|^2=2|A|^2
 \tag{5}
\]
when \(|A|>0\). The first identity uses
\(\int_0^{2\pi}\log|2\cos\vartheta|\,d\vartheta=0\);
the usual sine/cosine doubling identity proves this with integrable
endpoint logarithms. If \(\mu\) is the probability measure
\(K\,dx/q_X\) on the window and the logarithms are integrable, the
corresponding joint Jensen gap is
\[
 \frac{q_X}{2}\log2+
 q_X\left(\frac12\log\mathbb E_\mu|A|^2
                   -\mathbb E_\mu\log|A|\right).
 \tag{6}
\]
The second term is a distinct, amplitude-dependent gap. Formula (6)
does not assert independent uniform phase for the physical field.
At \(A=0\), use the regularization in E04 instead of subtracting
infinite logarithms.

Test the most favorable *phase-only* proposal by defining the diagnostic
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal B_{\rm ph}(X,c)=
 \mathcal B_J(X,c)-\frac{q_X}{2}\log2.}
 \tag{7}
\]
No claim is made that (7) is an actual upper bound for \(J\).
Granting this subtraction only makes the following obstruction stronger
as a decision test: a proof of that constant gap alone would not make
its resulting allowance small enough.

**Theorem.** For every sufficiently large \(A\), there is an \(X\ge A\)
such that
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal B_{\rm ph}(X,1)\ge m_*+\frac1{1000}.}
 \tag{8}
\]
The same conclusion holds if \(M\) in (2) is replaced by any valid
upper bound \(M^\#\ge M\), or if additional nonnegative payments are
added. An additional error tending uniformly to zero cannot restore
eventual every-center success below \(m_*\). This is a statement about
this corrected quadratic allowance, not the actual log or Green value.

## 3. The missing ingredient in the earlier mean comparison

At \(c=1\), put
\[
 P_0(X)=P_{1,0}(X)=\sum_p F_p\left(\frac X2\log p\right),\qquad
 F_p(\theta)=\sum_{k\ge1}
 \frac{p^{-11k/4}}{k(1+p^{-4k})}\cos(k\theta),
\]
\[
 b_p=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{p^{-11k/4}}{k(1+p^{-4k})}.
 \tag{9}
\]
The complete prime-power series is absolutely and uniformly convergent.
Each block has mean zero and lies in \([-b_p,b_p]\).

For a finite prime set, the height mean equals the product Haar mean:
\[
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_T^{2T}
 \exp\!\left(4\sum_{p\le Q}F_p((X/2)\log p)\right)dX
 =\prod_{p\le Q}\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{4F_p(\theta)}d\theta.
 \tag{10}
\]
Here is a direct justification, so no quantitative equidistribution
estimate is imported. The absolutely convergent Fourier series of the
finite-prime expression can be integrated term by term. Any nonconstant
mode has frequency \(\frac12\sum_{p\le Q}j_p\log p\ne0\), since
unique factorization makes \(\prod p^{j_p}=1\) imply every \(j_p=0\).
Its dyadic mean tends to zero. Uniform convergence first permits a
finite Fourier approximation, and then its removal. The exponentials
also have a uniform absolutely convergent Fourier expansion, by the
exponential power series in the absolutely summable Fourier algebra.

For \(Y\in[-b,b]\), convexity bounds \(e^{4Y}\) by the chord joining
its endpoint values. Taking expectations with \(\mathbb EY=0\) gives
\(\mathbb Ee^{4Y}\le\cosh(4b)\le e^{8b^2}\).
The last inequality follows from
\((\log\cosh u)'=\tanh u\le u\) for \(u\ge0\), and evenness.
Apply this to every prime block. Since \(\sum_p b_p<\infty\),
the omitted-prime exponential tail tends uniformly to zero. Thus the
complete height mean exists and satisfies
\[
 \mathfrak E:=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_T^{2T}e^{4P_0(X)}dX
 \le\exp\!\left(8\sum_pb_p^2\right).
 \tag{11}
\]
Unlike a bound using \(\sup|P_0|\), (11) retains the prime oscillations
inside the exponential. It is the quantitative extra input beyond E03.

### Rational constants

Set \(r_p=p^{-11/4}\). For the terms with \(k\ge2\),
\[
 \sum_{k\ge2}\frac{r_p^k}{k(1+p^{-4k})}
 \le\frac{r_p^2}{2(1-r_p)}.
\]
The first coefficients at \(p=2,3\) are less than \(.14,.049\),
respectively, by B02 (B13), checked in E03. The integer inequalities
\(2^{11}>(20/3)^4\), \(3^{11}>20^4\) give
\(r_2<3/20\), \(r_3<1/20\). Hence
\[
 b_2<\frac7{50}+\frac9{680}=\frac{521}{3400}<\frac{77}{500},
 \quad
 b_3<\frac{49}{1000}+\frac1{760}=\frac{239}{4750}<\frac{51}{1000}.
\]
For \(p\ge5\), \(5^{11}>80^4\) gives
\(b_p\le r_p/(1-r_p)<(80/79)p^{-11/4}\). Summing over all integers
at least five only enlarges the sum. Since \(\sqrt5>2\),
\[
 \sum_{p\ge5}b_p^2
 <\left(\frac{80}{79}\right)^2
 \left(5^{-11/2}+\int_5^\infty u^{-11/2}du\right)
 <\left(\frac{80}{79}\right)^2
 \left(\frac1{6250}+\frac1{5625}\right)
 =\frac{2432}{7021125}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \sum_pb_p^2<\frac{1497655573}{56169000000}<\frac{27}{1000},
 \qquad \mathfrak E<e^{27/125}.
 \tag{12}
\]
These are analytical rational inequalities, not floating-point
certificates. A bounded exact-rational scratch check reproduced the
displayed fractions and integer powers; no sampling or numerical campaign
was used to infer (10) or (11).

## 4. Proof of the strengthened obstruction

Suppose (8) fails. Then for every sufficiently large \(X\),
\(\mathcal B_{\rm ph}(X,1)<m_*+1/1000\). Rearranging (2), (7),
and dropping only the nonnegative payments \(T_X+C_*/L_X\), gives
\[
 M(X,1)<2q_X
 \exp\!\left\{\frac{2(m_*+1/1000+P_{1,1/L_X}(X))}{q_X}\right\}.
 \tag{13}
\]
The inherited bounds are \(q_X\to1/2\), \(|P_{1,\lambda}|<23/100\)
and \(P_{1,1/L_X}(X)-P_0(X)=O(1/L_X)\), the latter from B02 (B11).
Therefore the right side of (13) equals
\[
 e^{4(m_*+1/1000)}e^{4P_0(X)}+o(1)
 \tag{14}
\]
uniformly as \(X\to\infty\). This uses a bounded exponent and a
denominator \(q_X\) bounded away from zero; it does not assume any
limit for the oscillating \(P_0(X)\).

Average (13) over \([T,2T]\). The exact [E03 law][e03] is
\(T^{-1}\int_T^{2T}M(X,1)dX\to\zeta(3/2)\). Combining it with
(11)–(14) and the inherited \(m_*<9/50\) yields
\[
 \zeta(3/2)\le e^{4(m_*+1/1000)}\mathfrak E
 <e^{4(9/50+1/1000)+27/125}=e^{47/50}.
 \tag{15}
\]
For \(0\le a<3\), the elementary factorial bound used in E03 gives
\(e^a\le1+a+a^2/[2(1-a/3)]\). At \(a=47/50\),
\[
 e^{47/50}\le\frac{26609}{10300}
 =\frac{13}{5}-\frac{171}{10300}<\frac{13}{5}<\zeta(3/2),
 \tag{16}
\]
a contradiction. This proves (8). Monotonicity of the logarithm proves
the assertion for \(M^\#\) and nonnegative added payments.
The \(c=1\) subfamily alone defeats eventual every-center success on
the full unchanged band. No mean estimate is being presented as a
favorable every-center upper bound.

## 5. Selected acquisition and the first unpaid estimate

E04 retains the entire arm in (4). Its complete real-line comparison
has \(|V-S|\le\epsilon_X\), with the normalized retained correction
\(O(X^{-1/4})\) and the provider residual \(O(X^{-3/4})\), uniformly
through cutoff crossings on the window. For \(\delta>0\), define
\(f_\delta(z)=\tfrac12\log(|z|^2+\delta^2)\).
Its real gradient has norm at most \(1/(2\delta)\), so
\[
 \log|V|\le f_\delta(V)
 \le f_\delta(S)+\frac{\epsilon_X}{2\delta}.
 \tag{17}
\]
For example, \(\delta_X=X^{-1/8}\) makes this field-transfer cost
vanish; the exact exterior still costs \(T_X\). Equation (17) does
not claim that the regularization bias itself vanishes uniformly.

The bounded execution proves the exact phase factorization of
\(f_\delta(S)\), including \(A=0\), and a fixed-prefix phase-averaging
estimate with all constants and the omitted complete field paid.
That task is useful even though it does not prove a signed upper value:
it avoids a real-line floor and cleanly separates acquired error from
unacquired arithmetic information.

The first unpaid mechanism is **uniform control of the complete,
amplitude-dependent logarithmic contribution together with its actual
phase discrepancy, after subtracting the existing prime series**.
A further quadratic-Jensen conversion that uses only the constant in
(6) is excluded by (8). There is no bound here for the amplitude gap
in (6) at the centers where it is needed. Likewise, replacing the
complete arm by a fixed prefix requires an independent, every-center
weighted remainder estimate at the chosen regulator; the E03 dyadic
mean law does not provide it.

This last distinction is substantive. For the full growing arm the
largest frequencies \((\log n)/2\) reach \(L_x/4\), the leading
carrier derivative. In the arm-pair phase
\(2\Theta-\nu\log(mn)\), the leading first derivative is
\((L_x-\log(mn))/2\), which reaches zero at an active cutoff
\(x=4\pi n^2,m=n\). A fixed-prefix fast-phase estimate cannot
therefore be declared uniform through the complete population by
calling its amplitude slow. E03 paid these terms only in a height
average, using a second derivative estimate. This diagnoses the
unpaid uniform estimate; it is not a theorem that every full-arm
phase method fails.
