## 2. The exact arithmetic coupling

Fix \(P\ge2\), independent of height, and define
\[
 \sigma_c=\tfrac12+c/4,\qquad
 \nu_\lambda(x)=x/2+\pi\lambda/8,\qquad
 E_P(x)=\prod_{p\le P}
       \left(1-p^{-\sigma_c+i\nu_\lambda(x)}\right)^{-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]
This is a nonvanishing comparison arm, not a change to the physical
field. Reflect it with the *same* physical phase \(\phi_\lambda=2\Theta\):
\[
 S_P(x)=E_P(x)+e^{i\phi_\lambda(x)}\overline{E_P(x)},\qquad
 R_P(X,c)=\int_{W_X}K(x-X)
       \frac{|V_\lambda(x)-S_P(x)|^2}{|E_P(x)|^2}\,dx.
 \tag{2}
\]
The complete field occurs in \(R_P\), including the retained correction
and the provider residual. No infinite heat-weighted Dirichlet series is
substituted for that field.

For fixed \(P\), the reference has uniform positive lower and upper
modulus bounds and bounded spatial derivatives. Its absolutely
convergent finite-prime logarithm gives the exact full-line identity
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}K(x-X)\log|E_P(x)|\,dx
 =P^{\le P}_{c,\lambda}(X),
 \tag{3}
\]
where the right side retains **all powers** of primes at most \(P\)
in the already established series \(P_{c,\lambda}\). Indeed the
frequency \((\log p^k)/2\) acquires the multiplier
\((p^{2k}+p^{-2k})^{-1}\), giving precisely the E01 coefficient.
This is the concrete coupling absent from a bare polynomial prefix.

For fixed \(\delta>0\), set
\(b_\delta=(\delta+\sqrt{\delta^2+4})/2\). Relative normalization
turns the reference arms into unit-modulus reflected arms. E04's
regularized log inequality, with their actual spatial phase paid, gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 J_\lambda(X)\le{}&P^{\le P}_{c,\lambda}(X)-P_{c,\lambda}(X)
       +q_X\log b_\delta\\
 &+\frac{q_X}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{R_P(X,c)}{q_X\delta^2}\right)
       +T_X+O_{P,\delta}(L_X^{-1})+O_P(X^{-4}).
 \end{split}
 \tag{4}
\]
The first remainder includes the original \(C_*/L_X\) and the paid
reference phase discrepancy; the second is the finite-window reference
log tail. The true log outside \(W_X\) still uses the exact \(T_X\).
All constants and onsets in (4) may depend on the fixed \(P,\delta\).
Formula (4) alone is an acquisition inequality, not a favorable upper
value. E05 supplies the two independent residual tests below.

## 3. A small mean gives a genuine, weaker conclusion

At \(c=1\), the exact residual mean is
\[
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_T^{2T}R_P(X,1)\,dX
 =\mu_P:=\zeta(3/2)\prod_{p\le P}(1-p^{-3/2})-1,
 \qquad \mu_P\downarrow0.
 \tag{5}
\]
The mechanism is coefficient cancellation, not an assumed infinite
Euler approximation at \(\sigma=3/4\). Multiplication of the retained
arm by \(E_P^{-1}\) is a *finite* Möbius polynomial. Its limiting
coefficients are \(n^{-3/4}\) when \(n>1\) is coprime to the product
of primes at most \(P\), and zero otherwise. Their square sum is
\(\mu_P\). The complete two-arm comparison, cutoff endpoints,
reflected cross terms, original fixed clock and kernel mass are paid
in E05 using the [E03 estimates][e03]. The product identifying
\(\mu_P\) is used at the absolutely convergent exponent \(3/2\).

For clarity, (5) has the following actual Green-value consequence:
\[
 \boxed{\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T
   \left|\left\{X\in[T,2T]:
        J_{1/L_X}(X)\ge m_*\right\}\right|=0.}
 \tag{6}
\]
Here \(|\cdot|\) denotes Lebesgue measure of **centers**. This is
neither zero counting nor density at one fixed physical time.
It is stated only on the moving-time \(c=1\) subfamily; no uniform
statement in \(c\) is inferred.

One explicit deduction of (6) keeps the limit order visible. Fix
\(\delta=m_*\). Since \(\log b_\delta=\operatorname{arsinh}(\delta/2)
\le\delta/2\) and \(q_X\le1/2\), the regulator costs at most
\(m_*/4\). Fix \(P\) large enough that the omitted prime subseries
has uniform modulus at most \(m_*/4\). Then take \(X\) beyond the
\(P,\delta\)-dependent onset where all remaining errors total at most
\(m_*/4\). If \(J\ge m_*\), (4) forces its residual payment to be
at least \(m_*/4\). Since \(\log(1+u)\le u\), this implies
\(R_P(X,1)\ge m_*^3/2\). Markov's inequality and (5) give
\[
 \limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac1T
  |\{X\in[T,2T]:J_{1/L_X}(X)\ge m_*\}|
 \le\frac{2\mu_P}{m_*^3}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Only now let the fixed \(P\) tend to infinity. No estimate for a
growing \(P(X)\), no effective height-density rate, and no exclusion
of an individual exceptional center enter this argument.

## 4. Why the same residual cannot pay every center

E05 separately proves
\[
 \boxed{\text{For each fixed }P\text{ and every }B>0,
   \text{ there are arbitrarily large }X
   \text{ with }R_P(X,1)>B.}
 \tag{8}
\]
Thus (5) is not merely insufficient evidence for a finite uniform
residual budget: any such budget is false for this fixed reference.

The proof uses the complete moment \(M(X,1)\), without claiming that
it lower-bounds the true logarithm. E03 extends to every *fixed*
rational-frequency twist. For coprime positive integers \(u,v\),
\[
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_T^{2T}
 e^{iX\log(u/v)/2}M(X,1)\,dX
 =2\zeta(3/2)(uv)^{-3/4}
       \widehat K(\tfrac12\log(u/v)).
 \tag{9}
\]
The resonant pairs give the displayed coefficient. The other pairs
have integer-separated frequencies and the reflected terms retain
E03's second derivative bound. All twist parameters are fixed before
the height limit.

For each fixed larger prime set, (9) determines the moment tested by
nonnegative finite trigonometric weights of its prime phases. A Fejér
weight concentrated near aligned phases selects a large smoothed
finite Euler product. This quantity grows without bound as that
*fixed test set* is subsequently enlarged. Only elementary prime
harmonic growth is needed: the harmonic sum up to \(Q\) is bounded
by \(\prod_{p\le Q}(1-1/p)^{-1}\), and the higher prime powers in
its logarithm have a bounded sum. Hence
\(\sum_{p\le Q}p^{-1}\ge\log\log Q-O(1)\).
For \(p\ge256\), \(p^{-3/4}\ge4/p\). On
\(|t|\le1/\log Q\), the first-prime cosines are at least \(1/2\)
and their higher-harmonic negative part is bounded, so the smoothed
squared product grows at least as a positive constant times
\((\log Q)^2\). This establishes arbitrarily high unbounded values
of the **exact** moment \(M\); it is not a numerical resonance search
or a growing-parameter equidistribution assertion.

Finally, the fixed reference is bounded, say \(|E_P|\le B_P\), and
\(|S_P|\le2B_P\). The weighted triangle inequality gives
\[
 R_P(X,1)\ge B_P^{-2}
       \bigl(\max\{\sqrt{M(X,1)}-2B_P\sqrt{q_X},0\}\bigr)^2.
 \tag{10}
\]
Equations (9)–(10), with the fixed-phase testing argument, prove (8).
In (4), the prime difference, regulator and other terms remain bounded
for fixed \(P,\delta\), whereas the separate nonnegative residual
payment is unbounded. **Stop the fixed-\(P\), fixed-regulator,
relative-quadratic-residual implementation as an every-center method.**
This is not a lower bound on \(J\). It excludes neither adaptive
references, signed nonlinear residual estimates, nor direct treatment
of the full envelope and physical phase together.

E05 §4 also rules out repairing this **same fixed-reference, separate
quadratic payment** by choosing the regulator separately at each center.
Before any phase estimate, its normalized reference integral is at least
\(q_X\log\delta\). Adding the residual payment gives the lower bound
\[
 q_X\log\delta+\frac{q_X}{2}
       \log\left(1+\frac{R_P}{q_X\delta^2}\right)
 \ge\frac{q_X}{2}\log(R_P/q_X),\qquad R_P>0.
 \tag{11}
\]
The restored reference log minus the prime subtraction is bounded for
fixed \(P\). Thus the exact-reference version of this allowance is
unbounded independently of any \(\delta(X)>0\) choice. This statement
does not import the fixed-regulator phase estimate into a varying
regulator regime. The prime set remains fixed throughout.
