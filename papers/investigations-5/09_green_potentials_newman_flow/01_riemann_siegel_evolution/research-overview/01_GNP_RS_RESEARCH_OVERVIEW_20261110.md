## 1. The question and the height requirement

Can the **complete reflected Riemann–Siegel expression under the unchanged
Newman evolution** yield an arithmetic estimate that controls a Green defect,
or a precisely specified collision consumer, at arbitrary height?
The parent owns the common Green theory and its time-zero signed-value
question. This child owns the evolved-saddle comparison and its transfer to
one consumer. The [parent manuscript][manuscript] remains the proof source;
this overview supplies the working dictionary and decision criteria.

The author's 11 November clarification is a requirement: **specific heights
are stepping stones, not the research objective**. A useful estimate must
have quantifiers of the form
\[
 \text{for every }X\ge X_0,\qquad \lambda\in I_X,
 \qquad\text{with explicitly declared }d_X,\ r_X
 \text{ and all constants/dependencies tracked}.
\]
Here \(I_X\) is the chosen Newman-time range; \(d_X\) is a transverse depth
and \(r_X\) a horizontal localization radius. They may be constant or
height-dependent, but their dependence is part of the theorem.
This is the **original B01 specification**; the selected family above
does not turn it into an acquired signed bound.
Evenness and real symmetry reflect the negative heights for the Green
consumer, so the high-height statement can be written with \(X\ge X_0\).
One must cover every center in the declared range, including exceptional
centers; a density-one result or mean estimate needs a separately proved
exceptional-set argument before it can serve this purpose.

An asymptotic result with a proved onset \(X_0\) can be combined with a
separately justified treatment of the finite initial range. Finite
termination for each supplied \(X\), a finite census, and a collection of
successful sample heights do not provide the intended uniform estimate.
Uniformity as \(X\to\infty\), as \(\lambda\downarrow0\), and as \(d_X\downarrow0\)
are distinct obligations. State which are achieved. A fixed-positive-time
result alone does not settle the time-zero problem.

## 2. Fixed source, normalization and coordinate dictionary

Use the complete theta source from [parent §2][source]:
\[
 p(u)=e^u\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}},\qquad
 \Phi(u)=\frac{p''(u)-p(u)}8,\qquad
 H_\lambda(z)=\frac12\int_{\mathbb R}e^{\lambda u^2}\Phi(u)e^{izu}\,du.
\]
The complete \(\Phi\) is positive, even and super-exponentially decaying.
It fixes
\[
 H_0(z)=\frac18\xi(s_-(z)),\quad s_\pm(z)=\frac{1\pm iz}{2},\quad
 \partial_\lambda H_\lambda=-\partial_z^2H_\lambda .
\]
The equality \(\xi(s_-)=\xi(s_+)\) uses the functional equation.
There is no division by \(H_\lambda(0)\). For real \(\lambda\), \(H_\lambda\)
is even and real symmetric. Complete-source bounds justify fixed
derivatives and Gaussian identities; they are not automatically bounds
uniform in a growing derivative or expansion order.

| Symbol/domain | Meaning and conversion |
| --- | --- |
| \(z=x+iy\), center \(X\) | Physical field coordinate and horizontal observation center; \(X\) is twice the absolute classical zeta height on the real boundary. |
| \(s=\sigma+i t_\zeta\) | Classical zeta variable. For \(x>0\), \(s_+(z)=(1-y)/2+ix/2\) gives \(t_\zeta=x/2>0\). The parent's \(s_-\) convention has negative imaginary part; do not substitute its \(t_\zeta\) into a positive-height saddle formula. |
| \(\lambda\) | Newman time, equal to Polymath's heat parameter \(t\); it is not \(t_\zeta\). |
| \(\tau=\lambda_s-\lambda\) | Forward heat clock for a collision interval; \(h(\tau,z)=H_{\lambda_s-\tau}(z)\) satisfies \(h_\tau=h_{zz}\). |
| \(T=-\lambda\) | HDA forward clock when based at time zero. Positive \(T\) is negative Newman time. |
| \(0\le\lambda\le1/2\) | Inherited canonical zero strip \(\lvert y_j\rvert\le1\); the fixed Green functional uses boundaries \(y=0,8\) and center \(y=4\). |

Keep arithmetic mode \(n\), RS truncation order \(K_{\rm RS}\), gamma
fluctuation order and factorial zero-statistic order separate.
The last is owned by the localized-correlation investigation.

## 3. What the classical saddles establish

With the oriented contour from northeast to southwest through \((0,1)\)
and the principal logarithm, put
\[
 \mathscr R(s)=\int_{\mathcal C}
 \frac{w^{-s}e^{\pi i w^2}}{e^{\pi iw}-e^{-\pi iw}}\,dw,\qquad
 \zeta(s)=\mathscr R(s)+\chi(s)\overline{\mathscr R(1-\bar s)},\qquad
 \chi(s)=\pi^{s-1/2}\frac{\Gamma((1-s)/2)}{\Gamma(s/2)}.
\]
The integral and reflected identity are the imported formulas in
[Arias de Reyna, *Basic Results*, v1, Definition 4 and Theorem 5][arias-basic];
exceptional factors are treated by continuation. Multiplication by
\(M(s)=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)/16\) gives the complete field.
Gamma factors and the reflected term are part of that field.

For the phase/amplitude split placing the denominator in the amplitude,
[parent §10][rs] differentiates
\(\varphi_+=\pi i w^2-s\log w\) and
\(\varphi_-=-\pi i w^2+(s-1)\log w\). For \(t_\zeta>0\),
\[
 w_+^2=\frac{t_\zeta-i\sigma}{2\pi},\quad
 w_-^2=\frac{t_\zeta+i(1-\sigma)}{2\pi},\quad
 \varphi_+''(w_+)=4\pi i,\quad \varphi_-''(w_-)=-4\pi i.
\]
Use the near-positive branches. The source's sector and contour conventions
are in [Arias de Reyna, *Asymptotic Expansions*, v1, §§2 and 8][arias-exp].
These are saddles of the chosen phases, not zeros of the complete field.

On \(\sigma=1/2\), conjugacy yields the real Hardy cosine expression,
with its RS remainder retained. It does not assume RH. Off the line a
zero requires cancellation of the **complete** reflected contributions;
it does not force these phase Hessians to vanish. Contour crossings add
residues/finite arithmetic sums. Nondegenerate saddles, their motion, or
one small remainder cannot alone rule out cancellation.

## 4. Evolution and the actual available domains

The source proof in [parent §10][rs] gives, for complex \(z\),
\[
 H_\lambda(z)=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
 e^{-v^2}H_0(z-2i\sqrt\lambda\,v)\,dv\quad(\lambda>0),
\]
\[
 H_{-T}(z)=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
 e^{-v^2}H_0(z+2\sqrt T\,v)\,dv\quad(T>0).
\]
These are convergent complete-source identities, not unrestricted reverse
heat evolution of arbitrary data. In the first, \(s_-\) becomes
\(s_- -\sqrt\lambda v\). Although a scalar mode acquires
\(e^{\lambda(\log n)^2/4}\), the gamma carrier, reflected contribution,
cutoff and remainder also evolve. No convergent infinite weighted
Dirichlet series is asserted.

[Polymath v2, §4 (35)–(41)][polymath] already supplies a heat-evolved
decomposition. Its separate meromorphic terms in (39) require
\(\Re z\ne0\); contour shifts (40)–(41) retain the sign of the imaginary
part of their \(s\)-arguments. Their pointwise convergence is not a uniform
height estimate. Its Theorem 1.3 states the explicit region
\(0<\lambda\le1/2,\ 0\le y\le1,\ x\ge200\).
The corrected formula (69) retains the \(C\) term; dropping it requires
the larger error in (70).
**Those stated constants do not cover the Green center \(y=4\) or upper
boundary \(y=8\).** The paper discusses wider regions with changed
constants; a usable extension or another complete-source estimate must be
paid before this consumer uses them.

| Inherited input | Available regime | Limit on reuse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exact Gaussian evolution, [§10][rs] | All fixed complex \(z\), real positive \(\lambda\) or real positive \(T=-\lambda\), as above | An approximation valid only for bounded \(\sigma\) cannot cover all Gaussian shifts. Bound the exterior separately and control gamma/contour continuation. |
| Time-zero complete RS remainder, [OG Note05 §§3–5][transfer] | Real \(x>0\), \(K_{\rm RS}\ge1\), with exact gamma normalization | This is real-boundary data, not a nonzero-time logarithmic estimate or a bound for jets on a complex disk. |
| HDA recurrence, [Note05 §2 and its Recurrence Note20/Review14 links][transfer] | \(1/L\le T\le2\), \(L=\log(x/(4\pi))\), with onset, order and endpoint conditions | Its strong \(e^{-L^2/4}\) allowance excludes \(T=0\). |
| HDA retained-arithmetic family, [Note05 §2 and Ansatz Notes04/26][transfer] | Separate signed crossover \(\lvert\lambda\rvert L\le8\) and positive band \(4/L\le\lambda\le1\), with original buffers/onsets | This does not extend the recurrence error rate through zero. Source domains and assessment scopes remain attached. |
| Fixed-positive-time real/simple tail, [cluster11][cluster11] and Polymath v2 Theorem 1.5 | Sufficiently large height depending on positive time | Existing baseline, not a new gain here. The onset grows as time tends to zero; no uniform time-zero limit follows. |

an arbitrary-height family and show why its proposed estimate adds to the
existing baseline there.

## 5. Quantitative consumers: what would count

### Green value

For \(0\le\lambda\le1/2\), [parent §5][green] proves
\[
 \begin{split}
 J_\lambda(X)
 &=\int_{\mathbb R}K(x-X)
   \{\log|H_\lambda(x)|+\log|H_\lambda(x+8i)|\}\,dx
   -\log|H_\lambda(X+4i)|\\
 &=\sum_{y_j>0}G(X-x_j,y_j)\ge0,\qquad
 K(t)=\frac1{16\cosh(\pi t/8)},\quad
 G(t,y)=\operatorname{atanh}\frac{\sin(\pi y/8)}{\cosh(\pi t/8)} .
 \end{split}
\]
Zeros have full multiplicity. Real zeros give no Green atom but remain in
the integrable boundary logarithm. Each boundary kernel has mass one half.
The imported single-defect test is
\[
 |x_j-X|\le r,\quad d\le y_j\le1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 J_\lambda(X)\ge m(d,r):=\frac{d\sin(\pi/8)}{\cosh(\pi r/8)} .
\]
A useful exclusion requires an **independently proved upper bound** below
this margin for every center and time in the declared family. A weaker
intermediate estimate can be useful if its quantitative contribution to
that comparison is stated; it is not yet exclusion. In particular,
an absolute \(o(1)\) allowance need not resolve a shrinking \(d_X\):
compare the complete error and retained value with \(m(d_X,r_X)\).

At \(\lambda=0\), [parent §6][prime] supplies the stronger arithmetic
identity
\[
 J_0(X)=\mathcal L(X)-P(X)+G(X,1),\quad
 \mathcal L(X)=\int K(x-X)\log|\zeta(1/2-ix/2)|\,dx,
\]
\[
 P(X)=\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda_{\rm vM}(n)}{\log n}
 \frac{n^{-5/2}}{1+n^{-4}}\cos\!\left(\frac X2\log n\right).
\]
For integer \(Q\ge2\), \(|P-P_Q|\le\beta_Q=2Q^{-3/2}/3\).
The plus pole correction occurs once. The pole-cancelled field
\(F=q(s_-)\zeta(s_-)=D H_0\), where \(q(s)=(s-1)/s\) and
\(D(z)=16\pi^{s_-/2}/[s_-^2\Gamma(s_-/2)]\), has the same boundary
logarithm on the real line.

[Parent §8][assembly] acquires an upper approximation \(U\) with
\[
 0\le U-J_0\le
 \mathcal E_{X,R}(\delta)+2\mathcal T(X,R)+2\beta_Q ,
\]
plus any directed evaluation allowance. These are the compact log error,
exact remote-boundary tail and prime tail. They pay accuracy, **not the
signed value of \(U\)**. For arbitrary height, all choices and errors must
be controlled as functions of \(X,d_X,r_X\), including the retained
arithmetic logarithm at exceptional centers.

At nonzero time the two-boundary \(J_\lambda\) identity survives; the
time-zero Euler subtraction and canonical \(F\)-anchor constants do not
automatically survive. A nonzero-time use must supply quantitative
logarithmic-transfer constants on both boundaries, an acquired lower
modulus at the subtracted center, and remote-boundary tails. The zero strip
alone gives no useful numerical size for that center floor. Gaussian
integration of a field identity does not commute with taking a logarithm.

The universal time-zero statement \(J_0(X)=0\) for every \(X\) is
RH-equivalent. State it once as the target reformulation; finite checks
are not evidence for it. Exclusion at a stated positive depth or a new
uniform error/value estimate must retain its weaker quantifiers.

### Optional collision consumer

The alternative remains the inherited **conditional finite-pair survival**
theorem, [OA §§2 and 4][oa-deflation]. In forward heat time, for
\(h=(z-r_-)(z-r_+)U\), \(q_{\rm gap}=(r_+-r_-)^2\), and \(g=U_z/U\),
\[
 q_{\rm gap}'=-8-4q_{\rm gap}B,\qquad
 B=\int_0^1g_z(\tau,r_-+t(r_+-r_-))\,dt .
\]
An upper force envelope \(B\le\beta\) and initial gap lower bound \(Q_-\)
give the survival requirement
\(Q_- >8\int_0^\Delta e^{4\beta t}\,dt\), together with the theorem's
midpoint-confinement and upper-gap inequalities.

An evolved RS input must acquire the model factor floor \(m_{\rm fac}>e_0\)
and derivative-error/force bounds on the **full virtual-node box throughout
the interval**, including coincident nodes. Alternatively, use the
[OA contour interface][oa-survival] with its complete cluster count,
contour floor and buffer. A floor on the actual trajectory or a terminal
curvature sign is insufficient. Arbitrary-height application additionally
needs a proved family of initial pairs/boxes and controlled height
dependence of every allowance. Local double-event formulas do not cover
all multiplicities or all heights by themselves.

## 6. The inherited RS-to-log gain, and its boundary

[OG Note05][transfer], reused in [parent §10][rs], cancels the gamma scale
exactly on the real boundary. For \(I_v=[v-1/2,v+1/2]\), \(v>1/2\),
\[
 \sup_{I_v}|F-Df_K|\le
 \delta_{v,K}=\frac{2^{7/4}}7\Gamma((K+1)/2)\,1.1^{K+1}
 \left(\frac{v-1/2}{4\pi}\right)^{-K/2-3/4}.
\]
Here \(f_K\) is the complete two-arm approximant defined there, including
its \(C_k\) terms and cutoff changes. The bound comes from the stated
time-zero remainder, not from discarding one saddle.

The canonical logarithmic envelope has prefactor \(O(\log^2v)\) and
exponent \(1/n_v\), where \(n_v\sim\log v/\kappa\) and
\(\kappa=\log(27/26)\). Taking
\(K=2p,\ p=\lceil(4/\kappa)\log\log v\rceil\) yields the inherited
sufficient local error \(O((\log v)^{-2})\). A fixed algebraic field-error
allowance does not make **this envelope** vanish; this is not a lower
bound on the actual error or an exclusion of sharper transfer methods.
Increasing order indefinitely at fixed height is also not convergence:
the successive even-order bound ratio is
\((p+1/2)1.1^2/a_-^2\), \(a_-^2=(v-1/2)/(4\pi)\).

This is an existing arbitrary-height **acquisition** gain worth using.
It leaves the signed main value open and retains about \(\sqrt v\)
arithmetic modes. It supplies neither a zero-free approximant nor a
collision factor floor. The finite E08 pilots and reused census in
[parent §15][evidence] test acquisition in already covered regions;
they do not settle the new uniform question.
