## Governing analytical objectives

1. Derive explicit expressions or well-posed analytical rules for
   \(\Gamma_n^\lambda\) and \(p_n^\lambda(X)\) over all real heights,
   with no upper cutoff. Study the contour and observation separately,
   then their interaction. The overview's graph formula is a starting
   parameterization, not a compulsory family.
2. Acquire canonical estimates for a first strict inward gain, uniformly
   across a declared unbounded height range. Prove all infinite-contour
   end terms or supply a uniform local cover. Name and cover the compact
   region outside an asymptotic theorem.
3. Examine from the outset how the estimates behave near the line:
   derive or test a contraction recurrence, track shrinking-domain
   losses and height onsets, and identify changes of strategy needed
   for iteration. A successful first step alone is not a line limit.
4. Keep the heat-time quantifiers explicit. A height-dependent heat
   schedule is allowed, but a common-time or time-zero conclusion needs
   its own argument. Never vary heat time along one contour integral.

Keep exploration open among graph and conformal contours, uniform local
domains, analytically controlled level curves, prescribed observation
laws, error-balancing rules and adaptive choices from proved bounds.
These examples are not an exhaustive list. An adaptive or implicit rule
needs existence, regularity and coverage arguments; selecting favorable
numerical samples is not an analytical protocol.

## GNP-BB-B01 — original first-step specification, completed

This section preserves the initial bounded task. Its restrictions apply
to B01, not to the revised B02 exploration.

**Scientific question.** Which of two mechanisms can use existing
canonical information to improve a proved enclosure on one specified
height/time domain: normalized-field extension or a complete Green
boundary comparison? The output must identify an attainable next estimate,
not just restate the desired zero-free conclusion.

**Inputs.** Read the overview, the

Read the B10 audit and integrated section if claiming its reuse.
Preserve the complete theta source and Newman clock.

**Decisions to settle.**

1. Fix one height/time domain family: interval in \(X\) and either a time
   slab or a prescribed time curve. Name the initial enclosure theorem,
   parameter range, observation buffer and target gain.
2. Compare at most the two mechanisms above, on compatible domains.
   For field extension compare \(m/(2M)\) with the buffer \(\eta\).
   For Green comparison compare \(\widehat J+E\) with the complete
   target margin \(g_K\). State the full source of each bound.
3. Choose the needed representation and normalization. Pay both RS arms,
   cutoff, correction, field/derivative errors, logarithmic floors and all
   sides/tails. Use a rectangle initially or explicitly pay curved-domain
   approximation. Rouché may check the selected contour using the same data.
4. Identify whether a one-step gain could support iteration or time
   propagation; do not solve either by adding it as a hypothesis. Name
   the relevant controls and exactly which premise each fails.

**Finite limit.** One conceptual pass, at most two mechanisms, one
height/time domain family and one quantitative consumer per mechanism.
Produce one decision note in brainstorm/notes/ and, only if justified,
one revisioned request in research-progress/handoffs/. No numerical field
run, parameter scan, new manuscript or broad literature survey is part
of this task. Targeted primary-source checks needed for the selected
formulas are permitted.

**Completion and evidence standard.** Complete with either:

- a specified analytical route whose inputs, attainable target and
  complete errors support a tractable E01 request; or
- a precise unproved input or a scoped failure of the tested allowance,
  explaining why no executable request is yet justified.

A conditional interface alone is not a new canonical estimate. If a gain
is already proved during the conceptual comparison, record its exact
evidence and scope; do not keep routine follow-through attached to B01.
Existing source results and model checks retain their original status.

**Routine choices and escalation.** Notation, equivalent algebra,
source verification and bounded contour parametrization are routine.
A different field, physical clock, zero target, third mechanism or
larger domain family requires a dated change to this B specification
before dependent work. An assumed log branch in the region being
proved zero-free, an unpaid signed margin, or a missing collision/side
argument is a named unresolved premise, not a permissible simplification.

## Numerical work supports the analytical program

Numerics may test an analytical prediction, diagnose a constant or failed
allowance, or certify a bounded compact region once an analytical theorem
covers the unbounded tail. Each proposed computation must state that
connection, its finite scope and its evidence level before execution.
No finite scan, fitted curve or collection of zero checks substitutes for
an infinite-height estimate, an observation rule valid for all heights,
or a contraction limit. No numerical campaign is scheduled here.

## Common acceptance requirements

Every returned claim specifies height/time/depth domains, all onsets and
constants or their explicit dependencies, boundary/side coverage, and
whether the statement is pointwise, averaged, conditional or uniform.
Keep iteration index and physical time distinct. A local improvement
does not automatically lower the global supremum of the allowed width.
An all-real limit needs a separate coverage and convergence argument.
For a recurrence \(a_{n+1}\le(1-\theta_n)a_n\), a divergent sum of proved
fractions at each fixed height is one sufficient convergence test; the
estimates must remain valid at that height for every step. Uniform
convergence over heights requires stronger uniform bounds. An onset
escaping to infinity or a positive limiting allowance must be recorded
as a barrier for that strategy, not hidden by changing the order of limits.
