\begin{abstract}
We organize the canonical Riemann theta heat flow around three finite
approximation mechanisms: source approximation, frozen Gaussian evolution,
and expansion at a moving gamma saddle. They describe different regions
of the same entire field and admit explicit error accounts. For negative
time, a completed fluctuation hierarchy has error
$O(X^{-p-3/4})$ in a natural gamma norm on fixed compact time families;
controlled order growth gives $e^{-\log^2(X/(4\pi))/4}$ in an outer
envelope norm on $1\le -t\le2$. For positive time, every fixed saddle
order $J$ gives eventual all-height envelope error
$O(t^{-J-1}\log^{-2J-3}t)$, together with spatial jets, direct time
error, and its exact nonzero heat residual. A compact-positive theorem
retains the arithmetic budget $X^{-t\log2/4}$ and gives a second-corrected
field of order $X^{-7}$ near the prescribed regulator switch at time 64.
These field estimates are set beside an existing finite construction
for the full regulated physical tuple at every real time and height,
including actual covariance and all joining defects. Positive polynomial
source compression and finite evaluation make that construction more
explicit, with polynomial additional-accuracy cost at fixed data and
query box. The main remaining task is a coherent, systematically
refinable asymptotic description across all regions: moving endpoints,
strong through-zero matching, corrected physical transfer, useful uniform
cost and sharper constants remain separate obligations. We present the
principal formulas and proof mechanisms, identify supporting proofs, and
state the exact scope of each conclusion.
\end{abstract}
\paragraph{Why ``Book''.}
The name follows the author's reference to Erd\H{o}s's ideal of proofs
from \emph{The Book}: economical formulas, visible mechanisms and errors
that can be understood and improved. It is an editorial and research
aspiration. This manuscript is a synthesis of the present results, with
its unfinished bridges displayed; it does not claim a completed best
possible description or a proof of the Riemann hypothesis.
