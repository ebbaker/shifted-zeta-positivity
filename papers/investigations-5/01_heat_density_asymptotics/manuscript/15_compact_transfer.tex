\section{Compact weak transfer and a local time join}\label{sec:compact-transfer}
This section integrates Note~34 \cite{n34}. Fix the physical regulator
and product kernel of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}. All caps below hold on
a neighborhood of a \emph{compact full convolution buffer}, including
all observation-boundary supports and initial/final time traces. In
particular $e_*:=\min\sqrt q>0$ is a compact-domain premise, not an
all-height lower bound after the switch. Let $H_t=-H_{zz}$ and let $F$
be holomorphic in space and $C^1$ in real time, with
\begin{equation}
 E_F=F_t+F_{zz},\qquad |H-F|\le d_0,\quad
 |H_{zz}-F_{zz}|\le d_2,\quad |E_F|\le\eta_E.
 \label{eq:cj-inputs}
\end{equation}
All needed field derivatives are smooth on that neighborhood. Derivatives
in time are at fixed physical coordinates; the filter is spatial and fixed.

\subsection{Frozen coefficients and elementary comparisons}
Choose caps $\bar d_j\ge d_j$ and $P_j\ge|F_j|+\bar d_j$ for $j=0,2$,
where $F_j=\partial_z^jF$. On the same buffer put
\begin{align}
 m_-&=(|F|-\bar d_0)_+,\quad m_+=|F|+\bar d_0,\notag\\
 a&=\sup_{m_-\le v\le m_+}\frac{v}{q+v^2},\quad
 b_*=(q+m_-^2)^{-1},\quad
 k_*=\sup_{m_-\le v\le m_+}\frac{2qv}{(q+v^2)^2}.
 \label{eq:cj-caps}
\end{align}
For scalar tile bounds take the suprema of these quantities on the full
buffer. They satisfy $a\le(2e_*)^{-1}$, $b_*\le e_*^{-2}$,
$k_*\le e_*^{-1}$. Freeze the caps before refining or splitting the source
errors. With $D_F=|F|^2+q$, define
\begin{align}
 c_F&=w_F(1-w_F),\quad
 V_{\nu,F}=w_F^\nu F_{zz}\overline F/D_F,\quad
 Z_\nu=w_F^\nu E_F\overline F/D_F,\qquad \nu=0,1,2,\notag\\
 s&=2ad_0,\quad \upsilon=k_*d_0,\quad
 v_\nu=ad_2+P_2b_*d_0+\nu P_2a\upsilon,\quad
 z_E=\eta_E/(2e_*).
 \label{eq:cj-elementary}
\end{align}
Here $s,\upsilon,v_\nu,z_E$ are nonnegative allowances, not fields.
They bound respectively $|S_H-S_F|$, $|w_H-w_F|$,
$|V_{\nu,H}-V_{\nu,F}|$ and $|Z_\nu|$; also
$|c_H-c_F|\le\upsilon$.
Indeed the straight segment from $F$ to $H$ has modulus between $m_-$
and $m_+$. The real gradient norms of $\log(q+|u|^2)$ and
$q/(q+|u|^2)$ are $2|u|/(q+|u|^2)$ and
$2q|u|/(q+|u|^2)^2$. The operator derivative norm of
$\overline u/(q+|u|^2)$ is at most $(q+|u|^2)^{-1}$.
Telescope the latter map, $F_{zz}$ and $w_F^\nu$, using
$|w_H^\nu-w_F^\nu|\le\nu|w_H-w_F|$ and the fixed $P_2$ cap.
Finally $c(w)=w(1-w)$ is 1-Lipschitz on $[0,1]$.
No simple-root or nonzero-field assumption is used.

Set $r=\log q$, so $r_y=0$ for the physical regulator, and write
$b=r_t+(r_{xx}+r_x^2)/2$, $c_r=r_{xx}+r_x^2$. Define buffer suprema
\begin{align}
 a_0&=\|r_t\|_\infty,& R_1&=\|\nabla r\|_\infty,&R_2&=\|\Delta r\|_\infty,\notag\\
 T_1&=\|\nabla r_t\|_\infty,&T_2&=\|\Delta r_t\|_\infty,
 &T_h&=\|2\nabla r\cdot\nabla r_t\|_\infty,\notag\\
 b_0&=\|b\|_\infty,&c_0&=\|c_r\|_\infty.
 \label{eq:cj-regulator-caps}
\end{align}
For $K_\alpha=\partial^\alpha K$, with Euclidean vector norms, put
\begin{equation}
 L_\alpha=\|K_\alpha\|_1,\quad
 M_\alpha=\||\nabla K_\alpha|\|_1,\quad
 D_\alpha=\|\Delta K_\alpha\|_1,\quad
 B_\alpha=\|\sqd K_\alpha\|_1.
 \label{eq:cj-kernel-caps}
\end{equation}
Only orders through four are used: $M_\alpha$ can be bounded by the sum
of the two first derivative norms, and $D_\alpha,B_\alpha$ by the sum
of the two pure second derivative norms. These are finite integrals of
the declared polynomial kernel.

For nonnegative arguments define the positive linear maps
\begin{align}
 \mathfrak C_\alpha(u,v)
 &=\frac{2M_\alpha R_1u+L_\alpha(R_2u+R_1^2v)}{4\pi},\notag\\
 \mathfrak P_\alpha(s,u,v)&=D_\alpha s/(4\pi)+\mathfrak C_\alpha(u,v),
 \label{eq:cj-static-maps}\\
 \mathfrak C^t_\alpha(u_t,u,v_t,v)
 &=\frac{1}{4\pi}\bigl[2M_\alpha(R_1u_t+T_1u)\notag\\
 &\hspace{35mm}+L_\alpha(R_2u_t+T_2u+R_1^2v_t+T_hv)\bigr],
 \label{eq:cj-time-map}\\
 \mathfrak J_\alpha&=\frac{M_\alpha v_0+L_\alpha R_1v_1}{2\pi},\notag\\
 \mathfrak D^E_\alpha
 &=\frac{[2D_\alpha+4M_\alpha R_1+2L_\alpha(R_2+3R_1^2)]z_E}{4\pi}.
 \label{eq:cj-current-map}
\end{align}
The direct time allowances, whose subscripts do not differentiate an
error bound, are
\begin{equation}
 s_t=2v_0+a_0\upsilon+2z_E,\quad
 \upsilon_t=a_0\upsilon+2v_1+2z_E,\quad
 c_t^e=a_0\upsilon+2v_1+4v_2+6z_E.
 \label{eq:cj-time-errors}
\end{equation}

For actual covariance choose fixed caps $U_\gamma^*$ for the potential
jets of both fields, $1\le|\gamma|\le3$. One elementary choice is
\begin{equation}
 U_\gamma^*=L_\gamma S_*,\qquad
 S_* =\max\{|\log q_{\min}|,|\log(P_0^2+q_{\max})|\}.
 \label{eq:cj-potential-caps}
\end{equation}
This retains the physical potential offset. Tighter enclosed fixed caps
are permitted. Set $e_\gamma=L_\gamma s$ and
\begin{align}
 N_\alpha&=\sum_{i=x,y}\sum_{\beta\le\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}
 \bigl(e_{\beta+i}U^*_{\alpha-\beta+i}
       +U^*_{\beta+i}e_{\alpha-\beta+i}\bigr),\notag\\
 \mathfrak Q_\alpha&=4L_\alpha v_0+B_\alpha s+L_\alpha c_0\upsilon+N_\alpha,\notag\\
 \mathfrak T_\alpha&=D_\alpha s_t/(4\pi)
       +\mathfrak C^t_\alpha(\upsilon_t,\upsilon,c_t^e,\upsilon).
 \label{eq:cj-covariance-map}
\end{align}
Every potential index here has order at most three for $|\alpha|\le2$.

\begin{theorem}[Complete compact filtered-system transfer]\label{thm:compact-transfer}
Under the full-buffer premises above, the following table bounds the
absolute difference between the $H$ and $F$ observables. All $Q$ and
signed-source terms are computed from their own physical fields.
\begingroup\small
\begin{longtable}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{.40\textwidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{.54\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule Observable difference and spatial order & Allowance\\\midrule\endhead
$\partial^\alpha U,\partial^\alpha W$, $|\alpha|\le4$ &
$L_\alpha s,\ L_\alpha\upsilon$\\
$\partial^\alpha R,\partial^\alpha\overline C$, $|\alpha|\le2$ &
$\mathfrak P_\alpha(s,\upsilon,\upsilon),\ \mathfrak C_\alpha(\upsilon,\upsilon)$\\
$\partial^\alpha J$, $|\alpha|\le3$ internally & $\mathfrak J_\alpha$\\
$\partial^\alpha Q$, $|\alpha|\le2$ & $\mathfrak Q_\alpha$\\
$\partial^\alpha U_t,\partial^\alpha W_t$, $|\alpha|\le2$ &
$L_\alpha s_t,\ L_\alpha\upsilon_t$\\
$\partial^\alpha\overline C_t$, $|\alpha|\le2$ &
$\mathfrak C^t_\alpha(\upsilon_t,\upsilon,c_t^e,\upsilon)$\\
$\partial^\alpha R_t$, $|\alpha|\le2$ & $\mathfrak T_\alpha$\\
$\partial^\alpha\overline{\mathcal B}$, $|\alpha|\le2$ & $L_\alpha b_0\upsilon$\\
$\partial^\alpha\overline{\mathcal S}$, $|\alpha|\le2$ &
$\mathfrak T_\alpha+\mathfrak J_{\alpha+x}+\mathfrak J_{\alpha+y}+\mathfrak D^E_\alpha$\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}\endgroup
For each row $V,\alpha$ the displayed finite substitutions give
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_{V,\alpha}=A_{V,\alpha}d_0+B_{V,\alpha}d_2
                       +C_{V,\alpha}\eta_E,\qquad A,B,C\ge0.
 \label{eq:cj-linear-table}
\end{equation}
Thus coefficients are explicit, rather than unspecified Lipschitz
constants. Laplacians use the sum of the $xx,yy$ allowances; normal
potential/current traces use absolute normal coefficients. Initial/final
time traces are included. Subrectangles are allowed when their whole
supports stay in the declared buffer.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use Lemma~\ref{lem:holomorphic-weak-density} against $K_\alpha$ and
subtract the two fields. The curvature terms cost
$\mathfrak C_\alpha(\upsilon,\upsilon)$, and the potential term costs
$D_\alpha s/(4\pi)$, proving the $R,\overline C$ rows.
Direct differentiation of $D_F$, using $r_y=0$, also gives
\begin{align}
 \pi j_F&=\partial_{\bar z}V_{0,F}+(\partial_{\bar z}r)V_{1,F},\notag\\
 S_{F,x}^2-S_{F,y}^2&=4\Re V_{0,F}-\sqd S_F+c_rw_F.
 \label{eq:cj-spatial-identities}
\end{align}
One integration by parts proves the current bound, since a real test has
$|\partial_{\bar z}\psi|=|\nabla\psi|/2$.
For direct time comparison use $F_t=-F_{zz}+E_F$ to obtain
\begin{align}
 S_{F,t}&=-2\Re V_{0,F}+r_tw_F+2\Re Z_0,\notag\\
 w_{F,t}&=r_tc_F+2\Re V_{1,F}-2\Re Z_1,\notag\\
 c_{F,t}&=r_tc_F(1-2w_F)+2\Re(V_{1,F}-2V_{2,F})
                                  -2\Re(Z_1-2Z_2).
 \label{eq:cj-direct-time}
\end{align}
The derivative of $w(1-w)(1-2w)$ is $1-6w+6w^2$, of modulus at
most one on $[0,1]$. This proves \eqref{eq:cj-time-errors}.
Differentiate the \emph{weak identity itself} in time. The derivatives of
$\nabla r$, $\Delta r$ and $|\nabla r|^2$ give precisely $T_1,T_2,T_h$
in \eqref{eq:cj-time-map}. Hence both curvature derivatives remain paid,
as does $R_t$, without introducing $d_3$.

Convolve the second identity in \eqref{eq:cj-spatial-identities} before
subtracting the actual squared filtered gradients. Its first part costs
$4L_\alpha v_0+B_\alpha s+L_\alpha c_0\upsilon$; Leibniz' rule for
the two products of mean gradients gives $N_\alpha$. This proves the
actual-covariance row, including $\Delta Q$.

For the signed source retain its definition
$\mathcal S_F=(1-2w_F)(r_t\rho_F+\nabla r\cdot\mathbf j_F)$.
The exact balance for a residual-bearing holomorphic field is
\begin{align}
 \rho_{F,t}+\operatorname{div}\mathbf j_F&=\mathcal S_F+D_E,\notag\\
 D_E&=\frac{2q\Re((E_F)_z\overline{F_z})}{\pi D_F^2}
          -4\rho_F\Re(E_F\overline F/D_F).
 \label{eq:cj-point-residual}
\end{align}
Vary the static weak identity at fixed $q$ in direction $\delta F=E_F$.
Since $\delta S_F=2\Re Z_0$, $\delta w_F=-2\Re Z_1$ and
$\delta c_F=-2\Re(Z_1-2Z_2)$, this gives the weak residual formula
\begin{align}
 4\pi\langle D_E,\psi\rangle
 ={}&2\Re\int Z_0\Delta\psi-4\Re\int Z_1\nabla r\cdot\nabla\psi\notag\\
 &-2\Re\int\{Z_1\Delta r+(Z_1-2Z_2)|\nabla r|^2\}\psi.
 \label{eq:cj-weak-residual}
\end{align}
It bounds the filtered residual by $\mathfrak D^E_\alpha$ without an
unpaid $(E_F)_z$. Subtracting the two exact balances proves the last
row of the table, for the already defined signed source.

The internal third current order uses kernel derivatives only through
four. The second orders of $R_t,Q$ also use at most four. The $C^3$
kernel \eqref{eq:kernel} has bounded weak fourth derivatives and no
fourth-order boundary delta. Smooth approximation therefore justifies
all weak pairings. No fifth kernel derivative, pointwise jet-order
reduction, moving-boundary assertion or arbitrary-filter extension is used.
\end{proof}

For an exact finite-source field $E_F=0$, use the separately recorded
interpolation, omitted-mode, complete-tail and nodal errors only at
orders $0,2$ in this route. After freezing caps, one sufficient choice
for positive targets $\Theta_{V,\alpha}$ is
\begin{equation}
 d_0\le\min\left\{\bar d_0,\min_{A>0}\frac{0.4\Theta_{V,\alpha}}{A_{V,\alpha}}\right\},
 \quad
 d_2\le\min\left\{\bar d_2,\min_{B>0}\frac{0.4\Theta_{V,\alpha}}{B_{V,\alpha}}\right\}.
 \label{eq:cj-budget}
\end{equation}
Empty minima impose no constraint. This reserves 80 percent for mapped
source errors and 10 percent each for later evaluation arithmetic and
filtering. Nodal arithmetic is inside the source ledger, separately
from later field evaluation. Other allocations can be declared. Compact
approximability gives existence, not an accepted mesh or manageable cost.
The local route in Section~\ref{sec:finite} remains useful: Note~35
\cite{n35} shows no uniform numerical dominance of weak transfer.
Rounded independent jet samples are not automatically an exact heat field.

\subsection{A holomorphic time blend with its full equations}
\begin{theorem}[Paid compact time join]\label{thm:compact-time-join}
On a common compact full buffer let $F_1,F_2$ have field-error bounds
$h_{i,0},h_{i,2}$ against the \emph{same} $H$, and heat-residual bounds
$\eta_i$. Let $0\le\theta(t)\le1$ be smooth, flat at both endpoints
of an overlap of width $\tau>0$, constant outside it, and satisfy
$|\theta'|\le c_\theta/\tau$. Then
\begin{align}
 F&=(1-\theta)F_1+\theta F_2,\notag\\
 E_F&=(1-\theta)E_1+\theta E_2+\theta'(F_2-F_1),\notag\\
 d_j&\le(1-\theta)h_{1,j}+\theta h_{2,j},\qquad j=0,2,\notag\\
 \eta_E&\le(1-\theta)\eta_1+\theta\eta_2
            +\frac{c_\theta}{\tau}(h_{1,0}+h_{2,0}).
 \label{eq:cj-join}
\end{align}
Theorem~\ref{thm:compact-transfer}, with caps for this whole overlap and
this actual $F$, proves its full observable and trace comparison.
The additional cutoff cost in row $V,\alpha$ is
\begin{equation}
 C_{V,\alpha}\frac{c_\theta}{\tau}(h_{1,0}+h_{2,0}).
 \label{eq:cj-cutoff-cost}
\end{equation}
With the actual $Q_F$ and $Z_0=E_F\overline F/D_F$, the exact equations are
\begin{align}
 (U_F)_t={}&-\tfrac12(\sqd U_F+U_{F,x}^2-U_{F,y}^2+Q_F)
       +\overline{\mathcal B}_F+2\Re(K*Z_0),\notag\\
 \Delta U_F={}&4\pi(R_F+\overline C_F),\label{eq:cj-join-potential}\\
 (R_F)_t={}&-\tfrac12\sqd R_F
       -\tfrac1{8\pi}\Delta(U_{F,x}^2-U_{F,y}^2+Q_F)
       +\tfrac1{4\pi}\Delta\overline{\mathcal B}_F\notag\\
 &-\overline C_{F,t}-\tfrac12\sqd\overline C_F
       +\tfrac1{2\pi}\Delta\Re(K*Z_0),\notag\\
 (R_F)_t+\operatorname{div}J_F={}&\overline{\mathcal S}_F+K*D_E.
 \label{eq:cj-join-density}
\end{align}
The residual corrections have derivative allowances $2L_\alpha z_E$,
$D_\alpha z_E/(2\pi)$ and $\mathfrak D^E_\alpha$, respectively, at the
orders used above; these are separate from the comparison error.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The time-only blend preserves spatial holomorphy and the component
real/even symmetries. Differentiating it gives the exact residual in
\eqref{eq:cj-join}; the triangle inequality through $H$ gives its bounds.
Apply the compact theorem. Convolution of the direct potential identity
and \eqref{eq:cj-point-residual} gives the first and last equations.
Taking the Laplacian of the potential equation, using the static
curvature identity, gives the density equation with both curvature
terms. Formula~\eqref{eq:cj-weak-residual} pays its current defect.
The covariance is computed from $S_F$; it is not the convex average of
the component covariances. No PDE propagation estimate is used.
\end{proof}

Observable overlap alone does not supply the field errors in
\eqref{eq:cj-cutoff-cost}. For negative $G_T$ and positive $P_*$,
the endpoint field comparisons must hold on the whole actual overlap,
with effective constants and component residuals. Triangle inequalities
through $H$ give observable differences but cannot replace that input.
A finer finite source does not remove endpoint field error or the saddle
residual. Increasing $\tau$ changes the domain, caps and regulator floor,
so it is not a free improvement. This is a local time join, not an
all-height splice of a fixed compact source.

\subsection{The spatial compatibility term and its later payment}
For a real spatial cutoff $\lambda(x,y)$ the uncorrected construction
satisfies exactly
\begin{equation}
 \partial_{\bar z}\bigl((1-\lambda)H_f+\lambda P_*\bigr)
       =(\partial_{\bar z}\lambda)(P_*-H_f).
 \label{eq:cj-spatial-defect}
\end{equation}
It generally loses holomorphy on the transition band. The holomorphic
identities above cannot be applied there unchanged. This diagnoses the
uncorrected spatial field cutoff only. Section~\ref{sec:spatial-join}
now supplies a nonholomorphic model with every defect paid on a fixed-gap
short slab; its source and effective outer-field inputs are developed in
Section~\ref{sec:effective-inputs}. A finite atlas of observable charts
alone is still not a globally joined field. The moving construction in Section~\ref{sec:uniform-assembly}
subsequently supplies fixed-width and switch coverage, and
Section~\ref{sec:temporal-cover} pays the negative endpoint attachment.
Section~\ref{sec:effective-positive} pays the positive effective overlap;
Sections~\ref{sec:all-time}--\ref{sec:source-conditioning} complete the
all-time construction and improve its sufficient source cost.
