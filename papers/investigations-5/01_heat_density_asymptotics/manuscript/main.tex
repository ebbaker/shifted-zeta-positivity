\title{Averaged densities for the theta heat flow:\\
explicit asymptotics and finite-time approximation}
\author{Drafted for Edward Baker}
\date{24 October 2026\\[3pt]\small Working draft; same-assistant review only}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
We develop quantitative approximations to the unnormalized regularized
zero density of the canonical theta heat flow. The regulator may vary
with time and height; its potential forcing, curvature correction and
current source are retained exactly. At negative time a Gaussian field
gives an absolute error uniform over the real height axis. At positive
time a complete-source score estimate sharpens the saddle mean and drift.
It yields an explicit density and horizontal current on windows of size
$\kappa^{1/3}$, where $\kappa$ is the source saddle curvature.
Their absolute errors are $O(\kappa^{-1/2})$ for density and
$O(L\kappa^{-7/6})$ for current and the density time derivative.
The formula retains the exact lattice phase through finitely many
periodic Bernoulli polynomials and satisfies continuity exactly.
Growing-block Vandermonde and pair-matrix comparisons follow from the
same root bounds. For compact time intervals we construct a finite
error-function heat field from prescribed source values, with explicit
errors for density, current, time derivative and the actual filtered
covariance. Cellwise source weights and local field amplitudes sharpen
these bounds without excluding multiple events. A smooth global regulator makes the endpoint and finite-time
constructions compatible as measurements. Informative overlap through
the transition, an outer-height positive-time profile and an autonomous
covariance closure remain open. Numerical checks are supporting material;
no earlier-time simplicity conclusion is drawn.
\end{abstract}
