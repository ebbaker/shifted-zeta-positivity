\begin{abstract}
For each prescribed positive scalar accuracy, we construct one finite
source/model approximation to the canonical theta heat flow on every
real heat time and every real height, on a declared transverse strip.
The same physical regulator and fixed spatial filter are used throughout.
The comparison controls unnormalized potential and density, current,
direct time derivatives, regulator forcing and curvature, and the actual
filtered gradient covariance. A Gaussian model supplies the negative
endpoint; an effective implicit gamma saddle supplies the positive
endpoint and the spatial exterior. One nonnegative finite source supplies
the core. Moving spatial and temporal joins retain their exact heat and
nonholomorphic compatibility defects. We prove finite constants for the
positive attachment and the intervening compact interval, so both
infinite-time error limits belong to the same construction.
Positive analytic-source compression now gives a sufficient descriptor
polynomial in the logarithm of source accuracy at fixed upper time.
A free-onset phase selector constructs the literal phase integer; a
cancellation-safe evaluator and continuous-filter ledger control the
complete tuple. For fixed certified descriptor and compact query box,
additional absolute accuracy has a sufficient polynomial bit cost, with
external scalar-provider costs retained. The constants may be enormous;
uniformly affordable all-height computation and executed acceptance
remain open. Sharper regional inputs now include a moving negative-time
endpoint, a high-height positive field atlas reaching zero time, and the
fixed-$P_4$ physical tuple on $[63,65]$ with output-specific absolute
rates. Their companion proofs preserve their separate norms and onsets;
these inputs have not been inserted into a new global join.
The construction supplies neither autonomous covariance
closure nor a collision exclusion or RH conclusion. Numerical diagnostics
are supporting material, with details referred to the research notes.
\end{abstract}
