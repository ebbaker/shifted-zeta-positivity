\section{Scope and exact evolution}\label{sec:framework}
Averaging can suppress the fine peaks near individual zeros while
preserving their spatial density and transport. The central question is
whether this averaged quantity admits an explicit approximation whose
error remains controlled as both time and the observed zero population
grow. A density approximation alone is insufficient for evolution: its
time derivative and current require separate estimates, and the filtered
potential equation contains an actual gradient covariance.

The target is an analytic approximation for every real heat time
$t\in\R$ and every real height $x\in\R$, on each declared bounded
transverse strip with its full buffers. It must retain quantitative
control as $|x|\to\infty$ and $t\to\pm\infty$, under one specified
measurement protocol for the canonical theta heat field. Its averaged density retains
its absolute scale. A useful global formula must compare density, potential,
current and the actual filtered gradient covariance on common support
buffers, retaining the regulator's forcing and curvature. Direct comparison
with the canonical source is the principal route; an autonomous reduced
evolution would require an additional closure and stability argument.

Different formulas cover negative time, a finite central region, and
positive time or large height. Joining them is a compatibility problem.
A time-dependent blend of holomorphic fields preserves spatial
holomorphy but adds a calculable heat residual; a spatial cutoff generally
destroys holomorphy. Alongside compact transfer and a local time join,
we now give a full-support spatial construction in a model zero-free gap,
with all nonholomorphic terms paid on a short slab. The eventual formula
may combine finite Gaussian primitives, an implicitly specified analytic
saddle and explicit smooth cutoffs, with their own observable formulas.
It must not conceal the unknown canonical field or covariance in a
closure. Neither a finite grid nor an expanding spatial window completes
the all-height, all-time target; finite subproblems must serve that target.

The chosen regulator also creates a thin crossover. At finite height the
regulated observables are smooth, but their normalized infinite-height
limit changes at the switch. The exact center and rounding width have
different scales. This motivates a finite-size-scaling comparison and a
future study of connected correlations and response, without identifying
the regulator switch as an intrinsic critical time.

We use $z=x+iy$; height means $x$. The physical density is never divided
by its mean except when stating a scaled limiting profile.

\subsection{Canonical normalization and a fixed regulator}
Define
\begin{align}
 \Phi(u)&=\sum_{n\ge1}
 (2\pi^2n^4e^{9u}-3\pi n^2e^{5u})e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}},\qquad u\ge0,
 \label{eq:phi}\\
 H(t,z)&=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du .
 \label{eq:H}
\end{align}
The source is positive on this half-line, has an even smooth extension,
and decays faster than every Gaussian. Consequently all the source
moments and differentiated transforms used below exist,
\begin{equation}
 H_t=-H_{zz},\qquad
 H(0,z)=\tfrac18\xi(\tfrac12+\tfrac i2z).
 \label{eq:heat}
\end{equation}
For a constant $\epsilon>0$, set
\begin{equation}
 D=|H|^2+\epsilon^2,\quad S=\log D,\quad
 \rh=\frac{\epsilon^2|H_z|^2}{\pi D^2},\quad
 j=\frac{\epsilon^2H_{zz}\overline{H_z}}{\pi D^2},
 \qquad \sqd=\partial_x^2-\partial_y^2.
 \label{eq:definitions}
\end{equation}
The real vector represented by $j$ is $(\Re j,\Im j)$.

\begin{proposition}[Potential and current identities]\label{prop:exact}
For real $t$ and all $z\in\C$,
\begin{align}
 \Delta S&=4\pi\rh, \label{eq:curvature}\\
 S_t&=-\tfrac12(\sqd S+S_x^2-S_y^2), \label{eq:potential}\\
 \partial_t\rh+\partial_x\Re j+\partial_y\Im j&=0. \label{eq:current}
\end{align}
These formulas are smooth at zeros of $H$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Holomorphy gives $\Delta |H|^2=4|H_z|^2$ and
$|\nabla |H|^2|^2=4|H|^2|H_z|^2$, proving
\eqref{eq:curvature}. Also
$\sqd D=4\Re(H_{zz}\overline H)$ and
$D_t=-2\Re(H_{zz}\overline H)$.
Since $\sqd S+S_x^2-S_y^2=\sqd D/D$, this proves
\eqref{eq:potential}. Differentiation of \eqref{eq:definitions},
using $H_t=-H_{zz}$ and the Cauchy--Riemann equations, gives
\eqref{eq:current}. Every denominator is bounded below by a positive
power of $\epsilon$.
\end{proof}

Let $F$ be convolution by a fixed, nonnegative, compactly supported,
normalized space-time kernel. It need not be a projection.
Define
\begin{equation}
 U=FS,\quad R=F\rh,\quad
 Q=F(S_x^2-S_y^2)-(U_x^2-U_y^2).
 \label{eq:filtereddefs}
\end{equation}
Derivatives commute with this fixed filter. Therefore
\begin{align}
 U_t&=-\tfrac12(\sqd U+U_x^2-U_y^2+Q),\qquad \Delta U=4\pi R,
 \label{eq:U}\\
 R_t&=-\tfrac12\sqd R-\frac1{8\pi}\Delta(U_x^2-U_y^2+Q),
 \label{eq:R}\\
 R_t+\partial_x\Re(Fj)+\partial_y\Im(Fj)&=0. \label{eq:Fcurrent}
\end{align}
The exact unresolved quantity is the difference of two gradient
variances, $Q=\Var_F(S_x)-\Var_F(S_y)$.
In particular, it is not a function of the density variance alone.
For example, the exact heat field $H=e^{-t}\cosh z$, at $t=0$,
$\epsilon=1$, averaged over $x=\pm\log2$, $y=0$, has zero density
variance but $Q=900/1681$ \cite{n02}.

A useful differentiated form is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta Q=2\{\Var_F(S_{xx})-\Var_F(S_{yy})\}
 +8\pi\{\Cov_F(S_x,\rho_x)-\Cov_F(S_y,\rho_y)\}.
 \label{eq:lapQ}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\Delta(g^2)=2|\nabla g|^2+2g\Delta g$,
$\Delta S_i=4\pi\rho_i$, and the two $S_{xy}$ variances cancel.

\subsection{A controlled small-width approximation}
Assume independent even kernels with variances $v_t,v_x,v_y$ and
supports scaled by a common small parameter $\delta$.
Taylor expansion of a covariance gives
\begin{align}
 Q_\nabla={}&v_t(U_{xt}^2-U_{yt}^2)
 +v_x(U_{xx}^2-U_{xy}^2)
 +v_y(U_{xy}^2-U_{yy}^2), \label{eq:gradient}\\
 Q-Q_\nabla&=O(\delta^4)\quad\hbox{in spatial }C^2.
 \label{eq:gradienterror}
\end{align}
Here $S$ has bounded $C^7$ norm on the compact filter buffer and
$\epsilon$ is fixed. To see the remainder, expand the two factors in
each covariance to second order; evenness removes odd total degrees,
and the fourth moments control the remainder and its two derivatives.
Replacing derivatives of $S$ by derivatives of $U$ changes the
quadratic terms by $O(\delta^4)$.
For spatial-only isotropic variance $v$ this reduces to
\begin{equation}
 Q_\nabla=v(U_{xx}^2-U_{yy}^2)
          =4\pi v R(U_{xx}-U_{yy}).
 \label{eq:isotropic}
\end{equation}
The bound is a consistency estimate, not a stability theorem for a
closed reduced PDE. Broad filters require different information.
Height-dependent widths are permitted in the static estimates below,
but their derivatives produce commutators if inserted in
\eqref{eq:U}--\eqref{eq:Fcurrent}.

\subsection{A variable regulator and its exact corrections}\label{sec:variable}
Let now $q=\epsilon(t,x,y)^2>0$ be smooth, set $r=\log q$ and
$w=q/(|H|^2+q)$, and retain \eqref{eq:definitions} with $\epsilon^2=q$.
The source exponent $q_t(u)$ introduced later is a different quantity.
The algebraic density $\rho$ remains nonnegative, whereas
$\Delta S/(4\pi)$ need not be. Define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal B_\epsilon&=w\{r_t+\tfrac12(r_{xx}-r_{yy}+r_x^2-r_y^2)\},
 \label{eq:adaptiveB}\\
 4\pi C_\epsilon&=2\operatorname{div}(w\nabla r)-w\Delta r
                     -w(1-w)|\nabla r|^2,\label{eq:adaptiveC}\\
 \mathcal S_\epsilon&=(1-2w)(r_t\rho+\nabla r\cdot\mathbf j).
 \label{eq:adaptiveSource}
\end{align}
\begin{proposition}[Exact adaptive equations]\label{prop:adaptive}
For a fixed positive normalized convolution $F$, put
$\overline C=FC_\epsilon$, $\overline{\mathcal B}=F\mathcal B_\epsilon$
and retain the actual covariance in \eqref{eq:filtereddefs}. Then
\begin{align}
 U_t&=-\tfrac12(\sqd U+U_x^2-U_y^2+Q)+\overline{\mathcal B},
 &\Delta U&=4\pi(R+\overline C),\label{eq:adaptiveU}\\
 R_t&=-\tfrac12\sqd R-\frac1{8\pi}\Delta(U_x^2-U_y^2+Q)
       +\frac1{4\pi}\Delta\overline{\mathcal B}
       -\partial_t\overline C-\tfrac12\sqd\overline C,
 \label{eq:adaptiveR}\\
 R_t+\operatorname{div}(F\mathbf j)&=F\mathcal S_\epsilon.
 \label{eq:adaptiveCurrent}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Writing $f=|H|^2$, direct differentiation gives
$S_t+\tfrac12(\sqd S+S_x^2-S_y^2)=(q_t+\sqd q/2)/(f+q)$
and
\[
 \Delta S-4\pi\rho=\frac{\Delta q}{f+q}
 -\frac{2\nabla f\cdot\nabla q+|\nabla q|^2}{(f+q)^2}.
\]
These are \eqref{eq:adaptiveB}--\eqref{eq:adaptiveC}.
At fixed $q$ the continuity identity holds; the chain rule adds
$q_t\partial_q\rho+\nabla q\cdot\partial_q\mathbf j$, with
$q\partial_q\rho=(1-2w)\rho$ and
$q\partial_q\mathbf j=(1-2w)\mathbf j$.
Convolution proves \eqref{eq:adaptiveU} and \eqref{eq:adaptiveCurrent};
taking the Laplacian of the potential equation proves
\eqref{eq:adaptiveR}, including both derivatives of $\overline C$.
\end{proof}
For time-only regulators $C_\epsilon=0$, but the other two corrections
remain. A constant nonzero heat field has $\rho=0$ and can have
$\Delta S\ne0$ when $q$ varies spatially, so the distinction is necessary.
The differentiated fixed-regulator formula \eqref{eq:lapQ} is used only
in its stated constant-regulator setting. Adaptive results below use
fixed spatial kernels; changing their widths in time or space would
introduce additional filter commutators \cite{n14}.

A weak form useful at zeros follows from the distributional identity
$\Delta\log|H|^2=4\pi\nu$, with multiplicities included in $\nu$.
For a compactly supported real $C^2$ test $\psi$, let
$\Lambda_q=\log(1+q/|H|^2)$. Then
\begin{align}
 4\pi\langle\rho-\nu,\psi\rangle
 ={}&\int\Lambda_q\Delta\psi+2\int w\nabla r\cdot\nabla\psi\notag\\
 &+\int w\{\Delta r+(1-w)|\nabla r|^2\}\psi.
 \label{eq:adaptiveWeak}
\end{align}
This retains the spatial correction without a pointwise bound on
$H_z/H$ near its poles. Neither \eqref{eq:adaptiveR} nor
\eqref{eq:adaptiveCurrent} is an autonomous equation for $R$ alone.

\begin{lemma}[Static weak density identity]\label{lem:holomorphic-weak-density}
Let $F$ be holomorphic on the full support buffer of a real compactly
supported $C^2$ test $\psi$, and let $q>0$ be smooth there. With
$S_F=\log(|F|^2+q)$, $w_F=q/(|F|^2+q)$ and $r=\log q$,
\begin{align}
 4\pi\langle\rho_F,\psi\rangle
 ={}&\int S_F\Delta\psi+2\int w_F\nabla r\cdot\nabla\psi\notag\\
 &+\int\{w_F\Delta r+w_F(1-w_F)|\nabla r|^2\}\psi.
 \label{eq:static-weak-density}
\end{align}
No heat equation is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The spatial calculation proving \eqref{eq:adaptiveC} uses only
holomorphy, so $\Delta S_F=4\pi(\rho_F+C_{\epsilon,F})$.
Pair this identity with $\psi$, move the Laplacian onto the test and
integrate the divergence in $C_{\epsilon,F}$ once. This gives the
formula, including both spatial regulator terms \cite{n28,n30}.
\end{proof}
Thus this lemma applies to the residual-bearing gamma fields below.
The exact adaptive evolution equations apply to $H$ and exact heat
fields. For the full-saddle comparison model,
Proposition~\ref{prop:positive-residual-equations} supplies the retained
residual corrections; the heat-field identities cannot be applied unchanged.
