Here $e_*=t^{3/2}/N_*^2$, $\mathfrak e_{\rm ev}=e_*\mathcal L^2$,
$d_*=t/N_*^3$, $N_*=1+X+t\mathcal L$ and
$\mathcal L=\log(2+t+X)$. The linear gamma model's sufficient restriction
$X\ge e^{4t}$ is not the paper's final spatial limitation. The full saddle
removes it and supplies static and direct evolution comparisons under
their separately stated quantifiers. Both models retain exact nonzero
heat residuals \eqref{eq:linear-heat-residual} and~\eqref{eq:full-saddle-residual}.
Static errors are not differentiated to obtain evolution; the full-saddle
model's observable equations include the additional residual corrections.

The governing objective remains one analytic approximation for every
$t\in\R$ and $x\in\R$ on a declared transverse strip, with explicit,
useful errors for the unnormalized density and its required evolution
observables. A finite special-function representation, specified saddle
branch and explicit smooth joins are allowed; a sampled lookup table,
unspecified covariance or finite window alone does not meet that goal.
The construction must have a finite evaluation prescription at each
point, quantitative full-height bounds throughout time, and controlled
limits as $|x|\to\infty$ and $t\to\pm\infty$. Smooth cutoffs need
not be real analytic, and the joined approximation need not be holomorphic;
``analytic form'' here requires a mathematical representation with proved
errors, not a new global analyticity premise. The regulator-to-zero-count
error is additional when that interpretation is claimed.
