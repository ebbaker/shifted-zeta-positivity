The full-saddle scales are distinct:
$e=t^{3/2}/[1+X+t\log(2+t+X)]^2$ for static comparison,
$e\log^2(2+t+X)$ for evolution, and
$d=t/[1+X+t\log(2+t+X)]^3$ for the heat residual.
A small residual has not been propagated into a field error; field
comparison is proved directly from the source. Similarly, the all-time
statement concerns errors in unnormalized physical observables, not
convergence of the growing density to a finite constant.

The central positive regulator comparison allows multiple events.
Simple-root collars are used only on domains where their field premises
have been proved. The general fixed-source positive-infinite-time
obstruction and the fixed-time off-axis obstruction for the
positive-terminal-value piecewise-linear class retain their original
scopes (Propositions~\ref{prop:fixedsource} and~\ref{prop:cutoff-potential}).
The present construction uses a finite source only where prescribed,
then a noncompact saddle model; those obstructions do not exclude it.
The improved sufficient exponential cost is likewise not a lower bound
on every possible source representation.

The strongest next analytical task is positive source compression for
the same four-jet tolerance \eqref{eq:sc-all-source}. An analytic
polynomial source such as $p_n^2$ is a candidate, but positivity on the
real axis alone gives no complex analytic square-root branch. A complete
argument must first prove a quantitative zero-free neighborhood and norm
bound for the canonical source (or provide an equivalent positive
construction), then pay approximation, coefficients, conversion,
rounding and the complete tail. Any claimed gain is judged against the
finite descriptor bound for the same continuous four-jet consumer.
A polynomial-cost result is not yet established.

Even a shorter source description may be expensive to evaluate.
Complex Gaussian moments, oscillatory cancellation, algebraic clipping
endpoints and nonlinear observables need a working-precision analysis.
After a representation has a proved useful bound, a frozen finite case
can test its separate source, evaluation and continuous-filter budgets.
The existing sampled diagnostics remain supporting evidence. Improving
huge sufficient onsets may later matter more than the asymptotic source
exponent; the finite $b$-dependent formulas identify that tradeoff.

immediate consumers, while inheriting the earlier contour, endpoint and
weak-transfer chain. It does not supply specialist validation. The
