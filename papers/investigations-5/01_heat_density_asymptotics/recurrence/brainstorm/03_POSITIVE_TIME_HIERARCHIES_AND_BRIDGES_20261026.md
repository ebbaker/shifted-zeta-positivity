## 1. Existing unconditional starting point

The parent [Note30][n30] already supplies a full gamma-saddle approximation,
with status **proved in-note; same-assistant review only**. It uses the
complete canonical source, not RH or a hypothesis on the zeros at time zero:

\[
H_t=-H_{zz},\qquad H(0,z)=\xi((1+iz)/2)/8,
\]
\[
H(t,z)=\frac1{\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{\mathbb R}
 e^{-v^2/t}\frac{\xi(s+v)}8\,dv,\qquad s=(1+iz)/2,\quad t>0.
\tag{P1}
\]

P1 is [Polymath v2, equation 35][poly]. The canonical source has sufficient
decay to define the exact field through zero; an asymptotic representation
can change there without a singularity of H.

Put
\[
\mathcal M(w)=\frac{w(w-1)}{16}\pi^{-w/2}\Gamma(w/2),
\qquad \alpha=(\log\mathcal M)'.
\]
For each arm w=s or 1-s, the selected large right-half-plane saddle satisfies
\[
p=w+\frac t2\alpha(p),\qquad B=1-\frac t2\alpha'(p),
\qquad
\mathcal S_t(w)=\frac{\mathcal M(p)e^{-(p-w)^2/t}}{\sqrt B}.
\tag{P2}
\]
The square root is the branch near1. The leading field is
\(P_*=\mathcal S_t(s)+\mathcal S_t(1-s)\).

Writing \(X=|x|\), \(R=2+t+X\), \(\mathcal L=\log R\) and
\(\mathcal N=1+X+t\mathcal L\), Note30 FS7 gives
\[
\frac{|H-P_*|}{|\mathcal S_t(s)|+|\mathcal S_t(1-s)|}
\le C\,\frac{t^{3/2}}{\mathcal N^2}
\tag{P3}
\]
on fixed buffered strips, for t>=32 and R sufficiently large. Thus the
error is \(O(1/(\sqrt t\log^2 t))\) as t tends to infinity, **uniformly
over all real heights**. Constants/onsets are not numerically instantiated.
This is an amplitude-envelope estimate, not division by H at its zeros.
[Note31][n31] supplies the subsequent positive-time evolution comparisons
at its own rates. We do not need to reprove or overstate those results.

The unabsorbed budget, Note30 FS20, matters for a higher hierarchy:
\[
C\left\{
\frac{t^{3/2}}{\mathcal N^2}
+R^{-t\log2/8}
+R^{b_0}e^{-ct\mathcal L^2}
\right\}.
\tag{P4}
\]
The three parts pay local saddle error, omitted arithmetic, and the
remaining contour/middle/tail contributions. A smaller local error alone
does not improve the complete canonical approximation.

## 2. A natural recursive positive-time hierarchy

The leading saddle already incorporates the linear and quadratic
variations of log M. Expand the remaining local exponent around p:
\[
E_p(u)=\log\mathcal M(p+u)-\log\mathcal M(p)
-\alpha(p)u-\frac12\alpha'(p)u^2
=\sum_{r\ge1}\beta_r u^{r+2},
\qquad
\beta_r=\frac{\alpha^{(r+1)}(p)}{(r+2)!}.
\tag{P5}
\]
Introduce a formal grading parameter lambda:
\[
\exp\left(\sum_{r\ge1}\beta_r u^{r+2}\lambda^r\right)
=\sum_{j\ge0}A_j(u)\lambda^j.
\]
Differentiating with respect to lambda gives the finite triangular rule
\[
A_0=1,\qquad
jA_j(u)=\sum_{r=1}^j r\beta_r u^{r+2}A_{j-r}(u).
\tag{P6}
\]
This is the same exponential-coefficient mechanism as the negative-time
recurrence, applied at a moving saddle and with the quadratic term already
resummed. It is ordinary saddle-expansion algebra, not a novelty claim;
[DLMF 2.4(iv)][dlmf] is the relevant general reference.

Each finite polynomial has exact normalized Gaussian moments for Re B>0:
\[
\mu_0=1,\quad\mu_1=0,\quad
\mu_{m+2}=(m+1)\frac{t}{2B}\mu_m,
\qquad
C_j=\sqrt{\frac{B}{\pi t}}\int_{\mathbb R}
e^{-Bu^2/t}A_j(u)\,du.
\tag{P7}
\]
All odd j give zero because A_j has parity j. In particular,
\[
A_1=\beta_1u^3,\qquad
A_2=\beta_2u^4+\frac12\beta_1^2u^6,
\]
\[
\boxed{C_2=
\frac{t^2\alpha'''(p)}{32B^2}
+\frac{5t^3(\alpha''(p))^2}{192B^3}.}
\tag{P8}
\]
The first candidate corrected field is therefore
\[
P_{*,2}(t,z)=
\sum_{w=s,\,1-s}\mathcal S_t(w)\bigl(1+C_2(t,w)\bigr).
\tag{P9}
\]
Higher candidates use C4,C6,... generated by P6--P7.

P5 is a local expansion. P7 integrates only finite polynomials against
a Gaussian; it does not assert that the unexpanded gamma integrand can
be continued across the whole real line without poles or endpoint terms.
The actual contour and its omitted pieces must still be paid.
The parameter lambda grades terms; setting it to1 does not establish
convergence or a uniform truncation theorem. These moving-saddle
truncations also do not inherit the exact zero heat residual of the
frozen negative-time models.

The formal next even terms suggest the size
\[
\mathfrak e_4=
\frac{t^3}{\mathcal N^5}+
\frac{t^4}{\mathcal N^6}+
\frac{t^5}{\mathcal N^7}+
\frac{t^6}{\mathcal N^8}.
\tag{P10}
\]
This is a **candidate remainder scale to prove or reject**, derived from
the degrees in A4 and the bounds on alpha derivatives. No error bound
with this rate has been proved in this discussion. The proof must pay
all of P4 again at the improved scale, with actual derivative losses.

## 3. What understanding large positive time should mean

First establish the hierarchy and its truncation errors for t tending to
infinity. Separate fixed spatial height from a theorem uniform over all
real heights. The existing scale \(\mathcal N\) suggests inspecting
\[
X\ll t\log(2+t+X),\quad
X\asymp t\log(2+t+X),\quad
X\gg t\log(2+t+X).
\]
These are changes in the useful asymptotic balance, not established phase
transitions. The implicit saddle is designed to span them.

For each order, identify which correction dominates, whether the complete
error improves, and how its constants depend on order. Only after fixed
orders are controlled should growing or optimally chosen order be asked
for. The all-order algebra alone does not establish convergent, optimally
truncated or beyond-all-powers accuracy.

At fixed positive t, P4's arithmetic power can eventually be larger than
the improved local saddle allowance. One may then have to retain more
Dirichlet modes and assign mode-specific saddles. This is another reason
to study the genuine large-t limit first, rather than promise arbitrary
fixed-t precision from gamma corrections alone.

RH, reality of every zero at t=0, and an assumed value of the
de Bruijn--Newman threshold are **not inputs**. The argument should derive
its conclusions from the exact source, functional equation, full ordinary
Dirichlet series on its convergence half-plane, and justified contour/gamma
bounds. Any zero conclusion needs its own unconditional argument from
the resulting field estimates; the coefficient recurrence supplies none
by itself.

## 4. Bridges remain a later, distinct stage

The finite frozen arithmetic recurrence is algebraically defined through
zero: substituting T=-t gives
\(Q=d-2t\partial_d\) and the base modes
\(\exp(\lambda_n d-t\lambda_n^2)\).
This does not continue the negative-time canonical completion/error
theorem to positive time.

Near zero the relative arithmetic weights involve tL, with
\(L=\log(X/(4\pi))\). The earlier [signed crossover][cross] already covers
\(|t|L\le8\) at weaker algebraic precision, including zero. Its window is
not a universal critical scale or a completed bridge for the newer strong
recurrence bound. The [moving negative endpoint packet][negative] stops
at T=1/L; the [small-positive slab packet][small] is only for [1/2,1].
Neither is being executed in this discussion.

After the large-t hierarchy is understood, compare models through their
common exact H on actual overlaps, using compatible positive envelopes
and derivative bounds. Term-by-term identification is unnecessary.
Cutoff-based joining adds derivative/residual terms and needs its own
budget. No all-time domain union follows from this plan.

## 5. The confirmed transition near 64

The author confirms that 64 was the remembered positive time. For the
fixed regulator protocol, [Note28 JT26--JT33][n28] has a center defined by
equality of the regulator and the named one-arm carrier amplitude, with
\[
t_c(X)-64=\frac{64}{\log X+c_0+O(1/\log X)}.
\]
Its rounding width is much smaller, of order \(1/(X\log X)\).
The exact implicit center, not just this expansion, is needed to resolve
that width. At finite height the regulated observable is smooth.

As [Note38][n38] explains, the switch point 64 and the limiting density
change depend on the regulator. They do not identify a singularity of H,
a gamma-saddle merger, or an intrinsic RH threshold. The floor32 in P3
is an analytical allowance, not another established transition. Refining
the positive hierarchy could later improve the description of the density
switch, but it is not the acceptance target of the first large-t task.
