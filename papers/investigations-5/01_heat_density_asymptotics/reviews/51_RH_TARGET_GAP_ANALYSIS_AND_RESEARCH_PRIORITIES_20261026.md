**Assessment.** The program has substantially improved the approximation and evaluation inputs available for zero calculations. Its decisive remaining application problem is to acquire arithmetic information that distinguishes a collision from a nearby noncollision, at the required spatial and temporal resolution. The present results do not supply such an estimate uniformly toward a possible endpoint. For RH-conditional simplicity, prioritize the Poisson second-moment target P, with the joint first-jet target J as the direct benchmark. For nearer-term quantitative gains, pursue a materially different microscopic or growing-block Rodgers–Tao consumer, or a complete specified positive Newman-bound certificate. Stronger negative-time approximation is most useful when driven by the vertical-moment target V's explicit error demand.

**The common collision calibration.** The exact local heat polynomial

\[
 h(s,z)=z^2-2s
\]

has a double endpoint at \(s=0\), real simple roots at \(s>0\), and a zero exterior drift. It passes a fixed enclosing-contour floor. It nevertheless has all four prohibited endpoint signatures:

| Quantity | Double-event value |
| --- | --- |
| Joint first jet at the event | \(h(0,0)=h_z(0,0)=0\) |
| Poisson excess at width \(\sqrt s\) | \(\mathscr D=2/3\) |
| Negative-time vertical zero moment | \(Y(s)=4s\) |
| Ordered inverse-square energy | \(E(s)=1/(4s)\) |

These are the conventions of the [simplicity target note](../../02_heat_flow_rh_applications/rh_simple_zeros/notes/02_TARGET_BOUNDS_FOR_RH_TO_SIMPLICITY_20261026.md). Ordered energy is essential: the unordered coefficient is half as large. Excellent exterior comparison, a smooth whole-cluster wall, forward repulsion and later simplicity all coexist with these signatures. More accurate versions of those generic facts cannot supply an endpoint contradiction without new canonical arithmetic information.

**First recommendation: a whole-cluster Poisson second-moment consumer.** This is the closest new application of the density program to RH-conditional simplicity. For a complete finite population \(a_1(t),\ldots,a_n(t)\), including all descendants of every endpoint in the target core, define

\[
 p_{t,y}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{y}{\pi((x-a_i(t))^2+y^2)},\qquad
 \mathscr D(t,y)=2\pi y\int_{\mathbb R}p_{t,y}(x)^2\,dx-n.
\]

The exact identity

\[
 \mathscr D(t,y)=\sum_{i\ne j}\frac{4y^2}{(a_i-a_j)^2+4y^2}
\]

preserves each pair's contribution while removing the known label self term exactly. Under RH, descendants of any multiple endpoint give \(\mathscr D(t,\sqrt t)\ge2/3\). Therefore a completely paid upper bound strictly below \(2/3\) at one allowed positive time suffices for that covered endpoint population. This one-time option is an advantage over first proving an energy theorem uniform all the way to zero. It still needs complete membership, arithmetic control and a justified choice of time.

The next bounded analytical task should be a transfer/acquisition theorem for this actual statistic, using the completed \(F_N\), an enclosing wall, and a complete prepared polynomial \(P\). The field quotient alone contains the exterior unit:

\[
 p_{t,y}(x)=-\pi^{-1}\Im(P_z/P)
 =-\pi^{-1}\Im(H_z/H-U_z/U),\qquad H=UP.
\]

Keep that subtraction and the arithmetic cross terms before estimating the square. The Book's covariance \(Q\) is a different observable. Its fixed-width density theorem does not already estimate this second moment.

Use the existing [HDC coefficient transport](../../../investigations-4/12_height_dependent_heat_contours/notes/12_COUPLED_COEFFICIENT_BACKWARD_TRANSPORT_20261023.md) to avoid dividing by individual root slopes, where its whole-wall assumptions are available. Its finite matrix exponential and correlated source numerator are already established interfaces; rederive neither as a supposed new gain. The unsupplied item is a useful canonical coefficient/source error package and its conversion to P's statistic. The [forest-filter theorem](../../../investigations-4/12_height_dependent_heat_contours/notes/11_FOREST_POLYNOMIAL_AND_GAP_FREE_FILTER_ENCLOSURE_20261023.md) is a comparator, not a universal solution: its complex coefficient quotient can have a nonremovable denominator problem at multiplicity three and above. Any new consumer must retain all multiplicities or state its bounded scope.

The natural scale is \(\tau=tL^2\). Even an equally spaced reference with \(n\) labels has an off-diagonal background \(O(n\tau)\), so a one-event bound asks for \(n\tau\) small in that calibration. An \(n+o(n)\) second-moment asymptotic can hide the entire signal of a single multiple site. The existing finite P3 budget requires, when the reference main term is of order \(n\), an \(L^2\)-density error \(o((ny)^{-1/2})\) to make its cross-error \(o(1)\). Population, tails, wall acquisition and quadrature must all be included.

A useful elementary precision check illustrates why more asymptotic orders should be requested only after the consumer is specified. In a unit-normalized quadratic heat model, compare \(u^2-2\tau\) and \(u^2-2\tau-\varepsilon\), with \(\varepsilon>0\), at width \(\sqrt\tau\). Their excesses differ by

\[
 \frac23-\frac{2\tau}{3\tau+\varepsilon}
 =\frac{2\varepsilon}{3(3\tau+\varepsilon)}.
\]

An \(o(1)\) transfer needs \(\varepsilon=o(\tau)\). With current normalized error \(\varepsilon=O(X^{-3/4})\), the scale requirements \(X^{-3/4}\ll\tau\ll1/n\) are compatible for bounded \(n\). This is only a quadratic conditioning calibration, not a canonical transfer theorem or a choice of time that passes P. Higher multiplicities, small unit coefficients and close but simple pairs require their own payments. In particular the simple control \(z^2-a^2-2t\) has \(\mathscr D=2t/(a^2+3t)\), arbitrarily close to \(2/3\) if \(a^2\ll t\). A universal convenient clock cannot be inferred from simplicity or average spacing.

The first success can be a smaller complete P3 error, a proved multiplicity-aware transfer rate, or a newly covered family. The final canonical inequality remains an aspirational bound; the qualitative fixed-population limit \(\mathscr D(t,\sqrt t)\to0\) is a simplicity reformulation under the stated hypotheses.

**Second recommendation: J as the direct arithmetic benchmark.** Freeze \(A_X=\mathcal E_o(0,X)>0\) and set

\[
 f_X(u)=F_N(0,X+u/L)/A_X,\qquad
 h_X(u)=H(0,X+u/L)/A_X.
\]

The present crossover already supplies

\[
 \|(h_X,h_X')-(f_X,f_X')\|_2\le CX^{-3/4}.
\]

Consequently an arithmetic lower bound \(\|(f_X,f_X')\|_2\ge cX^{-\beta}\), \(\beta<3/4\), would transfer. The proposed benchmark \(|f_X|^2+|f_X'|^2\ge cX^{-1}\) leaves an exponent gap of \(1/4\) in the norm. The missing theorem is the lower bound for the complete oscillatory arithmetic expression. Improving an upper remainder alone does not establish it, and the positive diagonal in the squared mode sum cannot replace its mixed terms.

This is the shortest transfer from current results to simplicity. Its drawback is mathematical strength: a uniform polynomial first-jet margin is not known to follow from qualitative simplicity. No expectation of that rate is established by a finite collection of simple zeros. The J2 reciprocal-jet integral is a possible alternate arithmetic quantity, but the full product of its integral bound with the derivative cap must fit its logarithmic threshold. An integral bound averaged over many height charts does not rule out a sparse exceptional zero.

Any new density, coefficient or transport method should be compared with J on the same input information. A more elaborate representation merits priority if it accesses a better correlated arithmetic estimate, widens the admissible conditioning, or reduces complete acquisition cost.

**Third recommendation: a different Rodgers–Tao family and a coupled exterior bound.** The [canonical two-root pilot](../../02_heat_flow_rh_applications/rodgers_tao/notes/03_CANONICAL_TWO_ROOT_MARGINS_AND_FORCE_BUDGET_20261026.md) changes the practical priority. Its complete source error is below \(6\times10^{-27}\), while the contour method's integrated model-exterior budget is approximately \(1.104\times10^{-5}\). The equally informed direct root-jet method gives a budget below \(3.566\times10^{-8}\), roughly 309 times smaller. These are ratios of certified upper budgets, not runtimes or actual errors. Extra field orders cannot repair the dominant loss on that box.

The useful new regime is one in which internal gaps may be small or poorly resolved, while a whole-block separating wall is still accessible. Reuse the contour method's cancellation of common drift, and estimate the full same-root exterior contribution or an appropriate correlated reference difference before taking absolute values. The favorable transfer \(|B_H-B_F[a]|\le2\kappa E_K\) controls approximation error; it does not bound \(B_F[a]\) sharply and does not itself make \(E_K\) small.

On a fixed microscopic contour, the current field error yields \(\kappa=O(L^2\epsilon_X)\) after an independent normalized contour margin. A step of length \(O(L^{-2})\) then has an \(O(\epsilon_X)\) integrated relative-error coefficient. This is a useful match of scales. At growing block size, contour length, buffer loss and margin deterioration must remain explicit. For the renormalized energy the known error involves

\[
 2\kappa\sum_{i\ne j}|g_{ij}d_{ij}^{-3}-g_{ij}^{-2}|,
\]

not \(2\kappa\widetilde E\). A multiplicative raw-energy estimate also retains the reference baseline. The corrected dyadic boundary and reference-strip payments therefore remain part of the task.

A concrete first deliverable is one complete bound on a growing or microscopic family where the root-slope method loses conditioning, or one fully paid corrected boundary contribution with explicit dependence on the new scale. Stop the comparison if the same-data direct estimate still dominates and no cost/range gain appears. A proof using an assumed internal gap floor cannot then be used to exclude an endpoint collision.

**Fourth recommendation: V as a demand-driven negative-time program.** V asks for a localized transverse zero moment with coefficient strictly below four, or \(o(s)\), at time \(-s\), after removing an auxiliary regulator. The weak-potential identity is a natural use of the parent machinery, but its signed cutoff terms and order of limits are essential. On a microscopic chart, the target note's sufficient transfer condition is

\[
 \delta_X(s)/\eta=o(sL^2),
\]

where \(\eta\) is an auxiliary amplitude regulator, not the existing fixed physical floor. If a hypothetical multiplicity-\(m\) event is resolved along a joint schedule, a sufficient local requirement is

\[
 \delta_X(s)=o\!\left(b(sL^2)^{m/2+1}\right),
\]

with the normalized leading coefficient \(b\ne0\) and its conditioning retained. The current crossover's fixed-height error does not tend to zero merely because \(s\downarrow0\). The strong recurrence estimate stops at \(s\ge1/L\). Both facts identify an actual analytic task: prove a joint small-time/regulator transfer, perhaps using shrinking transverse buffers to reduce the negative heat kernel's \(e^{Y^2/(4s)}\) loss, and acquire the signed canonical moment. Kernel derivatives, completion, multiplicities and source cost must be paid again; changing the buffer alone is no proof.

I rank V behind P/J for this application because it needs both a new favorable arithmetic estimate and the regulator-removal/negative-time bridge. It is a sensible reason to extend the recurrence toward \(s\asymp L^{-2}\), once the required accuracy is calculated. An extension only along \(X\to\infty\), \(s\asymp L^{-2}\) does not automatically prove a fixed-core \(s\downarrow0\) endpoint statement.

**E and the positive-threshold RH question.** The energy targets are calibrated correctly as ordered energy: a multiple endpoint forces \(tE_I(t)\to\sum_a m_a(m_a-1)/8\ge1/4\). Thus a complete bound with limiting upper coefficient below \(1/4\), or an integrated logarithmic coefficient below \(1/4\), would suffice under RH. These are final-strength endpoint requirements. The free double already saturates the sharp internal dissipation identity, so improving its universal dissipation coefficient is not the task. The new input must be canonical arithmetic control of the complete energy or a suitable correlated source expression. The inequality \(\mathscr D\le4tE\) should not be used to lose E's sharper direct threshold.

For RH itself, a hypothetical positive threshold entails a finite multiple event; the location can be arbitrarily high as the threshold tends to zero. The local targets can be centered at that hypothetical time, but the required quantitative hypotheses must be acquired for the shifted canonical source. Proving only the time-zero RH-conditional statements does not do this.

[Rodgers–Tao v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05914v5) uses an all-real negative-time interval ending at the actual zeta slice. Translating the time coordinate loses that arithmetic endpoint. [Polymath Theorem 1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438v2#S1) already gives eventual positive-time real/simple high zeros; their regularity can coexist with a finite earlier collision. These primary inputs confirm the distinction between later regularity and endpoint nonconcentration. A density-one result may miss the one finite event relevant to RH.

For a nearer-term RH-facing numerical milestone, choose a specified \(b>0\) below a documented complete upper-bound baseline. Either pay a complete first-jet cover on the positive slab and its exterior, or use an existing upper-bound criterion with every initial, final/exterior and barrier condition. Improvements in onset, complete margin or total cost then have an objective meaning. The current small-positive field theorem is relevant, but its symbolic onset and lower-margin acquisition must become concrete. One improved isolated block, or several successful slabs, does not prove a global Newman bound or justify passage to \(b=0\).

**Control screen and allocation of effort.** Polynomial/Hermite controls pass the local heat and whole-wall identities and fail the strict J/P/V/E bounds at their events. RH-conditional theta-close controls preserve later all-real/simple evolution and source closeness but fail ordinary Dirichlet arithmetic. Euler-factor controls change canonical coefficients; weighted-square and finite-tail controls lack full reflection/completion and must not be assigned finite all-real thresholds; reflected, smooth and positive-primitive controls lack full theta arithmetic; finite-prime/profile controls retain only their stated finite constraints. No new control certificates were executed. Missing the theta identity is an absent premise, not proof that a control fails every inequality derived from it.

The recommended order is: specify P's complete microscopic transfer and acquire its arithmetic inputs, keep J as the simplest same-information benchmark, use the shared whole-cluster data in a new RT force/boundary comparison, and request stronger negative approximation only against V's displayed demand. A specified complete Newman-bound improvement is the most concrete separate numerical objective. Continuing Summary76's \([63,64]\) cost coverage, adding late-time saddle orders, or sharpening the artificial regulator-switch profile remains worthwhile for the general approximation program, but has lower priority for these endpoint goals until it improves a named application budget.

The contribution is a current source-grounded prioritization, explicit scale/conditioning comparisons, and a bounded next consumer; it is not evidence for a new RH or simplicity claim.
