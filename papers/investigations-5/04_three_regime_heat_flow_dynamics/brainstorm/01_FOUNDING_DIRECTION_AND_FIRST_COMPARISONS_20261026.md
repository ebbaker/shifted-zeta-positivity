## What should be compared first

The fixed-window transverse moment has three distinct qualitative
behaviors: a positive limiting contribution when it sees an off-axis
time-zero zero; a linear response when it sees a multiple real endpoint;
and eventual disappearance on every fixed compact set under simple RH.
These inherited signatures justify studying response laws. They do not
supply an effective distinction on growing windows.

A bulk statistic can lose sparse events, and unresolved close simple
pairs can imitate multiplicity. The first response task therefore couples
the window, time and regulator limits, with explicit dependence on gaps
and multiplicities. This preserves the author's population question as a
substantive possible milestone rather than prematurely demanding that
every multiple zero be excluded.

The linked [Dobner paper][dobner] was already catalogued in 02. This turn
examined additional primary passages and prepared a
[parameter analysis][record]. The useful next question is whether the
full witness budget can be made informative as negative time approaches
zero. The approximation error, seed contour, recurrence distance and
coordinate map must be measured together. This supplies the second
initial research task, alongside response resolution.

There is a crucial direction check. A construction of a high nonreal zero
does not prove that all nonreal zeros are high. Since the three regimes
all permit negative-time nonreal zeros, a witness construction becomes a
discriminator only when combined with another constraint on the same
location or response. The first budget may reveal that missing constraint
more clearly; it is not already a contradiction.
