## Canonical field and parameter contract

Use the unchanged theta source and normalization

\[
H(t,z)=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du,
\qquad H_t=-H_{zz},\qquad H(0,z)=\xi((1+iz)/2)/8.
\tag{E1}
\]

For a threshold contradiction assume \(t_c=\Lambda>0\). At a finite real
multiple zero \(a\), let the exact multiplicity be \(m\ge2\), and put
\(c=\partial_z^mH(t_c,a)/m!\ne0\). The existence of a finite event follows
from the canonical uniform exterior reduction in [Investigation02][threshold];
it is not a claim about every heat flow. Above \(t_c\) the canonical zeros
are real and simple. Local identities may hold under weaker hypotheses;
each statement must name whether it uses a local event or the global
threshold assumption.

| Parameter | Meaning and permitted dependence |
| --- | --- |
| \(t_c,a,m\) | Arbitrary candidate event; do not restrict to double zeros or bounded height without an explicit reduction |
| \(b,B\) | \(0<b<t_c\le B\), with \(\Lambda\le B\) an established anchor; \(b\) is a target parameter, not a new physical time origin |
| \(r,s\) | \(t=t_c+r^2s\), \(z=a+rw\); use \(s\in[1,4]\) for an energy annulus and a fixed bounded scaled spatial region |
| \(\rho,W\) | A fixed isolating neighborhood and real window around the event, with zero-free boundary; carry their acquisition and exterior costs |
| \(N,J\) | Hierarchy order and required derivative order; retain order-dependent constants and domains |
| \(c\) | Nonzero event amplitude; its inverse may enter fixed-event constants, but a uniform lower bound is not assumed |

Keep a small right-time neighborhood of each candidate when using energy,
even at the upper anchor. If a comparison also uses the pre-event slice,
choose \(r\) so that its physical time remains in the declared domain.
No spatial symmetry reduction or practical finite height cutoff is needed
for the initial analytical work. High-height asymptotics remain one part
of a complete argument; finite heights require their own coverage.

## Exact recentering and the theorem still needed

The time change is exact:

\[
G(\tau,z)=H(t_c+\tau,z),\qquad
\Phi_c(u)=e^{t_cu^2}\Phi(u),\qquad
G(\tau,z)=\int_0^\infty e^{\tau u^2}\Phi_c(u)\cos(zu)\,du.
\tag{E2}
\]

It leaves \(t_c\) in the source. At \(\tau=-r^2\), the combined weight is
\(e^{(t_c-r^2)u^2}\Phi(u)\), so negative relative time is not the original
canonical negative-time regime. Spatial translation also retains the
oscillatory factors involving \(a\); it does not produce a fresh positive
cosine source with unchanged arithmetic.

[The fixed-order recurrence][fixed] and [the moving lower endpoint theorem][moving]
provide algebra and complete-error methods to examine. Their original
negative-time domains, arithmetic modes, correction terms and buffers must
not be transferred by relabeling time. In particular, the theorem with
\(T=-t\ge1/\log(X/(4\pi))\) does not cover a fixed-height limit
\(r\downarrow0\) merely because \(r^2\) has been called \(T\).

The intended new theorem would bound a complete canonical approximation in
scaled norms, with an explicit parameter dependence such as

\[
\max_{(s,w)\in K}\max_{0\le j\le J}
\left|\partial_w^j\frac{H(t_c+r^2s,a+rw)-\mathcal H_N(t_c+r^2s,a+rw)}{cr^m}\right|
\le \delta_{N,J}(r;t_c,a,m,\rho,\ldots).
\tag{E3}
\]

The model, the compact scaled set \(K\), time derivatives when needed,
source tails and every omitted term must be declared. Hold the center,
normalization and chosen chart fixed under local differentiation; moving
choices require their own derivative terms. E3 specifies an interface,
not an acquired bound. Fixed-event jet cancellations can be
used as consequences of the contradiction hypothesis, but the resulting
Hermite approximation cannot be counted as an independent upper estimate.

The Gaussian loss \(e^{y^2/(4\epsilon)}\) becomes
\(e^{(\Im w)^2/(4\theta)}\) when \(\epsilon=r^2\theta\) and \(y=r\Im w\).
This is bounded on fixed scaled sets away from \(\theta=0\), as shown in
Note07. Physical buffers and derivatives must scale too. The amplitude
\(|c|r^m\), localized tails and source cancellation still have to be paid.

## Energy signal and exclusion target

For the \(m\) descendants of an isolated event, use **unordered pairs**:

\[
E_{\rm cl}(t_c+u)=\sum_{i<j}(x_i(t_c+u)-x_j(t_c+u))^{-2}.
\]

The inherited Hermite expansion gives

\[
E_{\rm cl}(t_c+u)=\frac{\kappa_m}{u}+O(u^{-1/2}),
\qquad \kappa_m=\frac{m(m-1)}{16},
\tag{E4}
\]

and consequently

\[
I_r:=\int_{r^2}^{4r^2}E_{\rm cl}(t_c+u)\,du
=\kappa_m\log4+O(r).
\tag{E5}
\]

A single event contributes a fixed positive amount on each logarithmic time
annulus. An energy \(E_W\) containing this cluster and additional real-root
pairs is nonnegative and dominates it. Its integrated lower bound is
\(\kappa_m\log(u_0/\epsilon)+O(1)\).

The program seeks an independently derived canonical estimate

\[
\int_\epsilon^{u_0}E_W(t_c+u)\,du
\le [A_N+\eta_N]\log(u_0/\epsilon)+C_N,
\qquad \Delta_N:=\kappa_m-A_N-\eta_N>0.
\tag{E6}
\]

All coefficients retain their event and domain parameters; \(\eta_N\ge0\)
is a complete error allowance. \(C_N\) must be independent of \(\epsilon\)
and an assumed minimum gap. An \(o(\log(1/\epsilon))\) upper bound is another
acceptable outcome. The stronger, multiplicity-independent coefficient
\(A_N+\eta_N<1/8\) suffices for every \(m\), but the program should retain
\(\kappa_m\) rather than require this stronger condition unnecessarily.

E6 is a final-strength exclusion target when asserted at every possible
positive threshold, and a conditional upper-bound specification on a
restricted positive slab. It has not been proved. More accurate evaluation
of the same event-conditioned Hermite profile converges to its coefficient
\(\kappa_m\); that accuracy cannot create the strict saving in E6.

## Exact source route and hierarchy responsibilities

Start with a fixed isolating contour and the factorization \(H=UP\), where
\(P\) is the complete monic cluster polynomial and \(U\) is nonvanishing
locally. Import [complete coefficient transport][coeff] with its actual
wall-source data \(H_{zz}/H\), its full exterior forcing and its wall
floor. It remains regular through internal collisions, but wall acquisition
and source errors are not thereby solved.

The cluster discriminant \(D=\prod_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)^2\) and its logarithmic
variation provide a first energy representation to audit. Keep the exterior
terms from \(U_z/U\). Replacing energy by \(\log D\) without bounding its
endpoint is only a change of representation. A second available consumer
is Note07's scaled transverse moment, whose pre-event limit is
\(2m(m-1)\). Use it only if it provides a genuinely different source estimate
or resolves a specified energy-transfer obstruction.

Separate four obligations: the exact coupled source formula; a complete
hierarchy remainder on its actual domain; transfer to the singular energy
observable; and a signed canonical estimate for the main term. Work on the
last obligation early, rather than postpone it until an extensive hierarchy
has been constructed. Preserve arithmetic cancellations before taking
absolute values, and compare any prospective advantage with a direct
first-jet exclusion using the same information.

For \(r_j=2^{-j}r_0\), errors \(O(r_j^\alpha)\), \(\alpha>0\), have a finite
sum and affect only \(C_N\). A persistent error per annulus affects the
logarithmic coefficient \(\eta_N\). Classify each actual term this way.
Do not assume an absolute field bound already controls inverse gaps.
Choose a finite order for each fixed parameter set first. If the argument
requires \(N\) to grow with resolution, prove the corresponding order
uniformity before summing or taking limits.

## Partial upper bounds and the zero endpoint

An established anchor \(\Lambda\le B\), the finite-event reduction, and
exclusion of every candidate in \(b<t_c\le B\) imply \(\Lambda\le b\).
Finite height verification alone is insufficient without the exterior.
For a fixed positive slab the published exterior onset has shape
\(\exp(C/b)\) in its stated range; this is different from the local complex
Gaussian loss removed by rescaling. See [Polymath Theorem 1.5][poly].

Three quantifier patterns should be distinguished.

1. **Every fixed positive event.** If each possible \((t_c,a,m)\) with
   \(t_c>0\) admits a finite order, a strict saving and a sufficiently small
   resolution, every positive threshold is excluded. Constants may
   deteriorate as \(t_c\downarrow0\). Uniform bounded constants at zero
   are not logically necessary for this route.
2. **Every positive target slab.** A theorem giving \(\Lambda\le b\) for
   every \(b>0\), or for a proved sequence \(b_k\downarrow0\), implies
   \(\Lambda\le0\). Orders and constants may depend on \(b\); all needed
   estimates must hold for the entire quantified family. Several isolated
   successful slabs do not provide this conclusion.
3. **Uniform estimates through zero.** A single controlled joint limit in
   \(b,r,a,N\) would be stronger and may give more useful quantitative
   information. Track which constants actually need such uniformity rather
   than make it a prerequisite for the two preceding logical routes.

The first two statements refine the earlier informal wording that reaching
zero necessarily requires uniform constants. What is indispensable is a
proved scope covering arbitrarily small positive times, with the limit
order justified. The present endpoint objective does not also demand
simplicity of the zeros at \(t=0\).

estimate. The inherited screen below is analytical; it does not rerun
