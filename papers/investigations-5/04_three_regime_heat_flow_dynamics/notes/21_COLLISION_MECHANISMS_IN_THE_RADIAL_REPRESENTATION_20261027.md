## 1. What changing the source coordinates does and does not change

Keep Note20's complete canonical source:

\[
r=e^{2u},\quad \Theta(r)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\pi n^2r^2},
\quad Q=\Delta_3\Theta,\quad
\Phi(u)=\tfrac14r^{5/2}Q(r).
\]

Here r is a source radius, not the small scale used to approach a collision in [Note07][scaling]. Write collision time difference as tau. At an exact double zero (T,a), put c=H_zz(T,a)/2, which is nonzero. Analyticity and H_t=-H_zz give

\[
H(T+\tau,a+y)=c(y^2-2\tau)
 +O(|y|^3+|\tau y|+|\tau|^2).
\tag{1}
\]

The higher-multiplicity leading profiles are the inherited Hermite heat polynomials. An invertible change of source variables cannot change these physical local unfoldings. The bounded exterior correction in [Note10][energy] still leaves the logarithmic energy coefficient unchanged.

But the representation may supply a different global constraint that prevents the local event from being realized by theta. This is the relevant opening. The source

\[
Q_t(r)=e^{(t/4)\log^2r}Q(r)
\tag{2}
\]

is positive for real t, smooth away from zero, and analytic in t through any finite candidate T. A collision does not create a singularity in Q_t. Positive radial integrals of it therefore do not automatically acquire a singular event cost. Any construction dividing by H or by its first-jet norm must disclose that extra degeneration.

## 2. The immediate Sturm-Liouville operator is exact but has a different spectral meaning

On r in [1,infinity), define

\[
k_a(r)=r^{-1/2}\cos\left(\tfrac a2\log r\right),\qquad
f_t(r)=r^2Q_t(r).
\]

Then the unchanged physical observation is

\[
8H(t,a)=\int_1^\infty f_t(r)k_a(r)\,dr.
\tag{3}
\]

The radial harmonic calculation gives the exact eigenfunction equation

\[
Lk_a=\lambda_a k_a,\quad
L=-\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^2\frac{d}{dr}\right),\quad
\lambda_a=\frac{1+a^2}{4},\quad
k_a'(1)+\tfrac12 k_a(1)=0.
\tag{4}
\]

There is a standard self-adjoint realization on L2([1,infinity),dr), specified without ambiguity by the following unitary conjugation. Put x=log r and

\[
(Uf)(x)=e^{x/2}f(e^x).
\]

Direct differentiation and measure conversion yield

\[
ULU^{-1}=-\partial_x^2+\tfrac14,\qquad
(Uk_a)(x)=\cos(ax/2),\qquad (Uk_a)'(0)=0.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus use the Neumann half-line realization on the right to define the operator on the left. It has continuous spectrum [1/4,infinity). For real a, k_a is a generalized eigenfunction, not a square-integrable eigenfunction. In particular, for a nonzero, its square integral has logarithmic divergence in r.

Equations (3)-(5) make H the cosine spectral coefficient of the source vector. In fact

\[
(Uf_t)(x)=4e^{tx^2/4}\Phi(x/2).
\]

The operator in (4) is fixed and independent of t. Its spectrum does not move when zeros of H move. At a physical double zero,

\[
\int_1^\infty f_Tk_a\,dr=0,
\qquad
\int_1^\infty f_T\partial_a k_a\,dr=0.
\tag{6}
\]

These are two source orthogonality conditions. They are not the existence of two eigenfunctions with the same eigenvalue, or an eigenvalue collision. Consequently Sturm eigenvalue simplicity cannot be applied directly to (3). This is a precise limitation of this immediate operator, not a prohibition on another source-dependent construction.

The true t evolution is multiplication by (log r)^2/4 in (2), not the semigroup generated by L. A Sturm zero-counting theorem for ordinary radial diffusion therefore cannot be imported without a separate intertwining theorem and its actual domain.

## 3. What a genuinely different collision proof could look like

A structural argument could become available if canonical source identities produce a boundary-value problem whose characteristic function is a nonvanishing multiple of H, with spectral parameter locally lambda=(1+a^2)/4. Such a construction is currently absent.

The distinction is concrete. Suppose on a regular finite interval [b,c] a real scalar equation has p,w>0,

\[
-(pY')'+qY=\lambda wY,
\]

with a lambda-independent separated condition and fixed normalization at b, and characteristic function F(lambda)=Y(c,lambda). Differentiate in lambda and apply Green's identity. At a zero lambda_0 of F,

\[
p(c)Y'(c,\lambda_0)F'(\lambda_0)
 =\int_b^c w(x)Y(x,\lambda_0)^2\,dx>0.
\tag{7}
\]

The lower boundary form vanishes because its condition is parameter independent; the upper form gives the displayed sign. The eigenfunction is nonzero by its normalization. Thus F' cannot vanish. If an exact local correspondence with H existed near a>0, (7) would exclude a double physical zero there. Singular endpoints or parameter-dependent boundaries require their actual additional boundary terms; (7) is not asserted for them.

This illustrates a different proof mechanism: a collision would violate a positive norm identity. It is not another estimate of the existing inverse-gap energy. However, merely naming an operator, using (4), or defining coefficients from the unknown zeros does not construct it. The source, coefficients, domain and boundary conditions would have to be derived independently from the canonical theta data. A global realization proving the conclusion for every positive t would already settle the desired positive-threshold question and is not a weaker established milestone. No converse or RH equivalence of this proposed realization is asserted.

A second possible mechanism uses the existing real collision index rather than energy. For the physical map (t,a) -> (H,H_a), a double zero has

\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}H_t&H_a\\H_{at}&H_{aa}\end{pmatrix}
 =-H_{aa}^2<0.
\tag{8}
\]

The earlier all-multiplicity theorem gives index -floor(m/2). Thus collisions contribute with the same sign. A source-derived global continuation or boundary-degree theorem could obstruct them without estimating their divergent local energy. The earlier topology and wall-accounting results are reusable for this purpose. But Kelvin symmetry by itself supplies no such boundary degree; reflection at a and -a gives equal negative local indices, not a cancellation. The needed new statement would concern the actual source-to-physical-map deformation with all boundary fluxes retained. Its validity is open, and no generic geometric norm supplies it.

## 4. Control screen

For any even source m(u), set Q_m(r)=4r^(-5/2)m(log(r)/2). It has the same weight-five Kelvin covariance. If m is positive and sufficiently decaying, the positivity, smooth source evolution, physical transform (3), and operator (4) all remain available. Therefore:

- Positive even colliding sources from the control register already pass this basic radial dictionary. They fail the unchanged canonical theta coefficients, not the elementary operator conjugation.
- The smooth positive radial inverse-marginal collision control in the earlier radial work rules out positivity of an ambient radial density as a general no-collision mechanism. That is a different radial construction; it is not identified with Q.
- Weighted-square and finite-tail controls have their documented events, but no all-real heat time; their events cannot be substituted for a finite global threshold.
- Euler-factor threshold controls have changed conductor/source arithmetic. They screen any purported threshold theorem using only their shared source properties.
- The local Hermite unfolding and negative real index are shared by the applicable analytic heat events. They are the facts an independent global obstruction must contradict, not theta-specific obstructions themselves.

No numerical control replay is needed for the exact substitution leading to (3)-(6), and none was run. No claim is made that a listed control satisfies every full theta premise or any unconstructed boundary-value realization.
