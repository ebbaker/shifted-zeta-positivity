## Goal and exact formulation

Use the half-line normalization
\[
H(z)=\int_0^\infty\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du
\]
from the [common conventions](../README.md#common-mathematical-normalization). Define the generalized spectral Laguerre expressions by
\[
|H(x+iy)|^2=\sum_{n\ge0}\Lambda_n[H](x)y^{2n},
\]
or explicitly
\[
\Lambda_n[H](x)=\frac1{(2n)!}\sum_{j=0}^{2n}(-1)^{j+n}
\binom{2n}{j}H^{(j)}(x)H^{(2n-j)}(x).
\]
For this entire function, RH is equivalent to
\[
\Lambda_n[H](x)\ge0\qquad\text{for every }n\ge0\text{ and every real }x.
\]
This classical equivalence is the fixed destination. It is not a new result. The first two nontrivial expressions are
\[
\Lambda_1[H]=H'^2-HH'',\qquad
\Lambda_2[H]=\frac{HH^{(4)}-4H'H^{(3)}+3H''^2}{12}.
\]
The hierarchy differs from repeatedly applying \(\mathcal L[f]=(f')^2-ff''\), and it acts on H rather than on the source \(S(x)=\Phi(\sqrt x)\). The [shared formulation note](../notes/03_RH_FORMULATIONS_AND_RESEARCH_DIRECTION_20261101.md) explains the distinction and why scalar source-concavity iteration is not a general real-zero criterion.

## Correlation formulation and its normalization

Define the associated kernels
\[
K_n(v)=\int_{\mathbb R}u^{2n}\Phi(u+v)\Phi(u-v)\,du.
\]
In the half-line convention above,
\[
\Lambda_n[H](x)=\frac{2^{2n-1}}{(2n)!}
\int_{\mathbb R}K_n(v)\cos(2xv)\,dv.
\]
In particular,
\[
H'(x)^2-H(x)H''(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}K_1(v)\cos(2xv)\,dv.
\]
The complete criterion is equivalently positive definiteness of every K_n. Here positive definiteness means
\[
\sum_{i,j=1}^m\alpha_i\overline{\alpha_j}K_n(v_i-v_j)\ge0
\]
for every finite choice of real points v_i and complex coefficients alpha_i. Pointwise positivity of K_n is not this property. See [Csordas, Theorems2.4 and3.7](https://arxiv.org/html/1309.0055v2). The constants here are derived from the full-line theorem; the printed half-line formula has the normalization inconsistency documented in the [source catalog](../references/sources.json).

The n=0 identity corresponds to H squared and provides a useful normalization check. Global nonnegativity of the n=1 expression would be a meaningful lower-order milestone, but would not by itself prove the complete criterion. A finite frequency interval has its own finite scope; all-order and all-frequency requirements remain explicit.

## What the existing tools contribute

Theta addition, modular and differential identities may constrain products of shifted sources. Exact primitive integration by parts can transfer derivatives while retaining endpoints. Joint algebra and rigorous tails can estimate a complete correlation rather than separately discarding its mixed terms. These tools are useful candidates; none currently supplies the required global sign.

The central issue is the passage from local source relations to an integrated positive-definite object. A finite differential system at each source point does not automatically close after products and integration. A raw cosine kernel also lacks the fixed determinant sign used in CNV's moment argument. The [closure note](../notes/02_CNV_DETERMINANT_TRANSFER_AND_THETA_DIFFERENTIAL_CLOSURE_20261101.md) describes both gaps.

## Questions for the first detailed notes

1. **Which complete expression should be studied first?** Start with a clearly stated member or structured family of the spectral hierarchy, recording whether the sought result is global, restricted in frequency, or uniform in order. Keep the distinction between an intermediate estimate and the RH-equivalent full family.
2. **Can theta identities reveal positive definiteness?** Investigate an exact autocorrelation or Gram representation, a sign-preserving integral transfer, or a decomposition with a rigorously controlled signed correction. State convergence and boundary obligations before asserting a sign.
3. **What survives the two-source integration?** Derive the relations satisfied by shifted theta products and identify any new mixed integrals generated by differentiation. Determine whether there is useful closure or a quantitative inequality, rather than assuming pointwise closure descends to correlations.
4. **How can the remaining range be controlled?** Identify what compact estimates, analytic tails or order-uniform arguments would be needed. A finite positive table is not a global certificate without a theorem covering what it omits.
5. **Which premise uses canonical theta information?** Test proposed sufficient estimates against the relevant controls. Positivity, evenness, rapid decay, source concavity or an ordinary Fourier–Gram identity alone may not distinguish the desired sign.

These are proposed derivation questions, not asserted new criteria or estimates. If an identity only reproduces the original unknown sign, its status remains an identity and the missing inequality is recorded explicitly.
