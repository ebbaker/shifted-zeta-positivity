## 2. Checks of the decisive arguments

### Branch01: actual canonical failure, not an abstract control

Write f=g2, g=g3, and fix a<b<c with
\[
\alpha=f[a,b,c]<0,\qquad \beta=g[a,b,c].
\]
The negative alpha follows from the inherited open interval C<0 and
f''(R(u))=C/(R')^3. Newton column elimination gives, with the stated determinant orientation,
\[
h_4(a,b,c,x)=\alpha g[a,b,c,x]-\beta f[a,b,c,x].
\]
The coordinator independently recovered the leading quotient coefficients
\[
R=16t^2-88t+O(1),\quad
\Phi^{(4)}/\Phi=256t^4-3840t^3+O(t^2),\quad
\Phi^{(6)}/\Phi=4096t^6-116736t^5+O(t^4).
\]
The differences from R² and R³ give
f(x)=x²-16x^(3/2)+O(x) and g(x)=x³-48x^(5/2)+O(x²).
The first derivative of the remainder is paid using the quotient identity and complete-source derivatives through order seven, not by differentiating an unspecified O term. Therefore
\[
\partial_xh_4(a,b,c,x)=24\alpha x^{-3/2}+O(x^{-2})<0
\]
eventually. The fixed quadratic interpolant in f's divided difference has leading coefficient alpha; its contribution remains one order smaller in the derivative. The confluent partial has denominator 48, while the full diagonal derivative has denominator 12, as required by symmetry.

The coordinator replayed the finite P0–P7 checker in memory and compared its output with the saved exact record without rewriting it. There is no identified defect in the scoped proof. A local negative derivative does not determine the complete covariance or determinant.

### Branch02: the complete balance and what it has not proved

The coordinator rederived the cancellation of A and Z only after both were retained. With q=product u_j² and A=E_k h,
\[
Q_{r,k}=\frac{E_k(hq)E_k(h/q)}{A^2},\qquad
B_{r,k}=\frac{(2k+2r)(2k+2r-1)}{2k(2k-1)},\qquad
\frac{D_{r,k}^2}{D_{r,k-1}D_{r,k+1}}=\frac BQ.
\]
The factorial product telescopes separately in its even and odd factors. Given positive neighboring rth and lower-order determinants, Q<B is exactly the next determinant's sign condition; this equivalence is not a new estimate. The stronger proposed bound
\[
Q_{r,k}\le \frac{2k+2r-1}{2k-1}
\]
would give
\[
D_{r+1,k}\ge\frac{r}{k+r}\frac{D_{r,k}^2}{D_{r-1,k}}>0.
\]
Gaussian reciprocal-factorial determinants verify every normalization, but do not establish the theta comparison.

For r=3, positivity of the continuous Mellin interpolant M(s) is justified by extending the accepted signed FKG argument to real nonnegative tilts. It does not use a nonexistent noninteger Toeplitz endpoint identity. Differentiation then gives, with T=log q, m=E_sT and A=E_sh,

\operatorname{Var}_s(T)
+\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_s(h,(T-m)^2)}A
-\left(\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_s(h,T)}A\right)^2. \tag{R1}
\]
The required selected upper comparator is
\[
\kappa_3(s)=\sum_{j=0}^2\sum_{\nu\ge0}
(s+j+\tfrac12+\nu)^{-2}. \tag{R2}

The triangular integration on [k-1,k+1] telescopes to the logarithm of the proposed Gaussian bound. Both the covariance differentiation and this telescoping were checked. The exact two-point relaxation gives Q=16705/576>28 at k=1; the coordinator verified this arithmetic. It disproves an inference from monotone A, positive-moment Z and association alone. It is explicitly not a Toeplitz realization or theta counterexample. The canonical bound (R1)<=(R2) remains unproved.

The rate and a finite sign-free prefactor are acquired; the stationary mean's sign is not. The negative stationary datum -p screens only a generic operator-level coercivity assertion, not the actual determinant datum.

## 4. Assessment of asymptotic integrand positivity

The author's conservation/negative-mass proposal remains mathematically valid as a sufficient route, but this evaluation did **not** obtain an independent theta estimate forcing its target.

1. **Conservation:** both tested diffusions preserve the full determinant integral M=F D, including complete tails and their declared boundaries.
2. **Mixing versus negative-mass removal:** ordinary product heat has an algebraic L¹ comparison; theta-quantile diffusion has an exponential one. Both converge in total negative mass to (-M)_+. The faster rate does not decide whether that limit is zero.
3. **The missing theta input:** for the selected cube heat orbit, one would need a quantitative lower bound on nodal-interface dissipation relative to N(s), with divergent integrated rate, proved from the actual determinant datum rather than an assumed sign of M. Naming theta in the generator does not supply this estimate.
4. **Strictness:** even successful N(s)->0 proves only M>=0. A nonzero mean-zero cosine mode gives negative-mass decay with M=0. A surviving positive-mass lower bound or an independently justified positive-anchor comparison is still needed for strict minors.
5. **Cross-index consistency:** separate fixed-k flows conserve separate unknown means. They do not compare k=1 to the easy k=0 anchor. Such a comparison would require its own theorem.

Thus the new flow is a useful complete analytical benchmark, not a hidden positivity proof. Pure invertible complete-density transport still preserves negative mass exactly. Branch05's source transport changes the determinant and therefore needs a root-transfer argument instead.
