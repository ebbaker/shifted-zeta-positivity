## Reading the result

The Green identity keeps the exact endpoint contribution when the differentiated theta source is replaced by its positive primitive. This lets the paper approximate the source and bound the error in a whole determinant expression while retaining cancellation. The three certified floors are for the products \(Q_{r,k}=D_{r+1,k}D_{r-1,k}\) at \((3,1),(3,4),(4,1)\). At \((4,1)\), the error bound uses less than one fifteenth of the reference value. These examples demonstrate an error estimate in previously known positive territory. Extending the same compact approximation with independent moment bounds eventually gives a negative lower bound at each fixed \(r\ge2\). [Green comparison and certificates](../../manuscript/sections/01_green.tex)

The three references isolate different limitations. With \(N\) fixed reciprocal poles the dual reference determinant vanishes for \(r\ge N,\ k>N\). The endpoint-matched Gaussian has positive minors at every size, but the theta determinant divided by its Gaussian counterpart tends to zero uniformly for \(1\le r\le Mk\) as \(k\to\infty\), for fixed \(M\). The complete pair reference also matches growth up to a bounded factor and gives a positive comparison when \((r+1)\Theta<\pi\). It uses implicit zero data. The paper then constructs a different algebraic example for each \(q\ge26\), with matching endpoints, leading radial zero count, a uniform weighted coefficient error of order \(q^{-2}\), and a negative \((q,q)\) determinant. This last example makes the diagonal a good place to distinguish competing ideas. ## What information did the diagonal example discard?

There is an elementary way to separate a subfamily of the paper's examples from theta. Write \(F(z)=\sum f_jz^j\). If \(F\) is a cosh transform of a positive measure \(\mu\), then \(M_j=(2j)!f_j=\int u^{2j}\,d\mu(u)\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(M_j^2\le M_{j-1}M_{j+1}\) for \(j\ge1\).

Fix \(q\). Before the infinite tail is attached, the paper's polynomial has degree \(m=q+1\), with \(M_m>0\) and \(M_{m+1}=0\). The inequality fails strictly at \(m\). As the tail cutoff \(J\) tends to infinity, the endpoint-adjusted completed products converge coefficientwise to that polynomial. For every sufficiently large \(J\), both this strict failure and the paper's strict negative diagonal therefore persist. The three coefficients in this test even lie inside the offending block's index range \(1,\ldots,2q-1\). Thus one can choose the witnesses to violate a necessary positive-source inequality on the very data that produce the negative sign. The choice is for each fixed \(q\) and gives no effective cutoff. [Construction and order of limits](../../manuscript/sections/03_pairs.tex)

There is also a quick check on how much this distinction buys. Take the positive measure \(\delta_1+2^{-10}\delta_4\). Its coefficients are \(f_j=(1+4^{2j}/1024)/(2j)!\), so \(f_1=65/128\), \(f_2=5/96\), and \(f_3=1/144\). Its diagonal determinant is
\[
 f_2^2-f_1f_3=-5/6144.
\]
It passes every raw-moment positivity test. Its strict sign persists if the two atoms are replaced by sufficiently narrow smooth nonnegative bumps, by continuity of the first three even moments. This example concerns one size and supplies neither the large-\(q\) coefficient comparison nor the zero-count conditions of the paper's theorem. Together the two checks explain why a positive-source representation is informative, and why a proof for theta needs more than invoking that representation.

**For further mathematics**, my first choice is one bounded test on \(r=k=q\). Here the Green determinant has only its full-volume term. Start with the boundary-preserving first-mode reference on the *whole* half-line and add the exact sum of the remaining square modes. Write the resulting coefficient matrix as \(A_q+sH_q\), \(0\le s\le1\). The coefficient of \(s\) in its determinant is \(\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(A_q)H_q)\). Every entry in \(H_q\) comes from the same omitted primitive, with the same weights, so its integral against this single signed cofactor sum can be studied before taking absolute values. This is one place where literal theta arithmetic could change a bound. The identity by itself gives no gain. [First-mode comparison](../../manuscript/sections/01_green.tex) · [Known first-mode limits and conditioning gap](../../notes/07_RGD_B03_R2_EXACT_PAYMENT_OBSTRUCTION_AND_TRUE_TAIL_20261104.md)

I would continue this route only if there is an independently justified positive reference margin on a specified growing diagonal family and a bound, derived from the source, that limits the negative part of this linear response to a stated fraction of that margin below one. The terms with two or more insertions would still need a separate bound before drawing a sign conclusion at \(s=1\). A fixed finite-mode reference already fails global total positivity, so its diagonal sign is a genuine first question. If either input is out of reach, record which one and keep the paper as the outcome. A secondary question would be to construct a positive-source control retaining the paper's growing diagonal, error rate and radial count simultaneously. A construction would show that these source and size conditions together still leave room for a negative determinant.

The proposed direction is exploratory. The portfolio's Euler-factor controls change the arithmetic source, the finite or reweighted square controls change the complete unit-weight sum, and the reflected shape and positive-primitive controls lack that square expansion. Finite-profile controls preserve selected data, atomic controls change the source, and variable-kernel controls change the observation. None of those distinctions supplies the proposed signed bound. For the large-index alternative, the manuscript's contour estimate assumes scalar bounds still to be obtained, while Michałowski reports positivity for \(k\ge10^{18}r^3\). That reported range excludes the growing diagonal and serves here only as context.
