## 3. Frozen finite-mode model, locality and time

Use H_N = span{|0>,...,|N>}, m=N+1 fermion modes, and the ordered states

\[
|J\rangle=a_{j_1}^\dagger\cdots a_{j_r}^\dagger|\Omega\rangle,
\qquad j_1<\cdots<j_r,
\qquad d\Gamma(X)=\sum_{i,j}X_{ij}a_i^\dagger a_j.
\]

The vacuum has energy zero and no-jump amplitude +1. No scalar energy offset, extra attenuation, alternative normalization, or moving occupation basis is allowed. All particle sectors use the **same** one-particle matrices; I,J,r enter state preparation and observation, not the dynamics.

Write alpha_n(v)=n b_n v^(n-1), with the continuous value at zero, and gamma(v)=F'(v)/F(v)=sum_(n>=1) alpha_n(v). Then

\[
\begin{split}
G_N&=-\gamma I+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_n S_N^n,\\
R_N&=2\gamma I-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_n(S_N^n+(S_N^\dagger)^n),\\
H_N^{(1)}&=\frac i2\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_n(S_N^n-(S_N^\dagger)^n),\\
B_N&=\sqrt{R_N},\qquad
L_{\mu,N}=\sum_{j=0}^{N}(B_N)_{\mu j}a_j,\quad 0\le\mu\le N,\\
H_{\mathcal F,N}&=d\Gamma(H_N^{(1)}).
\end{split} \tag{1}
\]

Here H_N^(1) names the Hermitian one-particle matrix, to distinguish it from the mode space. The diagonal gamma uses the **complete F** even when n>N makes S_N^n vanish. Replacing F(v) by a truncated sum changes the instrument. The generator convention is

\[
\mathcal L_N\rho=-i[H_{\mathcal F,N},\rho]
 +\sum_\mu\left(L_{\mu,N}\rho L_{\mu,N}^\dagger
 -\tfrac12\{L_{\mu,N}^\dagger L_{\mu,N},\rho\}\right). \tag{2}
\]

The clock is v, in units with hbar=1. A laboratory schedule v(t) is merely a prescribed increasing reparameterization: H_lab=dot(v) H_F and L_lab=sqrt(dot(v)) L. It supplies no Mellin-time bridge or autonomous semigroup.

**Locality is fixed as unrestricted mode coupling.** H_N^(1) permits hopping across the entire index range; B_N is allowed to be dense, with m monitored loss channels. The source gives |alpha_n(v)| <= n( v/4 )^(n-1)/96, hence a useful tail for the original hopping coefficients. It does not give a finite-range reservoir factor or a uniform exponential localization bound for sqrt(R). The infinite R is gapless. No physical spatial geometry or nearest-neighbor implementation is claimed.

The elementary CAR identity sum L_mu† L_mu=dGamma(R_N) selects dGamma(G_N) as the no-jump generator. ## 4. Environment and the count instrument

For a partition in v with step h, use one **fresh bin of m fermion modes** in its pure vacuum per step. The accumulated environment for M steps has mM modes; the continuum ideal has infinitely many temporal bins. Empty bins are uncorrelated with each other and with the initial system. No bin returns to interact. A finite device can reset/recycle bins, but the reset sink is part of its environment; this is not an exact finite closed reservoir.

Fix environment modes before system modes in the ordered Fock tensor representation. For a retained stream, order bins chronologically before the system; future bins remain in vacuum until their interaction. With bin parity Pi_E, its annihilators b_mu, and system operators represented by Pi_E tensor a_j, the Hermitian, total-number-conserving exchange is

\[
W_N(v)=\sum_\mu\left[
b_\mu^\dagger\Pi_E\otimes L_{\mu,N}(v)
 +\Pi_E b_\mu\otimes L_{\mu,N}(v)^\dagger\right]. \tag{3}
\]

The selected collision unitary is

\[
U_{N,h}(v)=\exp\{-i[h\,I_E\otimes H_{\mathcal F,N}(v)+\sqrt h\,W_N(v)]\}. \tag{4}
\]

The environment-first convention makes the leading one-particle emission block -i sqrt(h) L_mu. In another Jordan–Wigner ordering parity factors must be translated explicitly. System parity can change after a loss; **total** fermion parity is conserved, and the reduced map preserves the set of parity-even density operators.

At each outgoing bin measure its total occupation, retain time and count, and trace out/discard the channel label. This means an **incoherent sum** of channel outcomes, not replacement of the L_mu by their sum. The zero outcome is the unique vacuum projector. Assume unit efficiency, no dark counts and no unmonitored losses. With imperfect monitoring, no click need not mean no loss, and the selected conditional operator would change.

E02 must justify the finite-N expansions and their accumulated errors:

\[
\langle\Omega_E|U_{N,h}(v)|\Omega_E\rangle
 =I+h\,d\Gamma(G_N(v))+O(h^{3/2}),\qquad
\langle\mu|U_{N,h}(v)|\Omega_E\rangle
 =-i\sqrt h L_{\mu,N}(v)+O(h).
\tag{5}
\]

The required convergence is for the reduced map and the count instrument, including the coherent flag of Section 5. Fixed finite h is not asserted to give the exact K_v. E02 first takes the collision limit at **fixed N**. No interchange with an infinite-mode or infinite-particle limit, finite laboratory accuracy, or uniform microscopic resource bound is supplied by this decision.

## 5. Signed readout, phase and probability

For fixed increasing I,J of the same length r, put T=Gamma(K_(v,N)) and p_J=||T|J>||^2. For a Slater input,

\[
p_J=\det[(K_{v,N}^\dagger K_{v,N})_{J,J}],\qquad
p_J\ge(12/13)^{2r}, \tag{6}
\]

using the inherited inverse bound. This is a fixed-r floor, not a positive floor uniform over particle number. The ordinary conditional state is T|J>/sqrt(p_J).

Introduce a neutral qubit, with Q_1=|1><1|, and prepare

\[
|\Psi_{\rm in}\rangle=
(|0\rangle|I\rangle+|1\rangle|J\rangle)/\sqrt2.
\]

The reference arm bypasses the dynamics; the signal arm uses Q_1 tensor H_F,N and Q_1 tensor L_mu,N, with the **same** monitored environment. Both components contain r fermions, so this preparation respects fermion parity even when r is odd. Preparation uses number-preserving controlled mode operations and a calibrated relative phase; its known permutation sign must be compensated. No vacuum-to-odd-particle superposition is required.

The fixed no-count operator on qubit plus system is

\[
M_0=|0\rangle\langle0|\otimes I+|1\rangle\langle1|\otimes T,
\quad q_0=\|M_0\Psi_{\rm in}\|^2=\frac{1+p_J}{2}. \tag{7}
\]

For the Pauli X outcomes after no count, direct expansion gives

\[
P(0,\pm_X)=\frac{1+p_J\pm2\operatorname{Re}A_v(I,J)}4,
\quad
\boxed{A_v(I,J)=q_0\,\mathbb E[X\mid0]
       =P(0,+_X)-P(0,-_X)}. \tag{8}
\]

Theta's A is real in the fixed occupation convention, and for v>0 it is exactly

\[
A_v(I,J)=\frac{v^{\sum J-\sum I}}{F(v)^r}
                 \det[c_{j_b-i_a}]. \tag{9}
\]

The corresponding Y readout checks the imaginary part/phase convention. At v=0 use T=I directly, not ambiguous powers of zero in (9). The vacuum case r=0 gives A=p_J=q_0=1. The coherent-reference survival q_0 differs from the signal survival p_J; forgetting either normalization would change the claim. No-count records leave both arms in the same reservoir vacuum, so they do not reveal the arm. Reservoir/channel phases or an uncalibrated controlled implementation cannot be discarded merely because an unconditioned channel is unchanged.

Equation (8) is a readout identity, not a positivity bound or an efficient sampling claim. Small amplitudes can require many repetitions. Coherent control and ideal counting are explicit resources of this architecture, not consequences of having access to an arbitrary CPTP black box.

## 6. Why this choice admits a common cutoff contract

The key design identity is

\[
\mathcal J_{R_N}(\rho):=\sum_\mu L_{\mu,N}\rho L_{\mu,N}^\dagger
 =\sum_{j,k=0}^{N}(R_N)_{kj}a_j\rho a_k^\dagger,
\quad
\mathcal L_N\rho=d\Gamma(G_N)\rho+\rho d\Gamma(G_N)^\dagger
                         +\mathcal J_{R_N}(\rho). \tag{10}
\]

For M>=N and an operator supported on F(H_N), annihilation by an index above N vanishes. Also G_M leaves H_N invariant and restricts to G_N, while the corresponding block of R_M is R_N. Thus the right side of (10), embedded into F(H_M), equals the N-mode expression. This is algebraic compatibility of the **generator and channel-discarded count instrument**. It uses the directed no-jump generator; H alone need not leave H_N invariant.

This avoids, rather than assumes, the false square-root identity sqrt(R_N)=P_N sqrt(R) P_N. Individual channel-resolved jumps at different N are not identified. Only their summed CP jump map, the count record, and the no-jump operator are fixed as compatible objects. A change to channel-resolved monitoring would require another contract.

E02 must prove the consequences, not simply label (10) an infinite-mode theorem:

1. Exact restriction of the finite propagators and count instruments by uniqueness of their bounded equations.
2. A unique trace-norm continuous CPTP evolution on the vacuum Fock representation over ell^2, obtained from their consistent action on the dense union of finite-mode trace classes. This is not an infinite-density thermodynamic representation.
3. A common differentiability domain: the union of trace classes supported in sectors with at most r particles, r finite. On each such space the generator should obey the cutoff-independent bound
   \[
   \|\mathcal L(v)\|_{1\to1}\le 2r\|G(v)\|+r\|R(v)\|
      \le8r A_*,\qquad A_*=[96(1-1/100)^2]^{-1}.
   \]
   Do not call this the full generator domain or a graph core without a proof.
4. For the finite-mode Fock projection Pi_N and subnormalized rho_N=Pi_N rho Pi_N, derive, under the natural embedding,
   \[
   \|\mathcal T(b,a)\rho-\mathcal T_N(b,a)\rho_N\|_1
       \le\|\rho-\rho_N\|_1\longrightarrow0.
   \]
   This would be uniform in times on the stated interval for each fixed input. It is not operator-norm convergence uniformly over all inputs or a rate without an input tail bound.

The full-Fock no-jump operator is to be Gamma(V)=direct-sum_r wedge^r V. Its derivative need not be bounded on full Fock space, nor its inverse bounded uniformly in r. State continuity, particle caps and differentiability separately. E02's extension is a mathematical instrument theorem; no microscopic infinite reservoir limit is being smuggled through the cutoff argument.
