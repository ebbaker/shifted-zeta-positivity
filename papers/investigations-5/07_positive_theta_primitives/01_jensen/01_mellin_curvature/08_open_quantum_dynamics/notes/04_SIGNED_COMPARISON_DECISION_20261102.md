## 3. Explicit cone obstruction in the same three-particle sector

Let C_{r,N} be the nonnegative real span of the increasing occupation basis in wedge^r h_N. The tested mechanism requires
\[
\bigwedge^r V_N(b,a)\,C_{r,N}\subset C_{r,N}
\quad\hbox{for every intermediate }a\le b
\tag{5}
\]
in the fixed basis. In particular the derivative at b=a must have nonnegative off-diagonal entries. Indeed U(a+h,a)=I+hD_r(a)+o(h), where D_r=dGamma_r(G); an off-diagonal entry starts at zero.

Fix n>=2 and N>=n+2. A hop n->0 that passes no occupied site and one that passes one occupied site give the exact entries
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle0,n+1,n+2|D_3(v)|n,n+1,n+2\rangle&=+\alpha_n(v),\\
\langle0,1,n+2|D_3(v)|1,n,n+2\rangle&=-\alpha_n(v).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Each pair differs in exactly one occupied index, so only a_0^dagger a_n contributes; no other hop or diagonal term can cancel it. Removing the common last spectator gives the same two signs in the two-particle sector, at N>=n+1. This is a CAR sign, not a stochastic negative probability.

Consequently (5), even for r=3 alone and all cutoffs, forces alpha_n(v)=0 for every n>=2 at any positive interior v. Equation (1) then forces b_n=0 for every n>=2, hence by analyticity
\[
F(w)=c_0e^{b_1w}.
\tag{7}
\]
Conversely, this Gaussian generating function has G=b_1(S-I). Its only off-diagonal allowed fermion hops move to an adjacent empty site and carry positive sign. The finite-sector generators have nonnegative off-diagonal entries; their exponentials preserve C_{r,N}. Thus within this analytic Toeplitz family the every-intermediate-interval cone mechanism forces precisely the Gaussian case (with b_1>0 here).

This is the directed-family specialization of the classical tridiagonal differential-system obstruction. [Margaliot–Sontag, Section 3, Theorems 6–7 and Lemma 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.09590v2) were checked for attribution and hypotheses; [the source record](../references/02_MC08_B02_CONE_SOURCE_20261102.md) records the scope. ## 4. Why the unchanged complete theta source fails (7)

\[

\tag{8}
\]
For the last constant use e^{-(pi/2)j^2}<2^{-j}, sum j^4 2^{-j}=150, and pi^2<10. This bounds the **whole** mode sum and the entire integration tail.

For every A>0 the constant
\[
C_A=3000\exp\!\left(\sup_{u\ge0}\{9u+A u^2-\tfrac32e^{4u}\}\right)<\infty
\]
therefore satisfies Phi(u)<=C_A e^{-A u^2}. Tonelli and the Gaussian integral, for real w>=0, yield
\[
F(w)=\int_0^\infty\Phi(u)\cosh(\sqrt w\,u)\,du
\le\frac{C_A\sqrt\pi}{2\sqrt A}e^{w/(4A)}.
\tag{9}
\]

\[
\lim_{w\to+\infty}\frac{\log F(w)}{w}=0.
\tag{10}

Equation (7) instead has limit b_1>1/1000. Hence the actual theta source is not Gaussian and has at least one nonzero b_n with n>=2.
