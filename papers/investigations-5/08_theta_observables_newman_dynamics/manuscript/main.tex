\begin{abstract}
We collect the analytical foundation shared by two investigations of theta
observables and de Bruijn--Newman dynamics on the complete spherical cylinder.
The squared geometric Dirac operator supplies a continuous radial spectrum
and an angular Green weight that cancels the sphere's spinor multiplicities.
An explicitly prescribed angular-damping scan then recovers the canonical
theta source. We give its complete-source bounds, the Newman Fourier
dictionary, the scalar generating-field comparison, and the domains needed
for normalized source states. Two existing conditional constructions are
presented with proofs: a complete count instrument whose no-count branch
realizes decreasing Newman time through a transported quadrature, and a
left-acting proper-time response whose observation is the Newman transform
times an explicit nonvanishing factor. The first restores absolute
normalization through joint success probabilities; the second preserves
zeros and multiplicities and has unbounded inverse amplification at large
real frequency. The statements retain the full angular and radial spectra
and apply at every fixed finite derivative order, with order-dependent
bounds. The source preparations and specified measurement laws are supplied
model data. These representations establish no new signed estimate or
collision exclusion.
\end{abstract}
