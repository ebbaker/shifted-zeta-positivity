## 2. The explicit general range theorem

For the same kernel at h=ell=1, epsilon=1/4, let N singleton rectangular
cells have horizontal widths at most u, vertical widths at most v, and
adjacent horizontal interval distances at least s>0. Keep the exact
endpoint construction of [Note22](22_TND_OC_B10_R3_CELL_RANGES_AND_GAUSSIAN_WIDTH_20261109.md).
At index separation k, every gap is at least ks and its interval has
width at most 2u. For g(d)=exp(-25d²/36),

\[
g(d)\le e^{-d^2/4},\qquad |g'(d)|\le2e^{-d^2/4}.
\]

The ideal center range has width at most 2u/T and upper bound 2/T;
each mark has width at most v and upper bound one. Product telescoping
therefore bounds one ordered contribution's width by

\[
\frac{10u+4v}{T}e^{-(ks)^2/4}.
\]

There are 2(N-k) ordered pairs. For N>=2 and u+v>0,

\[
\frac{U_*-L_*}{2}
\le\frac{N(10u+4v)}{T}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}e^{-(ks)^2/4}
<\frac{N(10u+4v)\sqrt\pi}{sT}.
\]

The final inequality compares the decreasing Gaussian sum with its
integral. This exposes the geometric hypothesis behind the earlier s=1/2
bound. For fixed separation, it replaces a count-only quadratic
allowance by a linear-in-N upper allowance. It is not a lower bound on
actual error or a measured complexity advantage. Coincident labels are
covered by the separate centroid theorem, or by general same-cell ranges
on C×C; they are not covered by the singleton separation theorem.

The endpoint ball allowance is r_L+r_U, not half that sum. An exact endpoint
may lie at one edge of its enclosing ball, placing the outward edge twice
the radius away. A nonnegative tail t permits [a,b+t], but the existing
contract's conservative [a-t,b+t] is retained. Neither arithmetic nor the
target has been changed by exposing that optional sharper observation.
