## Final selected-passage ledger — 8 November 2026

This supersedes the discovery-only statuses above. All readings are
selected passages, not full proof audits. Consumer:
[Note07](../brainstorm/notes/07_TND_OC_LOCALIZED_PAIR_MARGINAL_LITERATURE_AND_DIRECTION_20261108.md)

**BGST2026PairI:** checked v3 §§1.2–1.3 (Theorems1–3 and horizontal
multiplicity), §3/(3.1)–(3.4) and the correction explanation; PDF p.7
visually inspected. For the stated dyadic complex-difference sum,
uniformly 1<=x<=T,
\[
\mathcal F(x,T)=\frac{T\log^2T}{2\pi x^2}
(1+O((\log T)^{-1/2}))+\frac{T}{2\pi}\log x
+O(T\sqrt{\log T}).
\]
The cumulative proof is also repaired. Narrow-box proportion conclusions
retain |beta-1/2|<b/(2 log T) for all zeros in the declared band.
Older BGST arXiv:2306.04799v1 pp.1–3 were reopened: its Theorem1 is the
formula reproduced in the parent manuscript. Flag its old uniform error
before reuse. The authors preserve their original applications; no
dependency-wide retraction or repaired manuscript is claimed.
v3's PDF date is 8 June 2026; submission is 1 September.

**TaoVu2014LocalZeros:** checked §1.3, Theorems2.1,3.1 and Remark3.2 in
corrected v3. The abstract transfer permits random degree-at-most-n
polynomials beyond independent-coefficient models. Required: quantitative
nondegeneracy, non-clustering, joint log-magnitude comparison for up to
n^c evaluation points against bounded smooth tests, and support/derivative
bounds for the consumer. The real case additionally requires weak
real-pair and near-axis complex repulsion for the comparator only.
The output is an n-dependent error for tested local correlations.
One-point L1 log accuracy alone is not this hypothesis.
v3 records a corrected Kac-model argument; superseded versions were not used.

**HKPV2009:** checked Theorem3.3.1 and Corollary3.4.2/(3.4.1)–(3.4.2),
printed pp.39–41; p.40 visually checked. General weak joint intensities
require local positive/negative moments and a nonzero random analytic
function. Gaussian density formulas require nonsingular value covariance
at distinct points; the conditional derivative covariance gives a
permanent, not a generic determinantal law. The 162-page author reading
copy has no independently asserted internal revision date. A canonical
law satisfying these assumptions remains to be supplied.

**MatsumotoShirai2013:** checked model (1.1), Theorem2.1 and
§2.5/Theorem2.7. The coefficients are iid real standard Gaussians, with
domain the unit disk. Real zeros and upper-half-disk complex
representatives have explicit Pfaffian correlations. This is not an
ordinary density across conjugate diagonals in the full plane.
The covariance, simplicity convention and sector reference measures
cannot be discarded in a transfer to H.

**HallHoJalowyKabluchko2025GAFHeat:** checked v2 Theorem1.1/(1.5),
Definition1.2, Theorem1.3 and its local-time qualification, and §1.1's
sign convention. For the plane GAF and |tau|<1, a rescaling and nonzero
quadratic factor turn exp[-tau D²/2]G into a field with the same law.
The coefficients are complex Gaussians. This fixed-time invariance is
not a real-coefficient model, a canonical estimate or a global
collision-free trajectory theorem. v2 lists minor corrections;
journal metadata and the examined preprint are distinguished.

**HallHoJalowyKabluchko2025PolynomialHeat:** checked §2.1, Theorem2.1,
assumptions (A1)–(A2), Theorem2.6 and finite-root ODE (3.1).
Coefficients have an iid nondegenerate law with finite logarithmic moment
and a deterministic uniform concave exponential profile.
For exp[-t D²/(2n)], the conclusions are weak empirical limits in
probability. The Weyl limit passes from disk to ellipse to real semicircle.
Real limiting support does not force each finite-n root real.
No microscopic pair theorem or full transport/PDE proof is imported.

**FyodorovSommersKhoruzhenko1998:** the printed author order is Fyodorov,
Sommers, Khoruzhenko, correcting the discovery row. Checked §III,
pp.468–474, especially (28), (32), (38)–(43), for the Gaussian matrix law,
eigenvalue joint density, marginal determinants and crossover scale.
The earlier general-universality argument is heuristic and is not
imported. The §III complex matrix model does not enforce samplewise
conjugate pairing. Use it for scale/marginal comparisons only.
