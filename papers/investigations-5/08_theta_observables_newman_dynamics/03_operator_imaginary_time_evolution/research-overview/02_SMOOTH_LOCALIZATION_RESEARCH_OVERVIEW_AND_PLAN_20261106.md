### 2. Canonical object, coordinates and time orientation

The complete source and family are fixed:

\[
H_\lambda(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{\lambda u^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du,
\qquad
H_0(z)=\frac18\xi\!\left(\frac12+\frac{iz}{2}\right),
\qquad \partial_\lambda H=-\partial_z^2H.
\]

Here \(\Phi\) is the complete normalized even theta source of M §2, not a
finite arithmetic surrogate. Its source variable u, the spatial variable
z, the auxiliary variable r below, and physical heat duration are distinct.
The absolute normalization and time sign are retained in every comparison.

A zeta zero \(\rho=\sigma+it\) corresponds to

\[
z=2t+i(1-2\sigma).
\]

Thus an off-critical-line pair at ordinate \(\theta\) corresponds to the
conjugate pair \(x\pm id\) of \(H_0\), with \(\theta=x/2\) and
\(d=2|\sigma-1/2|\). All zero sums count multiplicities. A nonreal zero is
not generally a purely imaginary zero; the latter is excluded for the
canonical real-time family by source positivity. See
[M §13.1, equation (13.1)](../manuscript/sections/11_complex_statistics.tex).

From a hypothetical positive collision time \(\lambda_*\), put
\(s=\lambda_*-\lambda\). Increasing \(s\) is forward heat evolution, and the
zeta slice is reached at \(s=\lambda_*\). The exact Gaussian convolution is

\[
H_{\lambda_*-s}(z)=\frac1{\sqrt{4\pi s}}
 \int_{\mathbb R}e^{-v^2/(4s)}H_{\lambda_*}(z+v)\,dv,
\qquad s>0.
\tag{O1}
\]

It follows by applying the Gaussian transform to the complete source;
the majorants of M §2 justify the interchange. G §3 records the derivation.
Its spatial variance is 2s. Positivity of the Gaussian does not make
the signed or complex field being averaged positive. A zero-free
conclusion still needs an estimate that controls cancellation.

The operator formulation in M §3 may help organize norms, kernels and
semigroup identities. The equally informed scalar formulation remains
the baseline: a change of representation alone supplies no new arithmetic
information or improvement in error.

### 3. What localization is intended to accomplish

An exceptional pair can be diluted in a bulk average over many zeros.
M §13 proves this sensitivity limitation for two specified leading
correlation asymptotics; it is not a statement about every statistic or
every remainder. Localizing around a height v may preserve a finite
signal, but it changes the arithmetic estimate needed to control it.

For a large dyadic height band [T,2T], use the manuscript's declared
leading density scale

\[
L_T=\frac{\log T}{2\pi},\qquad \Delta_T=L_T^{-1}.
\]

\(\Delta_T\) is asymptotic to the mean spacing of zeta ordinates. The more
accurate mean density contains \(\log(T/(2\pi))\); a proof using that convention
must keep its normalization consistently. Neither density formula is a
uniform assertion about every microscopic window.

A useful parameterization is \(h(T)=m(T)\Delta_T\), where \(h\) is the height
width. Microscopic, mesoscopic and broader windows are all candidates:

- \(m(T)\) of order one probes a few mean spacings and preserves a sparse signal.
- \(m(T)\) tending to infinity can improve averaging while diluting a normalized
  single-pair contribution. If \(h\) is to shrink, \(m(T)=o(\log T)\) is sufficient.
- Other powers, logarithmic corrections, adaptive widths, or a controlled
  family of overlapping scales may be preferable. They are permitted
  choices, not deviations from this program.

A proposed width is evaluated together with its center range, frequency,
tail losses, number of zeros, and the upper estimate actually available.
No default width is accepted merely because it matches mean spacing.

### 4. Smoothing choices and the variable they act on

There are three separate operations: a height window on the zeros, a
weight in an auxiliary Gram integral, and physical heat convolution.
Their widths must not be identified without a derivation.

| Operation | Example | Advantage and obligation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zero-height localization | Gaussian exp(-(t-v)^2/(2h^2)) | Explicit convolution and fast tails; the full zero sum and any tail-interference cost must be controlled |
| Exact spatial localization | A nonnegative smooth compact bump | Exact support permits phase-sector arguments; derivatives, Fourier costs and moving boundaries remain due |
| Harmonic height smoothing | Poisson kernel h/[pi(u^2+h^2)] | Natural connection with resolvents/harmonic extension; algebraic tails must be estimated |
| Auxiliary smoothing | A nonnegative weight in r, possibly Gaussian | Can simplify the pair kernel; must retain convergence and a justified comparison with the chosen observable |
| Physical evolution | The Gaussian in (O1) | Fixes the heat clock; cannot remove cancellation in H by taking absolute values |

Poisson-kernel smoothing does not assume a Poisson probability law for
zero counts. Gaussian windows likewise do not assume Gaussian zero
statistics. Positive kernels alone do not distinguish theta from the
colliding controls.

For illustration, put
\(F(r)=\sum_j w_j(v)e^{(\rho_j-1/2)(a+r)}\), where
\(\rho_j=\sigma_j+it_j\) runs over the complete zero set and the real
nonnegative height weights are reflection invariant. If \(E\) is the
manuscript's exponential Gram integral (O3), G §1 proves the Gaussian minorant

\[
E\ge\sqrt{2\pi}\,b e^{-2b^2}E_b^G,
\qquad
E_b^G=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}b}
 \int_{\mathbb R}e^{-r^2/(2b^2)}|F(r)|^2\,dr.
\tag{O2}
\]

For finite support, or after justifying convergence for the full zero sum,
the Gaussian transform and reflection relabeling give

\[
E_b^G=\sum_{j,k}w_jw_k
 \exp\!\left(a(\rho_j-\rho_k)+\frac{b^2}{2}(\rho_j-\rho_k)^2\right).
\]

An isolated reflected pair \(\rho_\pm=1/2\pm d/2+i\theta\) has squared norm
\(P_b^G=2w_0^2[1+e^{b^2d^2/2}\cosh(ad)]\). This calculation retains the
mixed terms with other zeros; it does not imply \(E_b^G\ge P_b^G\).
These are available comparisons, not prescribed normalizations or
optimization problems. In particular,
the prefactor, signal and required upper bound have to be assessed together.
A bare undamped Poisson kernel in r is unsuitable for this particular
exponentially growing off-line-pair integrand; G §2 demonstrates divergence
already for one pair. That scoped failure does not exclude Poisson
smoothing in height or a different resolvent observable.

### 5. Candidate signals and alternatives to the preliminary contradiction

The two inherited examples are useful baselines:

**Coincidence statistic.** With nonnegative weights \(w_j\) and a nonnegative
test \(\varphi\) with \(\varphi(0)=1\),

\[
S_{v,h,\varepsilon}=\sum_{j\ne k}w_j(v)w_k(v)
 \varphi\!\left(\frac{L_T(t_j-t_k)}{\varepsilon}\right).
\]

A reflected off-line pair has identical ordinates and contributes exactly
\(2w_0(v)^2\). Distinct copies of a multiple zero also contribute. M
Proposition 14.1 proves the coincidence atom; Proposition 14.2 computes
a sine-process reference, not a local zeta asymptotic. On a unit plateau,
an upper bound below two would exclude the pair, but obtaining such a
uniform absolute bound can demand more than excluding nonreal zeros.
All-window coincidence vanishing would impose simplicity as well as RH.

**Analytic Gram statistic.** For \(a\ge0\) and reflected height weights,

\[
E_{v,h}(a)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-2|r|}
 \left|\sum_j w_j(v)e^{(\rho_j-1/2)(a+r)}\right|^2dr.
\tag{O3}
\]

This equals the complex-difference sum in M Proposition 14.3. Its
isolated pair norm is \(P_v=2w_0^2[1+W(d)\cosh(ad)]\), with
\(W(d)=4/(4-d^2)\). In general the total norm need only obey

\[
E_{v,h}(a)\ge(\sqrt{P_v}-\sqrt{B_v})_+^2,
\]

where \(B_v\) is the squared norm of the remaining zero contribution.
Separate background estimation is one
possible sufficient method. Phase-sector estimates, collective cluster
bounds, a different positive form, or another observably equivalent
comparison may replace it if their hypotheses and costs are proved.

For a compact height window and a retained auxiliary interval \(|r|\le R\),
the preliminary condition \(h(a+R)<\pi/2\) gives a common positive projection
after removing the center phase. This supplies a transparent finite
comparison. It is not a required condition for all future kernels;
Gaussian-tail estimates or a different interference argument may be better.

Uniform and averaged upper estimates are distinct. If a lower signal
\(A_*\) persists on a center set \(J\), an illustrative moment contradiction is

\[
\frac1T\int_T^{2T}E_{v,h}(a)^p\,dv
 \ge \frac{|J|}{T}A_*^p
 > U_p(T,h,a).
\tag{O4}
\]

M Corollary 14.4 proves the lower comparison under its stated hypotheses;
it does not supply \(U_p\). The center-width cost cannot be dropped. If a
Gaussian has no exact plateau, use a quantitative lower-weight region or
integrate the actual profile. A growing p requires control of its constants.

The author permits alternatives to (O4) and to the two baseline statistics.
For example, one may pursue a direct relative heat-field estimate, an
integrated cluster/flux inequality, or a spatial barrier. Such a change
belongs in a recorded B decision, with a fresh proof contract. It need
not reproduce the preliminary sequence “pair size, background norm,
moment contradiction” if another structure controls the canonical target
more effectively. Only two candidates are developed in any one decision
cycle; these examples are not a mandate to open concurrent branches.

### 6. Collision geometry and conversion to a time bound

At a double event the analytic critical value and squared gap remain
regular although individual zero velocities are singular. M §§5–6
derive the coefficient identities and connect them with signed theta
moments, an endpoint-correct primitive, and contour formulas. The generic
collision signs also occur in the explicit control of M §10, so a useful
new restriction must use quantitative canonical information.

For an isolated conjugate pair, write

\[
H_{\lambda_*-s}(z)=[(z-x(s))^2+y(s)^2]U(s,z),\qquad
g=U_z/U,\qquad k(s)=\frac{\Im g(s,x+iy)}{y}.
\]

M §12.2 gives

\[
x_s=-2\Re g(s,x+iy),\qquad
u_s+4k(s)u=2,\qquad u=y^2,
\]
\[
u(s)=2\int_0^s\exp\!\left(-4\int_r^s k(q)\,dq\right)dr.
\tag{O5}
\]

These equations require a complete isolating region, nonzero exterior
factor, and simple labeling away from the initial event. Theorem 12.2
is conditional on bounds over the entire trajectory, not merely at the
collision. With \(\ell=g(0,x_*)\) and \(\kappa=g_z(0,x_*)\), the local jets are
\(x(s)=x_*-2\ell s+O(s^2)\) and
\(y(s)^2=2s-4\kappa s^2+O(s^3)\). The midpoint drift \(-2\ell\) differs
from the critical-point drift \(-3\ell\); a local expansion does not supply
complete-path continuation. M Proposition 12.4 retains the moving-window,
unequal-weight and exterior-force terms needed for localized cluster balances.

M Proposition 12.3 gives another possible input: for a simple root that
remains of maximal imaginary part, with \(m\) persistent real neighbors
within horizontal distance \(R\),
\(u_s\ge 2+4mu/(R^2+u)\). This relates a specified neighboring population
to transverse growth. Mean zero density alone supplies neither the required
realness nor maximality, persistence and simple labeling. A smoothing or
cluster argument that replaces those assumptions would be a new task.

One illustrative conversion from G §4 is: if the pair reaches time zero,
its final \(H_0\) displacement \(d\) obeys \(d\le D(T)\), and
\(\int_r^{\lambda_*}k(q)\,dq\le A(T)\) for every
\(r\in[0,\lambda_*]\), then

\[
\lambda_*\le\tfrac12e^{4A(T)}D(T)^2.
\tag{O6}
\]

If \(D(T)=O(h(T))\) and \(A(T)=O(1)\), this yields an \(O(h(T)^2)\) time envelope.
For \(h=m/L_T\) it suggests \(m(T)^2/\log^2 T\). These are conditional sufficient
estimates, not outputs already obtained from zero density. The plan may
instead use one-sided differential control, integrated cancellations,
cluster continuation, spatial barriers, or a direct heat estimate.
Neither \(D=O(h)\) nor \(A=O(1)\) is imposed as the only acceptable route.

Even a uniform bound \(k\le K\), with \(K>0\), illustrates a limitation:
inversion of the lower bound in Theorem 12.2 requires \(2KD(T)^2<1\).
Without it, that particular
saturating estimate supplies no finite time bound. This is a reason to
reconsider the estimate or regime, not to conclude that no method works.

### 8. What extending to infinite order or index means here

Analytical extensibility is an acceptance condition from the first
contract, not an optional final numerical extrapolation. Different limits
have different obligations:

| Limit or family | Required analytical control |
| --- | --- |
| Complete arithmetic source, N tending to infinity | Uniform source and derivative tails on the actual parameter region; preserve full reflection, normalization and required boundary margins |
| Complete zero set or increasing zero index | Absolute/paired convergence as appropriate, multiplicity, tails of the chosen window, and a justified relation between index and height |
| T tending to infinity with moving widths/frequencies | One stated joint regime, explicit parameter dependence and remainder relative to the signal; fixed-parameter estimates cannot silently become uniform |
| lambda(T) tending to zero | Quantitative height/time transition and error control; fixed-positive-time real tails are insufficient |
| Derivative or expansion order k increasing | A recurrence, generating function, operator bound or explicit coefficient-growth/remainder estimate; “every fixed k” alone is insufficient |
| Moment order p increasing | Bounds on the p-dependent constants and the allowable growth of p(T), including all center and tail costs |
| Cutoffs removed together | A justified order of limits or a uniform majorant in the selected coupled regime; no interchange solely by formal algebra |

M §2 already pays the complete source at each fixed derivative order.
It does not supply a new joint estimate as order, height, frequency and
time vary. Future proofs should reuse its exact identities while tracking
the additional growth that their proposed limit requires.

There is no requirement to send every parameter to infinity. A theorem
at fixed p that is uniform for all sufficiently large T can meet the
scientific goal. Conversely, computing many derivatives or many zero
indices is not a substitute for such a theorem. A finite intermediate
calculation is justified when it supplies a reusable recurrence, a
majorant, a summability mechanism or a decisive obstruction. If an
expansion is asymptotic rather than convergent, prove an optimally
truncated remainder or another valid limiting statement instead of
claiming an infinite convergent series.

### 12. What a fixed E contract must contain

Before an E task begins, its handoff packet freezes the exact statement,
source versions, domain, normalization, allowed parameter regime, proof
route, numerical-free acceptance test and permitted local refinements.
It also states the intended consumer and an explicit failure condition.

For infinite sums or orders, include a bound of the form actually needed
by the proof: a summable majorant, uniform remainder, operator norm,
recurrence with controlled constants, or justified asymptotic truncation.
Merely promising to increase a cutoff after a successful finite case is
not a contract. Relative estimates must include a legitimate denominator
margin; division by the field at the collision is not permitted.

An E task may sharpen a constant, optimize a parameter within the already
allowed family, correct a normalization or complete a standard tail bound.
Changing the observable, source, target, fundamental proof mechanism or
required uniformity returns to the corresponding B task before dependent
work continues. A failed E estimate may be the successful completion of
its diagnostic objective; it does not license an unlimited search.
