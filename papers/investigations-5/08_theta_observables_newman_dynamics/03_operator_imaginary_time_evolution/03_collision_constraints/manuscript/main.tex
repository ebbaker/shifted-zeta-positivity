\begin{abstract}
In forward heat time, the squared gap of two simple real zeros satisfies
\(q'=-8-4qB\), where \(B\) averages the logarithmic curvature of the
field after removal of the pair. We use a second divided difference at
the actual root nodes to estimate this curvature from a finite model.
Derivative errors transfer with constants \(1/2,1/6,1/12\) and without
inverse powers of the gap. Explicit real-axis theta tails and parameter
allowances make the error budget complete. A uniform factor floor and
three strict inequalities then confine the roots and preserve their
simplicity through a closed time interval. We give a test for when
unsigned confinement already pays the gap inequality, identify a cost
of a uniform ratio bound, and show that an upper Lehmer sum controls
the opposite force direction from the one survival requires. Exact heat
polynomials test these distinctions. Two canonical case studies delimit
the present evidence and leave a matched quantitative comparison open.
\end{abstract}
