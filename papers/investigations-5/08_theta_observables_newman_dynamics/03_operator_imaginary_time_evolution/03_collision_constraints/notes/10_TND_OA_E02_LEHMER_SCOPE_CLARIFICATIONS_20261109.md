- **Real-exterior premise.** The algebra \(G+2B=qS\) can extend to
  conjugate exterior zeros when its series and product identity are
  valid. For \(P=(a-\rho)(b-\rho)\), the conjugate pair contributes
  \(2\operatorname{Re}(P^{-2})\) to \(S\) and
  \(-2\operatorname{Re}(P^{-1})\) to \(B\). Those real parts have
  no fixed signs. For example at a real-polynomial snapshot with
  \(a=1,b=3\) and deflated factor \(U(z)=(z-1)^2+1\), one has
  \(P=-1-2i\) for \(\rho=1+i\). The pair gives \(S=-6/25\) and
  \(B=2/5\). This checks the loss of signs for complex exterior
  roots. The real-product comparison remains valid in its own class.
