## Which hierarchy transfers

The heat-density investigation (HDA) studies the same canonical H, with
forward heat clock T corresponding to Newman time −T. Its source moments
give H and its derivatives, while its gamma fluctuation hierarchy
organizes corrections without dropping the arithmetic modes. These are
not the factorial zero-statistic hierarchy of the correlation branch.
The Riemann–Siegel completion order is another separate index.

Passing directly from those field derivatives to H′/H leaves an unpaid
denominator near zeros. The current B02 theorem supplies a more useful
connection: a real measurable approximation to the field, including its
zeros and changing arithmetic cutoff, gives a one-sided integral estimate
for the boundary logarithm. No lower modulus on the real line is needed.

## A concrete local consequence

The exact gamma factor in HDA cancels against the Green branch's explicit
normalizing factor: on the real line, |D M|=1. Thus a gamma-relative H
remainder transfers directly to an absolute error for F=D H.

A fixed algebraic field allowance is insufficient to make the particular
B02 upper error vanish at growing height, because its exponent deteriorates
like 1/log X. This is a limitation of that bound, not an error lower bound
or a failure theorem for fixed-order methods.

HDA already retains the real, time-zero Riemann–Siegel completion to any
positive integer order. Composing its imported explicit remainder with
B02, Note05 derives the sufficient choice

\[

and a local integrated logarithmic error O((log X)^−2) on unit intervals,

not an independent review or a new classical expansion. The order rule
is conservative; the arithmetic population still has O(sqrt X) modes.
Coefficient evaluation, working precision and a practical onset remain
unpaid.

This use takes the existing real initial data directly. It does **not**
continue the strong negative-time exp(−L²/4) theorem through zero. Later
HDA results do include zero and positive Newman times, at their own
weaker error scales; describing all HDA work as excluding zero would
miss that distinction.

## A second connection and its limit

The earlier filtered evolution equations separate a mean field from
fluctuations. Their covariance term enters as a difference of directional
variances, with no automatic favorable sign. The recorded cosh control
rules out the specified density-variance-only closure. It does not rule
out all closures or estimate the canonical signed Green value.

This suggests keeping covariance, harmonic and boundary contributions
explicit in any dynamical approach. It does not currently supply a
second signed estimating mechanism.
