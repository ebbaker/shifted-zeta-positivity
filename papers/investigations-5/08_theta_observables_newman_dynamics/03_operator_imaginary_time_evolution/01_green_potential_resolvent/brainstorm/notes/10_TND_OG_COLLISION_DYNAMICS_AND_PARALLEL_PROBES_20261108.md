## 2. Specify which collision and which clock

Use the unchanged complete theta source and
\[
 h(\tau,z)=H_{\lambda_0-\tau}(z),\qquad h_\tau=h_{zz}.
\]
At a nondegenerate double event, put
\(a=h_{zz}(\tau_*,x_*)\ne0\), \(b=h_{zzz}(\tau_*,x_*)\).
The inherited local form gives, for \(s>0\),
\[
 z_\pm(\tau_*+s)=x_*-\frac{2b}{3a}s
                  \ \pm i\sqrt{2s}+O(s^{3/2}).
 \tag{1}
\]
Thus the upper-zero signal is born after the event in forward tau,
which is **before it in Newman lambda**. The pair's imaginary height
tends to zero at the event. The constants and permitted neighborhood
in (1) depend on the event; the formula supplies no uniform tube over
all heights.

OC's currently fixed B06/E08 center law and OG's current arithmetic
acquisition concern **lambda=0**. They cannot simply be inserted at
lambda near a hypothetical positive collision time. OA must either
obtain the probe at that time, prove a quantitative transport to time
zero, or prove that the hypothetical event forces another detectable
configuration there. The transport must preserve the required spatial
window, depth, multiplicity and complete error budget. The local germ
(1) does not accomplish a finite-time transport.

A first compact theorem may cover isolated double events only. State
that restriction and do not infer coverage of higher multiplicities,
events outside the box, or endpoints. A real multiple zero at
lambda=0 is compatible with RH; excluding it would also address
simplicity. If the intended target is a positive Newman threshold,
the implication from that hypothesis to the selected event class must
be supplied. These are different quantifiers.

Real-zero ordering and dissipative estimates also keep their declared
all-real regime, time interval and convergence assumptions. The common
heat equation does not transfer them unchanged to a complex zero cloud.
Parent [Note11 §4][jets] records this limitation for the inspected
Rodgers–Tao tools.

## 3. A bounded exterior force needs an initial gap and a time budget

For the isolated real pair, write
\[
 h=(z-r_-)(z-r_+)F,\quad q=(r_+-r_-)^2,\quad
 B=\int_0^1(\log F)_{zz}(r_-+t(r_+-r_-))\,dt.
\]
The inherited equation is \(q'=-8-4qB\). A finite value of B at an
assumed event is entirely compatible with collision: \(q'(\tau_*)=-8\).
Consequently a local statement that B is bounded is not a contradiction.
An estimate must act on the approach from an earlier, controlled gap.

For example, reset tau=0 at that earlier slice. If \(B(t)\le b\)
through an isolated-pair interval of length \(\Delta\), a sufficient
finite-interval comparison is
\[
 q(0)>8E_b(\Delta),\qquad
 E_b(\Delta)=
 \begin{cases}
 (e^{4b\Delta}-1)/(4b),&b\ne0,\\
 \Delta,&b=0.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the integrating factor
\(A(t)=\exp(4\int_0^tB)\le e^{4bt}\) gives
\(A(t)q(t)=q(0)-8\int_0^t A\); (2) keeps the bracket positive.
Use continuity to rule out a first pair collision before the specified
endpoint, provided the pair remains isolated and the bound persists
up to that endpoint. Loss of isolation is a separate failure, not an
excluded collision.

This is an elementary consequence of OA's existing gap law, not an
acquired theta bound. It fixes the **direction** of the desired signed
bound and the required numerical comparison. A bound on \(|B|\) can
give a conservative lifetime, but need not exploit useful cancellation.
The exact heat polynomial \(z^2+2\tau\), started before tau=0, has B=0
and \(q(0)=8\Delta\) at its collision horizon: it fails the strict
test exactly. The polynomial is a local diagnostic, not a theta source.

The local field F is safer than an unqualified infinite zero sum.
Replacing it by a product over exterior zeros must pay convergence,
normalization and every exterior contribution. [Parent Note11][jets]
already gives the contour-jet bridge and a finite unfavorable comparison
against direct source evaluation; reuse that baseline.

## 4. The environment of a selected pair can require three-zero data

Pairwise forces do not imply that a two-point marginal closes the
evolution of a selected pair. An exact finite calculation shows the issue.
For a polynomial with distinct zeros, define
\[
 {\cal B}_{ij}=-\sum_{k\ne i,j}
       \frac1{(z_i-z_k)(z_j-z_k)}.
 \tag{3}
\]
For an isolated real pair with a nonvanishing deflated factor on its
connecting segment, this is precisely B: each exterior zero contributes
\[
 \int_0^1-\frac{dt}{(z_i+t(z_j-z_i)-z_k)^2}
       =-\frac1{(z_i-z_k)(z_j-z_k)}.
\]
The formula also follows from the difference of the two exterior
logarithmic derivatives.

Let \(\alpha_3=\mathbb E\sum_{i,j,k\ {\rm distinct}}
\delta_{(z_i,z_j,z_k)}\) for a finite simple random cloud. If the
displayed kernels are integrable (or all relevant separations have a
fixed positive floor), then for a bounded pair test \(\psi\),
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_{i\ne j}\psi(z_i,z_j){\cal B}_{ij}
 =-\int\frac{\psi(a,b)}{(a-c)(b-c)}
             \,d\alpha_3(a,b,c).
 \tag{4}
\]
This is direct factorial bookkeeping. For real or conjugate-symmetric
configurations use the appropriate real sum; absolute inverse-square
interaction is not the same signed quantity.

Accordingly, a pair-conditioned exterior-force average generally
asks for a three-point statistic and its pair-normalization denominator.
Requiring the chosen pair to be adjacent or isolated adds a condition
on the other zeros, which an arbitrary test of two positions does not
encode. Close-to-collision conditioning can have very small probability;
an unconditional error must not be reused after conditioning without
paying that normalization.

Equation (4) is a **finite-cloud diagnostic**, not a canonical infinite
product theorem or an impossibility result for pair methods. A direct
uniform bound on the exterior field, a valid closure hypothesis, or an
appropriate integrated pair-force estimate could avoid acquiring a full
three-point function. OA should state which of these it requests.
OC [Note14 §5][oc14] already warns that marginal evolution introduces
higher information; (4) identifies it for this particular consumer.

## 5. Make a collision statistically visible before asking for a moment

There is a clean two-point consumer that does not require the force
closure. On a time slice where the positive, convergent Green
representation is justified in its declared strip, put
\[
 J_\lambda(X)=\langle g_X,\nu_\lambda\rangle,\qquad
 g_X(z)=1_{\Im z>0}G(X-\Re z,\Im z).
\]
OC [Note14 §3][oc14] gives
\[
 J_\lambda(X)^2
 =\langle g_X^2,\nu_\lambda\rangle+
   \langle g_X\otimes g_X,\alpha_{2,\lambda}\rangle.
 \tag{5}
\]
The one-point self term is essential. A connected covariance alone is
not the nonnegative total in (5), and distinct labels at a multiple
zero must not be deleted as though they were one self label.

Suppose collision dynamics has independently certified an upper zero
of height at least d>0 within distance r of every center in a set
\(A\subset I\), on the same time slice. Positivity then gives
\[
 \frac1{|I|}\int_I J_\lambda(X)^2\,dX
 \ \ge\ \frac{|A|}{|I|}\,m(d,r)^2,\qquad
 m(d,r)=\frac{d\sin(\pi/8)}{\cosh(\pi r/8)}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Thus a fully paid arithmetic upper bound U for the left side would
contradict that scenario only if
\[
             U<\frac{|A|}{|I|}m(d,r)^2. \tag{7}
\]
The set A is the visibility window forced by the event, not a
probability chosen after seeing a convenient center. For a fixed upper
zero one may take \(A=I\cap[x-r,x+r]\). If averaging also over a time
interval K, a certified visibility set \(S\subset K\times I\) with
the same d,r gives the factor \(|S|/(|K||I|)\) instead. No new
canonical upper estimate is asserted here.

This is the elementary positivity interface for the already identified
Green-square consumer. It is the same window-payment principle used in
[parent localized work][localized], now written for (5).
[OG B04][ogb04] also gives the exact first-moment identity
\(\int_{\mathbb R}G(X-x,d)\,dX=\pi d\): broad averaging dilutes
one upper zero to order d/|I|. Its failed density allowance is a
failure of that specified bound, not a prohibition on sharper statistics.

Combining (1) with the local Green expansion gives the selected upper
zero's contribution at its center of order \(\sqrt{s}\), and its square
of order s. Other zeros can add a background. An error merely tending
to zero is insufficient without its rate and the visibility factor.
To make these into uniform lower bounds, pay the remainder and drift
in (1), isolation, the positive strip representation and the chosen
time interval. Event-dependent big-O notation supplies no such constants.

The OC marginals are smoothed and windowed. Contracting them with a
test produces its smoothed version; the unsmoothed right side of (5)
cannot be substituted for free. Pay smoothing bias, real-zero
background, spatial/observation tails, unfolding factors and both
first- and second-intensity errors. OC's fixed-blur two-label example
has an O(d²) profile difference while inverse-square interaction is
O(d^(-2)). That excludes a fixed-positive-error inference for that
finite model only; it does not exclude shrinking blur or stronger
source information.

For OA's scalar consumer, an accurate **contracted** estimate in (5)
could suffice without a uniform reconstruction of the whole pair
profile. OC may still pursue the latter as its independent objective.
This is a useful way to exchange results without making either branch
wait for the other's entire program.

## 6. Premise-level collision controls

This is a documentary screen against [controls12][controls], not a
fresh numerical replay. The generic identities above permit collisions;
they are not claimed to reject the controls. The proposed missing input
is a complete arithmetic estimate with a useful strict margin.

| Recorded control | Premise it fails in a prospective canonical application |
| --- | --- |
| Euler-factor controls | The unchanged theta coefficients/completion; positivity, decay and heat evolution are shared and cannot distinguish them |
| Weighted-square controls | Complete unit weights and full normalized reflection; square support and finite endpoints are insufficient |
| Reflected super-Gaussian | The complete integer-square theta expansion; reflection and primitive endpoints alone survive its collision |
| Smooth shape and positive-primitive controls | Exact theta arithmetic; shape, primitive positivity and matched asymptotic data do not supply a signed estimate |
| Finite-source and finite-prime controls | Full theta reflection/source data and, where specified, the all-real-time or eventual-simplicity hypotheses; a fixed selected-prime list is insufficient |
| Fixed-event and finite-profile threshold controls | Unchanged theta coefficients, full profile growth and eventual simplicity; finite moments/profiles do not control the deflated jet |
| RH-conditional normalized endpoint control | Ordinary Dirichlet-series arithmetic; its escaping time-zero doubles do not contradict a fixed compact result, and its separate polynomial preservation list is not merged |
| Raw-kernel and actual-profile relaxations | Respectively the full cosine/Newman equation and a complete integer divisor; neither relaxation is a physical theta collision |

Merely failing the literal theta identity does not establish failure
of a derived inequality. The future estimate must name the *used*
premise or quantitative allowance that separates each relevant control.
A finite certificate need reject a control only when its claimed domain
contains the witness. No finite-profile or fixed-blur failure above is
a universal exclusion of statistical methods.
