### The heat-density/Riemann–Siegel connection

The relevant part of the heat-density asymptotics (HDA) work approximates
the **function itself**. The Green branch subsequently asks about a
weighted logarithm of that function. These are two stages, with a
nontrivial transfer between them.

Start with the same complete theta transform as manuscript (2.3)–(2.5):
\[
H_\lambda(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{\lambda u^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du,
\qquad H_0(z)=\frac18\xi\!\left(\frac{1-iz}{2}\right),
\qquad \partial_\lambda H_\lambda=-\partial_z^2H_\lambda.
\]
Putting \(\lambda=-T\) gives ordinary forward heat evolution:
\(\partial_T H_{-T}=\partial_z^2H_{-T}\). The factor
\(e^{-Tu^2}\) damps the source's high frequencies. The HDA expansions
organize the exact gamma factor, the integer arithmetic modes and
successive correction terms. For the proposed Green application we
reuse the **time-zero Riemann–Siegel completion component**. The strong
HDA recurrence bounds on positive forward heat time do not themselves
extend to \(T=0\) with their stated error rate.

The familiar Riemann–Siegel picture, for ordinary zeta height \(t>0\),
is the real Hardy function
\[
Z(t):=e^{i\vartheta(t)}\zeta(1/2+it)
=2\sum_{n\le\lfloor\sqrt{t/(2\pi)}\rfloor}
 \frac{\cos(\vartheta(t)-t\log n)}{\sqrt n}
 +\text{correction and remainder}.
\]
Here \(\vartheta\) is the standard gamma phase, chosen so that \(Z\)
is real. The short sum illustrates the two conjugate arithmetic arms;
the actual B03 input keeps named higher correction terms and an explicit
remainder. See [DLMF §25.9](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.9), especially
(25.9.2)–(25.9.3), for the classical approximation. In our coordinate
\(t=x/2\), the cutoff is \(\lfloor\sqrt{x/(4\pi)}\rfloor\).
Every integer mode through that cutoff is retained, not just primes.

The useful normalization is exact. B03 [Note05](../../notes/05_TND_OG_B03_HDA_HIERARCHY_LOG_TRANSFER_20261108.md),
(4)–(5), writes
\[
F(x)=D(x)H_0(x),\qquad |F(x)|=|\zeta(1/2-ix/2)|,
\qquad |D(x)|\,|M((1+ix)/2)|=1,
\]
where \(s_-(z)=(1-iz)/2\),
\(M(s)=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)/16\), and
\(D(z)=16\pi^{s_-(z)/2}/[s_-(z)^2\Gamma(s_-(z)/2)]\).
Thus a bound \(|H_0-f_K|\le |M|\varepsilon_K\) immediately becomes
\(|F-Df_K|\le\varepsilon_K\). The small gamma scale in the completed
xi function cancels; it does not create an extra exponential accuracy
loss when normalization is performed consistently.

There is a second difficulty: taking logarithms near a zero. A small
absolute error in \(F\) need not give a small pointwise error in
\(\log|F|\). Manuscript (8.9) instead controls the **integrated excess**
of the safe upper envelope:
\[
0\le\int_{I_v}
 [\log(|\widehat F|+\delta)-\log|F|]\,dx
\le\mathcal E_v(\delta),\qquad
\sup_{I_v}|F-\widehat F|\le\delta.
\]
This does not require locating, separating or assuming simplicity of
the real zeros. B03 Note05 combines that theorem with the explicit
all-order remainder already imported from
[Polymath, arXiv:1904.12438v2, Proposition 6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438v2#S6.SS2).
Its chosen growing order \(K=O(\log\log v)\) gives a sufficient local
logarithmic allowance \(O((\log v)^{-2})\) for sufficiently large \(v\).
This is an asymptotic existence estimate with its recorded internal
checking status, not a practical onset or full-cost numerical result.
The expansion is not asserted to converge as \(K\to\infty\) at fixed
height. The integer mode count still grows like \(\sqrt v\).

The bridge therefore has three stages:
\[
\text{arithmetic approximation of }H_0
\ \longrightarrow
\text{controlled approximation of }\log|\zeta|
\ \longrightarrow
\text{a signed Green value still to estimate}.
\]
The last stage is the open research question. In manuscript (4.2),
\[
J_0(X)=\mathcal L(X)-P(X)+G(X,1),\qquad
\mathcal L(X)=\int_{\mathbb R}
 \frac{\log|\zeta(1/2-ix/2)|}{16\cosh(\pi(x-X)/8)}\,dx.
\]
Knowing the approximation error is small does not say that this signed
combination is small. The unresolved estimate must control the
logarithm of the oscillatory arithmetic sum together with the prime
subtraction, at the specified localization scale. The finite sum is
inside the logarithm; replacing it by separate absolute bounds can lose
the information being sought. Global tails and evaluation errors must
also be included. This is why the first path was a high-risk analytical
candidate, rather than a nearly completed exclusion argument.

### What a specific collision input would look like

The alternative concerns a **named pair of actual theta zeros** at a
specified positive Newman time \(\lambda_s\), followed toward a smaller
Newman time. Put
\[
h(\tau,z)=H_{\lambda_s-\tau}(z),\qquad
h(\tau,z)=(z-a(\tau))(z-b(\tau))U(\tau,z),\qquad a<b.
\]
Then \(h_\tau=h_{zz}\). Dividing out the two roots leaves the regular
factor \(U\), containing the rest of the field. Define
\(q=(b-a)^2\), \(g=U_z/U\). The
[OA manuscript](../../../03_collision_constraints/manuscript/README.md),
**(1.1)**, gives
\[
q'=-8-4qB,\qquad
B=\frac{g(b)-g(a)}{b-a}
=\frac1{b-a}\int_a^b
 \left(\frac{U_{zz}}U-\left(\frac{U_z}U\right)^2\right)dx.
\]
Along the real segment, the integrand is
\(\partial_x^2\log|U|\). Thus \(B\) is the average logarithmic
curvature of the remaining field. The term \(-8\) is the mutual
contraction of the pair in this time direction. A negative \(B\)
opposes it, but its magnitude and duration matter. No assumption that
all exterior zeros are real is used in this formulation.

The requested new input would be a certified bound of the form
\[
B(\tau)\le\beta\quad\text{for all }0\le\tau\le\Delta,
\qquad q(0)\ge Q_->0,
\]
with a nonvanishing regular-factor bound and confinement that keep
these estimates applicable throughout the interval. OA's survival
theorem then uses the explicit numerical margin
\[
Q_->8\int_0^\Delta e^{4\beta s}\,ds,
\qquad
q(\tau)\ge e^{-4\beta\tau}
 \left[Q_- -8\int_0^\tau e^{4\beta s}\,ds\right]>0.
\]
This is the gap part of OA **(4.1)**, not a replacement for its other
confinement conditions. For \(\beta=0\), it reduces to
\(Q_->8\Delta\); a sufficiently negative upper bound extends the
certifiable time. The exact quadratic heat example
\(h=(z-X)^2+2(\tau-T)\) has \(U=1\), \(B=0\) and
\(q=8(T-\tau)\), so it fails the strict survival margin at its
collision time \(\Delta=T\). This is a control, not theta evidence.

A concrete acquisition could use complete theta-source enclosures for
\(h_{zz},h_{zzz},h_{zzzz}\), shared-node divided differences to recover
\(U,U_z,U_{zz}\) without dividing by a tiny gap, and a certified
\(|U|\ge m>0\). The signed curvature expression must be estimated
together, including the negative square, before adding errors. OA's
shared-node theorem requires its factor floor on the full virtual-node
parameter box, a stronger premise than nonvanishing on the actual
moving segment alone. A contour construction has its own boundary
floor and zero-count requirements. Neither floor is free.

The immediate research question is therefore whether one can produce
an actual tuple \((\lambda_s,a_0,b_0,\Delta,Q_-,\beta)\), with all
conditioning and confinement data paid, for which this signed estimate
improves the survival interval or cost over the strongest direct method
using the same source information. No such new useful tuple was
identified in this discussion. OA's prior low-box case and selected
time-zero Lehmer example did not meet its restart conditions; finite-pair
acquisition remains parked.

A sign measured only at a prospective collision is inadequate. At a
regular double event \(B\) stays finite and \(q'=-8\) when \(q=0\),
even if \(B<0\). OA's exact cubic control has negative event curvature
and still collides. Also, the screened Lehmer-type estimate gives a
**lower** bound on \(B\), whereas survival here needs an **upper**
bound. These are specific direction and interval failures, not a claim
that all Lehmer methods or all collision arguments are excluded.

## Follow-up idea: off-line zeros and the Riemann–Siegel saddles

10 November 2026, America/New_York; prepared for Edward Baker with OpenAI
Codex LLM assistance. Actual model/effort remain unavailable in the chat
registry. This records the author's question and an explanatory
calculation, checked by its authoring assistant only. No research task

The author noticed the general-strip formula on Wikipedia and asked
whether a hypothetical off-critical-line zero would change the
Riemann–Siegel saddle structure. The earlier real cosine formula was
chosen because the Green observable uses real-boundary data. Its
restriction to the line is not an RH assumption.

Two distinctions matter. The expansion is a large-height saddle
expansion, not a Taylor expansion in the distance from the critical
line. Also, equal arithmetic cutoffs can be chosen off the line:
DLMF (25.9.1) allows the balanced choice of real cutoffs
\(x=y=\sqrt{t/(2\pi)}\), hence equal integer sum lengths, for fixed
\(0\le\sigma\le1\). What is special at \(\sigma=1/2\) is the
conjugacy of the two completed contributions and the resulting real
cosine form. The general formula and its error must be retained off
the line. The Wikipedia page was inspected as the author's locator;
the mathematical sources are listed in
[parent Source-use18](../../../references/18_TND_O_RIEMANN_SIEGEL_SADDLES_SOURCE_USE_20261110.md).

Here is a direct calculation in the exact two-integral representation.
Use \(s=\sigma+it\), \(t>0\), and an integration variable \(w\).
The exponential phases of the two integrands are
\[
\phi_+(w;s)=\pi i w^2-s\log w,
\qquad
\phi_-(w;s)=-\pi i w^2+(s-1)\log w.
\]
The common meromorphic amplitude is
\((e^{\pi iw}-e^{-\pi iw})^{-1}\); its poles must still be accounted
for when deforming a contour. Choose the saddle branches near the
positive real axis. Differentiating the displayed phases gives
\[
w_+^2=\frac{s}{2\pi i}=\frac{t}{2\pi}-\frac{i\sigma}{2\pi},
\qquad
w_-^2=\frac{s-1}{2\pi i}
=\frac{t}{2\pi}+\frac{i(1-\sigma)}{2\pi}.
\tag{S1}
\]
These are the usual two phase saddles for this representation, in two
different contour integrals; a decomposition into phase and amplitude
must be specified when discussing saddle locations. In expansions
using only \(t\) in the phase, the real reference point
\(a=\sqrt{t/(2\pi)}\) is used instead, with the remaining dependence
carried by the amplitude/correction terms.

At \(\sigma=1/2\), (S1) makes the selected saddles conjugates.
Off the line this conjugacy is lost smoothly. To see the scale, write
\(\sigma=1/2+\delta\). A direct square-root expansion, uniform for
bounded \(\sigma\), gives
\[
w_+=a-\frac{i\sigma}{4\pi a}+O(t^{-3/2}),\qquad
w_-=a+\frac{i(1-\sigma)}{4\pi a}+O(t^{-3/2}).
\tag{S2}
\]
Thus both saddles shift by approximately \(-i\delta/(4\pi a)\)
relative to their critical-line positions. This occurs at ordinary
nonzero values of zeta too. The phase Hessians satisfy exactly
\[
\partial_w^2\phi_+(w_+;s)=4\pi i,\qquad
\partial_w^2\phi_-(w_-;s)=-4\pi i.
\tag{S3}
\]
Consequently an off-line zero does not force either of these phase
saddles to degenerate. This is a statement about this specified
representation, not a theorem against all useful saddle or Stokes
analysis. Pole/cutoff transitions require their usual treatment and
are not identified with zeta zeros by this calculation.

The exact zero condition concerns the **complete contributions**.
With Riemann's auxiliary function \(\mathcal R\),
\[
\zeta(s)=\mathcal R(s)+
\chi(s)\overline{\mathcal R(1-\overline s)},\qquad
\chi(s)=\pi^{s-1/2}
\frac{\Gamma((1-s)/2)}{\Gamma(s/2)}.
\tag{S4}
\]
At \(s_0=1/2+\delta+it\), a hypothetical zero therefore says that
these full terms cancel. When \(\delta=0\), the two arguments of
\(\mathcal R\) coincide and a phase rotation gives the real-part
formula underlying \(Z(t)\). When \(\delta\ne0\), the arguments
are distinct reflected points \(1/2\pm\delta+it\). Their amplitudes
and phases must satisfy a complex cancellation condition. Neither
term is asserted nonzero separately. If finite expansions replace
them, all correction terms and the remainder must enter that condition.
Contour deformation produces the arithmetic sums through residues;
the remaining integrals receive the saddle expansion. The full
Riemann–Siegel expression is not one lone saddle contribution.

This suggests a sharper exploratory question: how does transverse
displacement change the relative amplitudes and phases of the complete
reflected contributions, with controlled remainders? A global assertion
that (S4) cannot cancel anywhere off the line in the critical strip is
RH-equivalent, and is classified once as a reformulation, not progress.
No independent inequality establishing such noncancellation has been
obtained. A bounded-region estimate would need its own explicit gain
over existing direct exclusion.

## Clarification: Riemann–Siegel at nonzero Newman time

10 November 2026, same session, intended author and LLM provenance as
above; actual model/effort unavailable. This answers the author's
question about why Riemann–Siegel was being used only at \(\lambda=0\).
The restriction was to the selected Green application, not to the
availability of evolved Riemann–Siegel methods. Earlier wording that
suggested a general restriction should be read with this correction.

There are three distinct parameters: the zeta ordinate, the transverse
coordinate \(\sigma=\Re s\), and Newman time \(\lambda\). Moving
\(s\) off the critical line evaluates the same time-zero function at
a different point. Changing \(\lambda\) changes the function itself:
\[
H_\lambda(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{\lambda u^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du,
\qquad \partial_\lambda H_\lambda=-\partial_z^2H_\lambda.
\]
Only at zero does the unchanged classical relation identify it with
\(\xi((1-iz)/2)/8\). The full-strip Riemann–Siegel representation
therefore applies directly to this initial function.

For \(\lambda>0\), the exact Gaussian identity is
\[
H_\lambda(z)=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
 e^{-v^2}H_0(z-2i\sqrt\lambda\,v)\,dv.
\tag{N1}
\]
It follows by Gaussian averaging of the cosine in the complete source
integral: the average multiplies each frequency by
\(e^{\lambda u^2}\). The super-exponentially decaying source permits
the interchange for this canonical field. In the opposite direction,
writing \(\lambda=-T<0\), the corresponding representation has real
shifts \(z+2\sqrt T\,v\), and gives ordinary forward heat smoothing.

Inserting the classical completed representation into (N1), with its
contours and remainders controlled, gives an evolved Riemann–Siegel
representation. This is already present in
[Polymath arXiv:1904.12438v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438v2#S4):
§4 (35)–(41) derives the Gaussian convolution and the evolved arithmetic
terms and remainders. The exact split (39) is stated for positive time,
\(z\) not purely imaginary, and any nonnegative integer cutoff.
Theorem 1.3 supplies explicit approximations on its region (5):
\(0<\lambda\le1/2\), \(0\le\Im z\le1\), \(\Re z\ge200\).
The paper denotes Newman time by \(t\); it is renamed \(\lambda\)
here. Its finite sums have weights
\(b_n^\lambda=\exp(\lambda(\log n)^2/4)\), as well as shifted
exponents, changed gamma factors and separate remainder bounds.
This is a passage check of the existing source, not a new proof audit.

The reason for selecting zero time in the Green discussion is manuscript
**(4.2)**, \(J_0=\mathcal L-P+G\). Its prime-power subtraction uses
the ordinary zeta Euler product on the safe boundary and at the center.
That exact arithmetic identity is not carried unchanged to nonzero
Newman time. The geometric Green identity still has its stated
time-dependent domain, and evolved field approximation is available;
one must separately obtain the required boundary and center information.
This does not assert the impossibility of every alternative arithmetic
representation at nonzero time.
