### Keep the clocks and objects separate

Write, throughout the direct realization,
\[
 \alpha=\gamma t,\qquad \lambda=\tfrac12-\alpha,
 \qquad h_*=H_{1/2}(0),\qquad d_0=H_\lambda/h_*.
 \tag{1}
\]
Then operator forward heat time can be identified with \(\alpha\) when its
initial Newman parameter is \(1/2\). Its derivative is \(\partial_\alpha
H=H_{kk}\); a physical-time derivative carries an additional \(\gamma\).
The auxiliary parameter in
\(q(\tau)e^{-\tau k^2}H_\lambda(k)\) does not have this role.

The direct nonzero-count traces are analytical quantities of the existing
instrument. Products of two such traces are products of expectations,
not joint probabilities for two mutually exclusive outcomes of one trial.
No post-count detector, two-copy experiment, or new physical access is
being supplied by the following identities.

## 3. Primary B03 candidate: neighboring counts in a resolving signed test

The [operator Gaussian-Laguerre section][laguerre] supplies an unusually
concrete consumer for joint count information. For normalized Gaussian
weight \(w_v(k-X)\) of variance \(v>0\), set
\[
 A_n(\lambda,X,v)=\int_{\mathbb R}w_v(k-X)
 \left[(H_\lambda^{(n+1)}(k))^2
       -H_\lambda^{(n)}(k)H_\lambda^{(n+2)}(k)\right]dk.
 \tag{2}
\]
This quantity is signed. Positivity of a Gram matrix or of each count
probability does not fix it.

### Exact count dictionary

At \(\alpha>0\), the direct count calculus gives
\[
 d_j=\frac{(-\alpha)^j}{j!h_*}H_\lambda^{(2j)},
 \qquad d_j''=-\frac{j+1}{\alpha}d_{j+1}.
\]
Put \(c_j=j!h_*/\alpha^j\), \(r_j=(j+1)/\alpha\). Substitution into
(2), with primes denoting real \(k\)-derivatives, gives
\[
 \boxed{A_{2j}=c_j^2\int w_v[(d_j')^2+r_jd_jd_{j+1}]},
 \tag{3}
\]
\[
 \boxed{A_{2j+1}=c_j^2\int w_v[r_j^2d_{j+1}^2
                                  +r_jd_j'd_{j+1}']}.
 \tag{4}
\]
For example,
\[
 A_0/h_*^2=\int w_v[(d_0')^2+d_0d_1/\alpha].
 \tag{5}
\]
The positive square competes with a signed neighboring-sector product.
This is exactly the sort of joint quantity left open by the high-count
result. Equations (3)–(5) are **exact translations**, not new sign theorems
or an advantage of the physical representation. The divisions at
\(\alpha=0\) must not be used; (2) remains the original scalar definition.

### The event consequence is already quantified

At a finite real multiplicity \(m\ge2\), let
\(a_m=H_{\lambda_*}^{(m)}(k_*)\ne0\). The inherited theorem gives, for
\(0<c<2\) and sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 A_{m-2}(\lambda_*-\varepsilon,k_*,c\varepsilon)
 =a_m^2\varepsilon(c/2-1)+\mathcal E_m(\varepsilon),
 \quad |\mathcal E_m|=O(\varepsilon^{3/2}).
 \tag{6}
\]
Its source gives the full error polynomial, not just the displayed order.
The neighborhood depends on source derivative bounds divided by
\(|a_m|\); it is not uniform over unknown events or multiplicities.
Even and odd levels in (3)–(4) cover every finite \(m\). Level zero alone
does not: the exact cubic control in the operator manuscript has a
nonreal pair while its level-zero Laguerre expression is strictly positive.

For a specified event class, a prospective source estimate must contradict
the **paid** negative margin in (6). For example, after the error is at
most \((1-c/2)a_m^2\varepsilon/2\), a separately proved lower bound
strictly above \(-(1-c/2)a_m^2\varepsilon/2\) would do so. Merely asking
for that bound over every unknown event is a target-shaped request,
not an independently established premise. It earns no milestone until
its source proof and quantitative margin exist.

The hierarchy's convenient characteristic has \(dv/d\lambda=2\).
The resolving path in (6) has \(dv/d\lambda=-c\). Therefore the known
hierarchy identity does not propagate the desired sign along that path
without another estimate. This is a concrete obstruction to that proposed
propagation, not an exclusion of every coupled-count method.

### A useful local clarification: existing first-jet tails already control A0

There is no need to claim an unproved higher-derivative count-tail theorem
to acquire level zero. Gaussian integration by parts gives
\[
 A_0(H)=2\int w_v(H')^2-\tfrac12\int w_v''H^2.
 \tag{7}
\]
For the finite count reconstruction \(\widehat H\), suppose the assessed
direct theorem supplies \(|H-\widehat H|\le\eta_0\) and
\(|H'-\widehat H'|\le\eta_1\) on the whole real line. Let
\(M_0=H_\lambda(0)\) and
\(M_1=\int_0^\infty u e^{\lambda u^2}\Phi(u)du\); these bound
\(|H|,|H'|\). Since \(\int|w_v''|\le2/v\),
\[
 |A_0(H)-A_0(\widehat H)|
 \le 2\eta_1(2M_1+\eta_1)
       +\frac{\eta_0}{v}(2M_0+\eta_0).
 \tag{8}
\]
Boundary terms vanish under the Gaussian weight; the finite retained
transforms and their derivatives are bounded. This proves the displayed
transfer directly. Quadrature and retained-source evaluation errors must
still be added if numbers replace the exact retained integrals.

At the resolving variance \(v=c\varepsilon\), the value error is
amplified by \(1/\varepsilon\). For a fixed event and fixed nonzero
source bounds, making this allowance negligible relative to the
\(a_2^2\varepsilon\) signal needs, for example,
\(\eta_0=o(\varepsilon^2)\), \(\eta_1=o(\varepsilon)\), with the
amplitude constants retained. Thus (8) is an **acquisition corollary**,
not a collision margin or a claim of practical feasibility. Higher levels
need their own paid derivative errors or the operator branch's existing
complete-source estimates.

### What OA adds to the same candidate

At a nondegenerate double event the exterior-field coefficients become
\[
 \ell=\frac{H'''}{3H''}=\frac{d_1'}{3d_1},\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{H''''}{6H''}-\frac{(H''')^2}{9(H'')^2}
       =-\frac{d_2}{3\alpha d_1}
        -\left(\frac{d_1'}{3d_1}\right)^2.
 \tag{9}
\]
These identities expose another finite-sector interaction; they do not
bound the potentially small denominator \(d_1\).

OA provides a better finite-pair conditioner than division by a shrinking
internal gap. Its regular second divided difference has errors
\(\epsilon_2/2,\epsilon_3/6,\epsilon_4/12\) in the deflated factor and
its first two evaluation derivatives. It still requires a positive
signed factor floor over the entire declared virtual-node box. The
direct H/H' tail theorem alone does not pay those derivative errors or
that floor.

With a paid upper exterior-force bound \(B\le\beta\), pair squared gap
\(q\) satisfies \(q'=-8-4qB\). The finite sufficient comparison is
\[
 q(0)>8E_\beta(\Delta),\qquad
 E_\beta(\Delta)=\int_0^\Delta e^{4\beta s}ds,
 \tag{10}
\]
together with OA's confinement and isolation conditions. Bounded force
at the collision itself is compatible with \(q'=-8\), so it is not a
contradiction. OA's tested finite pilot was redundant against an existing
certificate, and its Lehmer-input screen supplies a bound in the wrong
direction. Its exact quartic control collides despite negative exterior
force. These are scoped reasons to park those applications; they do not
rule out a new pair/slab with a genuinely useful signed bound.

**Recommended conceptual focus:** investigate the signed joint structure
in (3)–(5), initially level zero for a declared double-event class, using
the complete theta source. OA is an optional conditioner for a specific
finite-pair consequence, not a separate new campaign. The missing input
is one independent signed estimate with a useful margin, rather than
another identity or generic positive upper bound.

## 4. Secondary candidate: count tails as input to the Green acquisition

The newer OG theorem gives the existing direct tail a more substantial
consumer than pointwise approximation alone. Work at \(\lambda=0\),
so \(\alpha=1/2\), and retain the direct normalization:
\[
 \widehat H_{0,N}=H_{1/2}-h_*\sum_{j=1}^N d_j,
 \quad H_0-\widehat H_{0,N}=-h_*R_N,
 \quad |H_0-\widehat H_{0,N}|\le
 E_N:=\frac{h_*B_{N+1}^{(0)}}{1-Q_N}.
 \tag{11}
\]
The direct theorem defines the admissible eventual \(N\), \(B_j^{(0)}\)
and \(Q_N\); its constants are not newly numerically evaluated here.

Use OG's exact normalization
\[
 s=(1-ix)/2,\quad F(x)=D_0(x)H_0(x),\quad
 D_0(x)=\frac{16\pi^{s/2}}{s^2\Gamma(s/2)}.
\]
For an integer \(R\ge1\), let
\(M_D\ge\sup_{|x-X|\le R}|D_0(x)|\), and set
\(\widehat F_N=D_0\widehat H_{0,N}\). Any
\(0<\delta_N\le1\) satisfying \(\delta_N\ge M_DE_N\) pays the
compact field error; add retained-term and multiplier errors when
actually evaluating it. The multiplier is used only on this compact
interval, not under an uncontrolled whole-line bound.

With \(K(u)=1/(16\cosh(\pi u/8))\), OG then supplies
\[
 U_N=\int_{X-R}^{X+R}K(x-X)\log(|\widehat F_N(x)|+\delta_N)dx
      +\mathcal T(X,R)-P_Q(X)+\beta_Q+G(X,1),
\]
\[
 0\le U_N-J_0^{\rm G}(X)
 \le\mathcal E_{X,R}(\delta_N)+2\mathcal T(X,R)+2\beta_Q.
 \tag{12}
\]
Here every tail and the prime approximation are exactly OG's quantities;
\(\beta_Q=\tfrac23Q^{-3/2}\) for integer \(Q\ge2\). Directed numerical
evaluation would add its own enclosure width. This is a direct
substitution into the assessed theorem, not a new claim about the sign
or size of the canonical value.

This transfer requires **no positive real-axis modulus floor** and allows
multiple real zeros and vanishing approximants. It uses the exact
canonical off-axis anchor already proved in OG, not an extension of the
direct real-axis error bound to complex arguments. The logarithmic
modulus has a height-dependent small exponent, of the form
\(\delta^{1/n}\), so an efficient count-tail estimate does not imply an
efficient Green evaluation.

To exclude a zero of height at least \(d\in(0,1]\) within distance \(r\)
of \(X\), the **computed or independently bounded** upper value must
satisfy
\[
 \overline U_N< m(d,r)=\frac{d\sin(\pi/8)}{\cosh(\pi r/8)}.
 \tag{13}
\]
Smaller errors alone do not supply (13). Real boundary multiple zeros
contribute no interior Green mass, so this is not a simplicity test.
Moving a hypothetical positive-time collision to time zero would also
require a complete quantitative transport, not just its local heat germ.

Under avoid01, (11)–(12) are an acquisition interface; (13) is a finite
sufficient test with an unpaid value. Requiring zero Green defect at all
centers is the familiar RH reformulation and is not offered as a new
criterion. The useful question for D is whether a **correlated** count
remainder gives a better certified enclosure or cost than direct source
evaluation using the same information. The existing absolute tail alone
shows no such gain. OG already owns a bounded feasibility screen; do not
duplicate it merely with count notation.

## 5. What not to import as a missing constraint

OC's joint-source formula is helpful precisely because it identifies
unpaid information. Averaging is over a common spatial center, not a new
random theta source or a physical count experiment. One-point estimates
do not determine the same-center mixed moments. Pair-force conditioning
can need three-label information and a conditioning denominator; the
[earlier advisory][prior] derives that finite-cloud bookkeeping.

The compact-window time modulus transports an already supplied profile
and its error; it does not supply the profile or its favorable sign.
Its fixed positive Gaussian observation width and compact physical
window cannot silently be replaced by the shrinking variance in (6)
or the later Gaussian physical window. The Gaussian-projection error
theorems are useful numerical methods, but a certified zero inventory
followed by a statistic is not independent information restricting
unknown zero configurations.

Likewise, the operator branch's smooth transverse moments, density
allowances and local pair tube do not already force an observable
contradiction. Its all-order *density-derived allowance* is larger than
a single-pair signal in the stated comparison; that is not an impossibility
theorem for actual moments. A full path tube needs its exterior bounds
through the whole interval. These qualifications prevent the same
missing input from being renamed as a completed result.

## 6. Analytical collision-control screen

This pass applies the [recorded controls][controls] analytically; it does
not rerun their existing numerical certificates or claim new source-domain
audits. A finite test may legitimately pass a box missing a control's
event. Test rejection is required on the relevant defective domain.

| Control family | Screen of the present proposals; absent premise relative to canonical use |
| --- | --- |
| Euler-factor controls | Generic count calculus and local heat diagnostics persist. Literal conductor-one coefficients and the unchanged xi/zeta normalization change; their own Green baseline/anchor must be used. Their collision witnesses prevent a generic-positivity proof. |
| Three-/four-weighted squares | Square support, positive moments and selected reflection/primitive endpoints do not supply full reflection with complete unit coefficients. Their finite-source event certificates are not finite all-real upper thresholds. |
| Reflected super-Gaussian | The exact event at \((\lambda,k)=(1/4,\pi/4)\) obeys the direct instrument laws. It fails the canonical square expansion and tail comparability. At \(\lambda=1/4-\varepsilon\), (6) gives a negative A0 at variance \(c\varepsilon\); hence no generic nonnegative neighboring-count claim can hold. |
| Smooth log-concave and positive-primitive controls | Shape, primitive positivity and even extensive endpoint/asymptotic data fail to impose the literal theta expansion. They block replacing the unpaid signed estimate by those qualitative properties. |
| Finite source changes and finite-prime orbits | Complete source coefficients or full reflection change despite retained finite data. A finite count/source approximation to unchanged theta must carry its remainder; it is not one of these substituted physical sources. Controlled infinite assembly is not excluded. |
| Atomic fixed-event and exact finite-profile controls | Smooth-state premises need separate justification for atomic sources; finite profiles do not provide the complete theta source, full imaginary-axis profile/growth or eventual simplicity. Their continuous extensions retain the stated information limitation. |
| RH-conditional normalized endpoint controls | Ordinary Dirichlet-series arithmetic fails even while completion, functional equation, leading tail and residue can remain. Their real double zeros are permitted by (12), and no time-zero simplicity conclusion follows. |
| Fixed amplitude with variable kernels | The literal cosine oscillator/Newman readout changes. Keeping the amplitude alone cannot justify (1)–(6) for the changed observable. |
| Actual-theta three-profile relaxation | Fractional positive slack measures are not integer zero configurations or physical theta events. Finite profile constraints do not supply the full-source joint estimate sought here. |

Missing literal arithmetic is **not itself a favorable theta inequality**.
Each generic acquisition identity may remain valid for a control with its
own constants. On an actual interior defect, correct Green acquisition
converges to that control's positive response, not to zero. OA's quartic
and the operator cubic are additional exact local diagnostics, not smooth
canonical-source examples: negative force alone and level-zero positivity
alone, respectively, fail to exclude their collisions/nonreal pairs.
