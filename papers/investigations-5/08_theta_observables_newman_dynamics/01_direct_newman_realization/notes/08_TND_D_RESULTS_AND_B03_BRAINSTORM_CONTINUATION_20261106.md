## What was established

### 1. The score identity does not add an event constraint

With w_lambda proportional to exp(lambda v^2)Phi(v) and s=Phi'/Phi, complete-source integration by parts gives

\[
kC=2\lambda C_k-\mathbb E[s(X)\sin(kX)].
\]

Thus C=C_k=0 already forces the displayed correlation to vanish. Subtracting a linear term from s merely changes the C_k coefficient. E04 checks this identity and its nonindependence at the event. ### 2. High counts localize, and their signed traces take both signs

For r>=0 the complete canonical sum gives fixed positive constants c_-,c_+ such that

\[
c_-e^{9r-\pi e^{4r}}\le\Phi(r)\le c_+e^{9r-\pi e^{4r}}.
\]

Let nu_{j,lambda} have density proportional to r^(2j)exp(lambda r^2)Phi(r), and define a_j>0 by

\[
j=2\pi a_je^{4a_j}.
\]

Note06 proves, with constants independent of lambda in [-1/2,1/2],

\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_{j,\lambda}}(r-a_j)^2\le C_2a_j/j
\quad(j\ge J),
\]
\[
\frac{d_j(t,k)}{P_j(t)}=\cos(k a_j)+O_K(\sqrt{a_j/j})
\quad (|k|\le K,\ 0<t\le1/\gamma).
\]

The proof pays the complete n-sum, a positive normalizing interval, the growing left tail and the unbounded right tail. It does not extrapolate fixed-order domain bounds into an order-uniform theorem. Since a_j increases to infinity and its increments tend to zero, for **each fixed real k!=0** the normalized trace has limsup 1 and liminf -1. Its positive and negative subsequences may be selected uniformly over physical t>0. At k=0 the ratio is exactly one. At t=0, P_j=d_j=0 for j>0, so the physical quotient is undefined. No uniform finite sign index near k=0 or positive absolute probability floor follows.

This is a quantitative asymptotic diagnostic and a scoped exclusion of **eventual one-sign literal d_j**. It does not exclude phase-dependent patterns, correlated cancellations or finite-sector methods. The same center appeared in prior MC03 saddle work, credited in Note06; this positive scalar theorem does not solve the signed three-particle/full-chamber problem. Its exact even-derivative translation for H is in Note06 (22)–(23).

### 3. The complete omitted tail has a paid absolute first-jet error

Let alpha=gamma*t, and for q=0,2 define

\[
B_j^{(q)}=\frac{\alpha^j}{j!h_*}
\int_0^\infty r^{2j+q}e^{\lambda r^2}\Phi(r)\,dr,
\qquad B_j^{(0)}=P_j.
\]

With D=(1+sqrt(C2))^2, choose a finite N0 satisfying the explicit inequalities in Note07 (5). Then, for N>=N0,

\[
Q_N=\alpha D\frac{a_{N+1}^2}{N+1}\le\tfrac12,
\qquad Q_N=O(\alpha\log^2N/N).
\]

For R_N=sum_{j>N}d_j, uniformly on the **closed** physical slab and all real k,

\[
|R_N|\le\frac{B_{N+1}^{(0)}}{1-Q_N},\qquad
|\partial_kR_N|\le
\frac{\sqrt{B_{N+1}^{(0)}B_{N+1}^{(2)}}}{1-Q_N}.
\]

The finite reconstruction is

\[
\widehat H_{\lambda,N}=H_{1/2}-h_*\sum_{j=1}^Nd_j,
\qquad H_\lambda-\widehat H_{\lambda,N}=-h_*R_N,
\]

with the same derivative identity. Note07 independently proves complete summation and first differentiation using the exact positive Poisson tail under the initial source measure. It includes alpha=0 without dividing by vanished masses. Retained signed terms are untouched; all omitted counts are paid. The threshold and constants are conservative and uncomputed. **This is an absolute error, not a relative margin at a zero or collision.** No practical cutoff, second-readout-derivative theorem or physical sampling advantage is asserted.
