\begin{abstract}
We study the Sonin-type intersection projections associated with the
unimodular phases
\(v_\sigma(t)=e^{2\ii\theta(t)}
\zeta(\sigma+\ii t)/\zeta(\sigma-\ii t)\), \(\sigma>1/2\),
where \(\theta\) is the Riemann--Siegel phase. The cutoff operators are
Toeplitz and Hankel compressions of these symbols.
A boundary-resolvent formula separates the positive projection trace from
the phase derivative. We prove uniform logarithmic bounds for the
unit-window masses of the projection, a positive majorant, and the
compressed cutoff square, uniformly over all \(\sigma>1/2\).
These bounds give finite traces and uniform
frequency tails for every compact smooth source. As \(\sigma\downarrow1/2\),
the majorant and every fixed Abel regularization converge, after source
smoothing, to the counting measure of critical-line zeros. Every
subsequential limit of the exact projection measure is supported on those
zeros, with weights between zero and their multiplicities. The remaining
projection defect is positive and concentrates at cutoff spectral value
one. An explicit rational phase has the same local concentration and a
positive gap at every prelimit parameter, but its exact intersection
projection vanishes identically. For each finite Euler product, we also
prove an exact boundary identity at every positive exponent, retaining
the inverse compressed metric. Expanding prepared sources obstruct an
all-support correction bound at any fixed finite set of primes; the
comparison with primes chosen for each source support remains open.
A contour comparison retains the pole and all off-line zero crossings,
distinguishing the critical-zero measure from the complete Weil form.
For one explicit prepared polynomial probe, we prove that subexponential
growth of its signed prime covariance is equivalent to the Riemann
hypothesis and all-window positivity; weighted integral criteria are
equivalent as well. We also give an explicit linear lower bound for the
negative index of the centered archimedean reference and an outward
seven-source Gram certificate with lower bound \(1/20\).
The global signed growth estimate, survival of all critical mass in the
exact zeta projection, and the Riemann hypothesis remain unproved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and main results}\label{sec:intro}

A unimodular phase can develop concentrated phase variation even while
its multiplier tends strongly to the identity. For the zeta-phase family
studied here, this concentration produces a smoothed trace limit counting
critical-line zeros. The trace belongs to a positive Hankel square and to
its fixed Abel regularizations. Whether all this mass survives in the
exact Toeplitz kernel is a separate question.

The functional equation makes the endpoint symbol identically one.
The identified majorant and fixed Abel limits therefore retain the
critical-zero count, whereas the complete Weil form also contains
off-line zero terms. Even complete survival of the critical mass would
not by itself identify the limiting trace with that arithmetic form.
The unknown exact-projection weights may depend on the global phase;
their domination by the critical-zero measure does not establish their
independence from off-line zeros. Finite-parameter phase comparisons
retain those zeros through the crossing terms derived below.

Connes and Consani express the archimedean Weil distribution as a Sonin
trace with a prolate correction \cite{CCWeil}. Their quasi-inner framework
constructs semilocal Sonin spaces and maps between them \cite{CCQuasi}.
Connes, Consani, and Moscovici further analyze semilocal transport and
the associated Hilbert spaces of entire functions \cite{CCM}.
The relation of Sonine spaces to zeta zeros has earlier developments
in de Branges's framework and Burnol's work \cite{Burnol}. Spectral
interpretations that distinguish critical zeros from possible noncritical
zeros also predate the present construction \cite{ConnesTrace}.

The contribution considered here is the critical-boundary behavior of
one specified phase family: uniform source-trace estimates, a precise
localization of possible projection loss, and a solvable example showing
the limits of local phase information. We use standard two-projection
algebra \cite{Halmos}, classical Toeplitz--Hankel identities
\cite{Peller}, Schwartz-kernel smoothing, local estimates for
\(\zeta'/\zeta\), and elementary contour arguments. The quasi-inner
framework already identifies Sonin spaces with compressed multiplier
kernels \cite[Proposition 5.5]{CCQuasi}. Toeplitz-kernel and
Beurling--Malliavin theory \cite{MPInner,MPBM} provide a natural setting
for the remaining kernel question, but their hypotheses and conclusions
must be compared with this family before applying them to its limiting
frequency weights. We do not claim a new general positivity principle
or a new version of the explicit formula. A theorem-by-theorem priority
comparison for the particular boundary estimates remains outstanding.

We also apply the bounded-transport and boundary arguments to a finite
Euler product. Its cutoff gap persists for every \(\sigma>0\), including
\(\sigma=1/2\). Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-boundary} gives the exact
projection metric and smoothed boundary identity; after all active primes
are included, this yields \(Q=B_{S,1/2}-K_{S,1/2}\) on prepared sources.
Proposition~\ref{prop:fixed-place-obstruction} shows that
\(K_{S,\sigma}>B_{S,\sigma}\) for suitable expanding prepared sources
at every fixed finite \(S\). This rules out a fixed-place all-support
comparison with the partial arithmetic bulk. It does not decide the
comparison when \(S\) grows with the support. Detailed transport,
trace-ideal, and sign calculations appear in the internal note
\cite{FiniteEulerNote}; the proofs below record the essential steps.

Section~\ref{sec:source-criteria} then specializes the arithmetic question
to compact sources with the additional zero-mean condition. One fixed
polynomial profile suffices: its signed prime covariance has
subexponential growth if and only if RH holds. The proof combines an
elementary zero-location lemma for the profile's entire transform with an
exact Laplace identity for \(-\zeta'/\zeta\). Its translates still range
over unbounded separations, so the support quantifier is retained.
A separate concavity argument gives a lower bound of
\(\max(0,\lceil Lt_*/\pi\rceil-4)\) for the centered reference's negative
index, with \(t_*/\pi\) certified near \(1.949925482\).
Finally, seven translates of that same profile have Gram matrix strictly
larger than \(\Id/20\), although the elementary absolute row-sum bound is
negative. This last statement concerns one finite-dimensional subspace.
The global growth estimate is open; no new unconditional positivity
principle or literature priority for the scalar criterion is claimed.

Here is the operator setting. On \(\HH=L^2(\mathbb R,\dd x)\), let
\[
P=1_{(0,\infty)},\qquad \chi=\Id-P,\qquad
(\J h)(x)=h(-x).
\]
With the Fourier convention fixed in Section~\ref{sec:setup}, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-intro}
v_\sigma(t)=
\frac{\Gamma(1/4+\ii t/2)}{\Gamma(1/4-\ii t/2)}
\pi^{-\ii t}
\frac{\zeta(\sigma+\ii t)}{\zeta(\sigma-\ii t)},
\qquad
\Fcal_\sigma=\J v_\sigma(D).
\end{equation}
The operator \(\Fcal_\sigma\) is a selfadjoint unitary involution. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operators-intro}
T_\sigma=\chi\Fcal_\sigma P,\quad
C_\sigma=\chi\Fcal_\sigma\chi,\quad
\Pi_\sigma=\operatorname{proj}\ker(T_\sigma|_{P\HH}),
\quad L_\sigma=P-T_\sigma^*T_\sigma.
\end{equation}
All compressed operators are extended by zero on the complementary
half-line. In particular \(0\le\Pi_\sigma\le L_\sigma\le P\).
We call
\[
H_\sigma=L_\sigma-\Pi_\sigma
\]
the \emph{return operator}; this is an independently defined positive
operator.

For a bounded positive operator \(G\), its frequency measure is initially
defined with infinite values allowed by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:measure-intro}
\nu_G(E)=\norm{1_E(D)G^{1/2}}_{\Stwo}^2.
\end{equation}
Local finiteness is part of the theorem. Write
\(\nu_\sigma=\nu_{\Pi_\sigma}\), and let
\[
\mucrit=\sum_{\rho=1/2+\ii\gamma}m_\rho\delta_\gamma
\]
sum over distinct nontrivial critical-line zeros, including both signs
of the ordinates, with multiplicity \(m_\rho\).

\begin{theorem}[Uniform bounds and the critical limit]\label{thm:main}
There is \(M<\infty\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-window}
\nu_G([j,j+1])\le M\log(2+|j|),\qquad j\in\mathbb Z,
\end{equation}
uniformly for every \(\sigma>1/2\) and
\(G\in\{\Pi_\sigma,L_\sigma,C_\sigma^2\}\).
Here \(C_\sigma^2\) acts on \(\chi\HH\) and is extended by zero on
\(P\HH\), as in the other compressions.
For every \(f\in\Sch(\mathbb R)\), all corresponding positive sandwich
traces are finite, with uniform tails in frequency. Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The projections \(\Pi_\sigma\) tend strongly to zero as
\(\sigma\downarrow1/2\), but
\[
\Tr(f(D)L_\sigma f(D)^*)\longrightarrow
\int |f|^2\dd\mucrit.
\]
\item With \(R_\sigma[f]=\Tr(f(D)H_\sigma f(D)^*)\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-defect}
\Tr(f(D)\Pi_\sigma f(D)^*)
=\int|f|^2\dd\mucrit-R_\sigma[f]+o_f(1).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\[
0\le R_\sigma[f]\le\int|f|^2\dd\mucrit+o_f(1).
\]
Every sequence \(\sigma_n\downarrow1/2\) has a subsequence for which
\(\nu_{\sigma_n}\) converges vaguely. Every such limit is
\[
\nu_*=\sum_{\rho=1/2+\ii\gamma}c_\gamma\delta_\gamma,
\qquad 0\le c_\gamma\le m_\rho.
\]
\item For each fixed \(0\le r<1\), the positive contractions
\[
\Pi_{\sigma,r}=P-T_\sigma^*(\Id_\chi-rC_\sigma^2)^{-1}T_\sigma
\]
satisfy
\[
\Tr(f(D)\Pi_{\sigma,r}f(D)^*)\longrightarrow
\int |f|^2\dd\mucrit.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The theorem does not assert convergence of the exact projection measures
or equality \(c_\gamma=m_\rho\).
Section~\ref{sec:return} identifies the remaining defect as mass
approaching the endpoint of the spectrum of \(C_\sigma^2\).
Section~\ref{sec:model} gives an explicit family for which every exact
projection is zero although the limiting majorant measure is a unit atom.
It also computes the dependence on a jointly varying Abel parameter.

To state the arithmetic consequence, let \(F\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\),
let \(C_F\) be convolution by \(F\), and put
\[
a_F(z)=\widehat F(z)\overline{\widehat F(\overline z)}.
\]
Write \(B_\sigma[F]=\Tr(C_F\Pi_\sigma C_F^*)\) and
\(\Zcrit[F]=\int|\widehat F|^2\dd\mucrit\).
For the prepared sources
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prepared}
F=(-\partial_x^2+1/4)h,\qquad h\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R),
\end{equation}
the pole terms in the critical explicit formula vanish.
With \(Q\) normalized in Section~\ref{sec:arithmetic}, the comparison is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-arithmetic}
B_\sigma[F]-Q[F]
=-R_\sigma[\widehat F]-\Zoff[F]+o_F(1),\qquad
\Zoff[F]=2\Rea\sum_{\beta>1/2}m_{\beta+\ii\gamma}
a_F\bigl(\gamma+\ii(\beta-1/2)\bigr).
\end{equation}
At nonreal arguments \(a_F\) is not a modulus square.
Consequently \(\Zoff\) has no asserted sign. The identified positive limits in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} count critical-line zeros; unconditional
identification with the complete Weil form is a separate problem.

\section{Conventions, cutoff geometry, and smoothing}\label{sec:setup}

We use inner products antilinear in the first variable and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourier}
\widehat h(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}h(x)e^{-\ii tx}\dd x,\qquad
h(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}\widehat h(t)e^{\ii tx}\frac{\dd t}{2\pi}.
\end{equation}
The notation \(b(D)\) denotes the Fourier multiplier with symbol \(b\).
The Riemann--Siegel phase in the abstract is
\[
\theta(t)=\Ima\log\Gamma(1/4+\ii t/2)-(t/2)\log\pi,
\]
with the continuous logarithm normalized at \(t=0\).
The reflection \(\J\) is linear. No complex conjugation is included
in \(\J\). The symbols \(\norm{\cdot}_{\Sone}\) and
\(\norm{\cdot}_{\Stwo}\) denote trace and Hilbert--Schmidt norms.

At a zero or pole on the line \(\Rea s=\sigma\), the numerator and
denominator of the zeta ratio in \eqref{eq:phase-intro} have the same
order as functions of real \(t\). The ratio therefore has a smooth
removable extension near that frequency. We always use this extension,
not a one-sided distributional limit in \(\sigma\).
On the real line
\[
|v_\sigma|=1,\qquad v_\sigma(-t)=\overline{v_\sigma(t)}.
\]
These identities imply that \(\Fcal_\sigma\) is a selfadjoint
unitary involution and preserves real functions.

For part of the argument it is useful to work with an arbitrary smooth
unimodular phase \(v\) having this symmetry. Set \(V=v(D)\),
\(\Fcal=\J V\), and use \(C,T,L,\Pi,H\) without subscripts.
The elementary block identities are
\begin{align}
TT^*&=\Id_\chi-C^2,\label{eq:tt}\\
L&=P V^*\chi V P=(P\Fcal P)^2,\label{eq:Lfactor}\\
C^2&=\chi V^*PV\chi,\label{eq:Cfactor}\\
\Qcal:=V^*PV-P
&=\begin{pmatrix}-L&T^*C\\ CT&C^2\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{on }P\HH\oplus\chi\HH.\label{eq:Qblocks}
\end{align}
Indeed \(V=\J\Fcal\), \(\J P\J=\chi\), and \(\Fcal^2=\Id\).
On \(P\HH\), \(L=\Id_{P\HH}-T^*T\); hence \(0\le L\le P\).
The kernel projection of \(T\) is the spectral projection of \(L\)
at \(1\), so \(L\Pi=\Pi\) and \(0\le\Pi\le L\).

\subsection{Toeplitz and Hankel interpretation}\label{sec:dictionary}

Under \eqref{eq:fourier}, \(P\HH\) is realized as the Hardy space of
boundary values analytic in the lower frequency half-plane. For a bounded
symbol \(v\), use distinct notation for its Toeplitz and Hankel blocks:
\[
\mathfrak T_v=Pv(D)P:P\HH\to P\HH,\qquad
\mathfrak H_v=\chi v(D)P:P\HH\to\chi\HH.
\]
For the unimodular symmetric symbols considered here,
\begin{align}
T&=\J\mathfrak T_v,&
L&=\mathfrak H_v^*\mathfrak H_v,&
C&=\J\mathfrak H_{\bar v}^*,\label{eq:dictionary}\\
\mathfrak T_{\bar v}\mathfrak T_v
&=\Id_{P\HH}-\mathfrak H_v^*\mathfrak H_v,&
C^2&=\mathfrak H_{\bar v}\mathfrak H_{\bar v}^*.\notag
\end{align}
The projection \(\Pi\) is therefore the projection onto
\(\ker\mathfrak T_v\), or equivalently onto the eigenspace of
\(L=|\mathfrak H_v|^2\) at \(1\). On \(P\HH\),
\[
\Pi=1_{\{1\}}(L),\qquad H=L1_{[0,1)}(L).
\]
Thus \(H\), the return operator, is different from the Hankel block
\(\mathfrak H_v\). These are classical compression identities
\cite{Peller}; the assertions about the particular zeta family require
the estimates proved below.

\begin{lemma}[Schwartz cutoff blocks]\label{lem:hankel}
If \(q\in\Sch(\mathbb R)\), then \(Pq(D)\chi\),
\(\chi q(D)P\), and \([P,q(D)]\) are trace class.
Their trace norms depend continuously on Schwartz seminorms of \(q\).
Furthermore,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comm-energy}
\norm{[P,q(D)]}_{\Stwo}^2
=\frac1{4\pi^2}\iint_{\mathbb R^2}
\frac{|q(t)-q(s)|^2}{(t-s)^2}\dd t\dd s.
\end{equation}
The last identity also applies whenever the right side is finite and
\(q\) is bounded.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
After reflection of the negative coordinate, the kernel of
\(Pq(D)\chi\) is \(\check q(x+y)\) for \(x,y>0\).
Choose a smooth function equal to one on \([0,\infty)\) and zero
on \((-\infty,-1]\) in each variable. Multiplying
\(\check q(x+y)\) by these cutoffs gives a Schwartz kernel on
\(\mathbb R^2\). Expansion in Hermite functions, or repeated application
of the harmonic oscillator in both variables, gives rapidly decaying
matrix coefficients. Thus the extended operator is trace class, with
trace norm controlled by finitely many Schwartz seminorms.
Compression proves the first assertion. This is also the smoothing
argument used in \cite[Appendix D]{CCWeil}.

In Fourier coordinates with measure \(\dd t/(2\pi)\), the kernel of
\(P\) is
\[
\pi\delta(t-s)-\ii\operatorname{pv}\frac1{t-s}.
\]
The commutator cancels the delta term and has kernel
\(-\ii(q(s)-q(t))/(t-s)\). Its squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm gives
\eqref{eq:comm-energy}. Approximation, or the same kernel calculation
in distributions, proves the finite-energy version.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Localized phase trace]\label{lem:phase-trace}
For \(b\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\), let \(\phi'\) be the smooth
local phase derivative of \(v\), so \(v'=\ii\phi'v\).
The positive operators \(b(D)Lb(D)^*\) and \(b(D)C^2b(D)^*\)
are trace class, as is \(b(D)\Qcal b(D)^*\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-trace}
-\Tr(b(D)\Qcal b(D)^*)=\int |b(t)|^2\phi'(t)\frac{\dd t}{2\pi}.
\end{equation}
Writing \(X_a=Pa(D)\chi\), \(a=|b|^2\), gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L-trace}
\Tr(b(D)Lb(D)^*)=
\int a(t)\phi'(t)\frac{\dd t}{2\pi}
+\Tr(b(D)C^2b(D)^*)+2\Rea\Tr_{\chi\HH}(CTX_a).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Whenever \(q,qv\) are Schwartz,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-comm}
q(D)\Qcal
=V^*\bigl([P,(qv)(D)]-[P,q(D)]V\bigr)\in\Sone.
\end{equation}
Apply this first with \(q=b\). Commuting \(b(D)\) past \(P\)
and \(\chi\), using Lemma~\ref{lem:hankel}, proves that \(b(D)\)
times every block in \eqref{eq:Qblocks} is trace class. In particular
the two positive sandwiches are trace class.

The Fourier kernel of \(\Qcal\) is
\[
-\ii\frac{\overline{v(t)}v(s)-1}{t-s}.
\]
Its diagonal value is \(\ii\overline v(t)v'(t)=-\phi'(t)\).
The kernel of its sandwich by \(b\) is smooth and compactly supported
in both frequency variables. The trace is therefore its diagonal
integral, proving \eqref{eq:bulk-trace}.
To take block traces, first cycle
\(\Tr(b(D)\Qcal b(D)^*)=\Tr(a(D)\Qcal)\):
\(b(D)\Qcal\) is trace class. The same ideal argument applies to
\(a(D)\) times each block of \eqref{eq:Qblocks}. The diagonal block
traces then give \eqref{eq:L-trace}, and the crossing term can be cycled
to the negative half-line because \(X_a\in\Sone\) and all other
factors are bounded. One must not split \(\Qcal=V^*PV-P\) into
two separately traced terms: \(a(D)P\) need not be trace class, so
formal cancellation by \(\Tr(aV^*PV)=\Tr(aP)\) is unavailable.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
For \(G\ge0\), an orthonormal-basis expansion of
\eqref{eq:measure-intro} and Tonelli's theorem give countable additivity
and
\[
\Tr(b(D)Gb(D)^*)=\int|b|^2\dd\nu_G.
\]
At a parameter where the measure is locally finite it is absolutely
continuous: a Lebesgue-null frequency set has zero spectral projection
\(1_E(D)\). This does not prevent an atomic limit of the measures.
\end{remark}

\section{Actual projections and boundary formulas}\label{sec:boundary}

\subsection{The bounded-transport region}

Let \(\Fcal_\infty=\J v_\infty(D)\), where
\[
v_\infty(t)=
\pi^{-\ii t}\frac{\Gamma(1/4+\ii t/2)}
{\Gamma(1/4-\ii t/2)}.
\]
This is the logarithmic form of the unitary cosine transform at physical
cutoff one. Its compressed negative-half-line operator
\(C_\infty=\chi\Fcal_\infty\chi\) is compact and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c0}
c_0:=\norm{C_\infty}<1.
\end{equation}
One way to see strictness is to return to the finite physical interval:
the cosine kernel is smooth there and hence compact. Equality of its
norm to one would give a nonzero function supported in that interval
whose cosine transform is also supported there. The transform of a
compactly supported function is entire, and vanishing on the exterior
interval forces it to be zero. Compactness makes this exclusion of an
extremal vector sufficient for \eqref{eq:c0}; compare \cite{CCWeil}.

For \(\sigma>1\), put \(U_ah(x)=h(x-a)\) and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport}
D_\sigma=\sum_{n\ge1}\mu(n)n^{-\sigma}U_{\log n},
\qquad
D_\sigma^{-1}=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-\sigma}U_{\log n}.
\end{equation}
Both series converge absolutely in operator norm; their multipliers
are \(1/\zeta(\sigma+\ii t)\) and \(\zeta(\sigma+\ii t)\).
Both preserve \(P\HH\). Set
\[
\ell_\sigma=\zeta(\sigma)^{-1},\qquad
u_\sigma=\frac{\zeta(\sigma)}{\zeta(2\sigma)},\qquad
D_+=D_\sigma|_{P\HH},\quad
D_-=\chi D_\sigma\chi|_{\chi\HH}.
\]
Then \(\norm{D_+}\le u_\sigma\) and
\(\norm{D_-^{-1}}\le\ell_\sigma^{-1}\).
The inverse assertion for \(D_-\) follows by compressing
\(D_\sigma^{-1}\): the triangularity
\(\chi D_\sigma P=\chi D_\sigma^{-1}P=0\) makes the two products identities.

\begin{proposition}[Gap and exact metric]\label{prop:gap}
For \(\sigma>1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
T_\sigma=D_-T_\infty D_+^{-1},\qquad
\Id_\chi-C_\sigma^2\ge
g_\sigma\Id_\chi,\quad
g_\sigma=(\ell_\sigma/u_\sigma)^2(1-c_0^2)>0,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection}
\Pi_\sigma=P-T_\sigma^*(\Id_\chi-C_\sigma^2)^{-1}T_\sigma.
\end{equation}
It is the ordinary orthogonal projection onto
\(D_\sigma\Ran\Pi_\infty\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The multiplier relation gives
\(\Fcal_\sigma=D_\sigma\Fcal_\infty D_\sigma^{-1}\).
Compress and use the triangularity just noted to obtain
the formula for \(T_\sigma\).
For \(y\in\chi\HH\),
\[
\norm{T_\sigma^*y}
\ge u_\sigma^{-1}\norm{T_\infty^*D_-^*y}
\ge u_\sigma^{-1}(1-c_0^2)^{1/2}\ell_\sigma\norm y.
\]
This proves the gap. Thus \(T_\sigma\) is onto, and the usual kernel
projection formula gives \eqref{eq:projection}.
The factorization of \(T_\sigma\) identifies its kernel.

Equivalently, if
\(M_\sigma=\Pi_\infty D_\sigma^*D_\sigma\Pi_\infty\)
on \(\Ran\Pi_\infty\), then
\[
\Pi_\sigma=D_\sigma\Pi_\infty M_\sigma^{-1}
\Pi_\infty D_\sigma^*.
\]
The inverse compressed metric is necessary in this formula.
\end{proof}

In Hardy-space language, with \(h_\sigma(t)=\zeta(\sigma+\ii t)\),
both \(h_\sigma\) and its inverse are bounded analytic in the lower
frequency half-plane when \(\sigma>1\). The corresponding
Wiener--Hopf factorization is
\[
\mathfrak T_{v_\sigma}
=\mathfrak T_{1/\overline{h_\sigma}}
\mathfrak T_{v_\infty}\mathfrak T_{h_\sigma}.
\]
Its kernel is \(h_\sigma^{-1}\ker\mathfrak T_{v_\infty}\), consistent
with the causal transport and its compressed metric above. This bounded
factorization is not asserted in the critical strip.

\subsection{A boundary resolvent with a finite crossing strip}

For \(F\in C_c^\infty\), put \(a=|\widehat F|^2\), and
\[
a_e(t)=\frac{a(t)+a(-t)}2,\qquad X_{F,e}=Pa_e(D)\chi.
\]
The phase derivative is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-deriv}
\phi_\sigma'(t)=\gamma_\infty(t)
+2\Rea\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+\ii t),\quad
\gamma_\infty(t)=\Rea\psi(1/4+\ii t/2)-\log\pi,
\end{equation}
with removable values used on exceptional lines.
Write
\[
\kappa_F(x)=\int\overline{F(y)}F(y+x)\dd y,\qquad
\Gamma[F]=\int\gamma_\infty(t)|\widehat F(t)|^2\frac{\dd t}{2\pi},
\]
and, for any real \(\sigma\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W}
W_\sigma[F]=\sum_{p,m\ge1}(\log p)p^{-m\sigma}
\{\kappa_F(m\log p)+\kappa_F(-m\log p)\}.
\end{equation}
If the support of \(F\) has diameter at most \(L\), only delays
\(m\log p<L\) contribute. All active prime powers are retained.

\begin{theorem}[Boundary-resolvent representation]\label{thm:boundary}
For \(\sigma>1\) and \(F\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-formula}
B_\sigma[F]=\Gamma[F]-W_\sigma[F]+K_\sigma[F],
\end{equation}
where the correction is independently represented by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:short-boundary}
K_\sigma[F]=
2\Rea\Tr_{\chi\HH}
\left(C_\sigma(\Id_\chi-C_\sigma^2)^{-1}T_\sigma X_{F,e}\right).
\end{equation}
The kernel of \(X_{F,e}\) is
\(1_{x>0>y}\check a_e(x-y)\); it vanishes unless
\(-L<y<0<x<L\) and \(x-y<L\).
With \(c_\sigma=\norm{C_\sigma}<1\), the series
\begin{equation}\label{eq:return-series}
K_\sigma[F]=2\Rea\sum_{n\ge0}
\Tr_{\chi\HH}(C_\sigma^{2n+1}T_\sigma X_{F,e})
\end{equation}
has truncation error after \(n=N\) bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:return-series-error}
\frac{2c_\sigma^{2N+3}}{\sqrt{1-c_\sigma^2}}\,
\norm{X_{F,e}}_{\Sone}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For fixed \(\sigma>1\), derivatives of the zeta ratio are bounded
at each order; the gamma ratio and its derivatives have at most
polynomial growth. Thus \eqref{eq:phase-comm} applies to every Schwartz
multiplier. The trace calculation of Lemma~\ref{lem:phase-trace}
extends from compact frequency tests by trace-norm approximation.
The absolutely convergent differentiated Euler series in
\eqref{eq:phase-deriv} gives the bulk
\(\Gamma[F]-W_\sigma[F]\).

For the remaining algebra suppress \(\sigma\), and set
\(s=(\Id_\chi-C^2)^{1/2}\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:projection} gives
\[
\Pi=L-H,\qquad H=T^*C^2s^{-2}T\ge0.
\]
Since \(0\le H\le L\), its source sandwich is trace class.
The first boundary formula is consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:long-boundary}
K_\sigma[F]=
\Tr(C_FC^2C_F^*)-\Tr(C_FHC_F^*)
+2\Rea\Tr_{\chi\HH}(CTPa(D)\chi).
\end{equation}
It follows directly from \eqref{eq:Qblocks}, without defining \(K\)
by arithmetic subtraction.

Real structure makes the frequency measure of \(\Pi\) even, and the
bulk derivative is even. We may therefore replace \(a\) by \(a_e\)
in \(K\). The multiplier \(a_e(D)\) commutes with \(\Fcal\).
The isometry \(U=T^*s^{-1}:\chi\HH\to(P-\Pi)\HH\)
gives \(H=UC^2U^*\). The subspace
\(\mathcal M=U\chi\HH\oplus\chi\HH\) reduces both \(\Fcal\) and
\(\Qcal\). Indeed \(\Ran\Pi\) is invariant under the involution,
and \(\Qcal\Pi=-\Pi\); both operators are selfadjoint.
On \(\mathcal M\), in the coordinates supplied by \(U\),
\[
\Fcal=\begin{pmatrix}-C&s\\s&C\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Pi+\Qcal=\begin{pmatrix}-C^2&sC\\Cs&C^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Write the compression of \(a_e(D)\) as
\(\begin{pmatrix}A_{++}&A_{+-}\\A_{+-}^*&A_{--}\end{pmatrix}\).
Its commutation with \(\Fcal|_{\mathcal M}\) gives
\[
sA_{--}-A_{++}s=CA_{+-}+A_{+-}C,\qquad
A_{+-}=s^{-1}TX_{F,e}\in\Sone.
\]
The diagonal products \(A_{++}C^2,A_{--}C^2\) are trace class:
compressing \(a_e(D)\Qcal\) gives diagonal blocks
\(-A_{++}C^2+A_{+-}Cs\) and \(A_{+-}^*sC+A_{--}C^2\).
The return trace with weight \(a_e\) equals
\(\Tr(CA_{++}C)=\Tr(A_{++}C^2)\), since \(H=UC^2U^*\).
The positive-product trace identity here can be justified with spectral
cutoffs \(1_{\{|C|\ge1/n\}}\): \(C\) has a bounded inverse on
each cutoff subspace, cyclicity applies there, and monotone convergence
of the positive sandwich gives the identity on the whole space.
This argument does not require an eigenbasis for \(C\).
Multiplying the commutation relation on the right by \(Cs^{-1}C\),
and using cyclicity with a trace-class factor, gives
\[
\Tr(C^2A_{--}-C^2A_{++})
=2\Tr(C^3s^{-1}A_{+-}).
\]
Adding the crossing traces and using \(C^2+s^2=\Id_\chi\) reduces
\eqref{eq:long-boundary} to
\(2\Rea\Tr(Cs^{-1}A_{+-})\), which is \eqref{eq:short-boundary}.

The Neumann series for \(s^{-2}\) proves
\eqref{eq:return-series}. The omitted bounded factor is
\(C^{2N+3}(\Id_\chi-C^2)^{-1}T\).
Multiplication by its adjoint and \eqref{eq:tt} give squared norm
at most \(c_\sigma^{4N+6}/(1-c_\sigma^2)\).
The trace-ideal inequality proves \eqref{eq:return-series-error}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Archimedean calibration]\label{rem:calibration}
As \(\sigma\to\infty\), \(D_\sigma,D_\sigma^{-1}\to\Id\) in norm.
The gap and the fixed trace-class crossing block show directly that
\eqref{eq:short-boundary} tends to its archimedean value.
If \(C_\infty\xi_n=\lambda_n\xi_n\) and
\(\zeta_n=T_\infty^*\xi_n/\sqrt{1-\lambda_n^2}\), that value is
\[
2\Rea\sum_n\frac{\lambda_n}{\sqrt{1-\lambda_n^2}}
\inner{\zeta_n}{a_e(D)\xi_n}.
\]
The smooth finite-interval cosine kernel makes \(C_\infty\) trace
class, so the sum is absolutely convergent.
This is the prolate correction of
\cite[Theorem 4.6]{CCWeil} in the 2021 version; the corresponding
statement is Theorem 4.7 in the 2020 arXiv version.
The correction is generally nonzero. If
\(\operatorname{supp}F\subset[-\tfrac12\log2,\tfrac12\log2]\)
and \(\widehat F(\pm\ii/2)=0\), the restricted-support bound of
\cite[Theorem 6.11]{CCWeil}, with \(g(\rho)=F(\log\rho)\), gives
\[
K_\infty[F]=B_\infty[F]-\Gamma[F]
\le c\,|\widehat F(0)|^2
\]
for the constant \(c\) in that theorem. In particular
\(K_\infty[F]\le0\) when also \(\widehat F(0)=0\), as in
\cite[Theorem 1]{CCWeil}. The preparation \eqref{eq:prepared}
alone does not impose this additional condition:
\(\widehat F(0)=\widehat h(0)/4\).
The series \eqref{eq:return-series} is an analytic expansion, with
convergence controlled by \(1-c_\sigma^2\); its error bound does not
assert efficient numerical convergence when that gap is small.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Finite Euler products at every positive exponent}
\label{sec:finite-euler}

For a finite set of primes \(S\) and \(\sigma>0\), define
\[
\zeta_S(s)=\prod_{p\in S}(1-p^{-s})^{-1},\qquad
v_{S,\sigma}(t)=v_\infty(t)
\frac{\zeta_S(\sigma+\ii t)}{\zeta_S(\sigma-\ii t)},\qquad
\Fcal_{S,\sigma}=\J v_{S,\sigma}(D).
\]
Use the same cutoff definitions for \(T_{S,\sigma},C_{S,\sigma},
L_{S,\sigma}\), and \(\Pi_{S,\sigma}\). The empty set gives the
archimedean operators, denoted by subscript \(\infty\) as above;
that subscript does not denote a limit over prime sets. Put
\begin{align}
\mathcal D_{S,\sigma}&=\prod_{p\in S}(\Id-p^{-\sigma}U_{\log p}),&
\mathcal D_{S,\sigma}^{-1}
&=\sum_{n\in\mathbb N_S}n^{-\sigma}U_{\log n},
\label{eq:finite-transport}\\
\ell_{S,\sigma}&=\prod_{p\in S}(1-p^{-\sigma}),&
u_{S,\sigma}&=\prod_{p\in S}(1+p^{-\sigma}),\notag
\end{align}
where \(\mathbb N_S\) consists of the positive integers with all prime
divisors in \(S\). The notation \(\mathcal D_{S,\sigma}\)
distinguishes the transport from the frequency operator \(D\).
The inverse series converges absolutely in operator norm. Both operators preserve \(P\HH\), and
\(\ell_{S,\sigma}\norm z\le\norm{\mathcal D_{S,\sigma}z}
\le u_{S,\sigma}\norm z\).

\begin{proposition}[Finite Euler boundary identity]\label{prop:finite-boundary}
For every finite \(S\), every \(\sigma>0\), and every
\(F\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R;\mathbb C)\), the following statements hold.
On \(\mathcal K_\infty=\Ran\Pi_\infty\), define
\[
M_{S,\sigma}
=\left.\Pi_\infty \mathcal D_{S,\sigma}^*\mathcal D_{S,\sigma}\Pi_\infty
\right|_{\mathcal K_\infty}.
\]
Then \(\ell_{S,\sigma}^2\Id\le M_{S,\sigma}
\le u_{S,\sigma}^2\Id\), and
\begin{align}
\Id_\chi-C_{S,\sigma}^2
&\ge (\ell_{S,\sigma}/u_{S,\sigma})^2(1-c_0^2)\Id_\chi,
\label{eq:finite-gap}\\
\Pi_{S,\sigma}
&=\mathcal D_{S,\sigma}\Pi_\infty M_{S,\sigma}^{-1}
\Pi_\infty \mathcal D_{S,\sigma}^*.
\label{eq:finite-metric}
\end{align}
The inverse in \eqref{eq:finite-metric} acts on
\(\mathcal K_\infty\). Moreover,
\begin{align}
B_{S,\sigma}[F]
&:=\norm{C_F\Pi_{S,\sigma}}_{\Stwo}^2
=\Gamma[F]-W_{S,\sigma}[F]+K_{S,\sigma}[F],
\label{eq:finite-boundary}\\
W_{S,\sigma}[F]
&=\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{m\ge1}(\log p)p^{-m\sigma}
\{\kappa_F(m\log p)+\kappa_F(-m\log p)\},\notag\\
K_{S,\sigma}[F]
&=2\Rea\Tr_{\chi\HH}\left(
C_{S,\sigma}(\Id_\chi-C_{S,\sigma}^2)^{-1}
T_{S,\sigma}X_{F,e}\right).
\label{eq:finite-correction}
\end{align}
All these quantities are finite. The finite crossing strip and
return-series estimate of Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary} hold with
\(c_{S,\sigma}=\norm{C_{S,\sigma}}<1\). No estimate is asserted
uniformly in growing \(S\) or as \(\sigma\downarrow0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The multiplier of \(\mathcal D_{S,\sigma}\) is
\(d(t)=\zeta_S(\sigma+\ii t)^{-1}\). Reflection reverses its
argument, giving
\[
\mathcal D_{S,\sigma}\Fcal_\infty \mathcal D_{S,\sigma}^{-1}
=\J\bigl(v_\infty(t)d(-t)/d(t)\bigr)(D)
=\Fcal_{S,\sigma}.
\]
Triangularity therefore gives
\(T_{S,\sigma}=\mathcal D_-T_\infty\mathcal D_+^{-1}\), with the two
compressions defined as in Proposition~\ref{prop:gap}. Its proof gives
\eqref{eq:finite-gap} and the ordinary orthogonal projection
\eqref{eq:finite-metric}. The transport is boundedly invertible,
not assumed isometric.

At fixed \(S,\sigma\), every derivative of the finite Euler factors
is bounded, so \(qv_{S,\sigma}\) is Schwartz for every Schwartz \(q\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:hankel} and \eqref{eq:phase-comm} make the smoothed
blocks trace class; \(0\le\Pi_{S,\sigma}\le L_{S,\sigma}\)
proves finiteness of \(B_{S,\sigma}\) before any arithmetic subtraction.
The phase derivative is
\[
\phi'_{S,\sigma}(t)=\gamma_\infty(t)
-\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{m\ge1}(\log p)p^{-m\sigma}
\bigl(e^{\ii tm\log p}+e^{-\ii tm\log p}\bigr).
\]
This series converges absolutely and uniformly. The smoothed diagonal
trace gives \(\Gamma-W_{S,\sigma}\). The gap permits precisely the
block algebra in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary}, yielding
\eqref{eq:finite-correction} and its return-series estimate. Averaging
the multiplier to \(a_e\) uses real structure and imposes no parity
or reality condition on \(F\). Only source-smoothed products are
traced; no trace of unsmoothed \(C_{S,\sigma}^2\) is needed.
The full trace-ideal justification is given in
\cite[Sections 2--4]{FiniteEulerNote}.
\end{proof}

\section{Abel regularization without a cutoff gap}\label{sec:abel}

For the full zeta family, the norm gap in
Proposition~\ref{prop:gap} is only established for \(\sigma>1\).
Bounded spectral calculus gives a replacement on every line
\(\sigma>1/2\).

\begin{proposition}[Positive Abel approximants]\label{prop:abel}
For \(0\le r<1\), define
\begin{align}
\Pi_{\sigma,r}
&=P-T_\sigma^*(\Id_\chi-rC_\sigma^2)^{-1}T_\sigma,\label{eq:abel}\\
H_{\sigma,r}
&=rT_\sigma^*C_\sigma^2(\Id_\chi-rC_\sigma^2)^{-1}T_\sigma.
\label{eq:abel-return}
\end{align}
Then
\[
0\le\Pi_\sigma\le\Pi_{\sigma,r}\le L_\sigma,\qquad
\Pi_{\sigma,r}\downarrow\Pi_\sigma,\qquad
H_{\sigma,r}\uparrow H_\sigma
\]
in the strong operator topology as \(r\uparrow1\), and
\(\Pi_{\sigma,r}=L_\sigma-H_{\sigma,r}\).
For every compact smooth frequency multiplier \(b\), these monotone
limits pass through the positive smoothed traces.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put \(E_\sigma=T_\sigma^*T_\sigma\) on \(P\HH\).
Using \(T_\sigma T_\sigma^*=\Id_\chi-C_\sigma^2\),
functional calculus gives
\[
\Pi_{\sigma,r}
=\frac{(1-r)(\Id_{P\HH}-E_\sigma)}
{(1-r)\Id_{P\HH}+rE_\sigma}
=g_r(L_\sigma),\qquad
g_r(\lambda)=\frac{(1-r)\lambda}{1-r\lambda},
\quad g_r(0)=0.
\]
At a spectral value \(\alpha\in[0,1]\) the multiplier is
\((1-r)(1-\alpha)/(1-r+r\alpha)\). It equals one at
\(\alpha=0\), decreases to zero at every \(\alpha>0\), and lies
between zero and \(1-\alpha\). This proves all operator assertions.
Lemma~\ref{lem:phase-trace} supplies the finite dominating trace
\(\Tr(b(D)L_\sigma b(D)^*)\), so monotone convergence applies.
\end{proof}

In particular \(\nu_\sigma\) is locally finite for every
\(\sigma>1/2\). A gap is unnecessary for this assertion.
The approximants in \eqref{eq:abel} are generally not projections.
Their positive sandwich trace is
\(\Tr(b(D)\Pi_{\sigma,r}b(D)^*)\); it must not be replaced by
\(\norm{b(D)\Pi_{\sigma,r}}_{\Stwo}^2\).

\section{Uniform frequency bounds}\label{sec:tails}

We now prove \eqref{eq:main-window} and extend local trace formulas to
all Schwartz source multipliers. The estimates are uniform when a zero
or the zeta pole approaches the chosen vertical line.

\subsection{Local factorization and a uniform energy bound}

The classical inputs are the local zero count and local partial-fraction
formula for \(\zeta'/\zeta\); see
\cite[Propositions 16 and 19]{Tao} and \cite{Elkies}.
Counting multiplicities,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero-count}
\#\{\rho=\beta+\ii\gamma:|\gamma-j|\le5\}
=O(\log(2+|j|)).
\end{equation}
Reflection in the critical line extends a count in a strip bounded
away from zero to the full critical strip.
For every \(\sigma\ge1/2\) and
\(t\in J_j=(j-3,j+3)\), the local logarithmic-derivative estimate gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-log}
\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+\ii t)
-\sum_{|\gamma-j|\le5}\frac{m_\rho}{\sigma+\ii t-\rho}
+\frac1{\sigma+\ii t-1}
=O(\log(2+|j|)),
\end{equation}
after removal of the displayed singularities.
To obtain this version, first extract zeros in a fixed small disk
about \(\sigma+\ii t\). Enlarging the extracted set to the
displayed list adds only \(O(\log(2+|j|))\) terms whose denominators
are bounded away from zero. The estimate for bounded \(j\) follows
by factoring finitely many singularities on a compact set.
It suffices to apply the cited local estimate on \(1/2\le\sigma\le3\).
For \(\sigma\ge3\), the absolutely convergent Euler series bounds
\(|\zeta'/\zeta(\sigma+\ii t)|\) by
\(\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)n^{-3}<\infty\); every displayed zero
denominator has modulus at least two, and the pole denominator does too.
Equation~\eqref{eq:zero-count} then gives the same remainder bound with
an absolute constant, independent of an upper strip endpoint.

For \(d\ne0\), define
\[
B_{d,\gamma}(t)=\frac{t-\gamma-\ii d}{t-\gamma+\ii d},
\qquad B_{0,\gamma}=1.
\]
The zero-width convention concerns the phase on the line itself.
The phase derivative of a nonconstant factor is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bderiv}
\frac{2d}{d^2+(t-\gamma)^2}.
\end{equation}
Include the inverse pole factor
\[
R_\sigma(t)=\frac{t+\ii(\sigma-1)}{t-\ii(\sigma-1)}
\quad(\sigma\ne1),\qquad R_1=1,
\]
and, on \(J_j\), set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-product}
\mathcal B_{j,\sigma}
=R_\sigma\prod_{|\gamma-j|\le5}
B_{\sigma-\beta,\gamma}^{\,m_\rho},
\qquad w_{j,\sigma}=v_\sigma/\mathcal B_{j,\sigma}.
\end{equation}
All factors have modulus one. Equation~\eqref{eq:local-log}
and the \(O(\log(2+|t|))\) bound for the archimedean phase derivative
show that the removable extension of \(w_{j,\sigma}\) obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:background}
\sup_{J_j}|w_{j,\sigma}'|
\le C\log(2+|j|).
\end{equation}
The estimate includes \(\sigma=1\) and lines containing zeros.
The real part of \(1/(\ii(t-\gamma))\) is zero away from
\(\gamma\); its phase contribution on the exceptional line is
removed, not interpreted as a delta mass.

For a scalar function \(q\), write
\[
\mathcal E(q)=\iint
\frac{|q(t)-q(s)|^2}{(t-s)^2}\dd t\dd s.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Width-independent factor energy]\label{lem:energy}
For \(d\ne0\), \(\mathcal E(B_{d,\gamma})=4\pi^2\).
There is a fixed real cutoff \(b_0\in C_c^\infty((-2,2))\),
equal to one on \([0,1]\), such that, with \(b_j(t)=b_0(t-j)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localized-energy}
\mathcal E(b_jv_\sigma)\le C\log(2+|j|)
\end{equation}
uniformly for every \(\sigma>1/2\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Direct subtraction gives
\[
\frac{|B_{d,\gamma}(t)-B_{d,\gamma}(s)|^2}{(t-s)^2}
=\frac{4d^2}
{((t-\gamma)^2+d^2)((s-\gamma)^2+d^2)}.
\]
Integration proves the first assertion, independently of the sign
or magnitude of \(d\). The inverse pole factor has the same energy.

For \(f\in H^1(\mathbb R)\), Parseval and the translation formula
for the energy give, with \(\widehat f\) now denoting the Fourier
transform of the frequency variable,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy-interpolation}
\mathcal E(f)=\int |\xi|\,|\widehat f(\xi)|^2\dd\xi
\le2\pi\norm f_2\norm{f'}_2.
\end{equation}
Extend \(w_{j,\sigma}\) constantly outside \(J_j\), and let
\(f_{j,\sigma}=b_jw_{j,\sigma}\). Its \(L^2\) norm is bounded
by a fixed constant and its derivative norm is
\(O(\log(2+|j|))\), by \eqref{eq:background}. Thus
\(\mathcal E(f_{j,\sigma})=O(\log(2+|j|))\).

For a finite Blaschke product \(\Theta\) of degree \(m\), analytic
in the lower half-plane, multiplication by \(\Theta\) preserves
\(P\HH\) and is an isometry there. Its model space
\(K_\Theta=P\HH\ominus\Theta(D)P\HH\) has dimension \(m\),
including repeated zeros \cite{Peller}. If
\(Z=P\Theta(D)\chi\), then
\[
ZZ^*=P-P\Theta(D)P\Theta(D)^*P
=\operatorname{proj}K_\Theta,
\qquad \chi\Theta(D)P=0.
\]
The commutator energy identity \eqref{eq:comm-energy} therefore gives
\(\mathcal E(\Theta)=4\pi^2m\). Complex conjugation gives the same
energy for a product of the opposite orientation.
Group the nonconstant factors of \(\mathcal B_{j,\sigma}\), including
the inverse pole factor, into products \(B_+\) and \(B_-\) of these
two orientations. There are
\(m_++m_-\le1+\sum_{|\gamma-j|\le5}m_\rho=O(\log(2+|j|))\)
factors. Since both products are unimodular,
\[
\mathcal E(B_+B_-)
\le2\mathcal E(B_+)+2\mathcal E(B_-)
\le8\pi^2(m_++m_-).
\]
Taking \(B=\mathcal B_{j,\sigma}\) in
\[
|f(t)B(t)-f(s)B(s)|^2
\le2|f(t)-f(s)|^2+
2\norm f_\infty^2|B(t)-B(s)|^2
\]
proves \eqref{eq:localized-energy}. The identity
\(b_jv_\sigma=f_{j,\sigma}\mathcal B_{j,\sigma}\) holds globally
because \(b_j\) is supported strictly inside \(J_j\).
No global Blaschke factorization has been assumed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Positive traces and source tails}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform trace control]\label{prop:tails}
The estimate \eqref{eq:main-window} holds. For every
\(f\in\Sch(\mathbb R)\) and any of the three positive measures there,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-sum}
\int |f(t)|^2\dd\nu_G(t)
\le M\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
\log(2+|j|)\sup_{t\in[j,j+1]}|f(t)|^2.
\end{equation}
For each integer \(N>1\), its tail on \(|t|>A\) is
\(O_{f,N}(A^{1-2N}\log(2+A))\), uniformly in \(\sigma\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use \(L_\sigma=\mathfrak H_{v_\sigma}^*\mathfrak H_{v_\sigma}\),
where \(\mathfrak H_{v_\sigma}=\chi V_\sigma P\) and
\(V_\sigma=v_\sigma(D)\). Then
\[
\mathfrak H_{v_\sigma}b_j(D)^*
=\chi(v_\sigma\overline b_j)(D)P+
\chi V_\sigma[P,b_j(D)^*].
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:hankel} and \eqref{eq:localized-energy} imply
\[
\Tr(b_j(D)L_\sigma b_j(D)^*)
=\norm{\mathfrak H_{v_\sigma}b_j(D)^*}_{\Stwo}^2
\le C\log(2+|j|).
\]
Since \(b_j=1\) on \([j,j+1]\), this proves the estimate for
\(\nu_{L_\sigma}\). Positive domination gives it for \(\nu_\sigma\).
Using \eqref{eq:Cfactor}, factor the small-cutoff sandwich through
\(PV_\sigma\chi b_j(D)^*\), and commute \(b_j(D)^*\) past
\(\chi\). Exactly the same estimates prove the bound for
\(\nu_{C_\sigma^2}\). This includes any subspace at spectral
value one of \(C_\sigma^2\).

Summing the unit-window estimates gives \eqref{eq:tail-sum}.
For the tail, use the order-\(N\) Schwartz bound and sum over
\(|j|>A-2\). The resulting scalar series has the asserted bound.
\end{proof}

The same bounds hold for \(H_\sigma\) and all \(\Pi_{\sigma,r}\),
by positive domination. Thus full compact smooth position sources
require no auxiliary finite-rank cutoff.

\begin{proposition}[Full-source boundary identity]\label{prop:full-boundary}
For every \(f\in\Sch(\mathbb R)\) and \(\sigma>1/2\),
\begin{align}
\Tr(f(D)\Pi_\sigma f(D)^*)
&=\int |f(t)|^2\phi_\sigma'(t)\frac{\dd t}{2\pi}+K_\sigma[f],
\label{eq:full-boundary}\\
K_\sigma[f]
&=\Tr(f(D)C_\sigma^2 f(D)^*)
-\Tr(f(D)H_\sigma f(D)^*)\notag\\
&\hspace{1em}+2\Rea\Tr_{\chi\HH}
\bigl(C_\sigma T_\sigma P|f|^2(D)\chi\bigr).
\label{eq:full-K}
\end{align}
All displayed quantities are finite. The two positive traces have
uniform frequency tails over all \(\sigma>1/2\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Local factorization also gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-L1}
\int_j^{j+1}|\phi_\sigma'(t)|\dd t
\le C\log(2+|j|).
\end{equation}
Indeed each rational factor has total absolute phase variation at
most \(2\pi\), and the remainder satisfies \eqref{eq:background}.
This proves absolute convergence and uniform Schwartz tails for
the bulk integral.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:phase-trace} and \(\Pi_\sigma=L_\sigma-H_\sigma\)
to \(f_N=f b_0(t/N)\), replacing \(b_0\) if necessary by a
cutoff equal to one near zero. Then \(f_N\to f\) in the Schwartz
topology. Proposition~\ref{prop:tails} treats the positive trace
terms. Lemma~\ref{lem:hankel} gives
\[
P|f_N|^2(D)\chi\longrightarrow P|f|^2(D)\chi
\quad\text{in trace norm}.
\]
The remaining factor \(C_\sigma T_\sigma\) is bounded in norm by one,
so the crossing traces converge. Equation~\eqref{eq:phase-L1}
treats the bulk integral and proves the result.
This proof assigns the crossing scalar directly to a trace-class
cutoff product; it does not require a global trace-class assertion
for an unsandwiched phase operator.
\end{proof}

\section{Localization at the critical boundary}\label{sec:critical}

\subsection{Strong collapse and local phase factors}

The functional equation, in the usual Riemann--Siegel convention
\cite{DLMF}, gives \(v_{1/2}(t)=1\) away from zero ordinates.
Bounded pointwise convergence therefore implies
\(V_\sigma\to\Id\) strongly as \(\sigma\downarrow1/2\).
Equations~\eqref{eq:Lfactor}--\eqref{eq:Cfactor} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong}
\Fcal_\sigma\longrightarrow\J,\qquad
L_\sigma\longrightarrow0,\qquad
C_\sigma\longrightarrow0,\qquad
\Pi_\sigma\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{strongly}.
\end{equation}
For the last assertion,
\(\norm{\Pi_\sigma h}^2\le\inner h{L_\sigma h}\).
In particular, for fixed finite-rank \(E\) and bounded \(f(D)\),
\(\norm{f(D)\Pi_\sigma E}_{\Stwo}\to0\).
A fixed compact frequency multiplier is generally infinite rank,
so this observation does not determine its trace limit.

Let \(\epsilon=\sigma-1/2>0\). On a fixed compact frequency interval,
choose a slightly larger rectangle about the critical line with
boundary free of zeros and no off-line zero in its interior.
There are only finitely many zeros in a bounded rectangle, so its
horizontal width can be chosen this way. Local analytic factorization
gives, on a neighborhood of the interval,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-factor}
v_{1/2+\epsilon}(t)=B_\epsilon(t)w_\epsilon(t),
\qquad
B_\epsilon(t)=\prod_\gamma
\left(\frac{t-\gamma-\ii\epsilon}
{t-\gamma+\ii\epsilon}\right)^{m_\gamma},
\qquad
w_\epsilon\longrightarrow1\quad\text{in }C^\infty.
\end{equation}
The product includes every critical zero in the rectangle. The factored
remainder is smooth jointly in \(\epsilon,t\); the functional equation
identifies its endpoint value. Zeros outside the rectangle do not
require any location hypothesis.

Every pole of \(B_\epsilon\) lies in the lower frequency half-plane.
Its inverse Fourier kernel is supported on \((-\infty,0]\).
For example
\begin{equation}\label{eq:onefactor-kernel}
\mathcal F^{-1}\left(\frac{t-\ii h}{t+\ii h}\right)(x)
=\delta_0(x)-2h e^{hx}1_{x<0},\qquad h>0.
\end{equation}
Partial fractions or convolution proves the corresponding support
statement for the finite product, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triangular-B}
P B_\epsilon(D)\chi=0.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Local phase concentration]\label{lem:phase-limit}
As \(\sigma\downarrow1/2\),
\[
\phi_\sigma'(t)\frac{\dd t}{2\pi}\longrightarrow\mucrit
\]
on compact smooth tests and on all Schwartz tests.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Differentiating the local factorization \eqref{eq:critical-factor}
gives a smooth term tending uniformly to zero and, for each critical
zero of multiplicity \(m_\gamma\), the term
\[
\frac{2m_\gamma\epsilon}{\epsilon^2+(t-\gamma)^2}.
\]
After division by \(2\pi\), this tends to
\(m_\gamma\delta_\gamma\). The local conclusion follows from the
Poisson-kernel approximate identity. The unit-window variation bound
\eqref{eq:phase-L1} and the local zero count give uniform Schwartz
tails for the prelimit and limit measures, respectively.
This proves the global assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Small-cutoff and crossing terms}

\begin{lemma}[Vanishing localized cutoff]\label{lem:Csmall}
For every \(b\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Csmall}
\norm{C_\sigma b(D)}_{\Stwo}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Furthermore,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crosssmall}
\Tr_{\chi\HH}\bigl(C_\sigma T_\sigma P|f|^2(D)\chi\bigr)
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{for every } f\in\Sch(\mathbb R).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose the factorization \eqref{eq:critical-factor} on a
neighborhood of \(\operatorname{supp}b\), and set
\(q_\epsilon=b(w_\epsilon-1)\), extended by zero.
Then \(q_\epsilon\to0\) in the Schwartz topology.
Using \(C_\sigma=\J P V_\sigma\chi\),
\(\chi b(D)=b(D)\chi-[b(D),\chi]\), and
\eqref{eq:triangular-B}, one obtains the exact identity
\begin{align}\label{eq:Csmall-id}
C_\sigma b(D)=\J\{&
Pq_\epsilon(D)\chi B_\epsilon(D)\chi\\
&+P(B_\epsilon(D)-V_\sigma)[b(D),\chi]\}.\notag
\end{align}
The first term tends to zero in Hilbert--Schmidt norm by
Lemma~\ref{lem:hankel}. Both \(B_\epsilon(D)\) and \(V_\sigma\)
converge strongly to the identity. Thus the second term is a
uniformly bounded strongly null family times a fixed
Hilbert--Schmidt operator, and tends to zero in that ideal.
This last implication follows by finite-rank approximation.

For \eqref{eq:crosssmall}, \(P|f|^2(D)\chi\) is trace class,
whereas \(C_\sigma T_\sigma\to0\) strongly by
\eqref{eq:strong}. Finite-rank approximation in trace norm
proves the scalar limit.
\end{proof}

Combining these lemmas with \eqref{eq:L-trace} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-Llimit}
\Tr(b(D)L_\sigma b(D)^*)\longrightarrow
\int|b|^2\dd\mucrit
\quad (b\in C_c^\infty).
\end{equation}
The uniform tails of Proposition~\ref{prop:tails} extend this
limit to every Schwartz \(f\). They likewise extend
\(\Tr(b(D)C_\sigma^2b(D)^*)\to0\) to all such \(f\).
Thus \eqref{eq:full-K} implies
\begin{align}
\Tr(f(D)\Pi_\sigma f(D)^*)
&=\int|f|^2\dd\mucrit-\Tr(f(D)H_\sigma f(D)^*)+o_f(1),
\label{eq:deficit}\\
K_\sigma[f]&=-\Tr(f(D)H_\sigma f(D)^*)+o_f(1).
\label{eq:Kdeficit}
\end{align}

\begin{corollary}[Possible exact-projection limits]\label{cor:subseq}
Every sequence \(\sigma_n\downarrow1/2\) has a vaguely convergent
subsequence of \(\nu_{\sigma_n}\). Any limit is bounded above by
\(\mucrit\) and therefore has precisely the form stated in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The unit-window bound gives compactness of the positive measures
on each bounded interval. A diagonal subsequence gives vague
convergence on \(\mathbb R\). Since
\(\nu_{\sigma}\le\nu_{L_\sigma}\), \eqref{eq:local-Llimit}
implies \(\nu_*\le\mucrit\).
One may pass from squared smooth tests to all compact continuous
tests by choosing a smooth function equal to one on their support
and uniformly approximating there; the local mass bounds justify
this step. A positive measure dominated by the discrete
\(\mucrit\) has no other support and has atom weights bounded
by its multiplicities.
\end{proof}

\section{The return defect at cutoff spectral value one}\label{sec:return}

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar}
T_\sigma=(\Id_\chi-C_\sigma^2)^{1/2}W_\sigma^{\mathrm{pol}}
\end{equation}
be the polar decomposition in this orientation.
The superscript distinguishes the partial isometry from the prime sum
\(W_\sigma[F]\). Its initial space is
\((\ker T_\sigma)^\perp\subset P\HH\), and its final space is
\(\ker(\Id_\chi-C_\sigma^2)^\perp\).
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hpolar}
H_\sigma=(W_\sigma^{\mathrm{pol}})^*C_\sigma^2
W_\sigma^{\mathrm{pol}}.
\end{equation}
For \(b\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\), define the positive spectral measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta}
\eta_{\sigma,b}(E)=
\Tr\!\left(b(D)(W_\sigma^{\mathrm{pol}})^*C_\sigma^2
1_E(C_\sigma^2)W_\sigma^{\mathrm{pol}}b(D)^*\right),
\quad E\subset[0,1].
\end{equation}
It is finite, its total mass is \(R_\sigma[b]\), and
\(\eta_{\sigma,b}(\{1\})=0\) at each fixed \(\sigma\).
The last fact follows from the final space of the polar partial
isometry. It gives no uniform exclusion of mass close to one.

\begin{theorem}[Return localization and Abel error]\label{thm:return}
For \(b\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\) and \(0<\delta<1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:return-localize}
\eta_{\sigma,b}([0,1-\delta])
\le \delta^{-1}\norm{C_\sigma T_\sigma b(D)^*}_{\Stwo}^2
\longrightarrow0
\quad(\sigma\downarrow1/2).
\end{equation}
For \(0\le r<1\),
\begin{align}
\Tr\!\left(b(D)(\Pi_{\sigma,r}-\Pi_\sigma)b(D)^*\right)
&=\int_{[0,1]}\frac{1-r}{1-r\lambda}\dd\eta_{\sigma,b}(\lambda)
\label{eq:Abel-error}\\
&\le
\frac{1-r}{1-r+r\delta}\eta_{\sigma,b}([0,1])
+\eta_{\sigma,b}((1-\delta,1)).
\notag
\end{align}
At each fixed \(\sigma\), this error tends to zero as \(r\uparrow1\).
For each fixed \(r<1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-Abel}
\Tr(f(D)\Pi_{\sigma,r}f(D)^*)\longrightarrow
\int |f|^2\dd\mucrit
\quad\text{for every }f\in\Sch(\mathbb R).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set \(b^\sharp(t)=\overline{b(-t)}\).
Since \(\Fcal_\sigma b(D)^*=b^\sharp(D)\Fcal_\sigma\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CTsmall}
C_\sigma T_\sigma b(D)^*
=C_\sigma b^\sharp(D)\Fcal_\sigma P
+C_\sigma\Fcal_\sigma[P,b(D)^*].
\end{equation}
The first term tends to zero in Hilbert--Schmidt norm by
Lemma~\ref{lem:Csmall}. The second does also: its commutator is
fixed Hilbert--Schmidt, and
\(C_\sigma\Fcal_\sigma\to0\) strongly.
This proves the limit on the right of \eqref{eq:return-localize}.

Under \eqref{eq:polar}, the squared norm in that equation contains
the spectral weight \(\lambda(1-\lambda)\), while \(\eta\)
contains \(\lambda\). On \([0,1-\delta]\), the latter is at most
\(\delta^{-1}\lambda(1-\lambda)\). This proves the inequality.

Subtracting the exact kernel projection from \eqref{eq:abel}
and using the polar decomposition gives
\[
\Pi_{\sigma,r}-\Pi_\sigma
=(W_\sigma^{\mathrm{pol}})^*
\frac{(1-r)C_\sigma^2}{\Id_\chi-rC_\sigma^2}
W_\sigma^{\mathrm{pol}}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:Abel-error}. Splitting its spectral integral
at \(1-\delta\) proves the bound. Dominated convergence applies
at fixed \(\sigma\), since there is no atom at one.

For fixed \(r<1\), \eqref{eq:abel-return} gives
\[
0\le\Tr(b(D)H_{\sigma,r}b(D)^*)
\le\frac{r}{1-r}
\norm{C_\sigma T_\sigma b(D)^*}_{\Stwo}^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
Combine this with \(\Pi_{\sigma,r}=L_\sigma-H_{\sigma,r}\)
and \eqref{eq:local-Llimit}. This proves \eqref{eq:fixed-Abel}
for compact smooth \(b\). The domination
\(0\le\Pi_{\sigma,r}\le L_\sigma\) and the uniform source tails
extend it to every Schwartz \(f\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Endpoint spectral calculus]\label{cor:endpoint-calculus}
Let \(g:[0,1]\to\mathbb C\) be bounded Borel, with \(g(0)=0\),
\(g(\lambda)=O(\lambda)\) near zero, and continuous at \(1\).
For every \(f\in\Sch(\mathbb R)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-calculus}
\Tr(f(D)g(L_\sigma)f(D)^*)
\longrightarrow g(1)\int|f|^2\dd\mucrit.
\end{equation}
Here the calculus on \(P\HH\) is extended by zero on \(\chi\HH\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For compact smooth \(b\), use the finite positive weighted measure
\[
\omega_{\sigma,b}(E)
=\Tr\bigl(b(D)L_\sigma1_E(L_\sigma)b(D)^*\bigr).
\]
Its total mass tends to \(m_b=\int|b|^2\dd\mucrit\).
Since \(T_\sigma^*C_\sigma^2T_\sigma=L_\sigma(\Id-L_\sigma)\),
\[
\omega_{\sigma,b}([0,1-\delta])
\le\delta^{-1}\norm{C_\sigma T_\sigma b(D)^*}_{\Stwo}^2
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Set \(k(\lambda)=g(\lambda)/\lambda\) for \(\lambda>0\), with
\(k(0)=0\). This function is bounded and continuous at \(1\).
Splitting its integral against \(\omega_{\sigma,b}\) at
\(1-\delta\), then using continuity at \(1\), gives
\(\int k\dd\omega_{\sigma,b}\to g(1)m_b\).
The positive operator sandwich corresponding to
\(|g(L_\sigma)|\le\norm k_\infty L_\sigma\) is trace class,
so this integral is the trace in \eqref{eq:endpoint-calculus}.
For Schwartz \(f\), truncate in frequency and use the same domination
and Proposition~\ref{prop:tails}. Factoring through \(L_\sigma^{1/2}\)
controls the mixed terms by the Hilbert--Schmidt norms of the truncated
source and its remainder, so the passage to the full source is uniform.
\end{proof}

This puts \(g(\lambda)=\lambda\) and the fixed Abel functions
\(g_r\) in a single endpoint statement. The exact projection uses
\(g=1_{\{1\}}\), which is discontinuous at \(1\) and is therefore
excluded. The weighted measure is finite; the unweighted sandwich of
\(1_E(L_\sigma)\) need not be finite near zero. The return measure
\eqref{eq:eta} retains the equivalent description on the cutoff side.

Theorems~\ref{thm:return} and~\ref{thm:main} leave open precisely
the total return mass. For a fixed compact frequency test, the
sufficient condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-integrability}
\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\,
\limsup_{\sigma\downarrow1/2}
\eta_{\sigma,b}((1-\delta,1))=0
\end{equation}
would imply \(R_\sigma[b]\to0\), using
\eqref{eq:return-localize}. Conversely, vanishing total mass implies
\eqref{eq:uniform-integrability}. A mere positive gap for each
prelimit parameter does not supply this condition.

For a jointly chosen \(r=r(\sigma)\uparrow1\), the sufficient
estimate
\[
\frac{\norm{C_\sigma T_\sigma b(D)^*}_{\Stwo}^2}
{1-r(\sigma)}\longrightarrow0
\]
gives the critical-zero limit for the corresponding Abel trace.
This is a source-dependent sufficient criterion, not a proved
uniform rate for the zeta family.

\section{A rational family with complete projection loss}\label{sec:model}

The following example concerns a different global phase. Its role
is to test what can be inferred from local critical factorization,
strong convergence, and a positive prelimit gap.

\begin{theorem}[Exact rational model]\label{thm:model}
For \(0<\epsilon<1\), set \(R=\epsilon^{-1}\) and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-phase}
B_h(t)=\frac{t-\ii h}{t+\ii h},\qquad
v_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}(t)=-\frac{B_\epsilon(t)}{B_R(t)},
\qquad
q=\frac{R-\epsilon}{R+\epsilon}
=\frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1+\epsilon^2}.
\end{equation}
Define the cutoff operators as before from
\(\Fcal_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}=\J v_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}(D)\).
Then the following statements hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(v_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}=B_\epsilon w_\epsilon\)
locally, with \(w_\epsilon=-B_R^{-1}\to1\) in \(C^\infty\);
the phase measures tend locally to \(\delta_0\).
\item The cutoff gap is strictly positive for every \(\epsilon\),
\[
1-\norm{C_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}}^2
=1-q^2=\frac{4\epsilon^2}{(1+\epsilon^2)^2}.
\]
Nevertheless \(\Pi_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}=0\) exactly.
\item For every Schwartz \(b\), the majorant trace tends to
\(|b(0)|^2\), and the entire limiting mass lies in the return defect.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The phase derivative is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-derivative}
(\phi_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}})'(t)
=\frac{2\epsilon}{t^2+\epsilon^2}
-\frac{2R}{t^2+R^2}.
\end{equation}
On compact tests, the first term divided by \(2\pi\) tends
to \(\delta_0\), and the second tends to zero. The asserted
factorization and smooth background limit follow directly.

Partial fractions, with the Fourier convention \eqref{eq:fourier},
give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-kernel}
\check v_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}(x)
=-\delta_0(x)-2\epsilon q e^{\epsilon x}1_{x<0}
+2Rq e^{-Rx}1_{x>0}.
\end{equation}
Let
\[
u_\epsilon(x)=\sqrt{2\epsilon}\,e^{-\epsilon x}1_{x>0},
\qquad
\xi_R(x)=\sqrt{2R}\,e^{Rx}1_{x<0}.
\]
These are unit vectors. The kernel of \(\Fcal_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}\)
is \(\check v_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}(-x-y)\), so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-blocks}
P\Fcal_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}P
=-q\,|u_\epsilon\rangle\langle u_\epsilon|,
\qquad
C_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}
=q\,|\xi_R\rangle\langle\xi_R|.
\end{equation}
Therefore
\[
L_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}
=q^2|u_\epsilon\rangle\langle u_\epsilon|,\qquad
\norm{L_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}}=q^2<1.
\]
An orthogonal projection dominated by a positive operator of
norm less than one is zero. Since \(\Pi\le L\), this proves
\(\Pi_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}=0\) and \(H_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}=L_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}\).

Finally,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-source}
\norm{b(D)u_\epsilon}_2^2
=\int |b(t)|^2
\frac{2\epsilon}{\epsilon^2+t^2}\frac{\dd t}{2\pi}
\longrightarrow |b(0)|^2.
\end{equation}
This proves the trace assertion for Schwartz \(b\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A contrasting exact projection]\label{rem:onefactor}
For the single factor \(v_\epsilon^{\mathrm{one}}=B_\epsilon\),
\eqref{eq:onefactor-kernel} gives
\[
C_\epsilon^{\mathrm{one}}=0,\qquad
\Pi_\epsilon^{\mathrm{one}}=L_\epsilon^{\mathrm{one}}
=|u_\epsilon\rangle\langle u_\epsilon|.
\]
Indeed \(P\Fcal_\epsilon^{\mathrm{one}}P
=-|u_\epsilon\rangle\langle u_\epsilon|\), and the projection onto
the eigenvalue \(1\) of its square is the displayed rank-one projection.
Since \(u_\epsilon\rightharpoonup0\), these projections tend strongly
to zero, while \eqref{eq:model-source} gives a nonzero smoothed trace
limit whenever \(b(0)\ne0\). This general projection phenomenon is
therefore explicit, but it has not been established for the exact zeta
projection. The single-factor and rational families have the same local
factor \(B_\epsilon\), a smooth local remainder tending to one, and
the same limiting majorant atom. Their exact-projection weights are
respectively one and zero. Local critical concentration and the endpoint
symbol alone do not determine exact-kernel mass.
\end{remark}

The spectral defect and both orders of limits are explicit.
Indeed \(T_\epsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}u_\epsilon
=-\sqrt{1-q^2}\,\xi_R\), so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-eta}
\eta_{\epsilon,b}^{\mathrm{mod}}
=q^2\norm{b(D)u_\epsilon}_2^2\,\delta_{q^2}.
\end{equation}
It has no atom at one for any \(\epsilon>0\), but asymptotically
all its mass approaches one.

\begin{corollary}[Abel crossover]\label{cor:crossover}
In the rational model,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-Abel}
\Pi_{\epsilon,r}^{\mathrm{mod}}
=\frac{q^2(1-r)}{1-rq^2}
|u_\epsilon\rangle\langle u_\epsilon|.
\end{equation}
Writing \(d_\epsilon=1-r(\epsilon)\), its smoothed trace tends to
\[
\begin{cases}
|b(0)|^2,& d_\epsilon/\epsilon^2\to\infty,\\
0,& d_\epsilon/\epsilon^2\to0,\\
\dfrac{c}{c+4}|b(0)|^2,& d_\epsilon/\epsilon^2\to c\in(0,\infty).
\end{cases}
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On the span of \(u_\epsilon\), \(T^*T\) has eigenvalue
\(1-q^2\); on its orthogonal complement it has eigenvalue one.
The functional-calculus formula in Proposition~\ref{prop:abel}
gives \eqref{eq:model-Abel}.
Use \(1-q^2\sim4\epsilon^2\) and \eqref{eq:model-source}.
\end{proof}

Thus local phase concentration can be canceled completely by an
exact-projection return term. The example shares the local structure
used in Section~\ref{sec:critical}, but it is not a counterexample
about the actual zeta phase. A theorem determining the weights
\(c_\gamma\) for zeta must use additional information about that
global phase.

\section{Arithmetic comparison and crossing residues}\label{sec:arithmetic}

The operator results above concern the frequency measure on critical
zeros. We now retain the arithmetic terms needed to compare it with
the full explicit formula. The residue calculation is classical;
its purpose here is to fix the normalization and identify which
terms remain after the operator limit.

\subsection{Entire source weights}

Let \(F\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\), possibly complex, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entire-a}
a(z)=\widehat F(z)\overline{\widehat F(\overline z)}.
\end{equation}
It is entire, satisfies \(a(\overline z)=\overline{a(z)}\),
and equals \(|\widehat F(t)|^2\) on the real line.
It need not be even. For every \(D<\infty\) and every integer \(N\),
compact support and integration by parts give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strip-decay}
\sup_{|\Ima z|\le D}(1+|\Rea z|)^N|a(z)|<\infty.
\end{equation}
The local zero count implies \(N(T)=O(T\log(T+2))\).
Consequently all zero sums below converge absolutely, uniformly
for bounded imaginary shifts in the source weight.

Define the phase bulk
\[
A_\sigma[F]=\int a(t)\phi_\sigma'(t)\frac{\dd t}{2\pi},
\]
and the crossing terms
\begin{align}
\mathcal P_\sigma[F]
&=
\begin{cases}
2\Rea a(\ii(1-\sigma)),&1/2<\sigma<1,\\
a(0),&\sigma=1,\\
0,&\sigma>1,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:Pcross}\\
\mathcal Z_\sigma[F]
&=2\Rea\sum_{\beta>\sigma}m_{\beta+\ii\gamma}
a\bigl(\gamma+\ii(\beta-\sigma)\bigr)
+\sum_{\beta=\sigma}m_{\beta+\ii\gamma}a(\gamma).
\label{eq:Zcross}
\end{align}
Both signs of \(\gamma\) occur. The on-line sum in
\eqref{eq:Zcross} is a half-residue contribution relative to
the strictly right-hand sum.

\begin{theorem}[Complete phase comparison]\label{thm:crossings}
For every \(\sigma>1/2\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crossing-formula}
A_\sigma[F]=\Gamma[F]-W_\sigma[F]
+\mathcal P_\sigma[F]-\mathcal Z_\sigma[F].
\end{equation}
The value on an exceptional line uses the removable phase itself.
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-explicit}
\Gamma[F]-W_{1/2}[F]
=\Zcrit[F]+\Zoff[F]-2\Rea a(\ii/2),
\end{equation}
where \(\Zoff\) is as in \eqref{eq:intro-arithmetic}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The differentiated Euler series proves
\eqref{eq:crossing-formula} when \(\sigma>1\).
For \(1/2<\sigma<2\), initially excluding the pole line and real
parts of zeros, move the logarithmic-derivative contour to
\(\Rea s=2\). Consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contour-integrand}
a(-\ii(s-\sigma))\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)\frac{\dd s}{2\pi\ii}.
\end{equation}
Choose heights \(T_n\in[n,n+1]\) at distance at least
\(c/\log(n+2)\) from every nearby zero ordinate. The local zero
count constructs such heights by excluding intervals of sufficiently
small total length. Conjugation gives the same property at \(-T_n\).
The local logarithmic-derivative estimate then bounds
\(\zeta'/\zeta\) by \(O(\log^2(T_n+2))\) along the horizontal
sides, uniformly for real parts in \([\sigma,2]\).
Together with \eqref{eq:strip-decay}, this makes the horizontal
integrals vanish.

Orient the left side upward and the right side downward. The
orientation is clockwise, so the left integral equals the right
integral minus the residues. The logarithmic derivative has residue
\(-1\) at the pole and \(+m_\rho\) at a zero. The crossing terms
from \eqref{eq:contour-integrand} are therefore
\[
1_{\sigma<1}a(-\ii(1-\sigma))
-\sum_{\beta>\sigma}m_{\beta+\ii\gamma}
a\bigl(\gamma-\ii(\beta-\sigma)\bigr).
\]
On the right side the Euler series is absolutely convergent.
Shifting the entire source weight gives
\[
\int a(t-\ii(2-\sigma))e^{-\ii t\log n}\frac{\dd t}{2\pi}
=n^{2-\sigma}\kappa_F(-\log n).
\]
Thus its integral is
\(-\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)n^{-\sigma}\kappa_F(-\log n)\).
Interchange the integral and series on the line two first;
compact correlation support then makes the final sum finite.

Repeat the calculation with \(a(-z)\), and add the finite-height
identities before taking the limit. The left side becomes
\[
\int_{-T_n}^{T_n}a(t)
\left(\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma+\ii t)+
\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma-\ii t)\right)\frac{\dd t}{2\pi}.
\]
The paired integral is absolutely convergent by
\eqref{eq:phase-L1} and the archimedean bound.
No separate absolute convergence of the imaginary logarithmic
derivative is needed. The prime terms add to \(-W_\sigma[F]\).
Conjugation of the zero set and Schwarz reflection of \(a\)
put the residue terms in the form
\(\mathcal P_\sigma-\mathcal Z_\sigma\).
This proves \eqref{eq:crossing-formula} on nonexceptional lines.

At an exceptional line, take the average of the two neighboring
limits through nonexceptional lines. On a fixed bounded frequency
window, local factorization shows that a zero's two Cauchy-kernel
limits give opposite atoms; their average is its removable phase
value. The pole behaves in the same way with the opposite sign.
The uniform tails from \eqref{eq:phase-L1} extend this averaging
to the full source. The absolute and uniform convergence of the
shifted zero sums justifies averaging their residues. This produces
exactly the on-line term in \eqref{eq:Zcross} and
\(\mathcal P_1=a(0)\).

Finally Lemma~\ref{lem:phase-limit} gives
\(A_\sigma[F]\to\Zcrit[F]\). The prime sum is finite and
continuous in \(\sigma\). Dominated convergence in the crossing
sums gives
\(\mathcal Z_\sigma[F]\to\Zoff[F]\) and
\(\mathcal P_\sigma[F]\to2\Rea a(\ii/2)\).
At exceptional parameters approaching \(1/2\), the on-line
terms are controlled by the same uniform summable bound.
Taking the limit proves \eqref{eq:critical-explicit}.
\end{proof}

As checks on the signs, crossing the pole line from right to left
changes the bulk by \(+2a(0)\), whereas crossing a zero line changes
its local bulk contribution by \(-2m_\rho a(\gamma)\).
Preparation at the fixed pole moments
\(\widehat F(\pm\ii/2)=0\) does not imply \(a(0)=0\);
intermediate pole corrections must therefore be kept.

\subsection{The complete Weil form and the unresolved identification}

Define the full arithmetic form in these conventions by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qdefinition}
Q[F]=\Gamma[F]-W_{1/2}[F]+2\Rea a_F(\ii/2).
\end{equation}
Reflection \(\beta+\ii\gamma\mapsto1-\beta+\ii\gamma\)
and Schwarz reflection of \(a_F\) pair the off-line terms as
conjugates. Thus \eqref{eq:critical-explicit} is equivalently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-zero-pairing}
Q[F]=\Zcrit[F]+\Zoff[F]
=\sum_\rho m_\rho
a_F\bigl(\Ima\rho+\ii(\Rea\rho-1/2)\bigr).
\end{equation}
This is the usual complete zero pairing, written in the chosen
Fourier orientation. For sources \eqref{eq:prepared},
\(\widehat F(z)=(z^2+1/4)\widehat h(z)\); hence
\(a_F(\pm\ii/2)=0\) and \(Q[F]=\Gamma[F]-W_{1/2}[F]\).

\begin{corollary}[Remaining arithmetic discrepancy]\label{cor:arithmetic}
For every compact smooth source, with \(Q\) defined by
\eqref{eq:Qdefinition},
\[
B_\sigma[F]-Q[F]
=-R_\sigma[\widehat F]-\Zoff[F]+o_F(1).
\]
In particular this gives \eqref{eq:intro-arithmetic} for prepared
sources.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Subtract \eqref{eq:full-zero-pairing} from \eqref{eq:deficit}
with \(f=\widehat F\).
\end{proof}

The identity separates two distinct questions. Even if the exact
return trace were shown to vanish, its identified limit would be
\(\Zcrit\). Recovering \(Q\) additionally requires addressing
the off-line pairing. On the other hand, the current domination
\(\nu_*\le\mucrit\) does not determine the surviving weights:
it does not rule out dependence of those weights on the global phase,
including off-line zeros. Conversely, under RH that pairing vanishes,
but the results of this paper still do not prove vanishing return
mass for the exact projection.

The all-support quantifier is essential in any use of Weil
positivity: a fixed finite list of sources or a fixed support window is
insufficient. The prepared-source version of the criterion is discussed
in \cite[Appendix C]{CCWeil}. Section~\ref{sec:source-criteria} uses one
fixed profile with unbounded translations and an additional transform
argument; it does not eliminate the all-support quantifier. No
unconditional all-support positivity conclusion is obtained here.

\subsection{Support-adapted finite products and a fixed-place obstruction}
\label{sec:finite-comparison}

The finite Euler identity gives a separate exact arithmetic comparison.
Let \(F\) be prepared as in \eqref{eq:prepared}, with support diameter
at most \(L_0\), and put
\[
S_{L_0}=\{p\text{ prime}:p\le e^{L_0}\}.
\]
If \(S\supseteq S_{L_0}\), every active prime and all its active
powers occur in \(W_{S,1/2}\). The correlation vanishes at the
endpoints of its support, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-comparison}
W_{S,1/2}[F]=W_{1/2}[F],\qquad
Q[F]=B_{S,1/2}[F]-K_{S,1/2}[F].
\end{equation}
This follows directly from \eqref{eq:finite-boundary} and pole
neutrality; it uses no sum over zeros. Preparation was unnecessary
for the operator identity itself. Before all active primes are
included, the complete comparison instead reads
\[
Q[F]=B_{S,1/2}[F]-K_{S,1/2}[F]
-\bigl(W_{1/2}[F]-W_{S,1/2}[F]\bigr).
\]
Thus the omitted prime terms cannot be absorbed into a signed
boundary correction. The reconciliation with the earlier transported
metric residual is recorded in \cite[Section 5]{FiniteEulerNote}.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-place obstruction]\label{prop:fixed-place-obstruction}
Fix a finite prime set \(S\) and \(\sigma>0\). Choose any nonzero
\(\eta\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R;\mathbb C)\), and define
\[
h_R(x)=R^{-1/2}\eta(x/R),\qquad
F_R=(-\partial_x^2+1/4)h_R.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-place-limit}
\Gamma[F_R]-W_{S,\sigma}[F_R]
\longrightarrow\frac{\norm\eta_2^2}{16}
\left(\gamma_\infty(0)
-2\sum_{p\in S}\frac{\log p}{p^\sigma-1}\right)<0.
\end{equation}
Consequently \(K_{S,\sigma}[F_R]>B_{S,\sigma}[F_R]\ge0\)
for all sufficiently large \(R\). The family is exactly pole neutral
for every \(R\); it can also be chosen to have zero mean.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
After rescaling Fourier variables,
\[
\Gamma[F_R]=\int\gamma_\infty(u/R)
(u^2/R^2+1/4)^2|\widehat\eta(u)|^2\frac{\dd u}{2\pi}
\longrightarrow \gamma_\infty(0)\norm\eta_2^2/16.
\]
Schwartz decay and the logarithmic growth of \(\gamma_\infty\)
give dominated convergence. For each fixed shift \(a\), continuity
of translations and the derivative correction of order \(R^{-2}\)
give \(\kappa_{F_R}(a)\to\norm\eta_2^2/16\).
The bound \(|\kappa_{F_R}(a)|\le\norm{F_R}_2^2\), uniformly
bounded for \(R\ge1\), permits dominated convergence through the
absolutely summable prime-power coefficients at fixed \(S,\sigma\).
This gives \eqref{eq:fixed-place-limit}. Its sign follows from
\[
\gamma_\infty(0)=-\gamma_{\mathrm E}-\pi/2-3\log2-\log\pi<0.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:finite-boundary} now gives \(K_{S,\sigma}>B_{S,\sigma}\).
Preparation gives \(\widehat F_R(\pm\ii/2)=0\), and choosing
\(\int\eta=0\) also gives \(\widehat F_R(0)=0\).
See \cite[Section 6]{FiniteEulerNote} for the detailed scaling calculation.
\end{proof}

The prime set required by \eqref{eq:finite-comparison} grows with
the support of \(F_R\). The fixed-set dominated-convergence argument
does not apply on that growing family. In particular the proposition
neither refutes the support-adapted inequality \(K_{S_{L_0},1/2}\le
B_{S_{L_0},1/2}\) nor implies a negative complete Weil form.
It does rule out \(K_{S,\sigma}\le0\), and even \(K_{S,\sigma}\le
B_{S,\sigma}\), on the all-support prepared class for every fixed
finite \(S\), including the empty set. This is compatible with the
restricted-support bound in Remark~\ref{rem:calibration}.

\section{Prepared-source criteria and finite translate certificates}
\label{sec:source-criteria}

We now separate three statements about the arithmetic form: a scalar
criterion equivalent to the all-window sign, an unconditional obstruction
for the centered reference, and a computer-assisted comparison on one
seven-dimensional source space. Only the last statement certifies a
particular joint arithmetic matrix. The first gives an exact unresolved
growth obligation, while the second bounds the size of the reference's
negative block.

\subsection{The three-moment source space and one fixed probe}
\label{sec:probe-setup}

The remaining all-window arithmetic question admits a scalar reduction
for one explicit source. We give the proof, including the transform
nonvanishing that is particular to this source; the underlying
derivation is recorded in \cite{SingleProbeNote}.
For \(L>0\), let \(I_L=(-L/2,L/2)\), extend its sources by zero, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-source-space}
E_L=\left\{F\in L^2(I_L):
\int F(x)\dd x=\int e^{x/2}F(x)\dd x
=\int e^{-x/2}F(x)\dd x=0\right\}.
\end{equation}
Complex sources are allowed. Use the logarithmic form domain
\[
\mathcal D_{\log}=
\left\{F\in L^2(\mathbb R):
\int\log(2+|t|)|\widehat F(t)|^2\frac{\dd t}{2\pi}<\infty\right\}.
\]
The growth \(|\gamma_\infty(t)|=O(\log(2+|t|))\) makes
\(\Gamma\) continuous in this form norm. On sources in one fixed
compact interval, the prime contribution is a finite sum of bounded
translation forms. Hence the restriction of \eqref{eq:Qdefinition} to
\(E_L\cap\mathcal D_{\log}\) is
\begin{align}
Q(F_1,F_2)
&=\int\gamma_\infty(t)
\overline{\widehat F_1(t)}\widehat F_2(t)
\frac{\dd t}{2\pi}\notag\\
&\quad-\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
\int\overline{F_1(x)}
\bigl(F_2(x+\log n)+F_2(x-\log n)\bigr)\dd x.
\label{eq:probe-sesquilinear}
\end{align}
This convention is antilinear in the first argument, with
\(Q[F]=Q(F,F)\). The pole term vanishes by the two exponential
moments. Formula~\eqref{eq:probe-sesquilinear} is compatible across
nested windows and is invariant under simultaneous translations.

The all-window assertion on these form domains is equivalent to the
same assertion on compact smooth sources with the three moments.
Indeed, convolution with a compact smooth approximate identity
preserves each vanishing moment, converges in \(\mathcal D_{\log}\),
and keeps all approximants in one slightly larger fixed interval.
Continuity of \eqref{eq:probe-sesquilinear} then passes its sign to the
limit. This use of a larger fixed interval is permitted by the
all-window quantifier.

To avoid confusing a spatial derivative with the Fourier multiplier
notation, write \(\partial_x=d/dx\). Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-polynomial}
\begin{gathered}
h_0(x)=
\begin{cases}(1-16x^2)^8,&|x|<1/4,\\0,&|x|\ge1/4,
\end{cases}
\\
g_0=(-\partial_x^2+1/4)\partial_xh_0,
\qquad g=\frac{g_0}{\norm{g_0}_2},\qquad \ell=\frac12.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The zero extension of \(h_0\) is \(C^7\), and \(g_0\) is \(C^4\).
The distributional derivatives in \eqref{eq:probe-polynomial}
therefore agree with the ordinary derivatives on the two sides of
each endpoint, without additional point masses. Integration by parts
shows that \(g\) has all three vanishing moments: the factor
\(\partial_x\) gives zero mean, and
\(-\partial_x^2+1/4\) gives the two exponential moments.
In particular \(g\in E_\ell\cap\mathcal D_{\log}\).
Alternatively, convolving \(h_0\) with smooth approximate identities
before applying its differential preparation gives exactly prepared
smooth approximants to \(g\) in the same form norm, in a fixed
slightly larger interval. The same holds for every finite linear
combination of translates of \(g\).

Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-transforms}
\begin{aligned}
H_0(z)&=\int h_0(x)e^{zx}\dd x,
&G(z)&=\int g(x)e^{zx}\dd x,\\
\varphi(u)&=\int g(x)g(x+u)\dd x,
&\Phi(z)&=\int\varphi(u)e^{zu}\dd u=G(z)G(-z).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These transforms are entire. The autocorrelation \(\varphi\) is real
and even, supported on \([-\ell,\ell]\), and \(\varphi(0)=1\).
Integration by parts and integration of the even monomials give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-entire-formula}
\begin{aligned}
G(z)&=\frac{z(z^2-1/4)H_0(z)}{\norm{g_0}_2},\\
\frac{H_0(z)}{H_0(0)}
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
\frac{(z^2/64)^k}{(19/2)_k\,k!}
={}_0F_1\left(;19/2;z^2/64\right),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \((a)_k\) is the rising factorial and \(H_0(0)>0\).

\begin{lemma}[Nonvanishing of the prepared transform]
\label{lem:probe-nonvanishing}
Every zero of \(H_0\) is nonzero and purely imaginary. Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-nonvanishing}
\Phi(z)\ne0\quad\text{if }\Rea z>0\text{ and }z\ne1/2.
\end{equation}
The zero of \(\Phi\) at \(1/2\) has order two.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set \(f(z)=H_0(z)/H_0(0)\). If \(f(z_0)=0\), then \(z_0\ne0\).
For \(y(t)=f(z_0t)\), termwise differentiation of
\eqref{eq:probe-entire-formula} gives
\[
(t^{18}y'(t))'=\frac{z_0^2}{16}t^{18}y(t),
\qquad y(0)=1,\quad y'(0)=0,\quad y(1)=0.
\]
Multiplication by \(\overline{y(t)}\) and integration by parts yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-ode-energy}
-\int_0^1t^{18}|y'(t)|^2\dd t
=\frac{z_0^2}{16}\int_0^1t^{18}|y(t)|^2\dd t.
\end{equation}
The boundary terms vanish, including at zero by regularity and the
factor \(t^{18}\). Both integrals in \eqref{eq:probe-ode-energy}
are strictly positive before the displayed minus sign: otherwise
\(y\) would be constant, contrary to its endpoint values.
Thus \(z_0^2\) is strictly negative real.
Formula~\eqref{eq:probe-entire-formula} now proves
\eqref{eq:probe-nonvanishing}. Since \(H_0(\pm1/2)>0\), the factors
\(G(z)\) and \(G(-z)\) each have a simple zero at \(z=1/2\), giving
exactly a double zero of \(\Phi\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The signed prime covariance and its Laplace transform}
\label{sec:probe-covariance}

For real \(r\), let \((\tau_rg)(x)=g(x-r)\), and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-covariance-def}
C_g(r)=Q(g,\tau_rg),\qquad q=Q[g],\qquad
M_g(r)=\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
\varphi(\log n-r)\quad(r\ge0).
\end{equation}
The function \(C_g\) is real and even, by the real symmetric kernels
and translation invariance in \eqref{eq:probe-sesquilinear}.
Because \(\ell<\log2\), no prime term is active on the diagonal,
so \(q=\Gamma[g]\). Each sum defining \(M_g(r)\) is finite;
locally its indices range over a fixed finite set. It follows that
\(M_g\) is continuous. The elementary estimate
\(\Lambda(n)\le\log n\) also gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-elementary-growth}
M_g(r)=O((1+r)e^{r/2}).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact covariance and Laplace identities]
\label{prop:probe-identities}
For \(r>\ell\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-covariance-identity}
C_g(r)=-M_g(r)+\epsilon_g(r),\qquad
|\epsilon_g(r)|\le
\frac{\ell e^{-5(r-\ell)/2}}{1-e^{-2(r-\ell)}}.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-laplace}
\int_0^\infty e^{-sr}M_g(r)\dd r
=-\Phi(s)\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(1/2+s),
\qquad \Rea s>1/2.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The digamma partial-fraction expansion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-digamma-kernel}
\gamma_\infty(t)-\gamma_\infty(0)
=2\int_0^\infty n(u)(1-\cos(tu))\dd u,
\qquad n(u)=\frac{e^{-u/2}}{1-e^{-2u}};
\end{equation}
see \cite[\S5.7, equation 5.7.6]{DLMF}.
Thus the off-diagonal convolution kernel of \(\Gamma\) is
\(-n(|u|)\). More explicitly, polarizing the Fourier identity
\eqref{eq:probe-digamma-kernel} and using the disjoint supports when
\(r>\ell\) gives
\[
\Gamma(g,\tau_rg)
=-\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}\varphi(u)n(r+u)\dd u.
\]
The two prime branches in \eqref{eq:probe-sesquilinear} are
\(\varphi(\log n-r)\) and \(\varphi(\log n+r)\).
Only the first can be nonzero for \(r>\ell\); hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-exact-remainder}
C_g(r)=-M_g(r)
-\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}\varphi(u)n(r+u)\dd u.
\end{equation}
There is no factor two in front of \(M_g(r)\).
Preparation gives
\(\int\varphi(u)e^{-u/2}\dd u=\Phi(-1/2)=0\).
It cancels the first term in the convergent expansion
\(n(v)=\sum_{k\ge0}e^{-(2k+1/2)v}\), \(v>0\).
Finally,
\(\norm\varphi_1\le\norm g_1^2\le\ell\norm g_2^2=\ell\).
Bounding the terms with \(k\ge1\) on \(r+u\ge r-\ell\) proves
\eqref{eq:probe-covariance-identity}.

For every \(n\ge2\), the support of
\(r\mapsto\varphi(\log n-r)\) lies strictly in \((0,\infty)\),
since \(\ell<\log2\). Substitution \(u=\log n-r\) therefore gives
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-sr}\varphi(\log n-r)\dd r
=n^{-s}\Phi(s).
\]
For \(\Rea s>1/2\), the corresponding absolute integrals sum to at
most a finite profile constant times
\(\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2-\Rea s}\).
Termwise integration is thus justified, and the logarithmic-derivative
Dirichlet series proves \eqref{eq:probe-laplace}; see
\cite[\S27.4, equation 27.4.12]{DLMF}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A single-probe equivalence theorem}
\label{sec:probe-equivalence}

\begin{theorem}[One prepared probe and all-window positivity]
\label{thm:probe-equivalence}
For the fixed normalized source \eqref{eq:probe-polynomial}, the
following seven statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item\label{item:probe-all-window}
For every \(L>0\), \(Q[F]\ge0\) for all
\(F\in E_L\cap\mathcal D_{\log}\).
\item\label{item:probe-rh} The Riemann hypothesis holds.
\item\label{item:probe-pairwise}
\(q\ge0\) and \(|C_g(r)|\le q\) for every real \(r\).
\item\label{item:probe-bounded} \(M_g\) is bounded on \([0,\infty)\).
\item\label{item:probe-subexponential}
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there is a finite
\(A_\varepsilon\) such that
\(|M_g(r)|\le A_\varepsilon e^{\varepsilon r}\) for all \(r\ge0\).
\item\label{item:probe-l1}
For every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\(\int_0^\infty e^{-\varepsilon r}|M_g(r)|\dd r<\infty\).
\item\label{item:probe-l2}
For every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\(\int_0^\infty e^{-2\varepsilon r}|M_g(r)|^2\dd r<\infty\).
\end{enumerate}
Statement~\ref{item:probe-pairwise} is exactly positivity of every
two-source translate Gram matrix for this probe, including complex
coefficients.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Under RH, \eqref{eq:full-zero-pairing} expresses \(Q[F]\), for every
compact smooth source satisfying the three moments, as the sum
\(\sum_\rho m_\rho|\widehat F(\Ima\rho)|^2\ge0\).
The form closure established in Section~\ref{sec:probe-setup}
extends positivity to the domains in
\ref{item:probe-all-window}. Hence
\ref{item:probe-rh} implies \ref{item:probe-all-window}.

Each finite linear combination of translates of \(g\) has the three
moments and lies in one compact window. If necessary translate that
window into an interval \(I_L\); simultaneous translation preserves
the form. Under \ref{item:probe-all-window}, its Gram matrix
\([C_g(r_j-r_i)]_{i,j}\) is consequently positive semidefinite over
\(\mathbb C\). In particular the two-source matrix has diagonal
\(q\) and off-diagonal entries \(C_g(r)\) and its conjugate, proving
\ref{item:probe-pairwise}.

Under \ref{item:probe-pairwise},
\eqref{eq:probe-covariance-identity} bounds \(M_g\) on
\([\ell+1,\infty)\); its continuity handles the remaining compact
interval. This proves \ref{item:probe-bounded}, which immediately
implies \ref{item:probe-subexponential}, \ref{item:probe-l1}, and
\ref{item:probe-l2}. Statement~\ref{item:probe-subexponential}
implies \ref{item:probe-l1} by using a growth exponent smaller than
the specified integration exponent. Statement~\ref{item:probe-l2}
also implies \ref{item:probe-l1}: for \(\delta>0\),
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-\delta r}|M_g(r)|\dd r
\le
\left(\int_0^\infty e^{-\delta r}|M_g(r)|^2\dd r\right)^{1/2}
\left(\int_0^\infty e^{-\delta r}\dd r\right)^{1/2},
\]
using \(\varepsilon=\delta/2\) in \ref{item:probe-l2}.

It remains to prove \ref{item:probe-l1} implies \ref{item:probe-rh}.
The weighted absolute integrability in \ref{item:probe-l1} makes
the left side of \eqref{eq:probe-laplace} holomorphic throughout
\(\Rea s>0\). On every compact subset of that half-plane, a smaller
positive exponential weight dominates the integrand and all its
derivatives with respect to \(s\). By meromorphic continuation and
the identity theorem, the right side of \eqref{eq:probe-laplace}
must coincide with this holomorphic function there. The zeta pole
at \(1\) causes no obstruction: the double zero of \(\Phi\) at
\(s=1/2\) cancels the simple pole of its logarithmic derivative.

Suppose instead that \(\rho\) is a nontrivial zero with
\(\Rea\rho>1/2\), and write \(s_\rho=\rho-1/2\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:probe-nonvanishing} gives \(\Phi(s_\rho)\ne0\),
since \(\rho\ne1\). If \(m_\rho\) is its multiplicity, the right
side of \eqref{eq:probe-laplace} then has a genuine simple pole with
residue
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-residue}
-m_\rho\Phi(s_\rho)\ne0.
\end{equation}
This contradicts holomorphy. Reflection of the nontrivial zeros under
the functional equation excludes zeros to the left as well, proving
RH; see \cite[\S\S25.4, 25.10]{DLMF}. The argument does not require
simple zeros or a rightmost off-line zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Growth forced by an off-line zero]
\label{cor:probe-growth}
Let \(a\ge0\). Either of the assumptions
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-ar}|M_g(r)|\dd r<\infty,
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
|M_g(r)|\le A(1+r)^k e^{ar}\quad(r\ge0)
\]
for finite constants \(A\) and \(k\ge0\) implies that zeta has no
zero with real part greater than \(1/2+a\).
Conversely, a hypothetical zero \(\rho\) with
\(\beta=\Rea\rho>1/2\) forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-forced-growth}
\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log(1+|M_g(r)|)}{r}
\ge\beta-1/2.
\end{equation}
For every \(0<\varepsilon<\beta-1/2\),
\(\int_0^\infty e^{-\varepsilon r}|M_g(r)|\dd r=\infty\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Either displayed hypothesis makes the Laplace transform holomorphic
in \(\Rea s>a\). The same nonzero-residue argument therefore
excludes zeros with \(\Rea\rho>1/2+a\).
If the limsup in \eqref{eq:probe-forced-growth} were smaller than
\(\beta-1/2\), an intermediate positive exponent would bound
\(|M_g|\) eventually, and continuity would extend that bound to
all \(r\ge0\). The preceding implication would exclude \(\rho\).
The weighted-integral divergence follows from the first implication
with \(a=\varepsilon\).
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:probe-equivalence} uses the exact arithmetic transform
of this particular source. Pairwise positivity of an arbitrary
covariance function does not imply positivity of every larger Gram
matrix. Here the pairwise bounds imply RH through
\eqref{eq:probe-laplace}, and the complete explicit formula then gives
the larger-matrix and all-source conclusions.
No bound in statements~\ref{item:probe-pairwise}--\ref{item:probe-l2}
is established here for all separations. In particular,
\eqref{eq:probe-elementary-growth} does not have the subexponential
strength required by the theorem. The growth corollary supplies
neither an effective first violating separation nor a lower bound
at every large separation. The unresolved step remains a global
signed estimate for \(M_g\); the equivalence proof requires no
finite numerical certificate.


\subsection{The size of the negative centered reference block}
\label{subsec:centered-negative-index}

The centered comparison has a separate obstruction whose dimension can be
estimated explicitly.  For \(I_L=(-L/2,L/2)\), let
\[
 (J_LF)(x)=\int_{I_L}e^{-|x-y|/2}F(y)\dd y,
 \qquad x\in I_L.
\]
Thus \(J_L\) is the compression of the convolution operator with Fourier
multiplier \((t^2+1/4)^{-1}\); it is unrelated to the reflection operator
\(\J\).  On functions supported in \(I_L\), define the centered reference
form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered-reference-form}
 A_L[F]=\Gamma[F]+\inner{F}{J_LF}
       =\int_{\mathbb R}m(t)|\widehat F(t)|^2\frac{\dd t}{2\pi},
 \qquad m(t)=\gamma_\infty(t)+\frac{1}{t^2+1/4}.
\end{equation}
The restriction to \(E_L\) imposes the same three zero moments as before.
If \(W_L\) denotes the bounded selfadjoint operator representing
\(W_{1/2}\) on sources supported in \(I_L\), then adding \(J_L\) to
both terms gives the exact identity
\[
 Q[F]=A_L[F]-\inner{F}{(W_L+J_L)F},\qquad F\in E_L.
\]
Here \(W_L\) is bounded because only finitely many prime shifts are
active on a fixed support interval; the restriction of these forms to
\(E_L\) is understood.  This identity explains the reference form
without asserting a sign for either term.
Write \(\operatorname{ind}_{-}(A_L|_{E_L})\) for the supremum of the
dimensions of subspaces on which this form is strictly negative.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit centered negative index]
\label{thm:centered-negative-index}
There is a unique positive number \(t_*\) satisfying
\[
 \Rea\psi(5/4+\ii t_*/2)-\log\pi=0.
\]
For every \(L>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered-negative-index-bound}
 \operatorname{ind}_{-}(A_L|_{E_L})
 \geq \max\left\{0,\left\lceil\frac{Lt_*}{\pi}\right\rceil-4\right\}.
\end{equation}
The same lower bound holds when the negative subspaces are required to
consist of compact smooth sources satisfying all three moments.  In
particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered-index-density}
 \liminf_{L\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{ind}_{-}(A_L|_{E_L})}{L}
 \geq\frac{t_*}{\pi}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The digamma recurrence gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered-shifted-digamma}
 m(t)=\Rea\psi(5/4+\ii t/2)-\log\pi,
\end{equation}
since
\(\Rea(1/(1/4+\ii t/2))=(t^2+1/4)^{-1}\).
Put \(a_n=2n+5/2\).  The digamma series yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered-m-series}
 m(t)=m(0)+\sum_{n=0}^\infty
          \frac{2t^2}{a_n(a_n^2+t^2)},\qquad
 m(0)=4-\gamma_{\mathrm E}-\frac{\pi}{2}-3\log2-\log\pi<0.
\end{equation}
The series shows that \(m\) is even and strictly increasing on
\((0,\infty)\).  Integral comparison gives
\[
 m(t)-m(0)\geq\frac12\log\left(1+\frac{4t^2}{25}\right),
\]
so \(m(t)\to\infty\).  This proves existence and uniqueness of \(t_*\),
with \(m(t)<0\) precisely when \(|t|<t_*\).
Moreover, \(f(v)=m(\sqrt v)\) is increasing and concave on \([0,\infty)\):
each summand \(2v/(a_n(a_n^2+v))\) has those properties, and its first two
derivative series converge locally uniformly.

Let \(D_{n,L}\) be the span of the first \(n\) Dirichlet sine modes on
\(I_L\), extended by zero.  These extensions belong to \(H^1(\mathbb R)\),
and Plancherel gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}t^2|\widehat F(t)|^2\frac{\dd t}{2\pi}
 =\norm{F'}_2^2
 \leq\left(\frac{\pi n}{L}\right)^2\norm F_2^2,
 \qquad F\in D_{n,L}.
\]
For \(F\ne0\), apply Jensen's inequality to the concave function \(f\)
and the probability measure
\(|\widehat F(t)|^2\dd t/(2\pi\norm F_2^2)\).  Its second moment is
finite, and \(m\) has logarithmic growth, so this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered-dirichlet-bound}
 A_L[F]\leq m\!\left(\frac{\pi n}{L}\right)\norm F_2^2
 \quad(F\in D_{n,L}).
\end{equation}
Since \(E_L\) imposes three linear conditions,
\(\dim(D_{n,L}\cap E_L)\geq n-3\).  Whenever \(\pi n/L<t_*\),
\eqref{eq:centered-dirichlet-bound} is a strict negative bound throughout
that intersection, including for complex linear combinations.

To realize these finite dimensional negative subspaces in the smooth
source class, approximate a basis in \(H^1_0(I_L)\) by compact smooth
functions.  The three moment functionals are independent on
\(C_c^\infty(I_L)\), so fixed compact smooth dual functions can correct
the three approximation errors exactly.  The corrected approximations
converge in \(H^1\), hence in the logarithmic form norm.  Linear
independence and the strict negative margin persist for sufficiently
accurate approximations.

The largest nonnegative integer strictly below \(Lt_*/\pi\) is
\(\lceil Lt_*/\pi\rceil-1\).  Use this integer for \(n\), or the zero
subspace when necessary, to obtain
\eqref{eq:centered-negative-index-bound}.  The strict inequality on
\(n\) accounts for the ceiling also when \(Lt_*/\pi\) is an integer.
Dividing by \(L\) proves \eqref{eq:centered-index-density}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:centered-correction-rank}
Any bounded finite-rank selfadjoint correction that makes \(A_L|_{E_L}\)
nonnegative has rank at least the right side of
\eqref{eq:centered-negative-index-bound}.  Any additional linear
conditions that remove all its negative directions have at least that
codimension within \(E_L\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A negative subspace of dimension larger than the rank of the correction
has a nonzero intersection with its kernel.  The form is unchanged and
negative on such a vector.  Intersecting with the common kernel of the
additional conditions proves the second assertion.
\end{proof}

An outward scalar calculation in \cite{JointProbeCertificate} gives the
rational bracket
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered-root-bracket}
 \frac{113002385070995698543}{18446744073709551616}
 <t_*<
 \frac{113002385070995698544}{18446744073709551616},
\end{equation}
and consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered-density-bracket}
 1.94992548206433579713
 <\frac{t_*}{\pi}<
 1.94992548206433579715.
\end{equation}
The calculation checks opposite signs at \(6\) and \(7\), then performs
64 rational bisections using 192-bit outward digamma arithmetic.  Exact
rational endpoint enclosures of both final function values are stored
in the record; uniqueness is the analytic conclusion of the proof above.
Thus, for example, the exact rational
\(c=1.9499254820643357971\) gives the implementable lower bound
\(\max\{0,\lceil cL\rceil-4\}\).
The decimal bounds use this scalar certificate, while
Theorem~\ref{thm:centered-negative-index} does not require its numerical
evaluation.

This is a lower bound for the centered reference, not a matching
asymptotic formula and not a negative-index statement about \(Q\).
It quantifies the size of the block that a centered signed comparison
must retain.  The underlying derivation and its scope are recorded in
\cite{CenteredIndexNote}.

\subsection{A certified joint comparison for seven translates}
\label{subsec:seven-source-certificate}

The preceding global reduction can be tested on one fixed finite set
of translates without assuming its unresolved growth condition.  Keep
the normalized polynomial probe \(g\), its autocorrelation \(\varphi\),
and \(\ell=1/2\).  Put
\[
 g_j=\tau_{2j}g\quad(0\leq j\leq6),\qquad
 G_{ij}=Q(g_i,g_j).
\]
The sources have disjoint supports and are orthonormal in \(L^2\).
Their union lies in an interval of length \(25/2\); a common translation
centers that interval without changing the Gram matrix or the zero
moments.

\begin{proposition}[Computer-assisted seven-source comparison]
\label{prop:seven-source-gram}
The arithmetic Gram matrix satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:seven-source-gram-bound}
 G>\frac1{20}I_7.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for every nonzero \((a_0,\ldots,a_6)\in\mathbb C^7\),
\[
 Q\!\left[\sum_{j=0}^6a_j\tau_{2j}g\right]
 >\frac1{20}\sum_{j=0}^6|a_j|^2.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Certificate and verification]
Translation invariance and the real even covariance make \(G\) a real
symmetric Toeplitz matrix.  Let \(q_k=G_{0k}\) for \(0\leq k\leq6\).
Since \(\ell<\log2\), no prime term occurs on the diagonal.  With
\(\kappa(u)=e^{-u/2}/(1-e^{-2u})\), its exact value is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:seven-source-diagonal}
 q_0=\gamma_\infty(0)
 +2\int_0^\ell\kappa(u)(1-\varphi(u))\dd u
 +2\left[\operatorname{arctanh}(e^{-\ell/2})
                  +\arctan(e^{-\ell/2})\right].
\end{equation}
The autocorrelation on \([0,\ell]\) is computed as an exact rational
polynomial from the unnormalized source and its exact squared norm.
For validated integration, the removable origin in
\eqref{eq:seven-source-diagonal} is resolved by writing
\((1-\varphi(u))/u\) as a polynomial and using
\(u\kappa(u)=e^{u/2}/(2\sinh(u)/u)\), with its analytic value at zero.
The implementation evaluates the same entire denominator as
\({}_0F_1(;3/2;u^2/4)=\sinh(u)/u\).
The resulting enclosure implies
\[
 1.46694003100528<q_0<1.46694003100529.
\]

For each \(r\in\{2,4,6,8,10,12\}\), all active prime powers in
\(M_g(r)\) are enumerated.  The largest sieve endpoint is
\(\lceil e^{12+1/2}\rceil=268338\).  The entry with \(r=2k\) is
enclosed using the full signed prime sum and the symmetric interval
\begin{equation}\label{eq:seven-source-offdiagonal}
 q_k\in-M_g(2k)+[-b(2k),b(2k)],\qquad
 b(r)=\frac{\ell e^{-5(r-\ell)/2}}{1-e^{-2(r-\ell)}}.
\end{equation}
For completeness, the off-diagonal archimedean kernel is
\(-\kappa(|u|)\), so its contribution is
\(-\int_{-\ell}^\ell\varphi(u)\kappa(r+u)\dd u\).
The leading \(e^{-(r+u)/2}\) term vanishes by the exponential moments.
Bounding the remaining geometric series and using
\(\norm\varphi_1\leq\norm g_1^2\leq\ell\) gives exactly the radius
\(b(r)\).  No archimedean remainder is omitted.

The certificate forms \(G-\frac1{20}I_7\) with these interval entries
and computes an outward \(LDL^{\mathsf T}\) factorization, where the
lower triangular factor has unit diagonal.  Every pivot interval is
strictly positive.  Table~\ref{tab:seven-source-pivots} displays rational
decimal lower bounds rounded down from the recorded enclosures; each

Positive pivot enclosures inductively justify all divisions and enclose

The package \cite{JointProbeCertificate} records the exact polynomial,

intervals and directed rounding after each operation to a grid of size

The elementary Gershgorin lower bound for this same matrix is enclosed
between \(-1.511672\) and \(-1.495987\), so it cannot certify its
positivity; that negative lower bound is not evidence of a negative
eigenvalue.  The signed joint factorization proves a comparison on one
seven dimensional subspace.  It proves neither positivity for all
sources of width \(25/2\), nor positivity for every translate of \(g\),
nor a bound for \(M_g(r)\) at untested separations.  It supplies no input
to the analytic single-probe equivalence or to
Theorem~\ref{thm:centered-negative-index}.


\section{Scope and further questions}\label{sec:scope}

The estimates establish a finite positive trace family for every
\(\sigma>1/2\), together with an identified critical limit for its
majorant and fixed Abel regularizations. For exact projections they
give an upper limiting measure and locate every possible loss at
one cutoff spectral endpoint. The solvable rational family shows
that the gap between these statements can persist even when each
prelimit cutoff is bounded away from one.

The next question about this particular zeta family is therefore
concrete: determine whether \(R_\sigma[b]\to0\) for every compact
smooth frequency test, or exhibit a test with a nonzero limiting
return defect. Condition~\eqref{eq:uniform-integrability} is an
equivalent endpoint condition once Theorem~\ref{thm:return} is available.
In the dictionary of Section~\ref{sec:dictionary}, this asks for the
limiting frequency distribution of \(\ker\mathfrak T_{v_\sigma}\).
The phase criteria of \cite{MPInner,MPBM} suggest tools for studying
kernel existence and dimension; they do not by themselves provide the
source-localized limiting weights required here. An approximate kernel
vector alone does not settle the question. When nonzero singular values
of \(T_\sigma\) approach zero, a small constraint residual does
not bound distance to the exact kernel.

The finite-product result establishes bounded transport, the exact
inverse metric, and the smoothed boundary identity at the critical
exponent for each fixed finite prime set. Its comparison with the
complete Weil form requires all active primes for each source support.
The present arithmetic target is the weaker signed comparison
\[
K_{S_L,1/2}[F]\le B_{S_L,1/2}[F],\qquad
F\in E_L\cap C_c^\infty(I_L),
\]
on an unbounded sequence of nested windows, with the same three moments.
The earlier mean-only upper-bound proposal is retired in the accompanying
first-prime analysis \cite{FirstPrimeResonance}: it constructs positive
corrections on prepared mean-zero sources already at \(L=1\), \(S=\{2\}\).
That separate obstruction concerns \(K>0\), not \(K>B\), and leaves the
present comparison open.

For the explicit polynomial profile in Section~\ref{sec:source-criteria},
an equivalent target is
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-\varepsilon r}|M_g(r)|\dd r<\infty
\qquad\text{for every }\varepsilon>0.
\]
The equivalence is proved, but this global signed bound is not.
Replacing the signed prime sum by its absolute majorant does not supply
the required estimate. The centered route must also accommodate a growing
negative reference block; its size has the explicit lower bound proved
above. The seven-source certificate retains simultaneous signed
correlations, but covers neither its infinite-dimensional complement nor
untested translations. It therefore supplies no unbounded-window
continuation or termination theorem.

The global phase path and an increasing finite set of primes at the
critical exponent remain distinct limiting procedures. In the
absolute-convergence region, \eqref{eq:transport} connects the
constructions, but no equality of their critical limits is asserted.
Likewise the two-parameter crossover in
Corollary~\ref{cor:crossover} concerns the rational model; its
scale is not an estimate for zeta.

The theorem package can therefore be assessed independently of
whether this phase family ultimately yields an arithmetic
positivity mechanism. Its content is the source-uniform trace
control, the majorant limit, the distinction between phase concentration
and exact-kernel mass, and the finite-place identity with its fixed-set
obstruction, together with the single-probe growth criterion and the
quantitative centered negative-index bound. The finite joint certificate
adds one verified arithmetic subspace. Neither the global signed growth
estimate nor convergence along growing prime sets is established.
