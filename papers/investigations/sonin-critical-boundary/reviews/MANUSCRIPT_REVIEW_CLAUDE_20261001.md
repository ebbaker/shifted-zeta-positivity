## Verdict

The manuscript is correct as far as I can determine. I found no mathematical error in any statement or proof, the signs and normalizations that are easy to get wrong (functional equation, phase derivative, pole and zero crossing terms, half residues on exceptional lines, the Blaschke energy) all check numerically to machine precision, and the operator identity at the heart of the paper (eq. L-trace, line 315) holds on the zeta phase itself to the accuracy of a discretization. The paper is also unusually candid about what it does not prove. As an internal consolidation of the September 29 notes it does its job and can be filed as such.

It is not yet a paper that should circulate, for three reasons that are about framing rather than correctness. First, every object in it is a classical Toeplitz or Hankel operator with a unimodular symbol, and the paper never says so; the identification is needed for the priority comparison the authors themselves flag as outstanding, and it is also where the tools for the open question live (Section 3 below). Second, the main uniform estimate is stated with \(\log^2(2+|j|)\) where \(\log(2+|j|)\) is both true and what one expects from the zero density; two one-line changes give it (Section 4). Third, the structural fact that drives everything, \(v_{1/2}\equiv1\), implies that the critical limit of this family is blind to off-line zeros, so the limit can never carry arithmetic information beyond the critical-zero count; the paper knows this (Sections 9.2 and 10) but the introduction reads as if the exact-projection question were the only gap between the construction and Weil's form (Section 5). There is also a list of smaller editorial items, including a citation number that appears to be wrong and four references that are now published (Section 7).

Recommendation: keep as the current working draft; before any circulation, reframe in Toeplitz–Hankel language with a literature comparison, sharpen the window bound, rewrite the opening paragraph, and fix the bibliography. No proof needs repair.

## 1. What the manuscript proves, in standard language

The following dictionary is not in the paper and should be. With \(V=v(D)\), \(\mathcal F=\mathcal JV\), and \(\chi\mathcal J=\mathcal JP\):

- \(T_\sigma=\chi\mathcal F_\sigma P=\mathcal J\,(PV_\sigma P)\). Up to the unitary \(\mathcal J\), the "cutoff transfer operator" is the Toeplitz operator \(T_{v_\sigma}\) with symbol \(v_\sigma\) on the Hardy space \(P\mathcal H\) (Fourier transforms analytic in the lower half-plane in the convention of eq. fourier).
- \(L_\sigma=PV_\sigma^*\chi V_\sigma P=H_{v_\sigma}^*H_{v_\sigma}\), where \(H_v=\chi VP\) is the Hankel operator with symbol \(v\). Equation Lfactor (line 258) is the basic Toeplitz–Hankel identity \(T_{\bar v}T_v=I-H_v^*H_v\).
- \(C_\sigma=\chi\mathcal F_\sigma\chi=\mathcal J\,(PV_\sigma\chi)\) is \(\mathcal J\) times the adjoint of the Hankel operator with symbol \(\bar v_\sigma\); \(C_\sigma^2=\chi V_\sigma^*PV_\sigma\chi\) and \(TT^*=I_\chi-C^2\) is the same identity for \(\bar v\).
- \(\Pi_\sigma\) is the orthogonal projection onto the Toeplitz kernel \(\ker T_{v_\sigma}\), equivalently the eigenspace of \(|H_{v_\sigma}|\) at the eigenvalue \(1\). Since \(\lVert H_v\rVert\le1\) for unimodular \(v\), the "return operator" \(H_\sigma=L_\sigma-\Pi_\sigma=L_\sigma 1_{[0,1)}(L_\sigma)\) is the part of \(|H_{v_\sigma}|^2\) strictly below its top.
- The Abel approximant of eq. abel is a functional calculus in \(L_\sigma\): \(\Pi_{\sigma,r}=g_r(L_\sigma)\) with \(g_r(\lambda)=(1-r)\lambda/(1-r\lambda)\), as the proof of Proposition abel (line 602) shows in the variable \(A=P-L\). The exact projection is \(g=1_{\{1\}}\).
- Proposition gap (line 402) is a Wiener–Hopf factorization: for \(\sigma>1\), \(v_\sigma=v_\infty\,h/\bar h\) with \(h(t)=\zeta(\sigma+it)\), and \(h^{\pm1}\) bounded analytic in the lower half-plane, so \(T_{v_\sigma}=T_{1/\bar h}T_{v_\infty}T_h\) and \(\ker T_{v_\sigma}=h^{-1}\ker T_{v_\infty}\). The transport \(D_\sigma\) of eq. transport is multiplication by \(1/h\) in frequency. This is the Widom–Devinatz setting for symbols of the form \(\bar g/g\).
- \(v_\infty\) is the symbol of the cosine transform \(g\mapsto2\int_0^\infty g(u)\cos(2\pi u\xi)\,du\) in logarithmic coordinates. I verified the orientation: with \(h(x)=e^{x/2}g(e^x)\) and the paper's Fourier convention, \(\widehat{U\mathcal CU^{-1}h}(t)=c(1/2-it)\hat h(-t)\) with \(c(s)=\pi^{1/2-s}\Gamma(s/2)/\Gamma((1-s)/2)\), which is exactly \(\mathcal Jv_\infty(D)\) with the \(v_\infty\) of line 363. This matters because the two orientations are not equivalent: by Coburn's lemma at most one of \(\ker T_{v_\infty}\), \(\ker T_{\bar v_\infty}\) is nonzero, and it is the paper's orientation that makes \(\ker T_{v_\infty}\) the Sonin space and \(\lVert C_\infty\rVert<1\).
- \(v_{1/2}\equiv1\) by the functional equation (checked numerically to \(10^{-20}\)), so at the critical line the Toeplitz operator is the identity and the Hankel operator is zero. Everything in Sections 6 and 7 is about how \(\ker T_{v_\sigma}\) and the singular values of \(H_{v_\sigma}\) behave as the symbol tends to \(1\) through symbols carrying one Blaschke factor of width \(\sigma-1/2\) per critical zero.

In this language the main theorem reads: \(\lVert H_{v_\sigma}f(D)^*\rVert_{\mathfrak S_2}^2\to\int|f|^2\,d\mu_{\mathrm{crit}}\); the singular values of \(H_{v_\sigma}\), localized by any compact frequency window, accumulate at \(1\); singular value exactly \(1\) occurs precisely on the Toeplitz kernel, whose limiting frequency weight \(c_\gamma\in[0,m_\rho]\) is unknown; and the rational model's symbol is a quotient of two Blaschke factors whose Hankel operator has top singular value \(q<1\) and trivial Toeplitz kernel. None of this diminishes the results, but a reader from operator theory will recognize it immediately, and the manuscript's own provenance note says a "theorem-by-theorem priority comparison with Hardy/model-space and Hankel-operator literature remains necessary." That comparison cannot be done without the dictionary.

Two observations from the operator runs that the paper may want to record. The localized Hankel block \(\Theta\chi V_\sigma Pb(D)^*\Theta\) is numerically rank one: its top squared singular value (\(0.739,0.811,0.897,0.945\)) carries essentially all of \(\operatorname{Tr}(bL_\sigma b^*)\), and the second is below \(5\times10^{-3}\). Second, the deficit \(1-\operatorname{Tr}(bL_\sigma b^*)\) is \(O(\sigma-1/2)\) and is entirely the Poisson smearing of the test function; nothing in a sandwiched trace can see the model's \(O(\epsilon^2)\) norm gap, which is consistent with the paper's statement that numerics cannot decide the exact-kernel question.

A methodological remark worth a footnote in the paper: on any finite grid or periodic box, \(\operatorname{Tr}(a(D)(V^*PV-P))=0\) identically by cyclicity, so the bulk term of eq. L-trace is an "anomaly" of the infinite-dimensional trace. On a circle the indicator \(P\) acquires a second, mirror-image interface at the seam that contributes, up to exponentially small terms, the line value of \(\operatorname{Tr}(bC^2b^*)\) to the circle's \(\operatorname{Tr}(bLb^*)\), and the two interfaces cancel the bulk. The identity is recovered only after localizing all three traces to one interface. Anyone who tries to check Lemma phase-trace numerically will hit this, and the illegitimate step (\(\operatorname{Tr}(aV^*PV)=\operatorname{Tr}(aP)\), false because \(aP\notin\mathfrak S_1\)) is exactly the one the lemma's proof must avoid.

## 3. The open problem is a Toeplitz-kernel problem, and there is literature for it

Writing \(\epsilon=\sigma-1/2\), the paper's local factorization says \(v_\sigma=B_\epsilon w_\epsilon\) near any compact frequency set. Globally, the critical-zero Blaschke product
\[
\Theta_\epsilon(t)=\prod_{\gamma}\Bigl(\frac{t-\gamma+i\epsilon}{t-\gamma-i\epsilon}\Bigr)^{m_\gamma}
\]
converges (Blaschke condition \(\sum\epsilon/(1+\gamma^2)<\infty\)) and is a meromorphic inner function for the lower half-plane; \(v_\sigma=\bar\Theta_\epsilon\,w^{\mathrm{glob}}_\epsilon\) with \(w^{\mathrm{glob}}_\epsilon\) unimodular, smooth, and tending to \(1\) locally uniformly. The exact-projection question — which of the \(\sum m_\gamma\) local packets survive in \(\ker T_{\bar\Theta_\epsilon w}\), and with what weight — is precisely the kind of question treated by Makarov and Poltoratski ("Meromorphic inner functions, Toeplitz kernels and the uncertainty principle", 2005, and the Beurling–Malliavin papers that followed), whose criteria for the (non)triviality and dimension of \(N[\bar\Theta\phi]\) are stated in terms of the argument of the symbol, that is, in terms of the phase \(\phi_\sigma\) whose derivative the manuscript already controls. The structural results on Toeplitz kernels (Coburn's lemma; Hayashi 1986; Hitt 1988; Sarason 1994, nearly invariant subspaces; Dyakonov) and the Hankel side (AAK theory of singular values; Peller, *Hankel operators and their applications*; Böttcher–Silbermann; Nikolski, *Operators, functions, and systems*) are the natural references both for priority and for tools. Two immediate consequences of the dictionary:

- The two sides of the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma\) enter with opposite orientation. A zero with \(\beta<\sigma\) (every critical zero once \(\sigma>1/2\), and the reflection \(1-\beta\) of an off-line zero) gives a factor \(B_{\sigma-\beta,\gamma}\) with its pole in the lower half-plane, i.e. a conjugate inner factor \(\bar\theta\) for \(P\mathcal H\); a zero with \(\beta>\sigma\) gives a factor with its zero in the lower half-plane, i.e. an inner factor \(\theta\). (The pole at \(s=1\) behaves like a zero on the left.) Since \(T_{\bar\Theta_L u\Theta_R}=T_{\bar\Theta_L}T_uT_{\Theta_R}\), if \(T_u\) is invertible then \(\ker T_{\bar\Theta_Lu\Theta_R}=\Theta_R^{-1}\bigl(T_u^{-1}K_{\Theta_L}\cap\Theta_RH^2\bigr)\): each left zero adds a model-space direction, each right zero imposes a vanishing condition, and the dimension is \(\deg\Theta_L-\deg\Theta_R\) generically (the Fredholm index). An off-line pair \(\{\beta,1-\beta\}\) straddling \(\sigma\) therefore has zero net index but a nontrivial finite-\(\sigma\) footprint, and the sign pattern is exactly that of the crossing terms \(\mathcal Z_\sigma\) in Theorem crossings (right-hand zeros enter with a minus sign). This is the sense in which the family "sees" off-line zeros, and it is at finite \(\sigma\), not in the limit. The archimedean factor \(v_\infty\), whose Toeplitz operator is not invertible, is what prevents this count from being literal for zeta; stating the count for the Blaschke part alone would still be worth a remark.
- The model is the simplest instance: \(v^{\mathrm{mod}}=-\bar\theta_\epsilon\theta_R\) with one left factor of width \(\epsilon\) and one right factor at distance \(R=1/\epsilon\), index zero, and \(\ker T_{\bar\theta_\epsilon\theta_R}=\theta_R^{-1}(K_{\theta_\epsilon}\cap\theta_RH^2)=0\) because \(K_{\theta_\epsilon}\) is spanned by the Cauchy kernel at \(-i\epsilon\), which does not vanish at \(-iR\). The analogous question for zeta is whether \(w^{\mathrm{glob}}_\epsilon\) carries inner-type behaviour "transverse" to the model spaces \(K_{\Theta_\epsilon}\) in this sense. That is a sharper formulation of "additional information about that global phase" (line 1234).

A speculative but concrete direction, offered as a suggestion and not a claim: under RH, \(h(t)=\zeta(\sigma+it)\) is zero-free in the lower half-plane for every \(\sigma>1/2\), with a single pole and polynomial growth, so the transport \(1/h\) still makes sense on a dense core of the Sonin space as an unbounded operator. Whether \(h^{-1}(\text{core})\subset\ker T_{v_\sigma}\) for \(1/2<\sigma\le1\), and what frequency mass that image carries as \(\sigma\downarrow1/2\), is a question about one explicit family of functions and seems more tractable than the abstract return-mass condition eq. uniform-integrability. Conversely, a zero with \(\beta>\sigma\) is exactly an obstruction to this transport, which would give a spectral interpretation of the type the introduction attributes to Connes.

## 4. Sharpness of the window bound

Theorem main states \(\nu_G([j,j+1])\le M_S\log^2(2+|j|)\). The true order is \(\log(2+|j|)\), which matches the zero density and is what the bulk term alone gives (eq. phase-L1). The square enters at two places in Lemma energy, and both are avoidable.

- For the Blaschke part: \(\mathcal E(B)=4\pi^2\deg B\) exactly when all zeros of the finite Blaschke product lie in one half-plane, because \(\frac1{4\pi^2}\mathcal E(u)=\lVert Pu\chi\rVert_{\mathfrak S_2}^2+\lVert\chi uP\rVert_{\mathfrak S_2}^2\), one Hankel operator vanishes for an inner \(u\), and the adjoint of the other, \(\chi\bar u(D)P\), is a partial isometry with initial space the model space \(K_u\), of dimension \(\deg u\). For mixed products \(\mathcal E(u_+u_-)\le2\mathcal E(u_+)+2\mathcal E(u_-)\le8\pi^2\deg\). So \(\mathcal E(\mathcal B_{j,\sigma})=O(\log(2+|j|))\), not \(O(\log^2)\); the telescoping Cauchy–Schwarz at line 731 loses a factor \(M\).
- For the background: \(\mathcal E(f)=\int|\xi||\hat f(\xi)|^2d\xi\le2\pi\lVert f\rVert_2\lVert f'\rVert_2\) by Cauchy–Schwarz in frequency, so \(\mathcal E(f_{j,\sigma})=O(1)\cdot O(\log(2+|j|))\) instead of \(O(\lVert f'\rVert_2^2)=O(\log^2)\).

With these, eq. main-window, eq. localized-energy and eq. tail-sum hold with \(\log(2+|j|)\). Separately, the constant can be taken independent of \(S\): for \(\sigma\ge3\) the remainder in eq. local-log is bounded by \(|\zeta'/\zeta(3)|\) plus \(O(\log(2+|j|))\) terms of size \(O(1)\), and the pole factor and archimedean term do not depend on \(S\). So the bound is uniform on \(\sigma\in(1/2,\infty)\). Neither change affects any later argument.

## 5. The critical limit cannot see off-line zeros; say so up front

Because \(v_{1/2}\equiv1\), a zero \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) with \(\beta\neq1/2\) and its reflection \(1-\beta+i\gamma\) contribute to \(\phi'_\sigma\) two Poisson bumps of opposite sign and equal width \(|\beta-1/2|\) as \(\sigma\downarrow1/2\); they cancel exactly in the limit. Consequently every limiting object in Theorem main — the majorant limit, the Abel limits, the upper bound on exact-projection limits — is a functional of \(\mu_{\mathrm{crit}}\) alone. Even a complete solution of the open problem (\(c_\gamma=m_\rho\) for all \(\gamma\)) would give \(B_\sigma[F]\to Z_{\mathrm{crit}}[F]\), which is nonnegative for trivial reasons and carries no information about \(Q[F]=Z_{\mathrm{crit}}[F]+Z_{\mathrm{off}}[F]\). Any arithmetic content of this family must therefore be extracted at finite \(\sigma\), where the Toeplitz kernel does respond to zeros on either side of the line, with opposite signs (Section 3), or from a quantitative rate in \(\sigma-1/2\).

The paper contains this conclusion (lines 1420–1425 and Section 10), but in softer words, and the introduction points the reader elsewhere. In particular the sentence at lines 86–88, "An orthogonal projection may converge strongly to zero while its smoothed trace retains a nonzero limit," describes a phenomenon the paper never exhibits for a projection: the trace limit is proved for \(L_\sigma\) and \(\Pi_{\sigma,r}\), which are not projections, it is open for \(\Pi_\sigma\), and in the only solvable example \(\Pi\equiv0\). I would rewrite the opening paragraph around the actual dichotomy (phase concentration versus exact-kernel mass, with the functional equation making the limit blind to off-line zeros), and move the remark on finite-\(\sigma\) sensitivity to off-line zeros into the introduction.

A related presentational point: Proposition abel, Theorem return and part (iii) of Theorem main are three views of one statement about the sandwiched spectral measure \(\tilde\eta_{\sigma,b}(E)=\operatorname{Tr}(b\,1_E(L_\sigma)\,b^*)\) on \((0,1]\): its \(\lambda\)-weighted mass on \([0,1-\delta]\) tends to zero and its \(\lambda\)-weighted mass near \(1\) tends to \(\int|b|^2d\mu_{\mathrm{crit}}\). Hence \(\operatorname{Tr}(b\,g(L_\sigma)\,b^*)\to g(1)\int|b|^2d\mu_{\mathrm{crit}}\) for every bounded Borel \(g\) on \([0,1]\) that is \(O(\lambda)\) at \(0\) and continuous at \(1\); the exact projection \(g=1_{\{1\}}\) is the natural choice that fails the continuity condition. Stating it this way would shorten Sections 4 and 7 and make the role of the endpoint transparent.

## 6. Points where the argument is terse and should be expanded

None of these is an error.

- Lemma phase-trace (line 306). The passage from \(\operatorname{Tr}(b\mathcal Qb^*)\) to the sum of block traces uses that \(a(D)\) times each block of eq. Qblocks is trace class and that cyclicity is applied only with one trace-class factor. Given the remark at the end of Section 2.2 above, one sentence saying which cyclic rearrangement is forbidden would help. The "diagonal integral" step for a smooth compactly supported kernel is standard but could cite a source.
- Theorem boundary (line 468). State \(H=JC^2J^*\) explicitly and the eigenbasis argument for \(\operatorname{Tr}(C_FHC_F^*)=\operatorname{Tr}(AC^2)\). The claim that \(\mathcal M=J\chi\mathcal H\oplus\chi\mathcal H\) reduces both \(\mathcal F\) and \(\mathcal Q\) deserves its own line (it follows from \([\mathcal F,\Pi]=0\) and \((\Pi+\mathcal Q)\Pi=0\)).
- Remark calibration (line 554). The archimedean value of eq. short-boundary is the correction term of Connes–Consani, but in arXiv:2006.13771v1 that statement is Theorem 3 of the introduction (Theorem 4.7 in the body), with \(\epsilon(\rho)=\sum_n\frac{\lambda(n)}{\sqrt{1-\lambda(n)^2}}\langle\xi_n|\vartheta(\rho^{-1})\zeta_n\rangle\), which matches the manuscript's formula at line 561 term for term. I could not locate a "Theorem 7"; please check against the published version before citing a number. Their Theorem 1 is also relevant here: for test functions supported in \([2^{-1/2},2^{1/2}]\) satisfying their vanishing conditions at the two pole points, they prove the Sonin trace is a lower bound for the archimedean Weil functional, i.e., in the manuscript's normalization, \(K_\infty[F]\le0\) for such prepared sources. "The correction is generally nonzero" (line 568) could be replaced by this sign statement, which is the one piece of sign information about \(K\) that exists.
- Section 5.1 (lines 629–631). The two inputs are classical and uniform in the stated range; I would cite a textbook (Titchmarsh §9.4–9.6; Davenport Ch. 15–16; Montgomery–Vaughan Ch. 10, 12) as the primary source rather than lecture notes, and I was unable to verify the proposition numbers "16 and 19" in Tao's post because the page did not load in full; please check them.
- Theorem crossings (line 1357). The exceptional-line treatment by averaging is sketched. It can be made direct: on \(\operatorname{Re}s=\beta\) the removable extension of \(2\operatorname{Re}(\zeta'/\zeta)\) equals the principal value of the paired integrand, and a principal value through a simple pole on the contour contributes half the residue.
