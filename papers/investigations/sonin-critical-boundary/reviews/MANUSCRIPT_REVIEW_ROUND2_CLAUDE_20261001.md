## 1. The response's corrections to the first review

The response disputes or qualifies three statements of mine. In each case the response is right.

- **Blindness of the limit to off-line zeros.** I wrote that every limiting object in Theorem 1.1 is a functional of \(\mu_{\mathrm{crit}}\) alone and concluded that "any arithmetic content must be extracted at finite \(\sigma\)". The first clause is true for the majorant limit, the Abel limits and the *upper bound* \(\nu_*\le\mu_{\mathrm{crit}}\), which is how I stated it, but the conclusion overreached: the exact-projection weights \(c_\gamma\) are determined by the global kernel \(\ker\mathfrak T_{v_\sigma}\), and nothing proved excludes their depending on off-line zeros. The correct statement, which the revised introduction (lines 93–101) and Section 9.2 (lines 1618–1622) now make, is that every limit is supported on the critical ordinates with weights in \([0,m_\rho]\), so whatever the limit encodes about off-line zeros must sit in those weights, and no positivity argument can extract a sign for \(Z_{\mathrm{off}}\) from a limit that is nonnegative by construction. I would add that last clause to the introduction; it is the operative consequence.
- **Sign of the archimedean correction.** I described Connes–Consani's Theorem 1 as requiring vanishing of the test function's transform "at the two pole points". Their hypothesis is support in \([2^{-1/2},2^{1/2}]\) and vanishing at \(i/2\) *and at \(0\)* (I re-read the statement in the 2021 author version: "Fourier transform vanishing at \(i/2\) and \(0\)"). The prepared sources of eq. 1.6 satisfy \(\widehat F(\pm i/2)=0\) but \(\widehat F(0)=\widehat h(0)/4\ne0\) in general, so the clean sign \(K_\infty[F]\le0\) is not available for them; the qualified bound \(K_\infty[F]\le c|\widehat F(0)|^2\) now stated in Remark 3.3 (lines 656–672) is the right import. One request in Section 5 below.
- **Kernel versus cokernel.** I wrote that for \(T_u\) invertible the kernel of \(T_{\bar\Theta_L u\Theta_R}\) has dimension \(\deg\Theta_L-\deg\Theta_R\) "generically (the Fredholm index)". That conflates the two: generically \(\dim\ker=\max(\deg\Theta_L-\deg\Theta_R,0)\), \(\dim\operatorname{coker}=\max(\deg\Theta_R-\deg\Theta_L,0)\), and the index is the difference. The orientation statement (left zeros add model-space directions, right zeros impose vanishing conditions) stands.

The response also, correctly, did not follow a suggestion of mine in the boundary proof. I had proposed taking the trace of \(A_{++}C^2\) "in an eigenbasis of \(C\)". That is fine at \(\sigma=\infty\), where \(C_\infty\) is trace class, but for \(1<\sigma<\infty\) the operator \(C_\sigma=D_-T_\infty PD_\sigma^{-1}\chi+D_-C_\infty D_-^{-1}\) contains the non-compact piece \(PD_\sigma^{-1}\chi=\sum_{n\ge2}n^{-\sigma}PU_{\log n}\chi\) and need not have an eigenbasis. The spectral-cutoff argument now in the proof (lines 618–624: cyclicity on \(\{|C|\ge1/n\}\), where \(C\) is boundedly invertible, then monotone convergence of the positive sandwich on one side and dominated convergence for the trace-class \(A_{++}C^2\) on the other) is the correct one. I checked it.

Items the response declined to change (theorem labels, the Neumann series with its new caveat, the averaging argument on exceptional lines, the Tao citation) are reasonable editorial choices and I do not press them. I was again unable to load Tao's post in full to confirm "Propositions 16 and 19"; the response reports having checked them, and I take that at face value.

### 2.4 Lemma 5.1 and the uniformity over \(\sigma\)

This is the main mathematical change and it is correct.

- Equation 5.7, \(\mathcal E(f)=\int|\xi||\widehat f(\xi)|^2d\xi\le2\pi\lVert f\rVert_2\lVert f'\rVert_2\): Plancherel in the manuscript's convention gives \(\lVert\widehat f\rVert_2^2=2\pi\lVert f\rVert_2^2\) and \(\lVert\xi\widehat f\rVert_2^2=2\pi\lVert f'\rVert_2^2\); Cauchy–Schwarz gives the bound ✓. Applied to \(f_{j,\sigma}=b_jw_{j,\sigma}\) with \(\lVert f\rVert_2=O(1)\) and \(\lVert f'\rVert_2=O(\log(2+|j|))\) by eq. 5.5, this gives \(\mathcal E(f_{j,\sigma})=O(\log(2+|j|))\) ✓.
- The finite-Blaschke identity (lines 840–850): for \(\Theta\) of degree \(m\) analytic in the lower half-plane, \(Z=P\Theta(D)\chi\) satisfies \(ZZ^*=P-\Theta P\Theta^*=\operatorname{proj}K_\Theta\) (because \(P\Theta P=\Theta P\) and \(\Theta P\) is an isometry onto \(\Theta P\mathcal H\)), \(\chi\Theta(D)P=0\), hence \(\frac1{4\pi^2}\mathcal E(\Theta)=\lVert Z\rVert_{\mathfrak S_2}^2+0=\dim K_\Theta=m\) ✓. Complex conjugation handles the other orientation ✓. Numerically (Section 3, part A) the identity holds to five digits for \(m=1,2,3\) with unequal widths and for a triple zero.
- The grouping into \(B_+B_-\) with \(m_++m_-\le1+\sum_{|\gamma-j|\le5}m_\rho\) and the bound \(\mathcal E(B_+B_-)\le2\mathcal E(B_+)+2\mathcal E(B_-)\le8\pi^2(m_++m_-)\) ✓ (one small improvement in Section 5).
- Uniformity in \(\sigma\) (lines 760–766): for \(\sigma\ge3\), \(|\zeta'/\zeta(\sigma+it)|\le\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)n^{-3}\), every subtracted zero denominator has modulus \(\ge\sigma-\beta\ge2\), the pole denominator has modulus \(\ge2\), and the number of subtracted terms is \(O(\log(2+|j|))\) by eq. 5.1 ✓. So eq. 5.2 holds with an absolute constant, and eq. 5.5, 5.6, 1.4, 5.8, 5.11 and the tail \(O_{f,N}(A^{1-2N}\log(2+A))\) follow uniformly on \((1/2,\infty)\) ✓. The retained \(O(\log^2(T_n+2))\) on the contour heights in Theorem 9.1 is a different estimate and is correctly left alone.

Part C of the numerical checks tests the conclusion directly on the zeta phase: at \(\sigma=0.6\) and \(0.51\), for \(j=10,30,\dots,30000\), the quantity \(\mathcal E(b_jv_\sigma)/4\pi^2\) rises from \(0.26\) to about \(3.6\)–\(3.9\), tracking \(0.26+\sum_{\gamma\in(j-2,j+2)}b_j(\gamma)^2\) (the cutoff's own energy plus one unit per critical zero weighted by the cutoff), and \(\mathcal E/\log(2+j)\) stays between \(8\) and \(15\) while \(\mathcal E/\log^2(2+j)\) falls from \(1.7\) to \(1.35\). The bound is therefore not only correct but of the right order: each zero in the window costs exactly \(4\pi^2\) of energy, so the window mass is \(\asymp\) the local zero count.

### 2.5 Corollary 7.2 (endpoint spectral calculus)

The passage to Schwartz \(f\) through \(L_\sigma^{1/2}\), bounding the mixed terms by \(\lVert k\rVert_\infty\lVert L^{1/2}r_N^*\rVert_{\mathfrak S_2}\lVert L^{1/2}f^*\rVert_{\mathfrak S_2}\) with \(\lVert L^{1/2}r_N^*\rVert_{\mathfrak S_2}^2=\int|r_N|^2d\nu_{L_\sigma}\) controlled uniformly by Proposition 5.2, is right and is needed because the sandwich is not positive for complex \(g\). ### 2.6 Remark 8.2 (a contrasting exact projection)

For \(v=B_\epsilon=(t-i\epsilon)/(t+i\epsilon)\): the kernel \(\check B_\epsilon(-x-y)\) vanishes for \(x,y<0\), so \(C=0\); for \(x,y>0\) it equals \(-2\epsilon e^{-\epsilon(x+y)}=-u_\epsilon(x)u_\epsilon(y)\), so \(P\mathcal FP=-|u_\epsilon\rangle\langle u_\epsilon|\), \(L=|u_\epsilon\rangle\langle u_\epsilon|\) with eigenvalue \(1\), hence \(\Pi=L\) ✓. \(u_\epsilon\rightharpoonup0\) gives strong convergence of the projections to \(0\) while \(\operatorname{Tr}(b\Pi b^*)=\lVert b(D)u_\epsilon\rVert^2\to|b(0)|^2\) ✓. This is exactly the one-dimensional model space \(K_{\bar B_\epsilon}\), and paired with the rational model (same local factor, weights \(1\) and \(0\)) it is the cleanest possible statement of why local data cannot determine the weights. The sentence "This general projection phenomenon is therefore explicit, but it has not been established for the exact zeta projection" is the correct scope.

## 5. Remaining items

None of these needs to precede the author's review.

- **Constant in the mixed-product bound (line 858–861).** \(\mathcal E(B_+B_-)\le4\pi^2(m_++m_-)\), not only \(8\pi^2(m_++m_-)\): since \(B_-\) preserves \(P\mathcal H\) and \(B_+\) preserves \(\chi\mathcal H\), one has \(\chi B_+B_-P=(\chi B_+P)(B_-P)\) and \(PB_+B_-\chi=(PB_-\chi)(B_+\chi)\), so \(\lVert\chi B_+B_-P\rVert_{\mathfrak S_2}^2\le m_+\) and \(\lVert PB_+B_-\chi\rVert_{\mathfrak S_2}^2\le m_-\). The numerics in Section 3 are consistent with this. It changes only a constant.
- **Range of the cited local estimate (line 760).** "It suffices to apply the cited local estimate on \(1/2\le\sigma\le3\)" should match the range in which the cited source states it (the textbook versions are for \(-1\le\sigma\le2\)); if the source covers only up to \(2\), say "on \(1/2\le\sigma\le2\)" and let the Euler-series paragraph start at \(\sigma\ge2\) (denominators then have modulus at least one, which is all that is used).
- **Normalization behind the imported sign bound (lines 656–672).** The remark applies Connes–Consani's inequality "in the manuscript's conventions" via \(g(\rho)=F(\log\rho)\). What makes this legitimate is that their archimedean distribution satisfies \(W_\infty(g\star g^*)=\Gamma[F]\) and their Sonin trace equals \(B_\infty[F]\) under this identification, including the \(dt/2\pi\) and the sign of the \(\log\pi\) term. The first-round numerical check of eq. 9.6 pins the manuscript's side of this; the Connes–Consani side should be stated in one sentence (their explicit formula is reproduced in their Appendix B) so that the constant \(c\) is known to transfer unchanged. The qualitative conclusion, \(K_\infty[F]\le0\) when \(\widehat F(0)=0\) as well, does not depend on this.
- **One sentence for the introduction (after line 101).** Whatever the weights \(c_\gamma\), every limit \(\sum c_\gamma|\widehat F(\gamma)|^2\) is nonnegative, so no sign information about \(Z_{\mathrm{off}}\) can come from positivity of the limit alone. This is the practical content of the blindness discussion and it is currently implicit.
- **Section 10, second citation of [CCQuasi, Proposition 5.5].** The paragraph on the semilocal construction is where the reader needs to be told that the Toeplitz-kernel description of the archimedean Sonin space is Connes–Consani's own, and that the \(\sigma>1\) phase is their quasi-inner product over all primes at exponent \(\sigma\).
- **Draft history.** The 2026-10-01 row says "uncommitted revision"; the revision is committed in `5a431c1` and the row should cite it. The response's "Source states" section has the same wording and could be left as a historical record with one added line.
- **Sharpness remark worth adding to Section 5.** Since each critical zero in the window contributes exactly \(4\pi^2\) to \(\mathcal E(b_jv_\sigma)\) and the archimedean chirp contributes \(\asymp\log(2+|j|)\) through eq. 5.7 (nearly tight for chirps), the \(\log(2+|j|)\) bound is of the correct order; one sentence saying so would preempt the question.
