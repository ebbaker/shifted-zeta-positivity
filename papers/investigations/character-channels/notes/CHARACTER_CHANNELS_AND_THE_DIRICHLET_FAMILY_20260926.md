## 0. Summary

In the YM manuscript, winding a character packet $a$ times splits it into $a$ phase channels at the rational angles $\beta = 2\pi j/a$ (eq. (4.6)); only the identity channel contributes to the prime term of the Weil form for $\zeta$, and the other channels — carrying the norm $(1-a^{-1})\|f\|^2$ in the Haar limit, eq. (7.9) — defeated recentering and positivity (Sections 5.4 and 7.1). This note shows:

1. **[proof]** In the Haar limit the winding operators $V_a$ and the phase multiplications $\chi_n \mapsto e^{2\pi i r n}\chi_n$ ($r \in \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$) satisfy exactly the defining relations of the Bost–Connes algebra; the branching law (4.6) *is* the Bost–Connes relation $\mu_a e(r)\mu_a^* = a^{-1}\sum_{as=r} e(s)$. The limiting module of Theorem 6.1, $L^2(\mathbb{R}\times\hat{\mathbb{Z}})$, is $L^2$ of the "integral" part $\mathbb{R}\times\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ of the adeles, and on it the Galois group $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^* = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{ab}/\mathbb{Q})$ acts unitarily, commuting with $\mathcal{V}_a$ and with translation.
2. **[proof]** The $\chi$-isotypic combinations of the phase channels (Gauss sums) are the multiplicative twists $\sum_n n^{-1/2}\chi(n) f(\log(n/N))\chi_n$, and their winding limits are the prime terms of the explicit formula for $L(s,\chi)$: $\lim_R \langle M^\chi_R f, V_a M^\chi_R g\rangle = a^{-1/2}\overline{\chi(a)}\langle f, U_{\log a} g\rangle$. The norm the $\zeta$-realization wasted is exactly the norm the nontrivial $L(s,\chi)$ need. **[floating]** Confirmed to $3\cdot10^{-13}$ at $N=1024$ and $2\cdot10^{-16}$ at $N=4096$ (Haar), including a complex character of order 4.
3. **[proof]** In the interacting state the character sectors are normalized by one state constant $\bar\rho_q$ (the average of the marginal over the primitive $q$-th roots of unity), the direct analogue of $\rho(0)$; but sectors of the *same parity* mix, with Gram matrix $G_{\chi\chi'} = q^{-1}\sum_{j\in(\mathbb{Z}/q)^*}\chi(j)\overline{\chi'(j)}\rho(2\pi j/q)$, unless $\rho$ is constant on the primitive roots. Since the SU(2) marginal is even in $\theta$, the state preserves exactly the subgroup $\{\pm 1\}\subset\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^*$ (complex conjugation) and breaks the rest of the Galois symmetry. **[floating]** Confirmed for the two cubic characters mod 7 ($G = 0.0747 - 0.0047i$ for the five-harmonic test marginal, $0$ for Haar), with $O(N^{-2})$ convergence.
4. **[proof + floating]** The manuscript's compensated archimedean insertion $C_\rho$ (5.12) applied to a character packet diverges as $R\|\cdot\|^2$ with finite part $\langle f, xf\rangle/\|f\|^2 + \varphi(q)^{-1}\sum_{j\in(\mathbb{Z}/q)^*}\log\frac{\min(j,q-j)}{q}$: it produces the conductor $-\log q$ together with a spurious constant and no parity factor. The reason is exact: the angle observable measures distance from the identity, and a character packet lives at the nonidentity roots of unity. The two parities of the manuscript's insertion (cosine with the polar map $B$, sine without it), giving $\mathrm{Re}\,\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{i\tau}{2})$ and $\mathrm{Re}\,\psi(\tfrac34+\tfrac{i\tau}{2})$, are precisely the archimedean factors $\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)$ and $\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s+1)$ of even and odd characters.
5. **[known + floating]** The natural target of the machinery is therefore not the Weil form of $\zeta$ but the family $\mathcal{Q} = \bigoplus_\chi Q_\chi$ of Weil forms of all Dirichlet $L$-functions, block-diagonal under the unit action, whose positivity is equivalent to GRH for that family (Weil 1952). The normalization of $Q_\chi$ — parity-dependent digamma, conductor $\log(q/\pi)$, prime terms $-\Lambda(n)\chi(n)n^{-1/2}$ — is verified on the manuscript's probe $f_*$ against the zeros of $L(s,\chi)$ for the six real primitive characters of conductor $3, 4, 5, 7, 8$, to $2\cdot10^{-9}$ or better at six translates (the residual is the weight of the omitted zeros above height 320).

What is *not* gained: any positivity mechanism. The archimedean operators on the module are assigned, not derived; the family form is Weil's explicit formula reorganized. What changes is the diagnosis: the profinite fiber is required rather than obstructive, the winding norm loss is not a loss, and the interacting Yang–Mills state is an obstruction in a further way (it breaks the Galois symmetry that keeps the $L$-function sectors apart).

## 1. Setting

Class sector $\mathcal{H}_{\rm cl}$ with basis $\chi_n$ ($n\ge 1$), Gram matrix $\langle\chi_n,\chi_m\rangle_\nu = t_{n-m}-t_{n+m}$ where $\rho(\theta) = \sum_k t_k e^{ik\theta}$ is the plaquette marginal, even and $2\pi$-periodic, $t_0 - t_2 = 1$ (eq. (2.11)). Haar is $\rho\equiv 1$, in which case $\{\chi_n\}$ is orthonormal. Winding $V_a\chi_n = \chi_{an}$, with $V_a^{*\nu}V_a = M_{\rho_a/\rho}$ (eq. (4.3)); phase packets
$$S^\beta_R f = \rho(\beta)^{-1/2}\sum_{n\ge1} n^{-1/2} e^{in\beta} f(\log(n/N))\,\chi_n,\qquad N = e^R,$$
with $\langle S^\alpha_R f, S^\beta_R g\rangle\to\delta_{\alpha\beta}\langle f,g\rangle$ (eq. (4.5)) and the branching law (4.6). Tests $f, g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$; $U_t f(x) = f(x-t)$; $\langle f, U_t g\rangle = \int \bar f(x) g(x-t)\,dx$.

For a Dirichlet character $\chi$ mod $q$ write $\varphi(q)$ for Euler's function, $\tau(\chi) = \sum_{r \bmod q}\chi(r)e^{2\pi i r/q}$ for the Gauss sum, $\kappa(\chi)\in\{0,1\}$ for the parity ($\chi(-1) = (-1)^\kappa$). For primitive $\chi$, $|\tau(\chi)|^2 = q$ and
$$\sum_{r \bmod q}\overline{\chi(r)}\,e^{2\pi i r m/q} = \chi(m)\,\tau(\bar\chi)\qquad\text{for all } m\in\mathbb{Z}, \tag{1.1}$$
both sides vanishing when $\gcd(m,q)>1$.

## 2. The phase channels carry the Bost–Connes algebra **[proof]**

**Proposition 2.1.** On the Haar class sector, define $\mu_a = V_a$ ($a\in\mathbb{N}$) and $e(r)\chi_n = e^{2\pi i r n}\chi_n$ ($r\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$). Then $\mu_a^*\mu_a = 1$, $\mu_a\mu_b = \mu_{ab}$, $e(r)e(s) = e(r+s)$, $e(r)^* = e(-r)$, $\mu_a\mu_b^* = \mu_b^*\mu_a$ for $\gcd(a,b)=1$, and
$$\mu_a\, e(r)\, \mu_a^* = \frac1a\sum_{s:\ as = r} e(s). \tag{2.1}$$
These are the defining relations of the Bost–Connes algebra [BC], in its standard representation on $\ell^2(\mathbb{N})$.

*Proof.* With $\{\chi_n\}$ orthonormal, $\mu_a$ is the isometry $e_n\mapsto e_{an}$ and $\mu_a^* e_m = e_{m/a}$ if $a\mid m$, else $0$. Both sides of (2.1) applied to $\chi_m$ give $\mathbf{1}_{a\mid m}\,e^{2\pi i r m/a}\chi_m$: on the right because $\frac1a\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}e^{2\pi i (r+j)m/a} = e^{2\pi i r m/a}\mathbf{1}_{a\mid m}$. The remaining relations are immediate. $\square$

**Remark 2.2.** The branching law (4.6) for $\rho\equiv1$ is (2.1) applied to a packet: $V_a S^\alpha_R f = a^{-1/2}\sum_{a\beta = \alpha}S^\beta_R U_{\log a} f$ is the statement $\mu_a e(\alpha/2\pi) = \big(a^{-1}\sum_{a\beta=\alpha}e(\beta/2\pi)\big)\mu_a$ together with $\mu_a$ acting on the Mellin coefficients as $n^{-1/2}\mapsto (an)^{-1/2}\sqrt a$ and $\log(n/N)\mapsto\log(an/N) = \log(n/N)+\log a$. In the interacting state $V_a^{*\nu}V_a = M_{\rho_a/\rho}\ne 1$, so the isometry relation fails and (4.6) acquires the factors $\sqrt{\rho(\beta)/\rho(\alpha)}$: the state *deforms* the Bost–Connes relations.

**Proposition 2.3.** The limiting module of Theorem 6.1, $\mathcal{K}_1 = \bigoplus_{\alpha\in 2\pi\mathbb{Q}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}}L^2(\mathbb{R}) \cong L^2(\mathbb{R}\times\hat{\mathbb{Z}})$ with $(\mathcal{V}_a F)(x,z) = \sqrt a\,\mathbf{1}_{a\hat{\mathbb{Z}}}(z)F(x-\log a, z/a)$, carries a second Bost–Connes representation ($\mu_a = \mathcal{V}_a$, $e(r)$ = multiplication by the character $z\mapsto e^{2\pi i r z}$ of $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$), and the group $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^*$ acts on it unitarily by $(W_u F)(x,z) = F(x, u^{-1}z)$, with $W_u\mathcal{V}_a = \mathcal{V}_a W_u$, $W_u e(r) W_u^* = e(ur)$, and $W_u$ commuting with translation in $x$.

*Proof.* Multiplication by $u\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^*$ preserves Haar measure on $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and the sets $a\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$, and commutes with $z\mapsto z/a$ on $a\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$; the relation (2.1) is checked as in 2.1, using $\frac1a\sum_{j}e^{2\pi i j z/a} = \mathbf{1}_{a\hat{\mathbb{Z}}}(z)$. $\square$

$\mathbb{R}\times\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is the ring of integral adeles inside $\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}} = \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{A}_f$, the action of $\mathbb{N}$ by $\mathcal{V}_a$ is multiplication by the positive rationals that preserve it, and $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^*$ is the unit group of the finite ideles; $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^*\cong\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{ab}/\mathbb{Q})$ is the symmetry group of the Bost–Connes system [BC, Thm 5]. On the Haar class sector $\ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ this symmetry is not unitarily implemented (it moves the joint spectrum $\mathbb{N}\subset\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ of the $e(r)$ off $\mathbb{N}$); it acts on the *limiting* data, permuting the phase channels $S^{2\pi j/q}_R\mapsto S^{2\pi uj/q}_R$.

**Remark 2.4 [heuristic].** The Mellin packet $\sum_n n^{-1/2}f(\log n - R)\chi_n$ is $f(H-R)\,e^{-H/2}\Omega$ for the Bost–Connes Hamiltonian $H\chi_n = (\log n)\chi_n$ and the formal vector $\Omega = \sum_n\chi_n$: a window, at scale $R$, of the $\beta = 1$ Gibbs vector $e^{-\beta H/2}\Omega$. The inverse temperature $\beta=1$ is the Bost–Connes phase transition, where the partition function $\zeta(\beta)$ diverges, and it corresponds to the critical line through $n^{-\beta/2}$. Nothing below depends on this remark.

## 3. Character sectors **[proof]**

**Definition 3.1.** For a primitive character $\chi$ mod $q$ and a smooth positive marginal $\rho$, put
$$\bar\rho_q = \frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{j\in(\mathbb{Z}/q)^*}\rho(2\pi j/q),\qquad
M^\chi_R f = \sqrt{\frac{q}{\varphi(q)\,\bar\rho_q}}\ \sum_{n\ge1} n^{-1/2}\chi(n)\,f(\log(n/N))\,\chi_n .$$
For $q=1$ this is the identity packet $S^0_R f$ of (4.4) with $\bar\rho_1 = \rho(0)$.

**Lemma 3.2 (Gauss-sum decomposition).** With the unnormalized phase packets $P^\beta_R f = \sum_n n^{-1/2}e^{in\beta}f(\log(n/N))\chi_n = \rho(\beta)^{1/2}S^\beta_R f$,
$$\sum_{n} n^{-1/2}\chi(n) f(\log(n/N))\chi_n = \frac{1}{\tau(\bar\chi)}\sum_{j\in(\mathbb{Z}/q)^*}\overline{\chi(j)}\,P^{2\pi j/q}_R f .$$
*Proof.* (1.1). $\square$

So the character packet is the $\chi$-isotypic combination of the phase channels at the primitive $q$-th roots of unity, and $V_a$ acting on it redistributes those channels by (4.6) with the $\rho$-factors cancelling against the $P$-normalization: $V_a P^{\beta}_R g = a^{-1/2}\sum_{a\beta' = \beta}P^{\beta'}_R U_{\log a}g$ exactly, in any state.

**Theorem 3.3 (character limits).** For primitive $\chi,\chi'$ mod $q$ and $f,g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$, with
$$G_{\chi\chi'} = \frac1q\sum_{j\in(\mathbb{Z}/q)^*}\chi(j)\overline{\chi'(j)}\,\rho(2\pi j/q),\qquad c_{\chi\chi'} = \frac{\tau(\bar\chi)}{\tau(\bar\chi')}\cdot\frac{q}{\varphi(q)\,\bar\rho_q},$$
one has, as $R\to\infty$,
$$\langle M^\chi_R f, M^{\chi'}_R g\rangle_\nu \to c_{\chi\chi'}G_{\chi\chi'}\langle f,g\rangle_2,\qquad
\langle M^\chi_R f, V_a M^{\chi'}_R g\rangle_\nu \to a^{-1/2}\,\overline{\chi'(a)}\,c_{\chi\chi'}G_{\chi\chi'}\langle f, U_{\log a}g\rangle_2 . \tag{3.1}$$
In particular, for $\chi=\chi'$: $G_{\chi\chi} = \varphi(q)\bar\rho_q/q$, $c_{\chi\chi}G_{\chi\chi} = 1$, and
$$\lim_R \|M^\chi_R f\|^2_\nu = \|f\|_2^2,\qquad \lim_R\langle M^\chi_R f, V_a M^\chi_R g\rangle_\nu = a^{-1/2}\overline{\chi(a)}\langle f, U_{\log a} g\rangle_2 , \tag{3.2}$$
the state entering only through the single constant $\bar\rho_q$ in the normalization. The right side of (3.2) vanishes when $\gcd(a,q)>1$.

*Proof.* Insert the Gram matrix. In the Toeplitz term put $m = an+k$ (for the second limit; $a=1$ for the first). The coefficient sum is a Riemann sum in $\log(n/N)$ against the $q$-periodic sequence $\overline{\chi(an+k)}\chi'(n)$, and a Riemann sum of a smooth profile against a periodic sequence converges to the integral times the mean of the sequence over a period: $\frac1q\sum_{r\bmod q}\overline{\chi(ar+k)}\chi'(r)$. The Hankel term has $n+am\asymp N$ and vanishes by the rapid decay of $t_k$, as in the proof of Theorem 4.1; the truncation in $k$ is uniform in $R$ for the same reason. Summing over $k$ with weights $t_k$ and using $\chi(m) = \tau(\bar\chi)^{-1}\sum_j \overline{\chi(j)}e^{2\pi i jm/q}$ twice gives
$$\sum_k t_k\,\frac1q\sum_{r}\overline{\chi(ar+k)}\chi'(r) = \frac{1}{q}\,\frac{\overline{\tau(\bar\chi')}}{\overline{\tau(\bar\chi)}}\,\overline{\chi'(a)}\sum_{j}\chi(j)\overline{\chi'(j)}\,\rho(2\pi j/q),$$
because $\sum_k t_k e^{2\pi i jk/q} = \rho(2\pi j/q)$ and $\sum_r \chi'(r)e^{-2\pi i jar/q} = \overline{\chi'(ja)}\,\overline{\tau(\bar\chi')}$ by (1.1). Since $|\tau|^2 = q$, $\overline{\tau(\bar\chi')}/\overline{\tau(\bar\chi)} = \tau(\bar\chi)/\tau(\bar\chi')$. The remaining factor $a^{-1/2}\langle f,U_{\log a}g\rangle$ comes from $(an)^{-1/2}n^{-1/2}$ and $f(\log(an/N)) = (U_{-\log a}f)(\log(n/N))$ exactly as in Theorem 4.1. Multiply by the normalizations. $\square$

**Corollary 3.4 (norm accounting).** In the Haar limit, $\|V_a S_R f\|^2\to\|f\|^2 = a^{-1}\|f\|^2 + (1-a^{-1})\|f\|^2$, and the second summand is the norm carried by the phase channels $\beta = 2\pi j/a$, $j\ne0$, i.e. (after the Gauss-sum change of basis over the divisors $d\mid a$) by the packets of the nontrivial characters of conductor dividing $a$. The recentering defect (7.9) is this norm. It is not lost; it is the norm the nontrivial $L(s,\chi)$ require.

**Corollary 3.5 (Galois breaking by the state).** $G_{\chi\chi'}$ for $\chi\ne\chi'$ vanishes for every marginal if $\chi$ and $\chi'$ have opposite parity. If $\rho$ is constant on the primitive $q$-th roots of unity, all off-diagonal $G_{\chi\chi'}$ vanish; conversely, for prime $q\ge5$, if $G_{\chi\chi'}=0$ for all pairs of distinct nontrivial characters then $\rho$ is constant on the units. In the interacting state, same-parity sectors mix.

*Proof.* $\rho$ is even, so $\rho(2\pi j/q) = \rho(2\pi(q-j)/q)$, while $\chi\overline{\chi'}(q-j) = \chi\overline{\chi'}(-1)\chi\overline{\chi'}(j)$; for opposite parity the terms $j$ and $q-j$ cancel. If $\rho$ is constant on the units the sum is the orthogonality relation for characters. Conversely, for prime $q$ every nontrivial character is primitive and every nontrivial $\psi$ is a product $\chi\overline{\chi'}$ of two distinct nontrivial characters once there are at least three of them ($q\ge5$); so $G_{\chi\chi'} = 0$ for all such pairs says that $j\mapsto\rho(2\pi j/q)$ has vanishing Fourier coefficient on $(\mathbb{Z}/q)^*$ at every nontrivial character, hence is constant. $\square$

The subgroup of $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^*$ that the SU(2) class-function marginal respects is exactly $\{\pm1\}$ (complex conjugation); a marginal that is not constant on primitive roots — every interacting marginal, and every marginal with a nonzero $t_k$, $k\ne0$ — breaks the rest.

## 4. The archimedean insertion on character sectors **[proof + floating]**

Take the Haar picture, $\phi_n = \chi_n$, $\mathcal{W}\chi_n = \sqrt{2/\pi}\sin n\theta$, and the insertion $C = \log D + B^*M_{\log(\theta/2\pi)}B$ of (5.12), whose identity-packet limit is Theorem 5.2.

**Proposition 4.1.** For a primitive character $\chi$ mod $q>1$,
$$\frac{\langle M^\chi_R f, C\,M^\chi_R f\rangle}{\|M^\chi_R f\|^2} - R \longrightarrow \frac{\langle f, xf\rangle}{\|f\|^2} + \frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{j\in(\mathbb{Z}/q)^*}\log\frac{\min(j,\,q-j)}{q} . \tag{4.1}$$
*Proof.* By Lemma 3.2 the packet is a combination of the phase packets at $\beta_j = 2\pi j/q$ with equal weights $1/\varphi(q)$ after normalization, asymptotically orthogonal. Each phase packet obeys (5.20): electric part $R + \langle f,xf\rangle/\|f\|^2$, angle part $\log(b_j/2\pi)$ with the folded angle $b_j = 2\pi\min(j,q-j)/q$, since the cosine packet $Bu$ localizes at $\theta = b_j$ where $\log(\theta/2\pi)$ is slowly varying. Cross terms between distinct phases vanish: the electric part is diagonal in $n$ and the cross sum is an oscillatory Riemann sum; the angle part pairs two localized profiles, which are either localized at different points or (for $j$ and $q-j$) at the same point but asymptotically orthogonal, so the slowly varying weight cannot create a limit. $\square$

The finite part contains $-\log q$, the conductor of $L(s,\chi)$, together with the spurious constant $\varphi(q)^{-1}\sum_j\log\min(j,q-j)$ and the divergent $R$. The mechanism is exact: for the identity packet, $\theta = 2\pi y/N$ turns $\log(\theta/2\pi)$ into $\log y - R$ and the $-R$ cancels the electric $+R$; a character packet lives at $\theta \approx 2\pi j/q$, where $\log(\theta/2\pi)\approx\log(j/q)$ contains no $-R$. The angle observable measures distance from the identity, not from the phase center; the insertion that would serve conductor $q$ must measure the distance to the nearest $q$-th root of unity, a conductor-dependent object — which is what the finite-place conductor operators are in the adelic picture [Bu2].

**[floating] Part B** confirms (4.1): for $\chi_{-3}$ the left side at $N = 256, 1024, 4096$ is $-0.986902, -0.986895, -0.986895$ against the prediction $-0.986895$ ($\langle f,xf\rangle/\|f\|^2 = 0.111717$, constant $-\log 3$); for $\chi_5$, $-1.151160, -1.151148, -1.151147$ against $-1.151147$ (constant $\tfrac12\log\tfrac{2}{25} = -1.262864$, against $-\log 5 = -1.609438$). Errors at $N = 4096$: $2.8\cdot10^{-8}$ and $4.9\cdot10^{-8}$. The same code applied to the identity packet reproduces Theorem 5.2 to $2\cdot10^{-16}$.

**Parity.** The archimedean factor of $L(s,\chi)$ is $\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s+\kappa) = \pi^{-(s+\kappa)/2}\Gamma((s+\kappa)/2)$, whose Weil multiplier is $\mathrm{Re}\,\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac\kappa2+\tfrac{i\tau}{2})$. For $\kappa=0$ this is the manuscript's cosine channel (with $B$), for $\kappa = 1$ its sine channel (without $B$), which the manuscript records after Theorem 5.2 as giving $\mathrm{Re}\,\psi(\tfrac34+\tfrac{i\tau}{2})$. The parity conversion is not an arbitrary normalization: it selects the parity of the character. Correspondingly, in the theta-function form of the archimedean term, even characters twist $e^{-\pi n^2 y}$ and odd characters twist $n\,e^{-\pi n^2y}$ [known: Hecke]. This is a match of structures, not a derivation of the character factor from the class sector: on the class sector Proposition 4.1 shows the insertion does not produce it.

## 5. The family form on the module **[known + floating]**

On $\mathcal{K} = L^2(\mathbb{R})\otimes\ell^2(\{\text{primitive Dirichlet characters}\})$, block-diagonal under $W_u$, define for each primitive $\chi$ mod $q$ the sesquilinear form
$$Q_\chi(f,g) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}} m_\chi(\tau)\,\overline{\hat f(\tau)}\hat g(\tau)\,d\tau
+ \delta_{q,1}P(f,g) - \sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\Big[\chi(n)\langle f,U_{\log n}g\rangle + \overline{\chi(n)}\langle f, U_{-\log n}g\rangle\Big], \tag{5.1}$$
$$m_\chi(\tau) = \mathrm{Re}\,\psi\!\Big(\frac14+\frac{\kappa(\chi)}{2}+\frac{i\tau}{2}\Big) + \log\frac{q}{\pi},$$
with $P$ the pole term (1.7), present only for $\chi = 1$. Weil's explicit formula [W, IK Thm 5.12] gives $Q_\chi(f,g) = \sum_{\varrho}\overline{F_f(\varrho)}\cdots$ over the nontrivial zeros of $L(s,\chi)$, and under GRH for $L(s,\chi)$, $Q_\chi[f] = \sum_\gamma m_\gamma|\hat f(\gamma)|^2\ge0$ for all $f\in C_c^\infty$ (no pole-neutrality is needed for $q>1$). Weil's theorem: GRH for all $L(s,\chi)$ is equivalent to $\mathcal{Q} = \bigoplus_\chi Q_\chi \ge 0$ [W]. The prime terms of (5.1) are the limits (3.2) with $V_a + V_a^{*}$ inserted, and the archimedean term is assigned as $m_\chi(P_x)\otimes 1$ on the $\chi$-block; Section 4 shows it is not produced by the class-sector insertion. Thus (5.1) on the module is the exact analogue, for the family, of the signed identity (5.19) for $\zeta$, and it is a rearrangement of the explicit formula, not a new positivity statement.

## 6. Assessment

**Established.** Propositions 2.1, 2.3, Theorem 3.3 and Corollaries 3.4–3.5, Proposition 4.1: all elementary given the manuscript's Theorem 4.1 and Section 5. The structural identifications (Bost–Connes relations, integral adeles, Galois symmetry, character sectors, parity of the insertion) are exact.

**Changed diagnosis.** (i) The branches created by winding are the arithmetic of the finite ideles; a realization that keeps only the identity channel is choosing $\zeta$ out of the family and paying $(1-a^{-1})\|f\|^2$ per winding for it. The right target for this machinery is Weil positivity for all Dirichlet $L$-functions at once, where the profinite fiber that Theorem 6.1 forbids in the class sector is the natural coordinate. (ii) The interacting Yang–Mills state is an obstruction in a way the $\zeta$-only analysis could not see: it deforms the Bost–Connes relations and mixes the $L$-function sectors of equal parity. Any realization on the class sector would have to undo that mixing. (iii) The parity conversion $B$ is the character parity.

**Not established.** Positivity of anything. The archimedean operators on the module are assigned.
