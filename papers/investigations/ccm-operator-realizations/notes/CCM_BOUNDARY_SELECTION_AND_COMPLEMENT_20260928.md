## 1. Outcome

The [preceding continuation](CCM_GROUND_SELECTION_TARGET_20260928.md) reduced the task to selecting the actual ground direction from a large arithmetic near-null family. This round obtains a genuine restricted selection theorem: **within the space generated by the Xi kernel and its second derivative, the lowest trial vector approaches the Xi kernel, with an explicit correction and error.** The proof uses the arithmetic tail identity and a relative boundary-form estimate, rather than fitting finite matrices.

The complementary-space analysis also makes the limitation precise. Adding the fourth derivative reduces the optimized trial energy by another asymptotic factor. At each fixed support, all even derivatives together are a form core, but taking support to infinity at fixed rank always produces near-zero energies, whether RH is true or false. These two limits cannot be interchanged without a new uniform theorem.

A bounded experiment shows why the complement matters: a three-profile space captures the finite ground geometrically to a few parts in a million at one cutoff, yet its uncorrected Ritz vector is much less accurate. The full finite complement correction cancels many orders of the trial block. Its successful numerical elimination is a diagnostic of what needs estimating, not an estimate of the infinite complement.

An additional exact reformulation turns the remaining even positivity question into a weighted Poincare inequality for positive prime and gamma jump energies. Its sharp constant is explicit, and infinitely many threshold eigenfunctions are known unconditionally. Excluding spectrum below that threshold remains equivalent to RH.

The [boundary derivation](CCM_DERIVATIVE_BOUNDARY_SELECTION_20260928.md), [complement and weighted-gap derivation](CCM_COMPLEMENT_DENSITY_AND_WEIGHTED_GAP_20260928.md), [numerical diagnostic](../reviews/CCM_DERIVATIVE_CLUSTER_DIAGNOSTIC_20260928.md), and [review](../reviews/CCM_BOUNDARY_SELECTION_REVIEW_20260928.md) give the details. Sonin-residual work remains deferred.

## 2. A scalar normalization correction

Keep the literal kernel used by the numerical programs:
\[
k(x)=e^{x/2}\sum_{n\ge1}h(ne^x),\qquad
h(u)=\frac\pi2u^2(2\pi u^2-3)e^{-\pi u^2}.
\]
For \(\widehat k(z)=\int k(x)e^{-izx}dx\), its precise normalization is
\[
\boxed{\widehat k(z)=\Xi(z)/4.}
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed,
\[
\int_0^\infty h(u)u^{s-1}du
=\frac{s(s-1)}8\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2).
\]
Multiplication by \(\zeta(s)\), initially where the sum and integral converge absolutely, and analytic continuation give (1) with \(s=1/2-iz\). Evenness of Xi removes the sign of z. In particular,
\[
\int k=\Xi(0)/4,\qquad
M:=\int k(x)\cosh(x/2)dx=1/8.
\tag{2}
\]

Two round-6 supporting notes had stated \(\widehat k=\Xi\) alongside this literal h. Those scalar statements are corrected. The annihilator property, normalized transforms, overlaps, signed moments, residual orders, and RH overlap criterion do not change. No numerical kernel or recorded observation was rescaled. The main round-6 synthesis already allowed a nonzero scalar multiple.

## 3. An explicit two-profile selection theorem

Put \(I_b=[-b,b]\), \(L=2b\),
\[
A=\pi e^{2b},\quad K_b=k(b),\quad h_b=K_b^2\log A/A,
\]
and use the original unshifted Weil form Q. Consider the continuum trial space
\[
S_{2,b}=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1_{I_b}k,\mathbf1_{I_b}k''\}.
\]
For all sufficiently large b its form is positive definite, unconditionally. Its lowest generalized Rayleigh vector is, up to normalization,
\[
f_b=\mathbf1_{I_b}(k+c_bk''),\qquad
c_b=-\frac1{4A^2}-\frac9{8A^3}
+O\!\left(\frac1{A^3\log A}\right).
\tag{3}
\]
The normalized vector differs from \(k/\|k\|_2\), after zero extension, by \(O(A^{-2})\). Its trial eigenvalue satisfies
\[
\rho_2(b)=\frac{K_b^2\log A}{2A^3\|k\|_2^2}
\left(1+O(1/\log A)\right).
\tag{4}
\]
The uncorrected restricted kernel has Rayleigh quotient
\[
\frac{K_b^2\log A}{2A\|k\|_2^2}
\left(1+O(1/\log A)\right).
\]
Thus a small change of the interior profile removes a factor asymptotic to \(A^2\) from its already small energy.

The decisive estimate retains cancellation before comparing forms. Because k and its derivatives are annihilators, the trial form equals the form on their omitted tails. Use the two tail profiles
\[
r_0=\mathbf1_{|x|>b}k,\qquad
r_1=\mathbf1_{|x|>b}\left(\frac{k''}{4A}-Ak\right).
\]
At \(x=b+y/A\), their ratios to K_b converge to
\[
q_0(y)=e^{-2y},\qquad q_1(y)=(4y-11/2)e^{-2y}.
\]
In this basis the entire form matrix has the relative expansion
\[
\frac1{h_b}\bigl(Q(r_i,r_j)\bigr)_{i,j=0,1}
=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&-9/4\\-9/4&85/8\end{pmatrix}
+O(1/\log A).
\tag{5}
\]
The leading matrix has determinant 1/4. The gamma multiplier supplies the leading logarithmic term; prime and pole contributions are lower order on this prescribed boundary space. They are not negligible on arbitrary test functions. Equation (5) controls the small eigenvalue after cancellation; unrelated entrywise accuracy in the original nearly rank-one matrix would not suffice.

This proves selection **inside S_{2,b}**. It does not compare that vector with the full ground eigenspace. The restricted trial transform also has no automatic real-zero theorem of the kind supplied by the actual finite CCM ground construction.

## 4. Why two or three profiles cannot settle the problem

Add \(\mathbf1_{I_b}k''''\) to obtain S_{3,b}. The explicit profile
\[
F_A=(1+4/A)k-\frac{k''}{2A^2}+\frac{k''''}{16A^4}
\tag{6}
\]
tends to k in L2 and cancels the first two boundary terms. The boundary estimate proves eventual positivity on S_{3,b} and
\[
0<\rho_3(b)\le C h_b/A^4,
\qquad \rho_3(b)/\rho_2(b)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]
This is an optimized-energy statement, not an assertion that the complement correction evaluated on the old Ritz vector equals its old energy. The energy is increasingly sensitive to small changes of direction.

There is a stronger general obstruction. For a finite-dimensional space S in the domain of a self-adjoint operator W, let D be the form compression to \(S^\perp\), and put \(r=\|W|_S\|\). Then
\[
\|W-(0_S\oplus D)\|\le2r,
\quad
\left|\inf\sigma(W)-\min(0,\inf\sigma(D))\right|\le2r.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently, if RH were false, removing any family with uniformly vanishing all-input residual would leave a negative direction bounded away from zero in its complement. This remains true for growing rank when the stated residual bound really is uniform over the whole trial space. Small residuals of individual columns do not establish that bound.

For every fixed rank, the complement also contains arbitrarily many eigenvalues near zero as support grows. Under RH these are its bottom levels. A positive complement gap independent of support is therefore unavailable. A gap relative to an even smaller spectral scale remains possible, but no such adequate estimate is proved here.

## 5. The derivative basis exposes the order of limits

Define
\[
S_{m,b}=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1_{I_b}k^{(2j)}:0\le j<m\},
\qquad
\rho_m(b)=\min_{f\in S_{m,b}\setminus\{0\}}Q(f,f)/\|f\|_2^2.
\]
At each fixed b, the union of these spaces is dense in the even Weil **form domain**. This is stronger than L2 density and therefore supports Rayleigh convergence:
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\rho_m(b)=\varepsilon_b^+.
\tag{9}
\]
The proof uses analytic translation of k and Fourier uniqueness for compact-support distributions; it does not assume RH. Hard restrictions are essential to this density statement. A fixed smooth cutoff that vanishes on an outer strip would miss functions supported in that strip.

For each fixed m, on the other hand,
\[
\lim_{b\to\infty}\rho_m(b)=0
\tag{10}
\]
unconditionally. In fact the boundary argument proves eventual positive definiteness at **every fixed rank**. After choosing a basis by its boundary derivatives, the leading matrix is
\[
(M_m)_{rs}=\frac{2(r+s)!}{4^{r+s+1}},\qquad 0\le r,s<m,
\]
the positive Gram matrix of polynomials for the weight \(e^{-4y}dy\). Its error constants and the required support threshold depend on m; no uniform-rank estimate is supplied. Thus
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{b\to\infty}\rho_m(b)=0,
\quad
\lim_{b\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\rho_m(b)
=\lim_{b\to\infty}\varepsilon_b^+.
\tag{11}
\]
The second quantity is zero under RH and strictly negative, possibly minus infinity, if RH fails. In that counterfactual case, the least derivative rank detecting a negative direction must tend to infinity with support. This is the exact limit-order trap behind a sequence of increasingly elaborate fixed-rank calculations. A growing-rank theorem needs quantitative control of conditioning, residuals, and local test approximation. Merely choosing some sequence with both parameters tending to infinity does not justify interchanging the limits.

## 6. A diagnostic of the actual finite complement

At X=13 and Fourier cutoffs N=32,64, the independently specified projected spaces generated by k,k'',k'''' were compared with the actual even ground. Endpoint terms were retained when computing derivative coefficients. No eigenvector or zero list was used to construct the trial spaces.

| N, dimension | Distance from uncorrected Ritz vector to ground | Best distance attainable in trial space | Distance after full finite harmonic lift |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 32, 2 | 0.0126688 | 2.27e-4 | 7.25e-17 |
| 32, 3 | 0.0105590 | 3.49e-6 | 4.98e-24 |
| 64, 2 | 0.0201339 | 5.44e-4 | 1.76e-18 |
| 64, 3 | 0.0179968 | 1.52e-5 | 2.44e-26 |

“Best distance” uses the numerically computed finite ground only as a reference projection, not as a candidate construction. The harmonic lift leaves the trial space and uses its entire finite complement.

For the orthogonal block decomposition
\[
W_N=\begin{pmatrix}H&C^*\\ C&D\end{pmatrix},\qquad D>0,
\]
the calculation uses
\[
Z=D^{-1}C,\quad F_0=H-C^*D^{-1}C,\quad G_0=I+Z^*Z,
\]
and solves the generalized pencil \(F_0p=\theta G_0p\). The lifted vector is \((p,-Zp)\). Including G_0 is required for its Rayleigh quotient.

The correction cancels approximately 18–28 decimal orders of the trial block's Frobenius norm in these four cases. The minimum of D also decreases substantially when N doubles. These are structured cancellations, not small corrections that can be discarded. The 130- and 160-digit records agree in all retained observable strings at 45-digit serialization, excluding precision and roundoff-check fields. They are floating diagnostics, not interval certificates. No cutoff convergence follows from the two values of N.

For a positive finite W_N with \(D\ge\delta I>0\), \(\lambda=\min\sigma(W_N)<\delta\), and the lowest harmonic Ritz value \(\theta\), the exact algebra gives
\[
\frac{\theta\delta}{\delta+\theta}\le\lambda\le\theta.
\tag{12}
\]
The proof and hypotheses are in the diagnostic. This explains the finite energy accuracy, but supplies no ground-angle estimate by itself and does not establish positivity of D or F_0 in an infinite setting. Computing the complete finite inverse is not an independent analytic comparison for it.

## 7. An exact alternative: a sharp weighted jump inequality

The positive profile k permits a useful ground-state transform, with a remaining variance term made explicit. Set
\[
d\mu=k(x)\cosh(x/2)dx,\quad M=1/8,\quad
\bar u=M^{-1}\int u\,d\mu,
\quad H(s)=\frac{e^{-s/2}}{1-e^{-2s}}.
\]
Define the nonnegative energies
\[
\mathcal E_\Gamma(u)=\frac12\iint H(|x-y|)k(x)k(y)
|u(x)-u(y)|^2dxdy,
\]
\[
\mathcal E_p(u)=\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
\int k(x)k(x+\log n)|u(x+\log n)-u(x)|^2dx.
\]
For even compact smooth u, the annihilator identity yields exactly
\[
\boxed{Q(ku,ku)=\mathcal E_\Gamma(u)+\mathcal E_p(u)
-\frac14\int|u-\bar u|^2d\mu.}
\tag{13}
\]
Since k is positive, this gives
\[
\mathrm{RH}\iff
\mathcal E_\Gamma(u)+\mathcal E_p(u)
\ge\frac14\int|u-\bar u|^2d\mu
\quad\text{for all even compact smooth }u.
\tag{14}
\]
The poles have become a variance subtraction, so positivity of the jump terms does not prove (14).

The functions
\[
u_j=k^{(2j)}/k-4^{-j},\qquad j\ge1,
\]
have zero \(\mu\)-mean and are independent eigenfunctions of the closed positive jump operator at eigenvalue 1/4. This infinite threshold eigenspace exists unconditionally. The unresolved question is whether the mean-zero even sector has spectrum below 1/4. The reformulation isolates a concrete functional inequality; it does not establish its sharp lower bound.

There is a precise truncation trap here too: retain the entire gamma jump form but only prime powers \(n\le P\), for any fixed finite P. The resulting whole-line form has **even mean-zero gap zero**. Narrow normalized pairs of bumps near \(\pm R\), of width \((\pi e^{2R})^{-1}\), have variance tending to one and energy tending to zero. Thus a fixed finite collection of positive prime terms cannot establish (14), even though the full sum converges on every fixed smooth test. The proof supplies explicit escaping-tail estimates.

This transformed truncation is not the original finite-window Weil form. The positive-k transform includes arbitrarily long jumps from the support of u to its exterior; their diagonal contributions enter the exact identity. Deleting those jumps changes the form. Any use of (14) must retain or rigorously control that infinite arithmetic contribution.

## 8. What should follow from this round

The two-profile theorem is evidence that boundary structure can select the Xi direction within a prescribed arithmetic space. The full operator still needs a mechanism controlling the directions that space omits. The complement obstruction and the two iterated limits prevent turning fixed-rank selection into an RH argument by extrapolation.

A useful next step must address one of two specific estimates:

1. A **joint rank/support comparison**, controlling the signed complement correction and the orientation of the low space with constants valid at the required spectral scale. The derivative form-core theorem specifies what completeness means; the boundary calculation supplies explicit initial terms. An adequate truncation schedule and its remainder are still missing.
2. A **global lower bound for the weighted jump form**, aimed at the sharp threshold in (14), including functions whose mass escapes far into the weighted tails. This uses positive terms with explicit arithmetic translations, while retaining the exact pole subtraction.

Neither target is solved by this continuation. Further fixed-support exact Schur inversions would mostly repeat the original finite eigenproblem unless accompanied by a signed analytic remainder estimate. The true-ground overlap lower bound from round 6, the general finite CCM hypotheses, and the Xi determinant limit remain open.
