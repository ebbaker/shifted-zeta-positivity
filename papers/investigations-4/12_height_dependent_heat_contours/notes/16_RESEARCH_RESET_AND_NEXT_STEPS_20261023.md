## A precise design for the first diagnostic

Fix a height chart and its spatial wall before examining its zeros. A candidate
clock is t=L^(-3), L=log(x/(4π)), with horizontal and vertical dimensions of
order 1/L. Natural unfolding ν∼L/(4π) then gives 4tν² of order 1/L, tending
to zero. This scale is prescribed from height, rather than selected after
measuring unknown endpoint gaps. Keep a fixed Riemann–Siegel cutoff or pay
its finite chart changes. The cutoff counts source terms, not the zeros in
the block; the complete spatial population must be counted separately.

Record, in order:

1. The normalized whole-wall floor of the corrected reference and its complete
   field error, including both vertical sides and reflected-carrier conversion.
2. The complete population and coefficient errors from that wall, without
   assuming root disks or excluding troublesome labels.
3. The forest denominator margin and the combined residual K=G−q_*D,
   bounded before separating its correlated terms.
4. The reference q itself and the final certified q bound. Small source error
   does not imply small reference q.
5. The same-information direct result, all shared acquisition costs, and
   any additional transport costs.

This identifies whether the obstruction is source approximation, a small
reference wall value, growing degree, coefficient conditioning, or the actual
reference spectrum. Once one component dominates, improving an already tiny
tail is not useful. A fixed-degree calculation also does not settle the cost
of growing blocks; any proposed family must track that dependence explicitly.

## Where a genuinely new global argument would have to enter

Under the stated RH-dependent complete adjacency law, if r_h endpoint labels
are omitted from a target population of N_h labels, the proved consumer is

\[
\frac{M_h}{N_h}\le\frac{r_h}{N_h}+\frac{4q_h(t_h)}{N_h}.
\]

The missing inputs are r_h=o(N_h) and an independent complete-source estimate
q_h(t_h)=o(N_h), with all acquisition errors included. The equivalent
microscopic short-gap statement is a useful description of the target,
not a source of it. For every-zero simplicity, complete covering blocks must
instead satisfy q<1/2; an average estimate may lose sparse doubles.

A possible research direction is an averaged one-sided joint small-value
estimate for the complete normalized field and its derivative on a
predeclared mesh. It is worth pursuing only if a proved interpolation and
root-counting consumer turns that estimate into the required clustering
bound while paying exceptional sets and uncovered labels. At present no
such arithmetic estimate has been established here. Generic real-root heat
laws, a mean density, positive source terms, or a random model do not supply it.

Even a good wall floor is not enough: it can surround an interior multiple
zero. The two separate tasks are trustworthy acquisition and an arithmetic
upper bound on the acquired spectrum. This distinction should govern the
next paper-level claim.

## What to set aside for now

The scalar late-time backward implementation with independent algebraic error
allowances has an unfavorable amplification budget. The coefficient theorem
avoids that particular generic estimate, but its integrated complete-source
moments and correlated residual still need to be bounded. Another abstract
inverse lemma without an actual source budget is lower value than acquiring
the now explicit corrected reference.

For exact heat fields, the homogeneous coefficient moments equal endpoint
wall-log integrals. Bending the path cannot change this algebraic information.
A curve can help only through a demonstrably better source or conditioning
budget, and the currently imported approximation theorem is for real time.
Suspend geometric optimization until such a source estimate is proposed.

The completed 512-leaf cap pilot has direct and deformed widths approximately
0.000283522 and 0.000283934, respectively. Spatial range integration dominates;
the source errors are already below 6×10^(-27). Both lower endpoints are
negative. More source precision would not repair this bottleneck. A higher-order
spatial calculation is a legitimate bounded numerical question, but should
receive less priority than the microscopic reference and arithmetic estimate.
