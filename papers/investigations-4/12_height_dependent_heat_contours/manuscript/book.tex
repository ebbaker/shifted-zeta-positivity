\begin{abstract}
Assuming the Riemann hypothesis, with multiplicities allowed, we prove
a relative short-time expansion for the complete theta heat field on
strips at a fixed positive distance from the real axis. On the scale
$s\le c/(\log Y)^2$, extracting the full complex Gaussian exponential
gives an explicit first correction and a relative remainder
$O((\log Y)^{-2})$. The proof controls the entire Gaussian tail relative
to the small initial field. It acquires nonvanishing and justifies the
actual contours preserving $H\,dz$, with displacement and tangent change
$O((\log Y)^{-1})$, including their finite spatial containment and upward
motion as heat time runs backward.

The overarching goal is to approach the implication from the Riemann
hypothesis to simple zeros. We connect the acquired outer wall to an
exact collision balance and sharp multiplicity bounds, retaining the
earlier nonreal population and one-sided inward flux. An explicit
positive-source field with the same coarse zero count and no simple
zeros satisfies the contour estimates. The remaining task is therefore
a multiplicity-sensitive arithmetic estimate inside the wall. The paper
proves the contour theorem and identifies this missing step; it proves
no new simplicity proportion.
\end{abstract}

\section{The main comparison and the simplicity question}
\label{sec:main}

The Riemann hypothesis places every nontrivial zero on the critical line.
It does not by itself state that those zeros are simple. Our objective is
to determine what additional control can be proved from the actual theta
source, assuming RH but retaining every possible endpoint multiplicity.
A useful intermediate theorem must acquire an analytic estimate before
it is used in a multiplicity argument.

Use the normalization
\begin{align}
 H(t,z)&=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\dd u,
 &H_t&=-H_{zz},\label{eq:theta}\\
 \Phi(u)&=\sum_{n\ge1}
 (2\pi^2n^4e^{9u}-3\pi n^2e^{5u})e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}},
 &F(z):=H(0,z)&=\tfrac18\xi(\tfrac12+iz/2).
 \label{eq:canonical}
\end{align}
Here $\xi(w)=\tfrac12w(w-1)\pi^{-w/2}\Gamma(w/2)\zeta(w)$.
These conventions agree with \cite{polymath,DLMF}. The complete even
source is positive and superexponentially decaying. Its decay justifies
all fixed-strip Gaussian integrals below. The field is even, is of real
type for real $t$, and is jointly entire in $t$ and $z$.

Under RH, $F$ has only real zeros. We do not assume they are simple.
Set $p=F'/F$, which is holomorphic in the upper half-plane. Throughout,
$s\ge0$ denotes backward duration: the earlier field is $H(-s,z)$.
The spatial offset $\Im z$ is a different variable.

\begin{theorem}[Complete relative Gaussian comparison]
\label{thm:gaussian}
Assume RH. Fix $0<b_-<b_+$ and $c>0$. For $L=\log Y$, uniformly on
\[
 Y\le\Re z\le2Y,\qquad b_-\le\Im z\le b_+,\qquad
 0\le s\le cL^{-2},
\]
one has, as $Y\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\frac{H(-s,z)}{F(z)e^{sp(z)^2}}
 =1+s p'(z)\bigl(1+2s p(z)^2\bigr)+O(L^{-2}).}
 \label{eq:main-comparison}
\end{equation}
The same estimate holds with any fixed horizontal end extensions and
on an enlarged fixed positive-offset strip. On a smaller strip with a
fixed margin, every fixed-order spatial derivative of the remainder is
$O_k(L^{-2})$. Constants and the sufficiently-large-height threshold may
depend on $b_-,b_+,c$, the margins and the derivative order. At $s=0$
the formula is exact.

In particular the field is nonzero throughout this region for large
$Y$, and, for some strip constant $C$,
\begin{equation}
 |H(-s,z)|\ge\tfrac12e^{-C^2c}|F(z)|.
 \label{eq:acquired-floor}
\end{equation}
Writing $p_s(z)=H_z(-s,z)/H(-s,z)$, one also has
\begin{align}
 p_s(z)&=p(z)+2s p(z)p'(z)+O(L^{-1}),\label{eq:velocity-expansion}\\
 \partial_zp_s(z)&=p'(z)+2s\bigl((p'(z))^2+p(z)p''(z)\bigr)
                      +O(L^{-1})=p'(z)+O(1).
 \label{eq:strain-expansion}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

This theorem has a useful geometric consequence. An initially flat
arc $z_a=a+ib$, with $b$ fixed and positive and with fixed horizontal
end extensions, has an actual continuation satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \partial_sZ(s,a)=-p_s(Z(s,a)),\qquad Z(0,a)=z_a.
 \label{eq:material-ode-intro}
\end{equation}
It remains inside a prescribed finite buffer, is a graph over an
interval covering $[Y,2Y]$, and obeys
\begin{equation}
 Z(s,a)=z_a-sp(z_a)+O(L^{-2}),\qquad
 \partial_a Z(s,a)=1-sp'(z_a)+O(L^{-2}).
 \label{eq:material-intro}
\end{equation}
The full statement and proof are given in Section~\ref{sec:scf-contours}.
This motion preserves $H(-s,Z)\partial_aZ=F(z_a)$; it is the literal
$H\,dz$ conservation law.

The point is to obtain the moving contour from initial RH and complete
field information, rather than to assume a zero-free moving tube.
The raw xi field can be exponentially small at large height.
Consequently an absolutely small omitted Gaussian tail would not be
enough. The proof integrates the initial logarithmic derivative to
control the entire tail \emph{after division by $F$}, and retains the
phase of $e^{sp^2}$ until its relative error has been paid.

The theorem is a conditional intermediate result, not a criterion
asserted to be equivalent to RH or to simplicity. Its offsets stay
bounded away from zero; its time is real; $c$ is fixed before $Y$ tends
to infinity. No numerical height threshold is supplied. Sections
\ref{sec:limits} and \ref{sec:resource} explain respectively why the
estimates admit multiple zeros and how an additional bound on the
complete nonreal resource would restrict them.

\section{Proof of the Gaussian comparison}
\label{scf-proof}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian} has two separate analytic tasks.
The local Gaussian calculation must retain its complex leading exponential,
and the omitted horizontal tail must be small relative to the complete
initial field.  We first establish the bounds that accomplish both tasks.
Throughout this section RH is assumed, with multiplicities retained.
Constants may depend on fixed positive-offset strips, fixed margins and
$c$, but not on $Y$, $z$ or $0\le s\le c/(\log Y)^2$.

\subsection{The full product and a logarithmic anchor}

Let $\mathcal Z$ be the signed multiset of zeros of $F$, and let $N_F(X)$
count its positive members up to $X$, with multiplicity.  The classical
order-one product, paired using evenness, gives
\begin{equation}\label{scf-product}
 F(z)=F(0)\prod_{\lambda>0}\left(1-\frac{z^2}{\lambda^2}\right),
 \qquad
 p(z)=\sum_{\lambda>0}
 \left(\frac1{z-\lambda}+\frac1{z+\lambda}\right).
\end{equation}
Here $\lambda$ is twice a positive zeta ordinate.  Pairing specifies the
convergence; evenness removes the linear exponential allowed by the
general order-one factorization.  For every integer $k\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{scf-derivative-product}
 p^{(k)}(z)=(-1)^k k!\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal Z}
                     (z-\lambda)^{-k-1},
\end{equation}
with absolute locally uniform convergence away from the real axis.
The zero-counting formula in the present normalization is
\begin{equation}\label{scf-count}
 N_F(X)=\frac{X}{4\pi}\log\frac{X}{4\pi}-\frac{X}{4\pi}
              +O(\log(2+X)).
\end{equation}
These standard inputs include multiplicities; see \cite{Elkies}.
The entire completion used here is $(w^2-w)/2$ times the meromorphic
completion denoted by $\xi$ in that reference.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-strip logarithmic bounds]\label{scf-strip-bounds}
For $0<\alpha<\beta<\infty$ and every fixed nonnegative integer $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{scf-logbound}
 |p^{(k)}(x+iy)|\le C_{k,\alpha,\beta}\log(2+|x|),
 \qquad x\in\R,\quad \alpha\le y\le\beta.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Formula~\eqref{scf-count} bounds the number of zeros in each unit real
interval near $v$ by $O(\log(2+|v|))$.  For $k\ge1$, grouping the absolute
sum in \eqref{scf-derivative-product} into unit intervals about $x$ gives
\[
 \sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal Z}|x+iy-\lambda|^{-k-1}
 \le C_k\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{\log(2+|x|+n)}{(1+n)^{k+1}}
 \le C_k\log(2+|x|).
\]
The fixed lower bound on $y$ pays the central intervals.  This argument
uses the coarse count with multiplicity and requires no simplicity bound.

For $k=0$ we use a logarithmic anchor instead of an absolute unpaired
reciprocal sum.  Choose $B>\max\{\beta,1\}$ with a fixed positive margin.
The functional equation, with $w=(1+B)/2-ix/2$, yields
\[
 p(x+iB)=-\frac i2\left\{\frac1w+\frac1{w-1}
 -\frac12\log\pi+\frac12\psi(w/2)
 +\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(w)\right\}
 =O(\log(2+|x|)).
\]
Indeed $\Re w>1$ by a fixed amount, so the Euler logarithmic derivative
converges absolutely; the digamma estimate controls the gamma factor
\cite[\S\S 5.11, 25.4]{DLMF}.  Bounded $x$ is handled by compactness
and Euler nonvanishing.  Integrating $p'$ vertically from $x+iB$ to
$x+iy$ proves \eqref{scf-logbound} for $k=0$.  RH pays this passage
through the strip; no Euler product is asserted there.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A global relative bound for the Gaussian tail}

For real $s>0$, the full theta transform gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{scf-gaussian-integral}
 H(-s,z)=\frac1{\sqrt{4\pi s}}\int_\R
                 e^{-u^2/(4s)}F(z+u)\,\dd u.
\end{equation}
To see the normalization, insert
$F(z)=\int_\R\rho(v)e^{izv}\,\dd v$: the Gaussian integral in $u$
contributes $e^{-sv^2}$.  The positive even theta density $\rho$ has
superexponential decay, justifying Fubini for every fixed $\Im z$.
This representation retains the complete source.

Since $F$ has no upper-half-plane zeros under RH, integration along the
horizontal segment gives
\[
 \frac{F(z+u)}{F(z)}
       =\exp\left(\int_0^u p(z+v)\,\dd v\right).
\]
Consequently Lemma~\ref{scf-strip-bounds} implies, for all real $u$,
\begin{equation}\label{scf-global-ratio}
 \left|\frac{F(z+u)}{F(z)}\right|
 \le\exp\{C|u|\log(2+|\Re z|+|u|)\}.
\end{equation}
This is already a relative estimate, even when $F(z)$ is exponentially
small.  It covers shifts to bounded real height and arbitrarily far
beyond the target interval.

Fix $r>0$ independent of $Y$.  If $x\in[Y-O(1),2Y+O(1)]$, then for
$v\ge r$ and sufficiently large $Y$,
\[
 Cv\log(2+|x|+v)\le\frac{v^2}{8s},
 \qquad 0<s\le cL^{-2},\qquad L=\log Y.
\]
Indeed $\log(2+|x|+v)\le C_0+L+\log(1+v)$, and division by $v$
bounds the right-hand expression inside this logarithmic comparison by
$L/r+O_r(1)$, whereas $1/(8s)$ grows quadratically in $L$.
Thus the part of \eqref{scf-gaussian-integral} with $|u|>r$, after
division by $F(z)$, is bounded in absolute value by
\begin{equation}\label{scf-tail}
 C\exp\left(-\frac{r^2}{16s}\right).
\end{equation}
Dividing further by $e^{sp(z)^2}$ costs at most
$\exp(s|p(z)|^2)\le\exp(C^2c)$.
The entire relative tail is therefore $O(L^{-M})$ for every fixed $M$,
uniformly throughout the stated real-time range.  The same Gaussian
argument controls the tails of $e^{pu}$ times each fixed polynomial in
$u$ that appears below.

\subsection{The first correction and its spatial derivatives}

For $|u|\le r$, integrate the Taylor expansion of $p$ along the real
segment.  With $p=p(z)$ and $p'=p'(z)$ this gives
\begin{equation}\label{scf-log-expansion}
 \log\frac{F(z+u)}{F(z)}=pu+\frac12p'u^2+R_3(z,u),
 \qquad |R_3(z,u)|\le CL|u|^3.
\end{equation}
The logarithm is continued from its value zero at $u=0$.
Writing $A=p'u^2/2$, and taking $r$ fixed and small, the elementary
exponential remainder estimate implies
\begin{equation}\label{scf-exp-remainder}
 |e^{A+R_3}-1-A|
 \le C\{L|u|^3+L^2|u|^4\}e^{CLu^2}.
\end{equation}

Let $E_s$ denote expectation for a centered real Gaussian of variance
$2s$.  For every fixed integer $j\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{scf-weighted-moment}
 \frac{E_s[|u|^j e^{(\Re p)u+CLu^2}]}{|e^{sp^2}|}
 \le C_{j,c}(\sqrt{s}+sL)^j.
\end{equation}
For completeness, put $d=1-4CLs\ge1/2$.  Completing the real square
changes the variance to $2s/d$, the mean to $2s\Re p/d$, and the scalar
mass to $d^{-1/2}e^{s(\Re p)^2/d}$.  Relative to $|e^{sp^2}|$, the
exponent of this mass is
\[
 s(\Im p)^2+s(\Re p)^2(d^{-1}-1)=O_c(1).
\]
The usual absolute moments of the shifted Gaussian prove
\eqref{scf-weighted-moment}.  This calculation pays the possible complex
cancellation without discarding the complex leading exponential.
Since $\sqrt{s}+sL=O_c(L^{-1})$, integrating
\eqref{scf-exp-remainder} gives a relative error $O(L^{-2})$.
The discarded original and polynomial tails are covered by
\eqref{scf-tail}.

The remaining Gaussian moments are exact, also for complex $p$:
\[
 E_s[e^{pu}]=e^{sp^2},\qquad
 E_s[u^2e^{pu}]=(2s+4s^2p^2)e^{sp^2}.
\]
Combining them with \eqref{scf-log-expansion} proves
\begin{equation}\label{scf-first-correction}
 \frac{H(-s,z)}{F(z)e^{sp(z)^2}}
 =1+sp'(z)\{1+2sp(z)^2\}+O(L^{-2}),
\end{equation}
and hence the $1+O(L^{-1})$ comparison of
Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian}.  At $s=0$ the ratio is exactly one, and
these conclusions hold by continuity with zero remainder there.

Carry out the proof on a fixed larger positive-offset strip and with
fixed horizontal extensions.  The remainder in
\eqref{scf-first-correction} is holomorphic in $z$ on that larger
region: its denominator $F(z)e^{sp(z)^2}$ never vanishes.
Cauchy's estimate on fixed-radius disks then bounds every fixed spatial
derivative of this remainder by $O_k(L^{-2})$ on the smaller region.
The corresponding derivatives of the $O(L^{-1})$ remainder have order
$O_k(L^{-1})$.  The margins are essential here.

For sufficiently large $Y$ the relative ratio lies within $O(L^{-1})$
of one.  It follows that $H(-s,z)$ is nonzero and
\[
 |H(-s,z)|\ge\tfrac12 e^{-C^2c}|F(z)|.
\]
Only now define the negative-time logarithmic derivative by
\begin{equation}\label{scf-ps-definition}
 p_s(z):=\frac{H_z(-s,z)}{H(-s,z)}.
\end{equation}
Taking the analytic logarithm of the relative ratio and differentiating
in $z$ gives, with the same fixed margins,
\begin{align}
 p_s(z)&=p(z)+2sp(z)p'(z)+O(L^{-1}),\label{scf-ps-estimate}\\
 \partial_zp_s(z)&=p'(z)+2s\{p'(z)^2+p(z)p''(z)\}+O(L^{-1})
                  =p'(z)+O(1).\label{scf-ps-derivative}
\end{align}
All time parameters in this argument are real.  Neither complex-time
uniformity nor uniformity as the positive offset tends to zero has been
established.

\section{Conserved spatial contours}
\label{sec:scf-contours}

The comparison theorem acquires a region on which material transport is
well defined.  We next turn that analytic fact into an actual continued
contour, including its horizontal coverage.  Its endpoints are allowed
to move.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-offset material graphs]\label{scf-flow-theorem}
Assume RH.  Fix $0<b_-<b_+$, $c>0$ and $d>0$.
For all sufficiently large $Y$, for every $b\in[b_-,b_+]$ and every
$a\in[Y-d,2Y+d]$, the initial point $z_a=a+ib$ has a unique material
continuation
\begin{equation}\label{scf-flow}
 \partial_s Z(s,a)=-p_s(Z(s,a)),\qquad Z(0,a)=z_a,
 \qquad 0\le s\le cL^{-2}.
\end{equation}
Uniformly in these parameters, with $J=\partial_a Z$,
\begin{align}
 Z(s,a)&=z_a-sp(z_a)+O(L^{-2}),\label{scf-position-expansion}\\
 J(s,a)&=1-sp'(z_a)+O(L^{-2}).\label{scf-tangent-expansion}
\end{align}
The image is a graph over its advected real interval, covers $[Y,2Y]$,
and has displacement, tangent angle and slope $O(L^{-1})$.
Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{scf-material}
 H(-s,Z(s,a))J(s,a)=F(z_a),\qquad
 P(-s,Z(s,a))=P(0,z_a),
\end{equation}
where $P(t,z)=\int_0^z H(t,w)\,\dd w$ is the canonical primitive of
the even field.  There are constants $0<\kappa<K$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{scf-upward}
 \kappa sL\le\Im Z(s,a)-b\le KsL.
\end{equation}
At fixed real coordinate the graph also moves upward with increasing
backward duration $s$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a rectangle containing the initial segment with additional fixed
horizontal margins and with fixed positive lower and finite upper
imaginary margins.  Establish the comparison and derivative estimates
on a still larger rectangle, leaving room for the Cauchy disks above.
On the containing rectangle,
$|p_s|+|\partial_zp_s|\le KL$.  Local ODE existence therefore applies.
Until the first exit,
\[
 |Z(s,a)-z_a|\le KLs\le Kc/L.
\]
For sufficiently large $Y$ this is less than half every initial spatial
buffer.  A first exit is impossible, and ordinary ODE continuation gives
the solution on the full interval.  This is continuation in a paid
finite rectangle; no global properness assumption is involved.

Differentiating in the label gives
\[
 \partial_sJ=-(\partial_zp_s)(Z)J,\qquad J(0,a)=1.
\]
The continuous logarithm with initial value zero satisfies
$|\log J|\le Kc/L$.  Hence $J=1+O(L^{-1})$, $\Re J>0$, and its
continuous argument is $O(L^{-1})$.  Strict increase of $\Re Z$ in $a$
makes the image a graph with slope $\Im J/\Re J=O(L^{-1})$.
The fixed initial end extensions and the displacement bound put its left
endpoint to the left of $Y$ and its right endpoint to the right of $2Y$.
Thus the graph covers the whole target interval without fixing endpoints.

For \eqref{scf-position-expansion},
\eqref{scf-ps-estimate} and $|Z-z_a|=O(sL)$ give
$p_s(Z)-p(z_a)=O(sL^2+L^{-1})$.  Integration over
$0\le s\le cL^{-2}$ costs $O(L^{-2})$.
Similarly \eqref{scf-ps-derivative} gives
$\partial_zp_s(Z)=p'(z_a)+O(1)$, while $J=1+O(L^{-1})$.
Inserting these into the equation for $J$ proves
\eqref{scf-tangent-expansion}.

Since $\partial_sH(-s,z)=H_{zz}(-s,z)$, differentiation gives
\[
 \partial_s\{H(-s,Z)J\}
  =\bigl[H_{zz}(-s,Z)-H_z(-s,Z)p_s(Z)
       -H(-s,Z)\partial_zp_s(Z)\bigr]J=0.
\]
This proves the first identity in \eqref{scf-material}.
Evenness gives $H_z(t,0)=0$, so $P_t=-H_z$ and
\[
 \partial_s P(-s,Z)=H_z(-s,Z)-H(-s,Z)p_s(Z)=0.
\]
This proves the second identity.  The continued contour is therefore the
selected local inverse-primitive lift.  Its finite-rectangle bound
prevents escape to an asymptotic value on this interval; other inverse
branches sharing the same primitive value do not affect this uniqueness.

It remains to prove the direction of motion.  Taking imaginary parts in
\eqref{scf-product} yields the absolutely convergent positive kernel
\begin{equation}\label{scf-positive-kernel}
 -\Im p(x+iy)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal Z}
                   \frac{y}{(x-\lambda)^2+y^2}.
\end{equation}
Its upper bound is $KL$ on the containing rectangle.
For the lower bound choose a fixed $R$ sufficiently large that the main
term in $N_F(x+R)-N_F(x-R)$,
$(R/(2\pi))\log x+O_R(1)$, dominates the two
$O(\log x)$ errors in \eqref{scf-count}.  A slightly enlarged fixed
window absorbs endpoint conventions.  It contains at least a positive
multiple of $\log x$ zeros, including multiplicity.  Each contributes a
fixed positive amount to \eqref{scf-positive-kernel} on the containing
strip.  Thus $\kappa_0L\le-\Im p\le K_0L$ there.
Equation~\eqref{scf-ps-estimate} changes $p$ by only $O(1)$.
The flow equation consequently gives
$\kappa L\le\partial_s\Im Z\le KL$ for large $Y$, and integration
proves \eqref{scf-upward}.

Finally write the graph as $y=\varepsilon(x,s)$, where $s$ denotes
backward duration.  Its transport equation is
\[
 \partial_s\varepsilon=-\Im p_s
                   +(\Re p_s)\partial_x\varepsilon.
\]
The first term is bounded below by a positive multiple of $L$; the
second is $O(1)$ because the slope is $O(L^{-1})$.
Thus the fixed-$x$ derivative is positive for sufficiently large $Y$.
\end{proof}

The upward direction is a statement about these spatial graphs.  It is
not a sign theorem for a regularized relative zero current or for a
physical three-form.  Material conservation transfers the acquired
field estimate into geometry; it does not supply an additional
multiplicity-sensitive arithmetic estimate.

\section{What regular contours do not distinguish}
\label{sec:limits}

The acquired wall is compatible with a multiple zero inside it. This
limitation can be seen both at the local heat scale and in a complete
positive-source field with the same two-term zero count as the target.
It concerns the information used in the preceding estimates; it is not
an exclusion of estimates using the exact theta arithmetic.

\begin{proposition}[A positive-source field with no simple zeros]
\label{prop:all-multiple}
Assume RH for $F$ and set
\begin{equation}
 a_* =\frac{\log2}{4},\qquad
 G_0(z)=F(z/2)^2\bigl(1+\cos(a_*z)\bigr).
 \label{eq:all-multiple-control}
\end{equation}
Every zero of $G_0$ is real and has even multiplicity. Its positive zero
count, with multiplicities, satisfies
\begin{equation}
 N_G(X)=\frac X{4\pi}\log\frac X{4\pi}-\frac X{4\pi}
             +O(\log X).
 \label{eq:matched-count}
\end{equation}
It is the Fourier transform of a positive even superexponentially
decaying source. Its heat evolution obeys the Gaussian comparison and
material-contour conclusions proved above, with its own constants.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Zeros of the squared factor have even multiplicity. The other factor
is $2\cos^2(a_*z/2)$, with double zeros at the odd multiples of
$\pi/a_*$. Multiplicities add at coincidences. Hence
\[
 N_G(X)=2N_F(X/2)+a_*X/\pi+O(1).
\]
The first term has the main expression in \eqref{eq:matched-count}
minus $X\log2/(4\pi)$; the second term restores exactly this linear
term. This proves the count.

The logarithmic derivative is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{G_0'}{G_0}(z)=p(z/2)-a_*\tan(a_*z/2).
 \label{eq:control-log-derivative}
\end{equation}
The tangent and every fixed derivative are bounded on a fixed positive
offset strip. The $F$ term inherits the bounds at the half-height and
half-offset. Thus all the initial logarithmic estimates, the global
horizontal ratio and the local Gaussian estimates apply. The positive
real-zero kernel and the count give the same upward-drift lower bound.
The exact canonical Euler factorization is absent, but the logarithmic
bound it supplied to our proof is already present in
\eqref{eq:control-log-derivative}.

For the source, write
$F(z)=\int_\R\rho(u)e^{izu}\dd u$ with
$\rho=\Phi_{\mathrm{even}}/2$. Put $h(u)=2\rho(2u)$ and $q=h*h$.
Then the full Fourier density of $G_0$ is
\begin{equation}
 \rho_G(u)=q(u)+\tfrac12q(u-a_*)+\tfrac12q(u+a_*).
 \label{eq:control-source}
\end{equation}
It is positive and even. To check its decay, split the convolution
integral into $|v|\ge|u|/2$ and its complement, where
$|u-v|\ge|u|/2$. It follows that
\[
 q(u)\le2\|h\|_1\sup_{|v|\ge|u|/2}h(v).
\]
Superexponential decay therefore survives convolution and fixed shifts.
The integral
$G(t,z)=\int_\R e^{tu^2}\rho_G(u)e^{izu}\dd u$
exists for every real $t$, gives $G_t=-G_{zz}$, and has the exact
Gaussian representation. Every step of the comparison and flow proof
now applies.
\end{proof}

The proposition shows that initial real-rootedness, the displayed coarse
count, a positive source, and the existence of the fixed-offset bounds
are compatible with \emph{zero simple zeros}. Their existence alone
cannot yield even a positive simplicity proportion. The failed premise
for this control is the exact canonical source identity, not the coarse
bounds already acquired. An estimate using actual theta coefficients,
exact invariant values or sharper quantitative constants may
still distinguish the target.

\subsection{The offset cannot simply be taken to zero}

The local model $K(t,z)=(z-a)^2-2t$ gives, at
$z=a+i\beta/L$ and $s=\kappa/L^2$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{K(-s,z)}{K(0,z)}=1-\frac{2\kappa}{\beta^2},
 \qquad
 \exp\left(s\left(\frac{K_z(0,z)}{K(0,z)}\right)^2\right)
       =e^{-4\kappa/\beta^2}.
 \label{eq:quadratic-obstruction}
\end{equation}
The first expression vanishes at $\kappa=\beta^2/2$; the second never
does. Here the initial logarithmic derivative has derivative
$2L^2/\beta^2$, beyond the fixed-offset $O(L)$ hypothesis. This is a
local obstruction to extending the same generic argument through an
allowed double. To put the model literally in the growing-height band,
one may take the translated family $a=Y$; the field then depends on $Y$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:all-multiple} separately supplies a single fixed
field with multiple zeros at arbitrarily large heights.

A near-axis argument must therefore keep the true local zero factor and
control its multiplicity-sensitive part. Assuming a floor through this
model would assume away the event to be studied.

\section{From a collision balance to simple zeros}
\label{sec:resource}

The contour theorem supplies a zero-free outer corridor. To use it toward
simplicity, one must also control the nonreal population inside the
corridor at the earlier time. The following exact balance states that
remaining requirement without replacing one-sided motion by signed
conservation. The time endpoints $a,b$ in this section are unrelated to
the height label of a material arc.

Let $H$ be a nonzero, jointly holomorphic solution of
\begin{equation}
 H_t=-H_{zz},\qquad H(t,\bar z)=\overline{H(t,z)}\quad(t\in\R),
 \label{eq:heat}
\end{equation}
on a connected complex neighborhood of a compact time--space cylinder. We study real times
$I=[a,b]$. A simple zero moves with velocity
\begin{equation}
 v(t,z)=\frac{H_{zz}(t,z)}{H_z(t,z)}.
 \label{eq:velocity}
\end{equation}
Individual velocities become singular at a collision. The weighted
population nevertheless has finite variation.

A \emph{moving window} is a function $\psi(t,z)$, $C^1$ in time and $C^2$
in space, with $0\le\psi\le1$, $\psi(t,\bar z)=\psi(t,z)$, and support
contained in a fixed compact spatial set. Its derivatives are continuous
on the cylinder. Every zero in a sum below is counted with its full
spatial multiplicity. Put
\begin{align}
 N_{\psi,\nr}(t)&=\sum_{\substack{H(t,z)=0\\\Im z\ne0}}\psi(t,z),
 \label{eq:nonreal-population}\\
 \FF_{\psi,\nr}^{\pm}
 &=\int_a^b\sum_{\substack{H(t,z)=0\\\Im z\ne0}}
 \bigl[\pm\{\psi_t+\nabla\psi\cdot v\}\bigr]_+\dd t.
 \label{eq:relative-flux}
\end{align}
The integrands are defined at regular times; the exceptional times have
measure zero. Here $[s]_+=\max(s,0)$ and complex velocities are viewed as
planar vectors. These are one-sided fluxes: repeated inward and outward
motion is counted each time.

At a real multiple zero $(t_e,x_e)$ of order $m_e$, let
\begin{equation}
 \BB_\psi=\sum_{a<t_e\le b}
 2\left\lfloor\frac{m_e}{2}\right\rfloor\psi(t_e,x_e).
 \label{eq:birth-weight}
\end{equation}
The endpoint convention is deliberate: an event at $b$ is included; an
event at $a$ has already occurred.

\begin{theorem}[Nonreal mass and relative flux]
\label{thm:balance}
Under the preceding hypotheses, the fluxes are finite and
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\quad
 N_{\psi,\nr}(b)+\BB_\psi
 =N_{\psi,\nr}(a)+\FF_{\psi,\nr}^{+}-\FF_{\psi,\nr}^{-}.
 \quad}
 \label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
If $\psi_t+u\cdot\nabla\psi=0$ for a smooth boundary velocity $u$, then
the integrand in \eqref{eq:relative-flux} is
$[\pm\nabla\psi\cdot(v-u)]_+$.
\end{theorem}

Thus the relevant motion is relative to the boundary. Formula
\eqref{eq:balance} applies equally when the window changes shape without
being carried by a globally regular velocity field.

Suppose $\psi(b,x)=1$ at each real nonsimple endpoint site in a prescribed
core $K$. Let
\[
 M_K=\sum_{\substack{x\in K\cap\R\\m_x\ge2}}m_x,
 \qquad D_K=\sum_{\substack{x\in K\cap\R\\m_x\ge2}}(m_x-1),
 \qquad \mathcal R_\psi=N_{\psi,\nr}(a)+\FF_{\psi,\nr}^{+}.
\]

\begin{corollary}[Sharp endpoint bounds]
\label{cor:multiplicity}
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{D_K\le\mathcal R_\psi,\qquad
 M_K\le\frac32\mathcal R_\psi.}
 \label{eq:sharp-bounds}
\end{equation}
Both constants are optimal. If $\mathcal R_\psi<2$, there is no nonsimple
real endpoint site in $K$.
\end{corollary}

These statements concern real endpoint zeros and need no Riemann
hypothesis. Endpoint reality is an additional hypothesis when all zeros
of a canonical target are to be covered.

\subsection{Why a collision costs an even number of nonreal labels}

\begin{lemma}[Local heat birth]
\label{lem:birth}
At a spatial zero of order $m$ at $(t_e,z_e)$, the leading parabolic
polynomial, with $s=t-t_e$ and $w=z-z_e$, is a nonzero multiple of
\begin{equation}
 p_m(s,w)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
 \frac{m!(-s)^k w^{m-2k}}{k!(m-2k)!}.
 \label{eq:hermite}
\end{equation}
The local roots are analytic in a one-sided square-root time parameter
and have integrable speeds $O(|s|^{-1/2})$. At a real event exactly
$2\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ nonreal labels disappear as real time increases.
Multiple events are isolated, and hence finite on the compact sets used
here.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Successive time derivatives are $(-\partial_z^2)^kH$, so the terms of
parabolic degree $m$ are precisely \eqref{eq:hermite}. For $s>0$,
$p_m(s,w)=(2s)^{m/2}\mathrm{He}_m(w/\sqrt{2s})$, where the monic
probabilists' Hermite polynomial has distinct real roots. For $s<0$ the
rescaled roots are purely imaginary, with one zero root if $m$ is odd.
The implicit function theorem after square-root rescaling continues each
simple rescaled root analytically. At a real event, real type and
uniqueness keep the forward roots real and the odd central backward
branch real. The other backward branches are nonreal.

The same rescaling is valid for complex $s$. It makes the local prepared
discriminant a nonzero multiple of
$s^{m(m-1)/2}$ to leading order. There is consequently no other multiple
event sufficiently near the given one. Compactness proves finiteness.
A spatial slice cannot vanish identically: the heat equation would make
all its time derivatives vanish, and analyticity would force $H$ to be
identically zero on the connected neighborhood.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:balance} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:multiplicity}]
Between events, differentiate the window along each root. Lemma
\ref{lem:birth} makes the resulting variation integrable. Roots entering
or leaving the compact support cause no jump because the window vanishes
there. An off-real collision preserves the weighted nonreal mass; a real
collision removes exactly the weight in \eqref{eq:birth-weight}.
Adding these changes proves \eqref{eq:balance}.

For $m\ge2$,
\[
 m-1\le2\lfloor m/2\rfloor,
 \qquad m\le\tfrac32\,2\lfloor m/2\rfloor.
\]
Each target site is counted with window value one. Discarding the
nonnegative terms $N_{\psi,\nr}(b)$ and $\FF^-_{\psi,\nr}$ proves
\eqref{eq:sharp-bounds}. A target multiple site costs at least two,
giving the final assertion. The field $H=z^3-6tz$, with a window equal
to one on its local roots, has earlier nonreal mass two and endpoint
values $D_K=2$, $M_K=3$. It attains both constants.
\end{proof}


\subsection{The part of the resource supplied by the new wall}
\label{sec:wall-resource}

The material velocity in forward heat time is $U=H_z/H$.
It differs from the root velocity $v=H_{zz}/H_z$ in
\eqref{eq:velocity}. Preserving $H\,dz$ therefore does not transport
individual zeros or annihilate their relative current.
Nevertheless the acquired zero-free corridor has an exact use in the
balance: the upper and lower outer transitions can be placed where
there are no roots.

\begin{corollary}[Reduction to earlier mass and height-end flux]
\label{cor:wall-resource}
Fix $b>0$, $c>0$ and fixed horizontal margins. Under RH, for all
sufficiently large $Y$ there is a conjugation-symmetric smooth window
$\psi_Y$ on $[-S,0]$, $S=c/(\log Y)^2$, equal to one at every real
point of $[Y,2Y]$, whose upper and lower transition corridors contain
no zeros at any time. Its nonreal flux is supported only in the fixed
horizontal end regions. If $M_Y$ counts labels at nonsimple endpoint
sites in $[Y,2Y]$, then
\begin{equation}
 M_Y\le\frac32\left\{
 N_{\psi_Y,\nr}(-S)+\FF_{\mathrm{ends},\nr}^{+}\right\}.
 \label{eq:wall-resource-consumer}
\end{equation}
The right-hand terms retain their full definitions; neither is estimated
in size by the contour theorem.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose the strip for Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian} to contain a fixed
neighborhood of $y=b$, and reflect it across the real axis. Use the
material graph $y=\varepsilon(x,-t)$ of
Theorem~\ref{scf-flow-theorem}, with sufficiently long fixed initial end
extensions. Its $O(1/\log Y)$ displacement leaves a fixed thin
transition about this graph inside the zero-free strip.

Take a smooth compact horizontal cutoff $\eta$, equal to one on
$[Y,2Y]$, supported inside the covered extended interval. Choose an
even smooth cutoff $\chi$ equal to one for $|r|\le1-\delta$ and zero
for $|r|\ge1+\delta$, with fixed small $\delta>0$, and set
\[
 \psi_Y(t,x+iy)=\eta(x)
       \chi\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon(x,-t)}\right).
\]
The graph is smooth and positive. Choosing $\delta$ small and then $Y$
large puts every point with $\chi'\ne0$ in the acquired zero-free
corridors. At a regular root, all terms containing $\chi'$ vanish.
The remaining window variation is exactly
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\psi_Y(t,z(t))
   =\eta'(\Re z)\chi\left(\frac{\Im z}
                                  {\varepsilon(\Re z,-t)}\right)
                         \Re v(t,z).
\]
It is supported at the horizontal ends. Corollary~\ref{cor:multiplicity}
then proves \eqref{eq:wall-resource-consumer}.
\end{proof}

A fixed upper wall inside the same acquired strip would also remove
these transition contributions. Thus the new analytic floor pays an
outer boundary; material conservation describes a natural evolution
of that boundary. Neither choice removes the interior earlier mass.
The construction is independent of the field regulator used below.

For $N_Y$ the total endpoint multiplicity in the covered band, the
number of simple zeros is $N_Y-M_Y$. An acquired estimate
$\mathcal R_Y\le\alpha N_Y$, with
$\mathcal R_Y=N_{\psi_Y,\nr}(-S)+\FF_{\mathrm{ends},\nr}^{+}$,
would give
\[
 N_Y-M_Y\ge(1-3\alpha/2)N_Y.
\]
A bound $\mathcal R_Y=o(N_Y)$ would give density-one simplicity on
these bands. An individual-site window with resource below two would
exclude a multiple zero there. These are quantitative consequences of
the proved balance, not bounds on $\mathcal R_Y$ already obtained.
Any cumulative conclusion also needs complete coverage of its height
range and its exceptional low-height region.

\section{The arithmetic estimate still required}
\label{sec:next-arithmetic}

The main theorem acquires a useful outer contour at the mean-spacing
squared heat clock. It does not estimate how many nonreal zeros have
already appeared inside that contour. The all-multiple field in
Proposition~\ref{prop:all-multiple} demonstrates that this distinction
persists even with the same two-term zero count and positive source.
There are two natural ways to advance: bound the complete resource in
\eqref{eq:wall-resource-consumer}, or obtain a near-axis comparison
which retains the actual cluster factor and controls its
multiplicity-sensitive part using canonical arithmetic.

\subsection{A complete field quantity, with ordered limits}

For $\D\psi=\psi_x-i\psi_y$, put
\begin{equation}
 A_\psi=\psi_t|H_z|^2+
          \Re\{\D\psi\,H_{zz}\overline{H_z}\},\qquad
 k_\rho(w)=\frac{\rho^2}{\pi(|w|^2+\rho^2)^2}.
 \label{eq:resource-numerator}
\end{equation}
Let $\vartheta_\delta(y)$ vanish for $|y|\le\delta$, equal one for
$|y|\ge2\delta$, and increase to $1_{y\ne0}$ as
$\delta\downarrow0$. The precise field representation, proved in
Appendix~\ref{sec:field}, is
\begin{align}
 N_{\psi,\nr}(-S)
 &=\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\lim_{\rho\downarrow0}
   \int\vartheta_\delta(\Im z)\psi(-S,z)
     k_\rho(H(-S,z))|H_z(-S,z)|^2\dd A_z,
 \label{eq:resource-population}\\
 \FF_{\psi,\nr}^{+}
 &=\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\lim_{\rho\downarrow0}
   \int_{-S}^0\int\vartheta_\delta(\Im z)
        k_\rho(H)[A_\psi]_+\dd A_z\dd t.
 \label{eq:resource-field-flux}
\end{align}
These are exact for each fixed analytic field and fixed smooth window.
They do not assert uniform convergence as $Y$ grows or as a window
itself depends on $\rho$. A finite-regulator approximation needs its
own uniform error before such limits can be interchanged. The cutoff
$\vartheta_\delta$ measures nonreal roots; it is not multiplied into
$\psi$ before differentiating.

\subsection{Retain the coupled relative current}

One possible arithmetic reference uses another analytic field $G$ of
real type and a smooth conjugation-symmetric cutoff
$\chi:\C\to[0,1]$, with $\psi=\chi(G)$ on a compact packet.
The packet condition means the reference stays outside $\supp\chi$
on a containing spatial collar, so the window extends smoothly by zero.
A direct chain rule gives
\begin{equation}
 A_\psi=\Re\{\D\chi(G)(G_tH_z-G_zH_t)\overline{H_z}\}.
 \label{eq:reference-determinant}
\end{equation}
If $G_t=-G_{zz}$, the determinant in this expression is
\[
 \WW(H,G)=H_{zz}G_z-H_zG_{zz}.
\]
No division by either derivative is required. If
$d_G=G_t+G_{zz}$, retain the additional term $d_GH_z$.
For $\psi=\eta\chi(G)$ without a packet condition, the right-hand
side of \eqref{eq:reference-determinant} is multiplied by $\eta$ and
one must add
$\chi(G)\Re\{\D\eta H_{zz}\overline{H_z}\}$.
The positive part is taken after summing these terms.

When $G$ is a constant multiple of $H$, the matched field-level window
has zero relative field-level flux. Its boundary velocity, where
regular, is $H_{zz}/H_z$, not $H_z/H$. This exact cancellation does not
bound its earlier nonreal mass. It is a different construction from
the conserved spatial contours of Section~\ref{sec:scf-contours}.

For the complete theta source a finite prefix is an exact heat
reference, but its entire omitted tail must remain in the determinant.
Writing $\Phi=A_N+T_N$ and
$G_N(t,z)=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}A_N(u)\cos(zu)\dd u$, one obtains
\begin{align}
 \WW(H,G_N)&=\int_0^\infty\!\int_0^\infty
 e^{t(u^2+v^2)}A_N(u)T_N(v)K_z(u,v)\dd u\dd v,
 \label{eq:complete-residual}\\
 K_z(u,v)&=\frac{uv}{2}\bigl\{(v-u)\sin(z(u+v))
                    +(u+v)\sin(z(u-v))\bigr\}.
 \label{eq:antisymmetric-kernel}
\end{align}
Indeed the prefix-prefix products cancel, and differentiating the
complete transforms leaves precisely the displayed prefix-tail terms.
The sine addition formulas give the stated kernel. It is
antisymmetric in $u,v$ and vanishes on the diagonal. This is an
available arithmetic quantity to estimate before taking absolute values;
its identity supplies neither a flux sign nor a small-value denominator.
A small absolute source tail is not necessarily a small relative field
error near a high zero.

The proof objective is now specific: use information that the control
\eqref{eq:all-multiple-control} does not possess to bound a complete,
multiplicity-sensitive expression such as
\eqref{eq:resource-population}--\eqref{eq:resource-field-flux}, with its
height ends, near-real cutoff, errors and coverage intact. More terms in
the fixed-offset asymptotic alone do not meet this objective. The
contour theorem supplies a proved outer boundary from which that
arithmetic problem can be pursued.

\appendix
\section{The complete field representation of the resource}
\label{sec:field}

Recall the moving-window numerator $A_\psi$ and define
\begin{equation}
 k_\rho(w)=\frac{\rho^2}{\pi(|w|^2+\rho^2)^2},\qquad
 A_\psi=\psi_t|H_z|^2+\Re\{\D\psi\,H_{zz}\overline{H_z}\}.
 \label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
The kernel has integral one on the target plane. Its pullback gives a
smooth density and current
\[
 \mu_\rho=k_\rho(H)|H_z|^2,\qquad
 j_\rho=k_\rho(H)H_{zz}\overline{H_z},\qquad
 \partial_t\mu_\rho+\operatorname{div}j_\rho=0.
\]
One can obtain the last identity by pulling back the closed target-plane
area form. The moving-window integrand is exactly
$k_\rho(H)A_\psi=\psi_t\mu_\rho+\nabla\psi\cdot j_\rho$. In a material window it is
$\nabla\psi\cdot(j_\rho-u\mu_\rho)$.

Let $0\le\vartheta_\delta(y)\le1$ be continuous, vanish for $|y|\le\delta$,
equal one for $|y|\ge2\delta$, and increase to $1_{y\ne0}$ as
$\delta\downarrow0$. Set
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\rho,\delta}^{\pm}
 =\int_I\int\vartheta_\delta(\Im z)k_\rho(H)
          [\pm A_\psi]_+\dd A_z\dd t.
 \label{eq:regularized-flux}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact field representation]
\label{thm:current-limit}
The complete nonreal flux is
\begin{equation}
 \FF_{\psi,\nr}^{\pm}
   =\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\lim_{\rho\downarrow0}
                    Q_{\rho,\delta}^{\pm}.
 \label{eq:nonreal-limit}
\end{equation}
Without $\vartheta_\delta$, the limit is the corresponding variation of
\emph{all} roots. In particular it bounds the nonreal positive flux
from above. There is no excess current concentrated at the collision
times.
\end{theorem}

The measuring cutoff in \eqref{eq:regularized-flux} is not part of the
moving window. Replacing $\psi$ by $\psi\vartheta_\delta$ would introduce
additional derivative terms and would be a different formula.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform variation of nearby levels]
\label{lem:levels}
Let $f$ be jointly holomorphic near a compact cylinder, with no identically
zero spatial slice. On a compact spatial set, the roots of $f(t,z)=w$
have locally bounded total variation, uniformly for almost every
sufficiently small $w$. Their speed sums have a uniform $L^p(I)$ bound
for some $p>1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Cover the compact zero set by finitely many time--disk charts on whose
spatial circles $f$ stays away from zero, throughout a complex time
collar. Outside the chart interiors the remaining compact set has a
positive minimum of $|f|$. Choose the target radius below this minimum
and the circle bounds, so that every nearby level is covered. In each
chart $f-w$ has a fixed-degree monic root polynomial, with coefficients
holomorphic in time and $w$. After normalizing its disk and time interval,
root containment bounds its coefficients; Cauchy's estimates bound their
$C^{n-1,1}$ norms uniformly in $w$.

The polynomial-root theorem of Parusi\'nski--Rainer
\cite[Theorem 1]{pr} gives $L^p$ derivatives of every continuous root
for $1<p<n/(n-1)$. Choose one admissible $p$ for the finite cover and
sum the bounds. Degree one is immediate. Returning to physical time
introduces the factor $|I_{\rm chart}|^{1/p-1}$, where
$I_{\rm chart}$ is that chart's physical time interval; all these
factors and any overlaps are included. No root separation is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:current-limit}]
For almost every target $w$, the spatial area formula, whose Jacobian
is $|H_z|^2$, rewrites the integral without the measuring cutoff as
\begin{equation}
 Q_\rho^{\pm}=\int_\C k_\rho(w)V^{\pm}(w)\dd A_w,
 \quad
 V^{\pm}(w)=\int_I\sum_{H(t,z)=w}
 [\pm\{\psi_t+\nabla\psi\cdot v_w\}]_+\dd t,
 \label{eq:area}
\end{equation}
where $v_w=-H_t/H_z$. Quotients are used only at regular preimages;
critical points contribute zero to the original density. Exceptional
fibers are handled by the almost-everywhere area formula and Fubini.

At every regular time for the zero level, roots and velocities converge
as $w\to0$. The additional term $\psi_t$ is bounded by the finite chart
degrees. Lemma~\ref{lem:levels} makes the velocity terms uniformly
integrable across the finitely many zero-level events. Hence
$V^{\pm}(w)\to V^{\pm}(0)$ in the essential target-plane sense. The
portion $|w|\ge r$ in \eqref{eq:area} is at most
\[
 \frac{\rho^2}{\pi(r^2+\rho^2)^2}
 \int_I\int\bigl(|\psi_t||H_z|^2+
                   |\nabla\psi||H_tH_z|\bigr)\dd A\dd t,
\]
which tends to zero. The approximate identity therefore converges to
the all-root flux. For fixed $\delta$, insert the continuous bounded
factor $\vartheta_\delta$ in the same proof; then apply monotone
convergence on the root trajectories as $\delta\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

The earlier population has a parallel field representation:
\begin{equation}
 N_{\psi,\nr}(a)=
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\lim_{\rho\downarrow0}
 \int\vartheta_\delta(\Im z)\psi(a,z)
                   k_\rho(H(a,z))|H_z(a,z)|^2\dd A_z.
 \label{eq:initial-field}
\end{equation}
At a multiple zero the pullback area counts its full multiplicity.
Thus \eqref{eq:balance}, \eqref{eq:nonreal-limit}, and
\eqref{eq:initial-field} describe the whole nonreal resource through
the complete field. They do not yet make that resource small.

\begin{remark}[The near-real strip]
For a conjugation-symmetric moving window the omitted flux is locally
$O(\delta^2)$. Indeed, near a real event put $q=\sqrt{t_e-t}$.
Uniqueness of the rescaled branches and real type give
$z_j(-q)=\overline{z_j(q)}$. Therefore $x_j$ is even and $y_j$ odd
in $q$. In time derivatives, $x_j'=O(1)$,
$y_j'=O(q^{-1})$, while $\psi_y=O(y_j)=O(q)$.
The total window derivative is bounded. If
$|y_j|\ge c_j\sqrt{t_e-t}$ and $|d\psi/dt|\le A_j$, the omitted
portion costs at most $4A_j\delta^2/c_j^2$. Sum over the finite incoming
branches after taking $\delta$ below the other tracks' clearance.
These constants are local field data; no uniform height estimate is
implicit in this observation.
\end{remark}
