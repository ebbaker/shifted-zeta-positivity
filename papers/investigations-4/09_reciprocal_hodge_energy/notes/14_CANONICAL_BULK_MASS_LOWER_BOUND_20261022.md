## 1. The exact signed consumer

Use the canonical complete source

\[
 H(t,z)=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du,
 \qquad J(t,z)=H(t,z)+iH_z(t,z),
\]

with the unchanged normalization and complete even reflection. For the
nonnegative upper-half-strip cutoff phi=psi(x)chi(y) of
[Note 10](10_SIGNED_JET_FLUX_AND_PHASE_TRACE_OBSTACLES_20261022.md),
its complete identity is

\[
 T_\psi:=\int\psi\frac{Q}{\sqrt E}\,dx
       =P_\phi-M_\phi,\qquad
 P_\phi=\int |J|\Delta\phi\,dA,\qquad
 M_\phi=\int\phi\frac{|J'|^2}{|J|}\,dA\ge0.           \tag{1}
\]

Suppose a closed disk D(z_c,r) lies inside the upper strip and phi>=m>0
on this disk. Let B>=sup_D |J|. Then

\[
 M_\phi\ge\frac mB\int_{D(z_c,r)}|J'|^2\,dA.          \tag{2}
\]

The inequality follows pointwise off zeros, extends by local
integrability, and assumes no lower |J|. A positive upper B is sufficient.

If one has a paid derivative anchor |J'(z_c)|>=a_c>0, holomorphic
submean gives the sharper local bound

\[
 \boxed{M_\phi\ge\delta_{\rm local}:=
              \frac{m\pi r^2 a_c^2}{B}>0.}             \tag{3}
\]

A ball acquisition of J'(z_c), not a positive physical E floor, is the
additional information. J may have zeros of arbitrary multiplicity
elsewhere in the disk. For uncertain anchors use the distance of the
complete complex ball from zero; for B use its complete outward upper.
A bound that contains zero gives no local positive a_c and must not be
replaced by its midpoint.

With P_phi in [P_-,P_+], the complete signed trace upper becomes

\[
                    T_\psi\le P_+-\delta_{\rm local}.   \tag{4}
\]

This improves the earlier P_+ by a definite amount using paid extra
information. It does not assert the resulting upper is negative. A
bulk upper would improve the lower trace endpoint instead.

A finite polynomial can use its other derivative coefficients at no
additional source acquisition. If F(z_c+zeta)=sum_{n=0}^N c_n zeta^n
approximates J' uniformly within epsilon on D(z_c,r), Taylor
orthogonality and the L^2 triangle inequality give

\[
 \int_{D(z_c,r)}|J'|^2dA\ge\pi r^2
 \left[\max\left(0,
 \sqrt{\sum_{n=0}^N\frac{|c_n|^2r^{2n}}{n+1}}
                      -\epsilon\right)\right]^2.       \tag{4a}
\]

Combine this with (2). Coefficient-ball lower moduli can pay the positive
sum, with all correlations still allowed. Maximize the resulting lower
with (3); neither is assigned an advantage merely because it retains
more coefficients. A weighted polynomial Gram integral could also use
the actual phi instead of its minimum m, but that is a separate declared
finite calculation.

## 2. A complete canonical imaginary-axis anchor

For real |t|<=S and y>=0, define

\[
 A(t,y)=\int e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cosh(yu)\,du,\quad
 B_1(t,y)=\int u e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\sinh(yu)\,du,\quad
 C_2(t,y)=\int u^2e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cosh(yu)\,du.
\]

All integration ranges are [0,infinity). Direct differentiation gives

\[
 J(t,iy)=A+B_1>0,\qquad
 J'(t,iy)=-i(B_1+C_2),\qquad
 |J'(t,iy)|\ge a_S:=\int_0^\infty u^2e^{-Su^2}\Phi(u)\,du>0.
                                                               \tag{5}
\]

No RH assumption is used. Upper moment tails are the complete all-mode
and exterior-ray payments already proved in the RHE manuscript. For a
lower it is legitimate to retain a declared positive portion of a
canonical mode; no reflection identity is assigned to that portion.

For example, for any v>0, retaining only n=1 and u in [v,2v] gives

\[
 \boxed{a_S\ge a_*(S,v):=
 \pi v^3 e^{5v-4Sv^2}
       (2\pi e^{4v}-3)e^{-\pi e^{8v}}>0.}             \tag{6}
\]

Indeed u^2>=v^2, exp(-Su^2)>=exp(-4Sv^2), exp(5u)>=exp(5v),
2pi exp(4u)−3>=2pi exp(4v)−3, and
exp(-pi exp(4u))>=exp(-pi exp(8v)); the interval length is v. All other
modes and rays are positive and can only improve the lower.
Choosing a fixed v such as1/16 makes this a closed elementary source
constant without any source quadrature. A larger certified partial
integral can replace it; its full numerical acquisition cost is additional.

The corresponding local amplitude upper is

\[
 B\le\mathcal B_Y(S):=M_{0,Y}(S)+M_{1,Y}(S),\qquad
 |J'|\le\mathcal L_Y(S):=M_{1,Y}(S)+M_{2,Y}(S),        \tag{7}
\]

on |Im z|<=Y. These are complete canonical positive moments. Together
(5)--(7) provide an actual source-paid mass lower for any disk centered
on the positive imaginary axis and contained in phi's positive region.

The same argument also gives a small zero-free anchor neighborhood:
if |z-iy|<=r and r L_{y+r}(S)<A(t,y)+B_1(t,y), then
|J(z)|>=A+B_1−r L_{y+r}>0. This is a derived finite anchor neighborhood;
it is not a physical all-height floor and is not needed for (2)--(3).

## 3. Quantitative propagation to every finite real height

An all-height *positive mass lower*, rather than a pointwise amplitude
floor, can be obtained from the same anchor. The explicit cost matters.

Let z_c=C+iy_0 with C real, y_0>0. Choose r>0 with D(z_c,r) contained
in phi>=m>0. Let d=|C|, assume r/2<d<R, and set

\[
 \alpha=\frac{\log(R/d)}{\log(2R/r)}\in(0,1),\qquad
 L_R=\mathcal L_{y_0+R}(S),\qquad
 b= a_S^{1/\alpha}L_R^{-(1-\alpha)/\alpha}.            \tag{8}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{M_\phi\ge\delta_{\rm prop}:=
       \frac{m\pi r^2}{4\mathcal B_{y_0+r}(S)}\,b^2>0.}
                                                               \tag{9}
\]

**Proof.** Write f=J'(t,.), and let m_f(s)=sup_{|z-z_c|<=s}|f(z)|.
The anchor iy_0 has distance d from z_c and |f(iy_0)|>=a_S, so
m_f(d)>=a_S. On the annulus r/2<|z-z_c|<R, compare the subharmonic
log|f| with the radial harmonic function taking boundary values
log m_f(r/2) and log L_R. The maximum principle gives

\[
 a_S\le m_f(d)\le m_f(r/2)^\alpha L_R^{1-\alpha}.
\]

Zeros cause no difficulty because log|f| tends to minus infinity there;
f is not identically zero by (5). Rearranging yields m_f(r/2)>=b.
At a maximizing point z_* in that disk, the radius-r/2 disk lies in
D(z_c,r). Holomorphic submean gives
integral_D |f|²>=pi r²|f(z_*)|²/4>=pi r² b²/4.
Combine this with (2) and the complete local amplitude upper in (7).
Boundary tangencies can be handled by slightly smaller radii and limits.
This proves (9).

If d<=r/2 the anchor already lies in the inner disk; the same submean
argument gives (9) with b=a_S, avoiding interpolation. If C=0, the
centered anchor gives the stronger factor in (3).

For the explicit cutoffs of Note10, choose y_0=h/2 and
0<r<min(L,h/2). A fully paid weight lower is

\[
 m=\left(1-r^2/L^2\right)^3
    \left(1-(1/2+r/h)^2\right)^3>0.                    \tag{10}
\]

One may take a_*(S,v) from (6) in place of a_S in (8), because
b=a (a/L_R)^((1-alpha)/alpha) increases with a>0. All-height here means
that each finite C has an effective positive lower with its displayed
C-dependent cost. It does **not** mean delta stays bounded away from
zero as |C| grows. The global source norm in L_R grows with R and the
exponent1/alpha can be large. No endpoint- or height-uniform exclusion
margin is inferred.

This is not a pointwise lower for J or E in the target disk. A holomorphic
function can vanish there while retaining a positive integral of |J'|².
Thus (9) does not assume the target's missing nonvanishing.

## 4. A source-positive control rules out automatic potential positivity

The following exact smooth positive even source has no canonical square
arithmetic:

\[
 H_*(z)=e^{-z^2/4}\left(1+\tfrac12\cos z\right),\qquad
 g_*(u)=\frac2{\sqrt\pi}
 \left[e^{-u^2}+\tfrac14e^{-(u-1)^2}+\tfrac14e^{-(u+1)^2}\right],
 \quad H_*(z)=\int_0^\infty g_*(u)\cos(zu)\,du.         \tag{11}
\]

At x=pi, H_*>0 and

\[
 (\log H_*)''(\pi)=-\tfrac12+1=\tfrac12,
 \qquad Q_*(\pi)=-\tfrac12H_*(\pi)^2<0.               \tag{12}
\]

By continuity choose a nonnegative smooth psi supported close to pi
with T_psi<0. The physical jet is nonzero throughout a sufficiently
small closed complex neighborhood of this support. On that fixed chart,
mu_J is bounded; for chi_h in Note10, M_phi=O(h), while T_psi is
independent of h. Hence P_phi=T_psi+M_phi<0 for all sufficiently small
positive h. This proves that positive smooth even cosine sources do not
force P_phi>=0. It does not assert this sign for the unchanged theta source.

For clarity, the source's exact heat continuation exists for t<1 and is

\[
 H_*(t,z)=\frac1{\sqrt{1-t}}e^{-z^2/[4(1-t)]}
 \left[1+\tfrac12 e^{t/(1-t)}
                    \cos\left(\frac z{1-t}\right)\right].
                                                               \tag{13}
\]

It has ordinary double events at
 t_*=log2/(1+log2),
followed by simple all-real slices for t_*<t<1. Its Gaussian source fails
the canonical super-exponential tail and the unchanged theta arithmetic;
no all-time Newman threshold or continuation beyond its heat domain is
assigned. The analytic anchor and positive bulk lower remain valid with
its own source constants. Thus those lower bounds are not collision
exclusions either.
