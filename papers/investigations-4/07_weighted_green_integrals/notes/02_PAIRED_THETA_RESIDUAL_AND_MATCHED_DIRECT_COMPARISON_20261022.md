**Status: proved in-note, in the stated conditional and estimator scopes.**

retaining the known lowest atom before bounding. A paired, common-source

this candidate does **not** establish the requested advantage over direct
evaluation. Its complete same-data comparison is a scoped tie, not an
exclusion of all weighted estimates or possible acquisition savings.

This is a quantitative source-bound improvement and a decided estimator
comparison, not a new RH criterion. Apply the [result index][results],

## 1. Keep the existing physical contract and the complete reflection

Keep the disk, time t_0=d/4, weight psi and derived actual energy floor
E_H>=e>0 from [Opening Note 01][opening]. No new event is acquired.
Let L[f]=integral f Delta(psi)/(4pi); its positive/negative masses are
4/9 each and ||L||=8/9. Use a real event center a. All source and physical
normalizations remain unchanged.

Use the canonical modes
phi_n(v)=(2lambda_n^2 exp(9v)−3lambda_n exp(5v))exp(−lambda_n exp(4v)),
lambda_n=pi n^2. For a fixed **source contour shift** theta=1/16 define

\[
 P[A](t,z)=\frac12\int_0^\infty
 \left[A(u+i\theta)e^{t(u+i\theta)^2+iz(u+i\theta)}
       +A(u-i\theta)e^{t(u-i\theta)^2-iz(u-i\theta)}\right]du.
\]

This theta is distinct from the heat-window d. Full reflection of the
complete Phi gives H=P[Phi]. A finite source S_m=sum_{n<=m}phi_n is not
even. [BW14 Note 02, (5)–(7)][bw14-02] gives H_{S_m}=P[S_m]+C_{S_m}
and C_{tail}=−C_{S_m}; the connectors cancel only in the complete source.
Set F=P[S_m] and R_H=H−F=P[tail]. Its first jet is g=(F,F_z),
r=(R_H,(R_H)_z). No individual connector has been set to zero.

## 2. Whole-ray tail payment and low-order log residual

Here is one elementary sufficient tail payment, assembled from the
inherited paired-ray and complete-mode estimates. It is not claimed to
improve the stronger [S01 paired-endpoint estimate][s01]. Grant both to
the direct comparator.

Put k=m+1, beta=pi k^2, c_0=31/32<=cos(4theta), and

\[
 q=\left({k+1\over k}\right)^4e^{-c_0\pi(2k+1)},\quad
 A_9=4c_0\beta-9-R>0,\quad A_5=A_9+4,
 \quad B_*=-\theta(a-R)-t_0\theta^2.
\]

Assume t_0<=8c_0 beta and q<1. For j=0,1,2,

\[
 \eta_j:=\sup_{D_R(a)}|\partial_z^j R_H|
 \le {e^{B_*-c_0\beta}\over1-q}
 \left[2\beta^2 I_j(A_9)+3\beta I_j(A_5)\right],          \tag{1}
\]

where I_0(A)=1/A, I_1(A)=theta/A+1/A^2 and
I_2(A)=theta^2/A+2/A^3. The second-order factor uses
|u+i theta|^2=u^2+theta^2 exactly. A Cauchy–Schwarz refinement of I_1
is A^(-1)sqrt(theta^2+2/A^2); either valid upper bound may be used.

For the proof, the positive mode majorants have successive ratio at
most q uniformly for u>=0. Use exp(4u)>=1+4u+8u^2 and retain the separate
9u and 5u source terms. Each ray's remaining integral is bounded by the
displayed exponential moments. The two rays have the same factor
exp(−theta Re z−t_0 theta^2); their half weights pay the factor two.
All omitted modes and the entire ray remain present. At fixed finite
(a,R,t_0), these bounds tend effectively to zero as m increases.
This is a sufficient source-accuracy schedule, not a complexity theorem.

Let eta=sqrt(eta_0^2+eta_1^2) for the exact holomorphic reference F;
its numerical evaluation errors are paid separately without changing F.
An actual replacement reference must itself be holomorphic with certified
jet errors, including order two for the direct density identity.
Require eta<=sqrt(e)/17. Then

\[
 \|g\|\ge\sqrt e-\eta\ge16\sqrt e/17,\qquad
 \rho:=\sup\|r\|/\|g\|\le1/16.                          \tag{2}
\]

Thus the reference floor is derived, not assumed. With
a_r=Re<g,r>/E_F and b_r=||r||^2/E_F,

\[
 M_\psi(H)=M_\psi(F)+L[2a_r]+L[b_r-2a_r^2]+\mathcal R_3,
 \qquad |\mathcal R_3|\le {1\over6480}.                 \tag{3}
\]

Indeed factor 1+2a_r s+b_r s^2=(1+alpha s)(1+conj(alpha)s),
alpha=a_r+i sqrt(b_r−a_r^2), |alpha|<=rho. The two log series give

\[
 |R_3(z)|\le -2\log(1-\rho)-2\rho-\rho^2
 \le {2\rho^3\over3(1-\rho)}.
\]

Multiplication by ||L||=8/9 proves (3). The full regular homotopy is
also paid directly: ||g+s r||>=sqrt(e)−(1−s)eta for 0<=s<=1.
No H−F divided by F is used: F can have simple zeros even though E_F>0.

At most two residual-source copies occur in the polynomial correction.
This algebraic-order reduction from Opening Note 01's high-degree
energy expansion is not a proved acquisition saving. Acquiring M_psi(F),
both signed corrections, source/ray integrals, spatial integrals and
rounding still needs a complete schedule. Their additional errors plus
1/6480 must be at most the declared 1/100 pilot error target.

## 3. The actual theta law can be used on the paired rays

Retain the exact masses from [BW14 Note 04, (3)–(8)][bw14-04]. To make
the atom subtraction explicit, put alpha=pi m^2, D=2alpha−3,
gamma=D/(2alpha), Delta=beta−alpha, delta_n=lambda_n−beta, and

\[
 A_n={\lambda_n^2\over\alpha^2}e^{-(\lambda_n-\alpha)},
 \quad B_n={3\lambda_n(\lambda_n-\alpha)\over\alpha^2D}
                     e^{-(\lambda_n-\alpha)},
 \quad E_m=\sum_{n>m}(A_n+B_n),\quad A=A_k.
\]

With Exp_gamma the probability density gamma exp(−gamma s) on s>=0,

\[
 \chi:=E_m\nu_m=
 \sum_{n>m}\left[A_n\delta_{\delta_n}
       +B_n\operatorname{Law}(\delta_n+\mathrm{Exp}_\gamma)\right],
 \quad \tau=\chi-A\delta_0,\quad c=\tau([0,\infty))=E_m-A.
\]

Let h(v)=exp(4v)−1 and W(v)=phi_m(v)exp(−Delta h(v)). The actual tail
is W(v) integral exp(−s h(v))dchi(s). Define V_s(t,z)=jet(P[W exp(−s h)])(t,z), evaluating at t_0
when time is omitted. Then

\[
 r=\int V_s\,d\chi(s)=A V_0+\int V_s\,d\tau(s).          \tag{4}
\]

This is **one** law shared by every point and jet. It is not legitimate
to contour-shift the incomplete-source cutoff indicators of the real-ray
monotone hull. Here the single-mixture source is holomorphic; the original
Laplace law is used instead.

The continuation and Fubini payment is explicit. Set kappa=gamma/2>1/4.
For v=u±i theta, Re h(v)>=cos(4theta)−1=−kappa_0, with
kappa_0<1/32<kappa. Hence |exp(−s h(v))|<=exp(kappa_0 s).
The source factor W(v) and physical derivative factors (u+theta)^j
have a complete superexponential u majorant. The finite exponential
moment of tau proved next pays the s integral, including j<=2.
The source is a product of entire functions, so no zero of phi_m is
introduced as a denominator in this continuation.

## 4. Strictly improve the canonical exponential-moment budget

Removing the known atom *before* bounding gives the exact identity

\[
 M_\kappa:=\int e^{\kappa s}d\tau(s)
 =2B_k+\sum_{n>k}(A_n+2B_n)e^{\kappa\delta_n}.            \tag{5}
\]

Here the exponential law contributes gamma/(gamma−kappa)=2. This is
sharper than first using p_m(−kappa)<4 from BW14 and then subtracting
the atom, which only gives M_kappa<4E_m−A.

Let

\[
 r_*=\left({k+1\over k}\right)^4
                 e^{-(1-\kappa)\pi(2k+1)}<1.
\]

The same successive-ratio argument, including B_n<=3A_n/D, proves

\[
 \boxed{M_\kappa\le \widehat M_\kappa
 :=2B_k+A(1+6/D){r_*\over1-r_*}.}                       \tag{6}
\]

This is an all-mode, not truncated, canonical estimate. Its strict
comparison with the coarse budget is uniform for m>=1. Since D>3,
B_k/A<1 and 1+6/D<3. Also 1−kappa>1/2 and k>=2 imply
r_*<(81/16)exp(−15/2)<81/2048, using pi>3 and exp(1)>2.
Consequently

\[
 \widehat M_\kappa< {4177\over1967}A<3A\le4E_m-A,
 \quad (4E_m-A)-\widehat M_\kappa>{1724\over1967}A.       \tag{7}
\]

The stronger inherited strip bound, BW14 Note 04 (12), must also be
compared. At kappa=gamma/2 it gives

\[
 \widehat M_{\rm strip}
 =A\left[{1+6/D\over1-r_*}-1\right],\qquad
 \widehat M_{\rm strip}-\widehat M_\kappa
 ={6A\alpha\over D\beta}>0.                            \tag{7a}
\]

Thus the new cap strictly improves that explicit bound as well, using
exactly the same source masses and ratio. The difference follows from
B_k=3A(beta−alpha)/(D beta). No comparison with an optimally evaluated
exact moment is claimed.

As m tends to infinity, B_k/A is asymptotic to 3/(pi m^3), while r_*
and the higher-mode mass are exponentially small. Thus
widehat(M_kappa)/c tends to **2**; the coarse (4E_m−A)/c instead grows
asymptotically like pi m^3; the stronger inherited strip budget divided
by c grows asymptotically like m. These are asymptotics of named budgets and
masses, not a measured speed or physical-error law.

## 5. A signed consumer of the moment improvement

Define ell_s(z)=Re<g,V_s>/E_F and the real scalar kernels

\[
 a_F(s)=2L[\ell_s],\qquad
 b_F(s,t)=L\left[{\operatorname{Re}\langle V_s,V_t\rangle\over E_F}
                         -2\ell_s\ell_t\right].
\]

The linear and quadratic corrections in (3) are exactly
S_1=integral a_F dchi and S_2=integral integral b_F dchi dchi.
The same chi is used twice in its product law; no arbitrary replacement
coupling or separately chosen jet extremizer is allowed. The known atom
contributes A a_F(0), A^2 b_F(0,0) and the two signed mixed terms.

For an example finite dual, take B(s)=V_s(0,a) in R^2. The event equations
give integral B d tau=b:=−jet(F)(0,a)−A B(0). One genuine reflection
constraint can be retained as a third coordinate:

\[
 D_\theta(s)={W(i\theta)e^{-s h(i\theta)}
                  -W(-i\theta)e^{-s h(-i\theta)}\over2i},
 \quad b_\theta=-{S_m(i\theta)-S_m(-i\theta)\over2i}
                    -A D_\theta(0).
\]

These are real, and integral D_theta d tau=b_theta. Let Btilde=(B,D_theta)
and btilde=(b,b_theta). For any real lambda in R^3 and zeta>0,

\[
 S_1\le A a_F(0)+\lambda\cdot\widetilde b
       +\zeta\widehat M_\kappa
       +c\sup_{s\ge0}
       [a_F(s)-\lambda\cdot\widetilde B(s)-\zeta e^{\kappa s}]. \tag{8}
\]

This is weak duality, not an assumed optimized separator. The supremum
is finite: a_F and Btilde grow at most as exp(kappa_0 s), with
kappa_0<kappa. It is generally unsafe to replace this by an unconstrained
paired-ray probability support function; that supremum can be infinite.
Full reflection was already used in (4); the added finite constraint
does not replace the full identity or enforce all canonical coefficients.

For the **same fixed** lambda,zeta, replacing the stronger inherited strip
budget by (6) lowers the linear upper certificate by exactly
zeta 6A alpha/(D beta)>0. Replacing the coarser 4E_m−A lowers it by more
than zeta A(1724/1967). The supremum and all other terms are unchanged.
This is a precise signed consumer of the source-moment improvement.
It is not a proved strict improvement of optimized endpoints: optimizing
both budgets may change the dual multipliers, and no actual optimizer
or physical 1/100 margin is acquired here. S_2 and every acquisition
error in (3) must still be included in the complete upper certificate.

## 6. The full same-data comparison is a scoped tie

For H_s=F+s R_H on the same disk, the paid path is regular for 0<=s<=1.
Green gives the exact identity for every s,

\[
 M_{\rm direct}(H_s)=\int\psi\,{|H_s(H_s)_{zz}-(H_s)_z^2|^2
                         \over\pi E_{H_s}^2}\,dA
                    =L[\log E_{H_s}].
\]

Differentiate under the compact integrals using the positive path floor.
The direct expression's coefficients of s and s^2 are exactly
L[2a_r] and L[b_r−2a_r^2]. The second derivative itself has twice the
second coefficient. Therefore the exact kernels a_F,b_F, the event and
reflection constraints, (6), and the cubic allowance all transfer to the
equally informed direct estimator.

More formally, fix any common feasible residual class C satisfying the paid
regularity and residual bounds (1)–(2), the same paid base M_psi(F), and the same cubic allowance. Both candidate estimators
give the identical upper certificate

\[
 M_\psi(F)+\sup_{R_H\in\mathcal C}\{S_1(R_H)+S_2(R_H)\}
                         +1/6480,                       \tag{9}
\]

before identical acquisition errors. Residual-dependent allowances can
be included in the same supremum. C may impose the full actual canonical
law, full reflection, event matching and any additional joint information;
the pointwise identities hold on every residual. The comparison does not
require the intermediate s sources to be canonical or event-admissible.

**Stop the superiority claim for the declared linear/quadratic shared-law
correction with the same uniform cubic payment.** It supplies no separation
from equally informed direct density evaluation. In particular (7)–(8)
improve the direct source estimator too. This is not a theorem about all
weighted estimates, different nonlinear payments, higher-order designs
or the cost of acquiring the common scalar kernels. A cost advantage
would need its own specified algorithms and full error/resource comparison;
no floating timing or source-order count proves it.
