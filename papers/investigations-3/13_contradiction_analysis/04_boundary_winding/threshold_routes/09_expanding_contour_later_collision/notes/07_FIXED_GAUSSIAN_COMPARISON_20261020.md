## 4. Why this estimator stops

The computed whole-cap polynomial interval \(I_0\) contains zero.
Its independent square range therefore has lower endpoint zero.
Even with exact arithmetic and all source/product/tail payments set to
zero, this range-only rule has upper deficit
\[
                  s\,m\,\sup_{v\in I_1}v^2\ge0.
\]
Consequently **the frozen whole-cap interval rule cannot certify a
negative deficit from these ranges**. Increasing source precision or
tightening the already negligible exterior tail does not repair this
loss of parameter and spatial correlation. This is an obstruction to
the specified information class, not a proof that the physical deficit
is nonnegative, nor an obstruction to correlated cap or source-kernel
estimates. No adaptive repair was performed.

A related exact comparison applies to a finite *independent Hermite-jet
box* estimator. If \(h_j\in I_j\), \(0\le R_{\rm tail}\le B_N\), and
\(\alpha_j=(j-2)(2s)^j/(2j!)\), put
\[
 l_j=\operatorname{dist}(0,I_j),\quad u_j=\sup|I_j|,
 \quad L=l_0^2+sl_1^2,\quad
 R_+=\sum_{j=3}^N\alpha_j u_j^2+B_N.
\]
The exact worst upper endpoint in that independent-box relaxation is
\(U=-L+R_+\). Its derived energy lower bound is
\(\max(0,L-R_+)\le L\), with loss \(\min(L,R_+)\).
This follows by independent minimization of the two negative squares
and maximization of the positive ones. Thus an independent-jet
implementation cannot outperform its strongest same-box direct
consumer. Correlated data, a different acquisition cost, and direct
cap/source-kernel estimators are outside this theorem.
