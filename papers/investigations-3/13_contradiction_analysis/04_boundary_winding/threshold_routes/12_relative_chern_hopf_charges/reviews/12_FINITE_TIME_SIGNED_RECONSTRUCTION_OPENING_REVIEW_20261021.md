## Paid window and baseline

[Note 13][n13] bounds the complete canonical tail by an elementary
geometric sum. The source inequality retains the quadratic exponential
term needed to absorb finite positive time, and its mode ratio works for
every nonnegative derivative order. The separate first-mode bound makes
the complete B2/B3 constants explicit. At a hypothetical ordinary double,
the least permitted cutoff gives both head root counts, external floor
at least 15qR²/32, and an actual residual ratio at most 1/15.

The additional R<=a/2 is valid for positive event height and guarantees
the right-half-plane kernel premise later used in Note 15. Evenness covers
negative heights; H(0,0)>0 excludes the origin. The construction still
depends on the nonzero local curvature, and is a conditional parameterized
domain, not a numerically discovered theta event. The off-event finite-jet
version is separately stated. No internal gap is assumed.

The complete direct interval includes the actual canonical law and the
cubic discriminant remainder. Its endpoints are symbolic integrals with
explicit bounds, not evaluated numerical intervals. The finite matching
time can exceed the small event time width because no root labels are
being continued to that time. The fixed-cell heat approximation returns
the same endpoint residual when reconstructed.

## Gaussian projection and the exact response quotient

[Note 14][n14] correctly normalizes the Gaussian variance as 2T and the
probabilists' Hermite polynomials. The standard generating function was
also checked against [NIST DLMF 18.12.16](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.12.E16).
The projection coefficient is (2T)^(n/2) E0^(n)(a)/sqrt(n!), so the
reconstructed polynomial is exactly the endpoint Taylor polynomial.
Complete heat-weighted source moments justify the integrations by parts,
Parseval and the source double integral for the Gaussian energy.

The projected kernel norm improves the unprojected fallback bound, but
the same weighted tail-energy datum gives exactly that bound at time
zero. The stronger comparison with the absolute full Taylor remainder
explicitly requires the full derivative information; it is not free
input to a finite-jet implementation. This is a scoped no-gain result for
the declared approximation and information, not all positive-time methods.

The contour polynomial projection modulo P² has degree at most three,
works at confluent roots and pays only external clearance. Divisibility
by P² annihilates every linear and bilinear response involving that
error. At a double the response only needs the first three jets of E/U;
the third derivative in the general quotient drops out. None of this
removes the cubic error in the full discriminant.

The ellipsoid completion-square bounds have the correct eigenvalue
conditions and signs, retain the mixed quadratic term, and allow a
singular Gram matrix. They require an actual acquired uncertainty set.
The explicit mode, source-ray and Gaussian spatial-tail formulas were
checked, including all factorials and incomplete-Gaussian normalizations.

## Canonical covariance gain and its matched scope

[Note 15][n15] compactifies the actual canonical cutoff law by x=e^-4w,
keeps its atom at zero exactly and proves a strictly positive density
floor from the first omitted exponential component. Its explicit contour
derivative bounds include the original source's lower-order term through
a conservative absolute envelope; they do not alter the source.

Cell barycentres cancel the first Taylor term. The common covariance
function e2 is evaluated in both the linear response and its mixed term
with the common residual approximation. The remainder is
L(r)+2B(barE,r)+B(e,e), with the entire final quadratic term retained.
Consequently the program's signed error endpoints are D_J±epsilon_J,
with epsilon_J<=K3 J^-3+K4 J^-4. The exact same wall ratio and cubic
remainder are used in every discriminant comparison.

The strict comparison is against **exact cell extrema** on a common
uniform x grid with a separately retained atom, not weakened sample
bounds. It does not compare fixed-J results on the earlier equal-N grid
or optimal/adaptive partitions. A fixed interior rectangle with a nonzero signed first
derivative of the response kernel gives both exact-range endpoint slacks
at least c*/J. The canonical density floor supplies its positive weight.
Thus 2epsilon_J<c*/J guarantees strict containment. The kernel-oscillation
lemma supplies such a rectangle in the right-half-plane geometry, even
when the reference roots coincide. No sign of the target is used.

The first and second cell moments are additional derived acquisitions.
They are obtained from the same complete canonical law, but their cost
and errors cannot be ignored. Note 15 gives a sufficient mesh threshold,
raw-moment accuracy schedule and final center-acquisition payment. The
review clarified that the first continuous-cell mass is P(h)-eta, with
the atom subtracted once, and that normalization must be jointly verified
with the moment-error bounds. No actual numerical g, J, endpoint interval
or complexity advantage was acquired in this round.

Gaussian reconstruction transports this entire covariance calculation
back to the same direct estimator. The gain is therefore a proved direct
canonical quadrature refinement, also expressible at finite time; it is
not evidence that positive-time information improves a competitive direct
method supplied with those same moments. At a hypothetical theta double,
the inherited head-tail cancellation remains and the valid full interval
contains zero.

## Controls and dispositions

The generic identities remain valid for colliding controls in their
admissible source classes. The exact theta mode/mass formulas are absent
from Euler-factor and reflected/primitive controls; weighted-square and
finite-prime controls lack the complete normalized reflection/coefficient
conjunction; close normalized endpoint controls fail ordinary Dirichlet
series arithmetic. Those literal differences do not prove failure of a
derived generic numerical inequality. Weighted-square variants can still
have a positive law and a covariance gain. No new exclusion is asserted,
so the calculations consistently allow zero discriminant on event domains.

The [exact reconstruction controls][script] passed 862 rational checks:
60 polynomial Gaussian transfers, 450 Hermite coefficients, 240 projected
energy/Parseval checks, 72 quotient-response checks with nonconstant units,
36 confluent three-jet checks, and four affine collision examples. A
separate agent reran and inspected the code. The [receipt][receipt] binds
the script, and its scope is finite algebra rather than theta acquisition
or formal verification. The [covariance controls][cov-script] additionally passed 96 exact
continuous-uniform-law-plus-atom cases and six strict exact-range
containment examples. Three negative checks reject omission of the mixed
correction. Their [receipt][cov-receipt] binds both script and helper.
The shared rational helper is imported rather than duplicated; a fresh
run after this packaging change passed. These finite generic controls
do not prove the canonical theorem or acquire theta data.

FTSR-01 is complete in its stated conditional scope. FTSR-02 has two
fully analyzed admissible candidates. FTSR-03 has a quantitative canonical
signed error bound for the chosen covariance approximation, plus the
separate generic quotient estimator. FTSR-04 has a strict direct-baseline
refinement, while a positive-time advantage is unestablished. FTSR-05 is
complete for these candidates. Growing-time development and endpoint/global
application retain their gates. In particular, improved quadrature is
not a positive complete lower endpoint, all-height signed control, or
higher-multiplicity exclusion. The manuscript and parent ECE/RCH statuses
are unchanged. No new snapshot was created.
