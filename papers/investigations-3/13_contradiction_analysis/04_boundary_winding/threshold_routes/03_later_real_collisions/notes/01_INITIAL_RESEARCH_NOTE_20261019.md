## Event and contradiction

Assume the ordinary double event at \(T=\Lambda>0\). Seek a later point \((t_1,a_1)\), \(t_1>T\), with \(H=H_x=0\). Any finite multiplicity at the later event suffices. Local splitting gives nonreal zeros at times arbitrarily close to t1 from below, including times still above T. This contradicts the proposed threshold. Equivalently, such an event would require \(\Lambda\ge t_1>T\); equality at t1 is allowed for this implication.

The same H must appear in both events. Differentiated, reweighted or perturbed functions require separate analysis and cannot furnish a later event of H merely because they satisfy the heat equation.

## Existence mechanisms to assess

An analytic forcing identity could link the initial cancellation to a later common zero. Alternatively, a validated existence method for the two real equations may be usable on a region wholly after T. A nonsingular interval Newton or comparable theorem would specifically produce a double event and needs all Jacobian bounds; a degree argument can include degenerate events and belongs to [BW-04](../../04_later_negative_winding/README.md).

Neither an interval box containing zero in each component nor a failed noncollision test proves a common zero. The arithmetic argument must force existence rather than leave it possible. A moving-root description must also account for wall flux, root identity, and tangencies; naming a later meeting is not a substitute for proving it.

## First bounded task and acceptance

Decide which existence theorem would consume the relation and list its precise hypotheses and complete error norms.

A useful first output is a nontrivial conditional restriction that reduces the later existence problem, or a proof that the selected relation cannot force existence. A future-event theorem must produce a real common zero with its order conditions or sufficient degree, and transfer that conclusion to the physical H. An iteration is unnecessary: one later event is enough.

## Controls and scope

The even quartic control has a positive all-real threshold and double events at that time, followed by simple real zeros. It defeats any universal assertion that an ordinary threshold event must generate a later event. The missing premise is complete theta arithmetic, not the generic threshold or heat law. The common [control screen][controls] limits other uses of source positivity and finite arithmetic compatibility.

An argument using only local root splitting cannot pass: it describes the new real roots rather than creating a second event. A failure of a selected arithmetic identity is not a universal obstruction to later-event arguments.
