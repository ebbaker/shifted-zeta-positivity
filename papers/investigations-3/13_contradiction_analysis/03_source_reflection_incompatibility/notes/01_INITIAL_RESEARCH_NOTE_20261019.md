## Hypothetical event and exact contradiction

The genuine normalized theta source satisfies full reflection and complete
arithmetic coefficient identities. In a precisely specified source class,
let \(\mathcal D(\Phi)\) denote a reflection-defect observation, with
\(\mathcal D(\Phi_\theta)=0\). The intended contradiction would show that
the physical cancellation required by a candidate collision forces a
strictly positive defect, violates an exact coefficient/dilation relation,
or lies outside the permitted range of a signed observation constrained
by these identities.

No particular defect operator or norm is selected by this opening note.
Choosing them is the first research question. A nonzero lower bound cannot
be obtained merely by renaming the collision condition as a defect.

## Existing rigidity and its limit

Within the polynomial-growth integer-comb class, the [reflection
argument][reflection] identifies theta from full normalized reflection.
The [finite-prime controls][finite-prime] show that a fixed finite prime
set, a finite coefficient prefix, and finitely many linear measurements
can survive positive source perturbations with remote collisions. Those
controls fail full reflection; their event heights are not prescribed on
a fixed compact set.

Exact uniqueness of a source is not a quantitative bound for its signed
heat transform. Approximate constraints may have a severely ill-conditioned
inverse, and ordinary absolute source closeness need not resolve a physical
quantity that becomes very small. The [arithmetic summary][arithmetic] and
[Project 11 assessment][review19] separate those issues.

## Questions for the first deeper discussion

1. Which signed observation is actually required by collision matching?
   Select it from the complete first jet, a threshold higher jet, or a
   complete exterior packet relation. Keep lower intervals and reflected
   terms. Avoid an inverse problem with no specified physical consumer.
2. Which source class and defect norm retain that observation at its
   required scale? State growth, normalization, lattice/support, cutoff,
   and physical heat weights before considering inversion.
3. Can a hierarchy of coefficient or dilation constraints grow with
   height at a controlled cost? Determine whether the necessary number,
   weights, or precision of constraints can be estimated.
4. Is direct control of the selected observation more plausible than
   reconstruction of the whole source? A weighted trace estimate may be
   useful even if a global stable inverse is unavailable.

A proposed quantitative relation must show its conditioning factor as
height increases and compare it with the scale required by the physical
consumer. An upper continuity bound alone does not contradict a collision:
it still needs a source of signed separation or an event-forced defect.

## Candidate approaches without a fixed program

One possibility is to search for an observable-level estimate from the
reflection defect and a growing finite constraint list. Another is an
inverse statement: simultaneous physical cancellation would require a
specific coefficient discrepancy that complete theta arithmetic forbids.
A third is to identify a stable finite functional that genuinely consumes
complete modular information without reconstructing every coefficient.

For each, begin by specifying the operator and testing its blind directions.
If finite-measurement controls place collisions in those directions,
determine whether height-dependent growth or a different norm removes the
obstruction. Do not infer from one failed operator that all modular
approaches are impossible. Projects 08–10 may contribute an estimate once
there is an exact observation request; their results remain in their
existing homes.

## Control screen

The finite-prime orbit construction is the principal obstruction to any
all-height argument using only a fixed finite measurement list and ordinary
closeness. Its failed premise is full normalized reflection, so it does
not disprove every effective use of that reflection. Weighted-square
controls likewise lack the full reflection/coefficient conjunction.

Reflected smooth and positive-primitive controls retain reflection and
substantial positivity but lack the normalized integer-square theta
expansion. Euler-factor modifications change coefficients, level, or
lattice. A source-class claim must distinguish both types; reflection by
itself and support by itself are each too weak. The quartic heat model
lacks this source class altogether. These are separate premise failures,
not a claim that one control satisfies every other control's hypotheses.

## Useful first results and remaining scope

A useful first result could be a quantitative growth requirement for the
measurement hierarchy, an observation-specific stability estimate with
its conditioning cost, or a scoped instability theorem that identifies a
better norm or operator. A finite-dimensional conditioning experiment
would be diagnostic only; it must specify the discretization and omitted
directions before informing a theorem.

The full event-forced defect implication for the genuine source would be
RH-strength if it covered all positive threshold events. Exact source
uniqueness is an inherited theorem, not progress toward that implication
by itself. This route is an alternative to direct retained-sum estimation;
it should stay attached to a concrete consumer. Physical transfer must
retain derivative, cutoff, normalization, boundary, and tail costs.
