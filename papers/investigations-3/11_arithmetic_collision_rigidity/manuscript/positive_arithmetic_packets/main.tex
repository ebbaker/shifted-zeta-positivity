\begin{abstract}
Finite Euler operations under the backwards heat equation retain an exact
arithmetic structure, but scalar factors become complex-shift operators,
mixed-prime coefficients change, and half-line transforms acquire boundary
corrections. Starting from the theta source, we prove that, for
$0\le t\le1/2$, every nonzero bounded nonnegative packet of modes beginning
at $m\ge2$ has positive real part throughout $|\Im z|\le\pi m^2$.
A disk-map argument then bounds its logarithmic slope and curvature:
$\beta^2+|\beta_x|\le1/(4m^4)$ on the real axis. For the arithmetic carrier
containing the second mode, this bounds its contribution to a normalized
Laguerre identity by $E/64$. The packet for primes $2,3$ approximates the
full higher-mode contribution with relative real-axis error below
$7.3\times10^{-25}$, with explicit derivative bounds and a complex-strip
relative error below $8.5\times10^{-25}$.
We derive the all-order endpoint cancellation forced by modular reflection,
then use that reflection before truncation to obtain a finite shifted-source
approximation with explicit errors for every fixed spectral derivative.
A near-boundary contour choice gives an asymptotically vanishing error
relative to the time-zero gamma envelope with $O(\sqrt{x})$ retained modes;
no effective onset or quadrature cost is asserted.
Finally, a scoped collision construction preserves any fixed finite list
of prime identities and linear measurements, including the stronger
positive-packet geometry, while failing complete source reflection.
The complete signed theta residual and a positive physical-scale margin
remain open. No theta collision exclusion or bound on the Newman constant
is claimed.
\end{abstract}
