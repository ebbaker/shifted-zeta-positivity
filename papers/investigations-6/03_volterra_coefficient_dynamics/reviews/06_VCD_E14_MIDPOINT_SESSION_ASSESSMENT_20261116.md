## E13 midpoint identity and memory

The convention \(\langle v,w\rangle=v^*w\) gives the bra
\(e_0^*e^{-i\bar zJ/2}\) for \(c(z)=e^{izJ/2}e_0\) on the spectral
operator's domain. The vector \(c(z)\) gives its bounded functional
extension. Conjugation sends \(c(z)\) to \(c(-\bar z)\) because the
polynomial basis is real. The continuous pairing \(B(v,w)=\langle
Cv,w\rangle\) integrates \((Uv)(Uw)\) and therefore yields
\(\sum_n c_n(z)^2=F_\lambda(z)=2H_\lambda(z)/Z_\lambda\).
In the real-height Hermitian formula the first state is at \(-x/2\) and
the second at \(x/2\). The real-axis parity split is an alternating sum
of real squares and the Hermitian norm is one there.

The absolute tail sum is \(\|Q_N c(z)\|^2\), with
\(\|c(z)\|^2=F_\lambda(i\operatorname{Im}z)\). Strong convergence of
\(Q_N\) to zero is uniform on each compact image of the fixed orbit by a
finite norm net. Independently \(Q_N J^p e_0=0\) for \(p<N\), so the
time-uniform logarithmic edge bound makes the midpoint exponential
Taylor majorant tail vanish uniformly on fixed disks in the full time
band. Both uses concern fixed initial vectors. The divergent moving
tail-origin bound in E04 remains applicable. The baseline finite
bilinear square is \(e_0^{\mathsf T}e^{izJ_N}e_0\) since \(J_N\) is
transpose-symmetric. The shortest excluded return walk in \(c_0\)
has length \(2N\) and contributes
\((-1)^N2^{-2N}\prod_{j=1}^N\beta_j\) to its \(2N\)-th derivative.
The scalar \(c_0(z)=F_\lambda(z/2)\) already contains full-chain data.
[E04 §§1, 4–5][e04]

Scaling the derivative and memory integration by \(1/2\) in the head
gives \(-\beta_N/4\). The tail amplitude has factor \(ia_N/2\), so its
bilinear square also has coefficient \(-\beta_N/4\).
Conjugation commutes with the real tail realization on its core.
The spectral pairing of the two tail vectors integrates their
exponentials at the summed height, giving \(k_N(z-(s+t)/2)\).
The head uses the segment through \(z/2\) and the tail square the
segment through \(z\). Vector holomorphy on the fixed orbits and
bilinear continuity justify these operations on complex segments.
The joint error estimate follows from
\(\|\mathbf c_N+\widetilde{\mathbf c}_N\|
\le 2\sqrt{F_\lambda(i\operatorname{Im}z)}+E_c\).
It yields compact approximation with convergent joint budgets.

The score interface uses \(T_N(t)e_0=\mathbf c_N(-2it)\) and
\(K_N(t)=k_N(-it)\). The score exponential terms at \(t=\pm4\)
use the full retained matrix at midpoint heights \(z=\mp8i\).
The canonical compression includes the exact correction
\(\Pi_N b(J)\Pi_N-b(J_N)\) of norm at most \(2C_b\).
Adding a site moves its exact square \(c_N^2\) between the two
terms. Degree propagation would still need a canonical quantitative
constraint, a justified initiation or limit and a signed Fourier

## E12 finite information and rank two

of the continuous symbols because the theta density is strictly
positive. For a nonzero real target combination
\(\operatorname{Re}(\alpha\cos(z_*u))\), dividing by
\(u^{\deg Q}e^{4u}\) when the \(V'Q\) term is present forces \(Q=0\).
The exponential rate \(|y|\) of the target is strictly below \(4\),
and \(e^{-4u}V'(u)\to4\pi\). The remaining constraint symbols have at
most polynomial growth times \(e^{|y|u/2}\). Along the positive peak
sequence of the sinusoid with frequency \(|x|\), the scaled target
tends to \(|\alpha|/2\). This contradicts dependence for every
\(\alpha\ne0\). The proof needs \(x\ne0\) and \(0<|y|<4\).
If the two real targets on the measurement kernel failed to span
\(\mathbb C\), a nonzero annihilating combination would factor through
the image of the measurement map and extend to its finite-dimensional
codomain. Hence the argument permits redundant measurement rows.
[E12 §1][e12], [E06 §2][e06]
