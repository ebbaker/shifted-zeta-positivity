## Source proof and directions

Proposition 3.1(i), Theorem 3.2 and the opening application of Proposition
3.3 have the same xi normalization and multiplier \(\exp(tu^2)\) as

width at \(t=0\) is one. The heat equation is \(\partial_t H_t=-H_t''\).

\(\sqrt{1-2t}\) on this slab. Conjugation supplies the other side,
and the gauge \(e^{\pi a/8}\) cancels in all logarithmic derivatives.
The generic restatement's decay hypothesis follows from the full
theta envelope. [Polymath, arXiv v2][polymath] · [Newman, Theorem 1][newman] ·
[Source record][source] · [Oracle][oracle]

The source envelope gives \(\log M_t(r)=O(r\log(2+r))\) uniformly:
absorb the polynomial exponent into half of the double-exponential
decay, reserve an integrable quarter, and maximize \(ru-(\pi/4)e^{4u}\).
Positivity gives \(H_t(0)\ge H_0(0)>0\). Jensen's estimate on \(2r\)
and dyadic summation pay \(\sum|\rho|^{-2}\). With genus-one factors,
the exponential prefactor is affine in the exponent. Both its
logarithmic derivative and the \(1/\rho\) terms cancel in the divided
difference. A zero of multiplicity \(m\) at the origin in a generic
field contributes \(m/z\) in the logarithmic derivative. It is counted
like any other occurrence as appropriate. Deflation removes both
target occurrences even at a double. The inverse-square tail is
locally uniform on the counted contour, permitting the derivative
at a double and grouping without tracking individual roots in time.

\(\tau=t-1/20\). Its squared roots \(a^2+6\tau\pm S\) have
\(S^2=8\tau(a^2+3\tau)\). The stated inequality
\(|S|^2<4a^2/9\) puts the near-\(a\) square roots within \(7/10\)
of \(a\), with the other two reflected. The center estimate
\(79/(200a)<7/100\) and
\(|D|=|S^2|/|z_1+z_2|^2<100/729<1/5\) follow. All roots lie within
the strip because \(7/10<\sqrt{1-2t}\) on the slab. The order-zero
field has a nondegenerate double at \(t=1/20\), and \(D\) has the
sign of \(\tau\). Its only exterior group is the reflected pair, so
\(K<0\) and the incoming margin across the event equals
\(W(u)D(u)<0\). The quartic is a polynomial heat field, with real-axis
growth excluding a Fourier representation by a finite positive
measure. The earlier shifted quadratic lacks the global evenness
used for the reflected group. Note18's nonzero Gaussian factors
give order two, allow a quadratic exponential in Hadamard
factorization and contribute \(2\kappa/s\) to the divided difference
without exterior zeros. [Note21 §4][proof] · [Note18][gaussian]
