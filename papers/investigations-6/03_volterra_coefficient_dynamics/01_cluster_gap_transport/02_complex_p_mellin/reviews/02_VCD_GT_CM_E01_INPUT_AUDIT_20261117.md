## Normalization and contour audit

Freeze \(T\ge64\), \(L=\log T\), \(\ell=L^{-1}\), \(c\in[T,2T]\),
\(r_0=a\ell\), and \(\max(2,4\sqrt d)\le a\le L\).
The two targets obey \(|z-c|\le r_0/4\), and every exterior occurrence
obeys \(|w_j|\ge r_0\). The bound \(|D|\le d\ell^2\le r_0^2/16\)
gives \(|a_j|\ge(15/16)|w_j|^2\).
Set \(m=\ell^2M\), \(\delta=D/\ell^2\), \(\sigma=(t-t_0)/\ell^2\),
\(\mu_j=\ell^2/a_j\) and \(Z(p,t)=\ell^{2p}S(p,t)\) with real \(\log\ell\).
At integer \(n\), \(Z(n,t)=\sum_j\mu_j^n=\ell^{2n}S_n\).
With the scale fixed,
\[
 \delta_\sigma=2-4m\delta,\qquad
 m_\sigma=\ell^4 M'=2m^2-F,\qquad
 F=12Z(2,t)+16\delta Z(3,t)=\ell^4(12S_2+16DS_3).
\]
The integer sums are real by conjugation. Their signed combination needs
an **upper** bound, which gives a lower differential bound for \(m\).

For a positively oriented compact simple contour \(C\), with boundary
and interior in \(\Re p>1/2\), both \(2,3\) strictly inside, and a fixed
slice branch prescription, Cauchy's formula gives
\[
 F=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_C
       \left(\frac{12}{p-2}+\frac{16\delta}{p-3}\right)Z(p,t)\,dp,
\qquad M'=2M^2-\ell^{-4}F.
\]
The residues are \(12Z(2,t)\) and \(16\delta Z(3,t)\).
At \(\delta=0\) the \(p=3\) pole is removable. On a separate positively
oriented contour \(C_1\) inside the same half-plane, enclosing \(1\),
\[
 m=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_1}\frac{Z(p,t)}{p-1}\,dp .
\]
The first kernel has no \(p=1\) pole. Writing \(2m^2\) in integral form
would require the square of the full \(C_1\) integral. Both Cauchy
evaluations are exact representations. Their proofs use no time
differentiation of \(Z\) or vertical limit.

The direct comparator in Note41 uses
\(B_n=14C_{\rm u}(16/15)^n/a^{2n}\), with \(|m|\le B_1L\)
and \(C_F=12B_2+16dB_3\). For scaled \(x\le y\),
\[
 W(x,y)=\exp\!\left(-4\int_x^y m(u)\,du\right)>0,\qquad
 W(x,y)\delta(x)=\delta(y)-2\int_x^y W(u,y)\,du .
\]
Here \(m\,d\sigma=M\,dt\) and the physical gap cost is \(\ell^2\)
times the scaled cost. Equality of terminal gap and exact cost yields
\(\delta(x)=0\), including at \(x=0\). An integrated improvement has to
cover the complete \(W\) or this cost on the same paid interval.

## Convergence, sectors and exact import conditions

- On the real tube the strip \(|\Im w|\le1\) and \(|D|\le1/16\)
  give \(\Re a_j=(\Re w_j)^2-(\Im w_j)^2-D\gg(\Re w_j)^2\)
  for \(|\Re w_j|\ge2\). Use the principal tail logarithms there.
  On horizontal shells \(n\le|\Re w_j|<n+1\) the count is
  \(O(C_{\rm u}(L+\log(n+1)))\). For a compact \(p\)-set with
  \(\alpha=\min\Re p>1/2\), the cutoff at shell \(N\ge2\)
  has absolute tail \(O(C_{\rm u}(L+\log N)N^{1-2\alpha})\) for
  \(S\), with constant depending on the compact set. This follows by
  summing \(n^{-2\alpha}(L+\log(n+1))\) and is uniform in real time
  on the paid tube. For \(Z\) the tail bound includes \(\ell^{2\alpha}\).
  For canonical modulus truncations the tails also vanish uniformly
  at fixed \(T\), by taking \(N\) beyond the height band and translating
  the strip/count bounds. Include each multiplicity and retain complete
  tied groups when pairing. Compact \(p\) bounds suffice to interchange
  the finite \(p\) contours and the sum. They leave unbounded \(\Im p\),
  continuation through \(\Re p=1/2\), and growing order as separate tasks.

- Locally at a finite exterior cluster choose a small fixed zero-free
  spatial circle enclosing that cluster alone and a common holomorphic
  logarithm of \((z-c(t))^2-D(t)\) on its enclosed neighborhood.
  The collar and shrinking the chart allow this near the cluster at
  the reference time. The integral of that holomorphic power times
  \(H_z/H\) counts the branch-compatible cluster trace. The integral
  is locally analytic, even at a merger of its counted occurrences.
  A global chart law requires agreement on overlaps or explicit branch
  corrections. Switching \(\operatorname{Log}a\) by \(2\pi i k\)
  multiplies the old occurrence term by \(e^{-2\pi i k p}\).
  A real negative denominator has possible arguments \(\pi,-\pi\),
  and other \(2\pi\)-shifts. Conjugacy requires tracking those choices
  and singleton multiplicities. No global conjugation, jump or
  complete time-derivative claim is inherited from the local trace.

- [DLMF 5.9.1][gamma] with its exponent parameter set to one gives
  \(\Gamma(p)a^{-p}=\int_0^\infty s^{p-1}e^{-sa}\,ds\) for
  \(\Re p>0,\Re a>0\) with principal powers. To sum over a class of
  denominators on this ray the absolute-interchange check is
  \[
   \int_0^\infty s^{u-1}\sum_j |e^{-sa_j}|\,ds
    =\Gamma(u)\sum_j(\Re a_j)^{-u}<\infty,\qquad u=\Re p .
  \]
  The far count and sector pay this for \(u>1/2\). For the retained
  near terms require positive real parts at a slice and a uniform
  positive lower bound for this argument on a compact tube, with
  principal branches of all included powers. The collar permits
  adverse near sectors. Keep their actual powers outside the integral,
  or justify a different ray with \(\Re(e^{i\theta}a)>0\), the phase
  of the ray and matching logarithms. This gamma formula integrates
  in the scale \(s\).

- [DLMF §2.5(i), 2.5.1–2.5.7][mellin] requires a convergent transform
  of the chosen locally integrable function, an inversion line within
  its strip and justified inversion for that function. The convolution
  formula uses a common strip for its two factors and an allowed
  interchange. For a shift one needs meromorphic continuation into the
  swept strip, permissible deformation, all crossed residues, and the
  surviving line integral. A finite rectangle followed by a height
  limit separately owes the top and bottom edges. A bare \(s^{-\beta}\)
  on \((0,\infty)\) requires \(\Re p>\Re\beta\) at zero and
  \(\Re p<\Re\beta\) at infinity. It supplies no ordinary Mellin strip.
  A regularization needs its own transform, reconstruction and correction.

- [DLMF 5.11.9][stirling] gives
  \(|\Gamma(u+iv)|\sim\sqrt{2\pi}|v|^{u-1/2}e^{-\pi|v|/2}\)
  as \(v\to\pm\infty\), uniformly for bounded real \(u\).
  This supplies an eventual bound on bounded strips. Any specific
  finite threshold or constant used needs its own payment, and growing
  \(u\) is outside this import. The weight of one power is
  \(|a|^{-u}e^{v\arg a}\) with the chosen argument. For one gamma
  factor and one such power, a sector strictly inside
  \(|\arg a|<\pi/2\) gives angular slack for both directions.
  On the boundary the exponential may cancel in one direction and
  remaining polynomial integrability depends on the full kernel.
  An adverse branch can produce exponential growth. The branch terms,
  entire kernel and the full deformation must be estimated jointly.

## Documentary premise screen and disposition

The screen below tests inputs of the inherited results and identifies
what the listed controls still permit. The Cauchy and count-based
convergence identities make no collision exclusion. No control
certificate was rerun. [Controls12][controls] · [Note41 §6][n41] ·

| Control | Actual failed or unpaid premise |
| --- | --- |
| Quadratic and quartic heat fields | Lack the canonical growing divisor. They satisfy the local moment and compact contour identities. The isolated quartic collision also fails the positive backward-margin test on an interval across its event. |
| Euler-factor translates | The added factor changes ordinary theta arithmetic and adds a linear projected counting contribution, violating the canonical discrepancy. A coarse unit-count upper bound of the form used for convergence need not distinguish them. |
| Weighted-square, finite-source, finite-score and finite-prime controls | Full reflected unit-weight expansion fails. The documented endpoint-defective subclasses also fail the global fixed strip. Finite calibrated measurements or prime identities do not pay those hypotheses. |
| Reflected super-Gaussian | Fails the order-at-most-one product premise. Failure of that premise does not itself test convergence of its complex-power series. |
| Smooth shape and positive-primitive controls | Exact theta coefficients change. The complete canonical strip, count and safe-line inputs have no recorded transfer solely from their shape, primitive positivity or endpoint matches. |
| Atomic or finite-profile threshold controls | Lack the canonical growing count and full-profile growth; the fixed-event examples also lack eventual simplicity at their threshold. A finite list of matched measurements leaves those properties untested. |
| RH-conditional endpoint mergers | Change ordinary Dirichlet arithmetic and assume RH. The local tube law includes \(D=0\) and the nonnegative endpoint objective permits it. |
| Changed observation or fractional profile | Fail respectively the physical cosine/Newman readout or the integer-occurrence premise. Complex exponents preserve occurrence multiplicities. |
| GR changed static slices | Retain coarse strip/count and broad-moment bounds with controlled enlargements yet allow linear forcing at negative interaction. They change the source and provide no paid canonical common-time tube. |
| GR scalar equilibrium | Satisfies the displayed scalar relations and envelopes, with no realized divisor or full time law and a negative terminal anchor. The scoped obstruction concerns that relaxation. |

compact-contour evaluations can be used within their paid domains.

for any noninteger evolution claim, a convergent kernel and all edges
for an unbounded contour, and a quantitative premise for any proposed
signed gain. A canonical source label cannot supply a missing quantitative
discriminator. The inherited finite polynomial, changed-slice and scalar
obstructions retain their stated classes. No scientific direction was
selected by this audit.
