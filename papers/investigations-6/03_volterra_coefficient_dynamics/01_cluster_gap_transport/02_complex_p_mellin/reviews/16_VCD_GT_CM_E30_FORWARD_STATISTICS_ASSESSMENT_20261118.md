## 4. Active minimum, edges and lower-tail transform

Accept. At an edge minimizing all N-1 internal gaps, the mth retained
node on either side has the cumulative distances used in the reciprocal
bound. The positive real internal sums telescope to
`g^-2(2-1/i-1/(N-i))`. Multiplication by `4D=g^2` in the pair law leaves
`1/i+1/(N-i)-4Cq`. Its minimum occurs at the central edge(s), giving
`a_N=N/[floor(N/2)ceil(N/2)]`.

The minimum of finitely many C1 gap-squares is locally Lipschitz; its
almost-everywhere derivative is furnished by active indices. Because
each active derivative satisfies the same inequality, ties and changes
of minimizing index do not invalidate the integrating-factor bound.
The C=0 limit is correct. The deterministic monotone transform implies
the displayed probability inclusion for a fixed finite block-index
population, without independence or random-matrix assumptions.

The boundary restriction is substantive and correctly stated. Taking a
minimum only over interior eligible edges would not show that excluded
boundary gaps are at least that minimum, so it would not justify the
distance bound. The proof uses every internal edge, including both ends,
and requires the exterior bound there too. The observation window's
12-unit margin cannot be silently assigned to outer census nodes.
Different N or C require different transforms; nonzero averaged failure
rates do not establish zero exceptions.

The exterior hypothesis is an upper bound on the complete signed
interaction. A paid |R_z| bound along each real joining segment is
sufficient by the fundamental theorem of calculus. No positivity of
the nonreal exterior is assumed. It supplies no automatic lower bound
for the backward interaction, and it has not been acquired here.

For the sharper consumer, direct differentiation gives
