## What transfers to the current pair

The nonconstant centered invariant family in Section 2 starts at degree
three, and its C4 requires N≥4. The current parent source record counts
**two** roots. A new quartet cannot be substituted without acquiring its
own count, geometry, source and consumer. For N=2 the centered clock D is
not an isolated first integral: D'=2 in the isolated system. Consequently
`thm:general-flux` cannot simply be invoked with J=D.

Its cancellation argument nevertheless transfers after keeping that
constant internal derivative. For a counted pair x±=c±s, D=s², define

    R=B−Qz/Q,  Q=(z−c)²−D,  b=Rz(c),  Z=z−c,
    <f>=(1/(2πi)) ∮Γ f(z) dz.

Subtracting the simple-root velocities gives

    D'=2+2s[R(c+s)−R(c−s)].

Cauchy's complete-exterior formula then gives the exact identity

    D'=2+4bD+E_D,
    E_D=4D² < B/[Z²(Z²−D)] >.                         (A)

Indeed R(c+s)−R(c−s)=2s <B/(Z²−D)> and b=<B/Z²>.
Subtracting these two rational kernels yields (A). The expression is
symmetric and regular through D=0 while the exterior contour remains
cleared. It is the pair version of the manuscript's affine subtraction,
with an explicitly retained internal clock.

If |Rzzz|≤C3 on both segments from c to c±s, the third-order Taylor
remainders give

    |E_D| ≤ (2/3) C3 |D|².                              (B)

The even quadratic Taylor term cancels between the two roots. This is
stronger in the small pair radius than a separate second-derivative
Taylor allowance. It still needs the displayed bound on the **complete**
exterior, not merely a near-root cutoff.

On the exact circle |Z|=A with |D|<A², a uniform error
supΓ |B−Bhat|≤ε contributes at most

    B_res=4|D|² ε/[A(A²−|D|)]                           (C)

to the residual in (A), at exact fixed coordinates. This follows from
|Z²−D|≥A²−|D| and the circle length. It is a valid intermediate allowance;
no actual new B-error or C3 bound has been acquired here.

### A precise total-error screen

The smaller residual alone is not a net gain. The uniform-B error in b
has allowance ε/A. If that error is treated separately, the affine term
4bD costs

    B_aff=4|D| ε/A.

Direct evaluation of the entire exterior contribution
4D <B/(Z²−D)> has the Cauchy allowance

    B_direct=4A|D| ε/(A²−|D|).

These displayed allowances satisfy the **exact equality**

    B_aff+B_res=B_direct.                              (D)

Thus affine subtraction followed by these independent triangle bounds
does not improve this particular total source-error allowance. This is a
screen for the stated exact-coordinate, single-circle, uniform-B error
accounting class. It is not a sharp-feasible-set theorem, an obstruction
to an integrated affine method, or a general failure of conservation tools.
Shared field errors, a separately stronger b enclosure, or additional
whole-time information could change the comparison; those inputs must be
specified and paid before a numerical campaign.

In the real pair setting, a time-integrated use of (A) also requires the
integrating factor μ=exp(−4∫b), both endpoint/source correlations, coordinate
uncertainty, contour quadrature and between-time errors. Its exact balance
is (μD)'=μ(2+E_D). Ignoring b's uncertainty merely relocates the error.
A direct method is entitled to the same centered kernels and source data.

The parent's acquired errors are presently in its complete field and
contour logarithms, whereas the child Section 7 assumes a uniform B error.
Those are different input norms. Differentiation or a logarithmic
integration-by-parts transfer would need its own clearance/branch/error
payment. Neither the child's radius factor nor (C) can be multiplied into
Record19's log-box width as though the input errors were identical.

## Collision screen and continuation decision

All local heat-flow controls preserve (A). In particular an isolated
quadratic has R=b=E_D=0 and D'=2 through its zero-gap event. The shifted
quartic retains C4>0 at its double zeros. A positive invariant or a small
affine residual therefore cannot itself be a collision-exclusion premise.

screen; its historical certificates were not rerun in this pass:

| Weighted-square sources | Full reflected, normalized unit-weight theta source equality; square support and finitely many endpoint equalities are insufficient. |
| Reflected super-Gaussian | Literal theta arithmetic and the canonical order-one global-product premise; the local Cauchy/flux identities themselves remain valid. |
