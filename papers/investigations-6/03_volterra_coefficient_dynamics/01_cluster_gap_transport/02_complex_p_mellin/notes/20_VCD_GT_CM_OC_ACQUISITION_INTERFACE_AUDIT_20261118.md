## 1. What OC actually established

The [OC marginal clarification][ocvoid], sections “Integrating out all but
two particles” and “Montgomery's F and the pair density are different
representations”, distinguishes a deterministic factorial marginal from a
nearest-neighbor law. Its precise warning is: “Random labeling supplies no
extra estimate.” The distinct-label measure retains m(m−1) terms at an
m-fold zero. Three consecutive gaps use four labels **and** absence of
intermediate labels. An unrestricted four-point measure alone is not that
adjacency-conditioned statistic.

[OC Proof17][ocproof], accepted unchanged in [OC Review14][ocaudit], proves
conditional transport for a full marked Gaussian pair at each fixed finite
T≥10, X uniform on [T,2T], ell=min(1,4π/log T), epsilon=ell/4,
J=ell², and observation square Q. Parameters are frozen in Newman time.
Its time allowance is

    ||Rbar_2(lambda) − Rhat_2(mu)||_(infinity,Q²)
       ≤ e0 + L2 |lambda−mu|,

where the initial enclosure e0 is independently supplied. For the connected
function the corresponding constant is LC. The exact formulas are OC
Proof17 (1); in this blur regime L2,LC=O_A((log T)^5). A is a finite inherited
canonical count/field constant, **not a numerically assigned constant**.
These are upper allowances, not lower bounds on actual variation, complexity
claims, or impossibility results for other evolution estimates. The exact
quadratic collision calculation in Proof17 §5 shows compatibility with a
collision.

Its full exterior difference remainder is paid, for integer M≥2, by

    e_M(X)=10 A [log(2+X)+log(M+1)+1]/(M−1).

With L=log(2+2T), m_*=2AL and e_M^* the corresponding uniform value,
Proof17 (5) gives

    B_(2,M)=(4R_*m_*a1+4m_*a0a1)e_M^*,
    B_(C,M)=(8R_*m_*a1+4m_*a0a1)e_M^*.

Multiplying these by the elapsed time pays the derivative truncation error.
The safe-line logarithmic derivative, exact divisor and undifferentiated
weights remain; these are not errors for evolving a truncated divisor.
No replacement of that operation is authorized by this interface.

Later OC work did acquire a canonical **finite scalar**, so the earlier
statement “initial profile unacquired” must not be read as “no statistic
was ever evaluated.” The [current OC register][ocregister] records E13 r2
as “Complete with certified finite projection”. Its P_1000 is at time zero,
with fixed Gaussian blur and both imaginary marks, based on the complete
388-cell census in (988,2012)+i(−3/2,3/2). [OC Note22][occells] records
horizontal cell widths ≤5/32768, imaginary widths ≤15/131072 and the exact
minimum separation of successive horizontal cells 5085/8192>1/2. The
returned projection radius is about 0.000020585 at its inherited winding
certificate scope. That one scalar and that finite census do not supply
any law at a chosen positive starting time, a varying-height rate, a
three-gap/void law, or global reality. This audit reads the recorded
certificate scope; it does not replay the source arithmetic.

## 2. Candidate A: neighbor/void acquisition

The concrete consumer is CM Note17's bad-block count

    B_Delta = #{k : min_(j in J_k) R_j(Delta) ≤ 0},
    R_j(Delta)=D_j(0)(1+2 b_j(0) Delta)−2 Delta.

For K uniformly sampled finite buffered blocks, zero exceptions follow
from a paid probability upper bound **strictly below 1/K**. All eligible
pair labels must be covered, including pairs crossing a block edge. A
probability merely tending to zero, or a small positive limiting value,
does not meet this integer threshold. For continuous random centers, an
additional positive visibility measure per exceptional pair is needed;
there is no universal replacement of 1/K without defining that measure.

The unconditioned pair count C_k(r) of all distinct unordered label pairs
with distance ≤r gives the valid one-sided inequality

    P(minimum eligible gap ≤ r) ≤ E C_X(r),

provided the counted comparison population covers every eligible pair.
This does not require a void law. But the favorable three-gap score depends
on the two exterior adjacent gaps. Exact recovery from a correlation
measure requires adjacency/void information or a separately proved majorant
that avoids recovering it. Neither randomizing labels nor assigning CUE,
GUE, sine-kernel or independence laws supplies that missing canonical input.

Resolution cannot be silently removed. If a sharp event is replaced by a
nonnegative C² majorant with transition width eta, its first and second
physical derivatives normally have eta^−1 and eta^−2 scale. The existing
OC compact-test calculation charges those derivatives. A majorant incurs
an excess count in its transition region; identifying that excess with a
small number requires a separate canonical estimate. A fixed Gaussian
blur is not such an estimate. Changing epsilon/ell from the frozen 1/4
requires rederiving its constants and the observation transfer, and no
fixed Q² sup-norm result evaluates an integral over the entire observation
plane without its observation-tail payment.

The later projected OC source contraction does keep an explicit ledger.
[OC Note19][ocsource] (11)–(14), for its own fixed Gaussian observable, has

    E_space ≤ 2 c_G B b_R,
    E_freq ≤ c_G B² erfc(3 ell K_f/4),
    E_source ≤ c_G(2B d+d²+e),
    d=g E_R, e=v E_R,
    E_joint = c_G [integral_(-K_f)^(K_f) rho_ell |Mhat−Mtilde|
                  + |Shat−Stilde|].

K_f here is a frequency cutoff, distinct from the number of sampled
blocks. E_R is a paid local L¹ log-field error; both theta-source tails,
anchors and approximation errors enter its acquisition. The entire
factorial self term is retained, even if the frequency integral is cut.
The spatial tail b_R has an explicit Gaussian-decay bound in Note19 (12).
The center factor I_T(m) retains both interval edges. The note explicitly
states that no contraction estimate was acquired in that r1 pass; later
E13 evaluated its finite scalar through complete root cells. This ledger
cannot be reassigned numerically to a sharp nearest-neighbor event.

**Audit disposition A:** the inspected OC results supply complete
observation/transfer interfaces and a finite fixed-blur census benchmark,
not the required high-height three-gap lower tail. They give no existing
paid error total below 1/K for the CM bad-block event. This is a statement
about these inspected inputs and this consumer, not an exclusion of
source-specific void methods or certified finite gap data.

## 3. Candidate B: complete interaction conditioned on a small gap

For an adjacent real pair a<b, g=b−a and D=g²/4, the complete initial
interaction is the positive sum

    M = sum_(x outside {a,b}) 1/[(x−a)(x−b)].

A certified finite exterior subsum is already a valid lower bound: omitted
terms are positive. There is no tail subtraction to make that lower bound
valid, although omission can destroy usefulness. Conversely, a two-sided
approximation or an asymptotic assertion about the **complete M** must
retain a bound for the omitted terms. The parent uniform local counts and
reciprocal-square summability are the relevant interface. The OC exterior
**velocity-difference** remainder above is not literally this scalar M;
its constants must not be copied without the corresponding denominator
comparison.

Conditioning on D≤d is substantive. Counts and bounds for unconditioned
moments, or separate positive estimates of D and M, do not determine the
lower tail of D(1+2M Delta)−2Delta. A same-pair joint acquisition is needed.
The fixed-xi superzeta representations and the OC joint-log identities do
not by themselves estimate that quantity at a heat-deformed positive time.

There is also a concrete limitation of the current **initial-value
Riccati consumer**, even with perfectly favorable complete information.
Take the exact two-sided equally spaced divisor gZ, and the pair 0,g.
The first n exterior labels on each side contribute

    b_n = (2/g²) sum_(k=1)^n 1/[k(k+1)]
        = 2n/[(n+1)g²].

The sum telescopes. Hence D b_n=n/[2(n+1)] and CM Note17's strict
comparison permits precisely

    Delta < g²(n+1)/[4(n+2)].

For n=1 this is g²/6. In the complete limit M=2/g², DM=1/2 and

    Delta < g²/4.

This is a factor-two improvement over the gap-only g²/8, but it remains
quadratic in g. It does not give a fixed positive Delta as g tends to zero.
The failure is in this chosen comparison allowance: the exact family

    H(t,z)=exp((pi/g)² t) sin(pi z/g)

satisfies H_t=−H_zz and its divisor is stationary. It has D_u=−2+4DM=0,
whereas the Riccati lower envelope discards positive moment terms and
allows M to fall. This example is a complete, noncanonical heat field;
it has no canonical theta order/count asymptotic and is not evidence of
an obstruction to actual theta descent.

The calculation is proved here by telescoping and direct differentiation,
with no sampled numerical support. A canonical high-height application
requires the independent source audit and a complete-tail argument; no
such application is asserted by this helper note. It is a suitable
bounded consumer-check input for a separately frozen E proof.

**Audit disposition B:** the existing source identities and OC estimates
have not acquired the small-gap-conditioned interaction law. Moreover,
complete initial interaction alone need not make this scalar consumer
height-uniform. Time-dependent canonical joint estimates retaining the
positive moment terms, or an independently paid uniform high-height
real/simple theorem, are distinct possible inputs outside this limitation.

## 4. Boundary, source and control screen

All-zero coverage and a common first-loss argument remain necessary;
protection of some blocks does not allow positivity to continue past a
different unprotected collision. Strict isolation is enough for CM's local
identity. It does not import the earlier strong-collar constants. E16's
common-time finite-product approximation remains separate and unexecuted
by this advisory.

The [collision control inventory][controls] is replayed analytically at
premise level, using the stated witness scopes. Numerical collision
certificates are not rerun and are not evidence for a favorable tail.

| Control class | Missing premise for canonical acquisition; what survives |
| --- | --- |
| Euler-factor | Unchanged theta coefficients and completion fail; its paid labelled divisor still obeys factorial counting and local heat identities. |
| Weighted-square and finite theta-tail | Full normalized reflection/complete unit coefficients fail; finite endpoint matches do not transfer the canonical count package. The finite-source endpoint obstruction also prevents silently assigning an all-real start. |
| Finite-prime orbit | Full reflection and all-coefficient identity fail despite finite prime constraints; no uniform canonical small-gap law follows. |
| Reflected super-Gaussian | Full integer-square theta expansion and canonical order/count input fail; positivity and reflection do not supply the needed joint statistic. |
| Smooth shape/positive primitive | Canonical arithmetic expansion and its strip/count transfer are unpaid; the generic observation identities can coexist with collision. |
| Atomic/exact finite-profile | Complete theta population, growth and profile fail; a finite list of matching observables is not the needed joint law. Multiplicities remain. |
| RH-conditional theta-close endpoint | Ordinary Dirichlet-series arithmetic fails, and RH remains its conditional premise; it supplies no unconditional canonical tail. |
| Variable observation kernel | Literal physical cosine/Newman evolution fails; this differs from smoothing an observation of the unchanged field. |
| Fractional relaxation | Integer labelled-divisor realizability fails; a fractional exceptional mass cannot invoke integer exclusion. |
| Static surgery/scalar controls | A common canonical heat evolution is missing; static counts alone do not supply its time-dependent environment. |
| Exact lattice used above | Complete theta arithmetic and its logarithmic count asymptotic fail; the example checks only the existing scalar consumer. |

A missing canonical premise in a control is not itself a proved theta
inequality. The quantitative return here is the exact lattice allowance
and the paid/unpaid interface audit. No new Lambda upper bound, canonical
three-gap law, independence assumption or universal no-go statement is
claimed. Further claims require the lead's frozen proof and nonauthor
assessment, not enlargement of a numerical campaign.
