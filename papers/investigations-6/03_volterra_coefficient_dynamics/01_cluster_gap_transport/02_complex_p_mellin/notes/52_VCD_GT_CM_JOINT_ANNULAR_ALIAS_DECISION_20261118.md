## 2. Candidate one: joint affine support is the direct support

For Z=z-c and Q=Z^2-D, Note44's two kernels obey

    K_aff=4D/Z^2,   K_res=4D^2/(Z^2 Q),
    K_aff+K_res=K_dir=4D/Q,
    D'=2+<B K_dir>,    B=H_z/H.                            (5)

For any identical joint admissible source-error set U, the support of
K_aff+K_res on U is exactly the support of K_dir. This follows from
pointwise equality of the linear functionals before taking their suprema;
U may include analytic, time and linear correlations. For uncertain c,D,
the identity first holds for each admissible (B,c,D), then for the same
joint set. Separating coordinates on one side would change the information.

Likewise, an exact integrating-factor reformulation of the full source
path gives the same endpoint functional. A derivative-width reduction
alone says nothing about the endpoint width without its anchor and full
time payment. Splitting and recombining this flux supplies no advantage
over an equally informed direct estimator. This is a representation-level
screen, not sharpness of Record22 and not an exclusion of new joint-time
estimates or independent information about the affine coefficient.

## 3. Candidate two: keep the annular aliases in one functional

Let T_N f be the N-node mean on |w|=rho and T_0 f the exact angular mean.
For f_h(w)=(w^2-hw)L(w), Laurent orthogonality gives

    (T_N-T_0)f_h
      = sum_(m != 0) (l_(mN-2)-h l_(mN-1)) rho^(mN).       (6)

Up to overall sign and real part, this is precisely eta2/2-h eta1 in
Note42's centered discriminant identity. Both factors k=1,2 in its moment
normalization are accounted for by p2/2 and h p1. There is no missing
factor of two. The constant centering kappa contributes no aliases because
N>2. Conjugation reduces storage to 65 nodes; (6) uses the full 128-node
mean and does not change the symmetry weights.

On |w|=r, Parseval yields

    sum_n |l_n|^2 r^(2n)
      = (1/(2pi)) integral |L(r exp(i theta))|^2 dtheta
      <= M_r^2.                                          (7)

Put q_-=(r_-/rho)^N and q_+=(rho/r_+)^N, both in (0,1).
Use the outer boundary for m>=1 in (6). Its two coefficient ladders have
weights r_+^2 q_+^m and -h r_+ q_+^m in the normalized sequence l_n r_+^n.
They are disjoint because N=128>2. Their squared norm is

    (r_+^4+h^2 r_+^2) q_+^2/(1-q_+^2).

For m<=-1 use the inner boundary; its weights are r_-^2 q_-^|m| and
-h r_- q_-^|m|, with the same disjointness and squared-norm calculation.
Cauchy--Schwarz and then a triangle bound between the two boundaries give

    |eta2/2-h eta1| <= A_joint,
    A_joint = sum_(sigma=+,-) M_sigma r_sigma
              sqrt(r_sigma^2+h^2) q_sigma/sqrt(1-q_sigma^2).       (8)

No independence of these Fourier coefficients is asserted. Their common
boundary L2 constraint is exactly the information retained. The two
boundary contributions are still bounded separately; the resulting
bound need not be sharp for the single analytic function.

For comparison define the unrounded previous allowance

    A_raw = sum_(sigma=+,-) M_sigma r_sigma(r_sigma+|h|)
                         q_sigma/(1-q_sigma).             (9)

The implementation rounds the positive tail weights and moment errors
upward, so A_raw<=A from (3). This distinction matters: stored epsilons
are allowances, not exact errors or independent analytic coefficients.

## 4. A strict total gain before numerical implementation

The exact inherited h satisfies 63/1000<h<8/125. Hence x=|h|/r_sigma
satisfies 3/50<x<1 on both circles. The function
sqrt(1+x^2)/(1+x) decreases on [0,1]. Consequently

    sqrt(1+x^2)/(1+x) <= sqrt(2509)/53 < 19/20,             (10)

where the last inequality is the integer comparison
400*2509 < 361*2809. Also (1-q)/sqrt(1-q^2)<1 for 0<q<1.
Comparing each positive summand of (8),(9) proves

    A_joint < (19/20) A_raw <= (19/20) A.                  (11)

Thus one may conservatively replace only A in (4) by 19A/20.
Each endpoint moves inward by A/20, leaving the complete square and
every source, time, coordinate and nominal-arithmetic payment unchanged.
The reduction is A/10 in the full width, not merely a saving in a
selected component. A direct estimator given these same analytic
constraints receives exactly this improvement.

For a fully reproducible rational implementation, let L_old^- and
U_old^+ be the lower/upper endpoints of the serialized enclosures of
Record22's already outward endpoints. Parse its epsilon1 and epsilon2
enclosures exactly, and let A_lo be their positive lower combination
epsilon2_lo/2+|h|epsilon1_lo. Then the following is a valid new interval:

    [L_old^-+A_lo/20, U_old^+-A_lo/20].                    (12)

Indeed A_lo<=A, so the inward displacement is no larger than the proved
one. Parsing widens the original interval outward and retains all its
previous rounding. Equation (12) requires neither re-solving the source
nor relying on rounded display endpoints. Use exact rational arithmetic;
any display rounding must be separately outward.

The inherited serialized bounds imply the deliberately loose rational
inequalities

    2 A_lo > 111/10^10,
    U_old^+-L_old^- < 23/10^9.                             (13)

Therefore the width saved by (12) exceeds 5.55e-10, whereas 2.4% of
the old parsed width is below 5.52e-10. The saving is strictly greater
than **2.4%**, with a 3e-12 margin in this coarse proof. This includes
the stored full-square payment (about 3.37e-17) and node-log contribution
(about 1.1870e-8), neither of which is removed. E43 checks the exact
rational premises in (10),(13); E44 will produce the new finite interval
and test the more conservative **2%** gate. No new endpoint or runtime
measurement is reported here.

This is a modest refinement of the existing direct relaxation. It is not
a second tenfold improvement, a recursive convergence rate, superiority

## 5. Collision controls and the failed premises

The alias estimate is a generic analytic inequality, not a collision
detector. It is valid for controls satisfying its annular/log premises.
It does not assert that every catalog control fails such premises.
At an exact quadratic merger, p1=2b, p2=2b^2 and
J_h=(b-h)^2: retaining the full square gives D=0. For the physical local
flow (w-b)^2-2(t-t_*), D'=2 through the collision. The quartet's C4
positivity likewise cannot replace D or exclude its double roots.

The numerical factor 19/20 additionally uses the displayed h/r condition.
It must not be applied unconditionally to Record22's zero-centered
quadratic control. E44 must check that condition and preserve the
inherited control enclosure when it fails. Retaining that old control
is a scope guard, not a fresh negative experiment or independent source
validation. An exact shifted quadratic can satisfy the h/r condition
and still collide; sound (8) then continues to enclose its exact D=0.

For the [control catalog][controls], the actual application-specific
premise is the unchanged normalized theta source plus its fixed paid
annular/time certificate. Euler-factor translates change that source and
completion; weighted-square and finite-prime perturbations fail its full
reflected unit-weight source; endpoint-defective subclasses also cannot
inherit the canonical strip. Reflected super-Gaussians do not have the
literal arithmetic/order-one canonical source. Smooth shape, positive
primitive, atomic and finite-profile controls lack the unchanged source
identification: this does NOT prove that they violate the particular
numerical inequality (8). Kernel-varied profiles fail the physical cosine
heat readout, fractional masses fail integer divisor multiplicities, and
free static divisor hierarchies have no supplied common canonical source
path. No control is excluded merely by a generic analytic or heat identity.
