    sup_{[u,v] subset I} |log W(u,v)-log W_N(u,v)| <= 10^(-6).       (1)

    x0=epsilon_0/h_G <= 10^(-11),
    x1=epsilon_1/h_G <= 10^(-8).                                 (6)

    e_M <= .4[20 b1+300*400*.1168 b1] < 6000 b1 <= .000156,
    4 Delta e_M < 6.09375*10^(-7) < 10^(-6).                     (8)

Rouche's epsilon_0<=h_G/2 and e_Q<=q/2 follow with slack. Only field
orders zero and one are needed for this consumer. For an implemented

The gates (6) are sufficient, not necessary; their failure alone is not

weights belong in these same total errors. Neither a fixed unweighted
coefficient error nor exact arithmetic on incomplete data suffices.

## 3. Strategy A: the published global allowance fails a finite cutoff cap

This screen concerns precisely the allowance

    epsilon_0^global = C(R,Delta) Ebar_N,
    Ebar_N=2K[log(2+N)+1]/N,  K=5 C_u>=5,                       (9)

with C from Note47 (21). It uses the complete-count **upper allowance**
Ebar_N, not an asserted lower bound for the true tail E_N.
Take the explicit cutoff cap N<=2^40 as a generous analytical screen,
not as an affordable acquisition proposal. No precision can repair a
failure of this exact allowance. No zero scan or feasibility run is made.

Any origin-centered disk covering our contours has R>=14000. At time
zero a real point on either circle obeys |H(0,x)|<=H(0,0)=h0 by positivity
of the literal source. Thus any valid common contour floor h_H<=h0.
Note47 has M_lambda=h0 C_near exp(B_lambda)>=h0, and
lambda=1/[8(1+Delta)]. Consequently

    C(R,Delta)/h_H >= 2R^2 sqrt(1+Delta)
                      *exp(R^2/[4(1+Delta)]).

Since Ebar_N>=10/N, even the first Rouche gate forces

    N >= 40R^2 sqrt(1+Delta) exp(R^2/[4(1+Delta)]),
    log N > 14000^2*256/1025 > 48,000,000.                       (10)

But N<=2^40 implies log N<40. The unchanged global allowance cannot
certify this tube under that cap, even with exact finite data. This is
a lower bound on the cutoff required by **formula (9)**. It is not a
necessary complexity bound for finite-divisor approximation, a lower
bound for its actual error, or a reason to replace the cutoff cap by an
astronomical acquisition campaign. A sharper actual-tail certificate is
a different information/budget input. A common constant rescaling of H
and P_N leaves this comparison unchanged.

## 4. Strategy B: local calibration, with the entire Gaussian path paid

A separate, fixed real calibration can remove an initial amplitude
mismatch. It is different from rescaling a previously fixed numerator
and denominator together. For N>a*, write the omitted product as F_N
and put

    F_N(w)=product_omitted(1-w^2/rho^2), A_N=F_N(a*)>0,
    H(0,w)=P_N(0,w)F_N(w),  P_N^cal(t,w)=A_N P_N(t,w).            (11)

Conjugation and N>a* give the positive real calibration. It is constant
in space and time; P_N^cal obeys the same heat equation and has exactly
the same divisor, c_N,D_N,M_N and W_N. We are bounding those observables
of the original P_N, not claiming a small uncalibrated field error.
The global and calibrated epsilon_j must not be mixed inside one
quotient bound. Spatially varying normalization would change the heat
equation and is outside this comparison.

For |w|<=s<N, the convergent logarithmic series gives

    |log F_N(w)-log F_N(a*)|
        <= |w^2-a*^2| E_N/(1-s^2/N^2).                         (12)

To prove it, integrate the derivative of log(1-v/rho^2) along the
straight segment from v=a*^2 to v=w^2; the denominator is at least
|rho|^2-s^2. Sum over omitted occurrences. This proof avoids division
by retained zero factors. From H-A_N P_N=H(1-A_N/F_N), the resulting
core error is bounded by the canonical initial field times exp of
(12) minus one, including at retained zeros by analytic continuation.

For a concrete comparison choose Cauchy padding p=1/16 about the two
new circles and physical Gaussian shift cutoff Y=1/4. Their padded
set has |z-a*|<=d0=.5265; let R=14011 and s=R+Y, and require N>s.
The core |y|<=Y lies in |z+iy-a*|<=d0+Y=.7765<1.05, so the inherited
G_0 upper bound B_core=5,200,000 applies. Put

    v_N=(d0+Y)(2a*+d0+Y)/(1-s^2/N^2).

The central part of the unchanged imaginary-Gaussian formula has error
at most B_core[exp(v_N E_N)-1] in the G gauge. This replaces the
radial R^2 factor in the initial logarithmic mismatch by a local
variation of order 2a*(d0+Y). It is a real estimate refinement, with no
claim yet about the complete error.

### Explicit complementary tail

Use Note47's lambda and M_lambda, set

    beta=1-8 lambda Delta=1/(1+Delta),
    u=Y/sqrt(2 Delta), v=u sqrt(beta/2),
    T0=M_lambda exp(2 lambda R^2) beta^(-1/2) erfc(v),
    T2=M_lambda exp(2 lambda R^2) [
         2R^2 beta^(-1/2) erfc(v)
         +4 Delta{beta^(-3/2) erfc(v)
                    +sqrt(2/pi) u beta^(-1) exp(-beta u^2/2)}].  (13)

These are H-gauge quantities; multiply by exp(pi a*/8) to obtain
T0_G,T2_G. For every 0<tau<=Delta, the event
|sqrt(2tau)X|>Y is contained in |X|>u. The same radial majorant as
Note47 and exact Gaussian integration give T0 for the initial product
and T2 for its |w|^2-weighted envelope on that event. At tau=0 the
event is empty. Thus the estimates are uniform on the whole interval.

The triangle decomposition

    H-A_N P_N=(H-P_N)+(1-A_N)P_N

is used only on the complementary path, with initial-time fields.
The omitted-product inequality gives |1-A_N|<=exp(a*^2 E_N)-1.
The **complete** resulting bound on the padded contours is

    epsilon_pad^cal <= B_core[exp(v_N E_N)-1]
               +E_N T2_G+[exp(a*^2 E_N)-1]T0_G
               +epsilon_pad^data.                             (14)

Here the finite-data contribution must be propagated over the entire
Gaussian path, using Note47/48's finite weighted sum. If A_N is supplied
as Ahat with error e_A and the initial polynomial has weighted error U,
a sufficient extra field budget is
|A_N|U+e_A(B_P+U), where B_P bounds the evolved exact P_N on the same
padded set. All three inputs need enclosures. Exact common amplitude
uncertainty cancels for log derivatives only when treated as a common
factor; independent shape/calibration errors cannot simply be omitted.
Cauchy gives epsilon_1<=epsilon_pad^cal/p, with any separately incurred
arithmetic errors added. All quantities must refer to this one calibrated
comparison. This is an error interface, not acquired calibration data.

### This particular global complement still defeats the local improvement

Replace E_N in (14) by Ebar_N as in strategy A and retain N<=2^40.
Even the positive summand Ebar_N T2_G fails the first Rouche gate.
Indeed for our Y (and even any Y<=1/2), v<8. Directly from the defining
integral, erfc(8)>exp(-81): integrate over [8,9] and use 2/sqrt(pi)>1.
Since M_lambda/h_H>=1 and beta<=1,

    Ebar_N T2_G/h_G
      >= (20R^2/N) exp(R^2/[4(1+Delta)]-81) > 1.                 (15)

The last inequality uses R>=14000, (10), and log N<40. This lower
bound is on a **positive summand of the chosen sufficient allowance**,
not the actual Gaussian error. Adding calibration, data or arithmetic
costs cannot make that allowance pass. Taking a much larger Y would
require a new certified core envelope outside the inherited disk; the
proof does not assert that every such choice fails.

The surviving missing input is therefore concrete: a useful bound on
the **entire centered imaginary Gaussian path**, including the omitted
product and calibrated finite-data sensitivity, relative to (4), that
avoids returning to this origin-centered complement. Core localization
and common scalar rescaling alone do not pay it. No result here excludes
signed integration, a better vertical envelope, tighter actual-tail
information, or a different approximation.
