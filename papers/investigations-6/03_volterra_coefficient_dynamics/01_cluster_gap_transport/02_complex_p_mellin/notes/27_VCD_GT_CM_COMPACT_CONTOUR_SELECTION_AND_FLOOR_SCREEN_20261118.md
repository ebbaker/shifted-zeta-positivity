## 1. Why select this compact source mechanism

The existing GT complete xi/Gaussian oracle and tensor certificate give
one concrete compact source, rather than asymptotic exterior information.
At a=140101/10 they certify a count-two annulus
3/4<=|z−a|<=21/20 on 0<=t<=1/10. The circle r=9/10 lies inside it.
[GT Note12][oracle], [GT Note14][tensor], [GT Note15][certnote] and
[certificate05][cert] own the source normalization, errors and acceptance.
The gauge G=exp(pi a/8)H is a fixed positive constant times the physical
H_t=−H_zz, so logarithmic derivatives and the heat equation are unchanged.

Choose this complete contour source over exterior-neighbor sign brackets.
The latter already use positive target-D cells on the same protected slab;
reusing that dependency would not acquire a new noncircular K estimate.
The OC census is at time zero, not a common-time positive-slab acquisition.
The small-height HDC/RT source domains give a very wide pair over very short
intervals and offer no demonstrated joint-K gain. These are source-domain
distinctions, not exclusions of other uses of those records.

The selected source keeps all exterior occurrences, including nonreal ones.
It therefore allows a *local* counted-pair theorem; no all-real assertion
about the complete canonical divisor at t=1/10 or below zero is imported.
A uniform count-two collar alone allows the target pair to become double
and then conjugate nonreal. It does not establish D>0 in the interior.

## 2. Exact trial-coordinate contour acquisition

Let a fixed conjugation-invariant circle Gamma enclose exactly two
occurrences, uniformly on a compact time interval. Put B=H_z/H on its
cleared boundary. For any real trial c,D whose quadratic

    Q(z)=(z−c)^2−D

has both roots inside Gamma, define, for p>=1,

    S_p^Gamma(t;c,D)=−(1/(2pi i)) integral_Gamma B(z,t)/Q(z)^p dz.  (1)

This equals the complete exterior sum over zeros outside Gamma of
Q(rho)^(-p), with multiplicities. The trial quadratic need not equal the
actual target quadratic. For each interior logarithmic-derivative pole,
all poles of its rational product with Q^(-p) are inside and its contour
integral is zero by decay at infinity. Each exterior pole contributes
minus Q(rho)^(-p). The constant genus-one regularizer integrates to zero.
The complete canonical product passage is locally uniform on the cleared
contour and the exterior inverse-square tail is absolute. This is the
trial-coordinate version of [CM Note09 §3][hierarchy]; no root velocities,
infinite T1 sum or arbitrary finite-divisor approximation is substituted.
For an extended time domain these source/product bounds must be paid on
that domain, rather than inferred from an older numerical certificate.

At the actual contour coordinates,

    A_j=(1/(2pi i)) integral_Gamma (z−a)^j B dz,
    c=a+A_1/2,       D=A_2/2−(A_1/2)^2,                         (2)

(1) gives M=S1 and F=12S2+16DS3. Evaluate the combined kernel

    K_Gamma=−(1/(2pi i)) integral_Gamma
              [24D^2/Q^2+32D^3/Q^3] B dz.                    (3)

For the actual pair this is exactly K=2D²F. Signed complete sums replace
positive omission of an exterior tail; all quantities are real by
conjugation. On a locally cleared heat tube the collision-regular laws
in Note09 give, in backward u=t*−t,

    D_u=−2+4DM,     M_u=−2M²+F.

Their algebra and complete contour passage do not require positive
exterior summands. Thus, while D>0 before a local stop, V=1−2DM satisfies

    D_u=−2V,       V_u=(1−V²−K)/D.                           (4)

This is the explicitly stated local extension of the all-real application
in Note25: a directly acquired signed complete K bound is required. One
cannot use Note25's positive-head omission with nonreal exterior zeros.
The independently cleared local tube and its source passage replace the
global first-loss hypothesis; no global descent or Lambda claim follows.

For the inherited annulus the single-valued function
L=Log(G/(z−a)^2) is available. Subtracting a real constant does not change
any integral below. Integration by parts in (1) gives

    S_p=−p/(2pi i) integral_Gamma L Q'/Q^(p+1) dz,
    K=−1/(2pi i) integral_Gamma L Q'
                         [48D²/Q³+96D³/Q⁴] dz.              (5)

The rational 2/(z−a) term from B contributes zero, because all its poles
are inside and the product decays at infinity. This reuses the bounded
annular log already acquired by the GT certificate and needs no fresh
H_z values for the screen in §6.

## 3. The noncircular stopping test

Acquire count two, boundary clearance and unconditional enclosures
|c−a|<=C and D<=Dmax over the desired interval. These enclosures may
include negative D. Independently acquire the start
D(0)>=d0>d_stop>0 and V(0)<=v0. Certify the conditional trial-domain bound

    K_Gamma(t;c,D)>=kappa
    for all t in I, |c−a|<=C, d_stop<=D<=Dmax.                (6)

With v=max(0,v0,sqrt(max(0,1−kappa))), the strict exit margin is

    sigma=d0−2v Delta−d_stop >0.                             (7)

Before a hypothetical first lower exit D=d_stop, (4) and the integrating
factor argument of Note25 give D>=d0−2vu>d_stop, a contradiction.
All other exits are excluded by the unconditional contour coordinates
and boundary checks. The full desired positivity interval is a conclusion,
not a premise of the source evaluation. If a source algorithm uses smaller
reachable parameter regions, their enclosure must be proved without
assuming that positivity; independent rectangular replacement can lose
useful correlations, as §7 shows.

An *unconditional* K>=k>0 throughout a regular count-two tube has a different
status. F remains finite at a target merger, while K=2D²F=0 there. Such a
floor already excludes the merger by continuity from the positive start.
It is not automatically circular when computed directly from the source,
but it cannot be advertised as a weaker positivity premise. The stopped
form (6) is what makes the proposed acquisition logically noncircular.

## 5. Complete error and same-information contracts

For any prospective acquisition, source approximations h,h1 must have
uniform whole-cell errors e0,e1 for G,G_z, with |h|>=b0>e0 and |h1|<=b1.
Then the error of B against h1/h is bounded by

    eB=e1/(b0−e0)+b1 e0/[b0(b0−e0)].                         (13)

For |c−a|<=C, |D|<=Dmax and q=(r−C)^2−Dmax>0, the source contributions are

    E_M <= r eB/q,
    E_K <= r eB(24Dmax²/q²+32Dmax³/q³),
    E_Aj <= r^(j+1)eB.                                      (14)

For the log route, if |L−Lhat|<=eL on the circle, corresponding bounds are

    E_M <= r eL 2(r+C)/q²,
    E_K <= r eL 2(r+C)(48Dmax²/q³+96Dmax³/q⁴).                (15)

Add finite quadrature, coefficient reconstruction, parameter-enclosure
and rounding errors, preserving source/time/state correlations if used.
For an angular integrand holomorphic in |Im theta|<=s with modulus <=A_s,
the N-node trapezoidal allowance is 2A_s/(exp(Ns)−1), by Fourier decay and
aliasing. Both G and every trial Q need clearance on the corresponding
complex angular strip; real-node checks alone do not pay it. The annular
version in GT Note14 may instead be reused only on its proved domain.

All e0,e1 or eL budgets must include the complete theta/xi source,
Gaussian/source tail, lattice or quadrature error, arithmetic, spatial
interpolation and true time interpolation. Whole-cell time bounds use
interval evaluation or the exact derivatives
partial_t^j G=(−1)^j partial_z^(2j)G with explicit remainders. Neither
time samples nor differentiating an asymptotic O term pays them.
The complete contour sums in (1) need no separately omitted zero tail;
the source-evaluation error already pays the full field.

On t<0 the analytic Gaussian identity becomes

    H_(−s)(z)=pi^(−1/2) integral_R exp(−r²) H_0(z+2sqrt(s)r) dr.

It follows from the complete Fourier source and the multiplier exp(−su²).
The real spatial shift changes the xi ordinate through the infinite
integral. The old nonnegative-time oracle, its conjugation shortcut and
bounded-height gamma/lattice constants cannot simply be reused. New
negative-time source and collar bounds would be required. They are not
computed here because §6 already rejects the fixed consumer.

Give the direct comparator exactly the same input and errors: (i) the
centered discriminant (2); (ii) direct integration of D_u=−2+4DM from the
same complete M enclosure; and (iii) a validated regular (D,M) evolution
with the same conditional F source. The old initial-M allowance is only
a weaker baseline. If direct D already proves positivity, K acquisition
is a magnitude or cost comparison, not additional zero coverage. The
existing GT direct endpoint is stronger on its old interval; neither
method has been evaluated on the new negative-time part.

## 6. Canonical pre-execution rejection of the constant floor

The interval (8) includes the already certified time t=0. At that slice,
Record05 and GT Note15 give the safe bounds

    |c(0)−a|<1/10,    0<D(0)<1/500,
    |L−L_center|<7/4 on |z−a|=9/10.                          (16)

The last bound follows from the certified centered-log bound <1.737 on
both annular boundaries and the maximum-modulus principle. It pays the
complete field, not a finite divisor. On this circle,
|Q|>(.9−.1)^2−.002=.638>3/5 and |Q'|<=2. Therefore (5) gives

    |S2| <= 2(.9)(7/4)2/(3/5)^3 =175/6,
    |S3| <= 3(.9)(7/4)2/(3/5)^4 =875/12,
    |F| <=12|S2|+16(1/500)|S3| <=1057/3.

Consequently the actual canonical forcing at this slice satisfies

    |K(0)| <2(1/500)^2(1057/3)
            =1057/375000 <3/1000.                           (17)

This is an upper bound on the actual complete K, not merely a failed
lower allowance. It uses the already protected t=0 slice only to test
a new consumer; it does not count that protection as new coverage.
Any constant kappa valid on the proposed interval must therefore be
less than 3/1000. Thus every admissible constant-barrier parameter has
v>=sqrt(1−kappa)>sqrt(.997)>.998. Even exact initial data, zero lower-stop
threshold and no additional errors cannot improve its duration to

    Delta < D(t*)/(2v)
          < .1787369/(2*.998)
          =1787369/19960000 <.0896.                          (18)

The required .1003 is larger. In particular kappa_req=.2079 from §4 is
impossible, regardless of precision or interval refinement. The same
screen even prevents this one constant-floor certificate from reaching
t=0 from t*=.1. A shorter interval, renewed anchors, a time-varying
barrier, an integral comparison or regular coupled evolution is a
different consumer and is not excluded by (18).

The scope is exact: the constant v=max(v0,sqrt(max(0,1−kappa))) consumer
of Note25, applied from this fixed Lehmer anchor over an interval containing
t=0. It is not a universal limitation of joint forcing, contour methods,
canonical heat dynamics or descent below zero. No numerical acquisition
request is issued for a target that this screen proves cannot pass.

## 7. Additional dependency and collision controls

A separate static screen explains why an independent F floor is risky.
At an equilibrium-scale start V0=0, suppose a rectangular reduction uses
F>=f with 0<=f<=1/(2d0²) and d_stop=alpha d0, 0<alpha<1. Then
kappa=2d_stop² f<=alpha², v>=sqrt(1−alpha²), and (7) implies

    Delta <(d0/2)sqrt((1−alpha)/(1+alpha))<d0/2.

This is a limitation of that independent rectangular F-floor reduction.
For the stationary cosine, lowering trial D while freezing the exterior
also lowers F, so a broad exact trial box can itself lose the equilibrium
advantage. It is not a no-go statement for correlated reachable regions.

The quadratic heat collision has F=K=0 and V=1; its finite margin fails
on a slab containing the collision. The cosine-plus-constant family in
Note23 has a cleared exterior but K tends to zero at its merger; it
fails any proposed positive full-time K floor. Stationary cosine passes
its actual K=1,V=0 barrier. The generic contour identities need not
reject collision-free domains of noncanonical controls.

For the [documented collision families][controls], canonical acquisition
must retain the exact complete theta field: Euler-factor translates change
theta arithmetic/endpoint discrepancy; weighted-square and finite-score
controls fail full reflected unit-weight theta equality; reflected
super-Gaussians fail the order-one source class; smooth positive/primitive
controls change theta arithmetic and lack the complete canonical source
transfer; finite-prime and finite-profile controls lack full reflection
or the complete theta profile; the RH-conditional normalized endpoint
control changes ordinary Dirichlet arithmetic and retains RH as an input;
changed observation kernels and fractional multiplicities fail the
physical readout/integer-divisor premises. No such source is silently
substituted in (9), (16) or (17). These controls were screened analytically
at their stated premises, not rerun as a numerical campaign.

## 8. Disposition

The compact mechanism and finite error contract are concrete. Its constant
floor consumer is rejected on the chosen canonical target before numerical
execution; the failed target is preserved rather than shortened to obtain
a nominal pass. A further B choice would have to retain time-varying K or
use regular (D,M) evolution, and compare with direct M transport before
issuing a source-extension computation. No such changed consumer is
selected or declared ready by this note. E16 r2 remains independently
optional and unchanged. E25 assesses this bounded decision, not a new
compact certificate. No manuscript, PDF or large archive is produced.
