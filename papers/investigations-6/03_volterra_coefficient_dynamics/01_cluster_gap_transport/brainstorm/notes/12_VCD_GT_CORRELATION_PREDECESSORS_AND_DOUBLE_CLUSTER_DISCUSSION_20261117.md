## 1. What the earlier work actually supplies

### Conditional probabilities on the source

[Direct-realization Note04][direct] uses the normalized complete source
`w_t(v) = exp(lambda(t) v^2) Phi(v) / Z_lambda`, where
`lambda(t)=1/2-gamma t`. For fixed observables with the paid moments it gives

    d/dt E_t a(V) = -gamma Cov_t(a(V), V^2).

This is an exact response identity. The signed cosine readout and its
derivatives describe a double event even though the probability density,
normalization and success probability remain regular and positive. The
note also separates the positive and negative cosine sectors and obtains
an exact derivative of their mass ratio. These are useful places to retain
joint information about oscillatory signs and source size. They do not
prove the needed covariance sign. Source-variable conditioning is not
conditioning the exterior zeros on a close zero pair. Its time variable
also runs oppositely to the Newman parameter used by VCD-GT.

### A positive Vandermonde carrier with a signed insertion

The [biorthogonal branch][ensemble] constructs a positive density on
ordered source nodes, with carrier proportional to

    product_j u_j^(2s) Phi(u_j) * V(u^2) * V(R(u)),
    R = Phi''/Phi.

The determinant target is represented as `M_r(s)=Z_r(s) E_s h_r`, with
a generally signed insertion `h_r`. Positivity of the carrier does not
by itself establish the sign or curvature of this inserted expectation.
Its nodes are source variables, not zeros of H. Its order r must not be
identified with the multiplicity of a zero cluster.

[MC04 Note01][ward] derives an undivided Ward identity, including the
derivative of the insertion and all boundary contributions. Its
[separate internal assessment][wardreview] accepts the identities and
the precise obstruction to a fixed finite-polynomial pointwise closure.
The favorable mixed-response bound remains open. This is a useful
example of organizing a joint estimate, with the insertion retained.

The [signed-averaging evaluation][signed] also finds an actual-source
obstruction to global coordinatewise monotonicity of the order-four
insertion. That rules out that particular sufficient association argument;
it does not rule out integrated covariance bounds, signed averaging in
general, or a four-zero cluster argument. The order-three signed-average
result retains its stated status in [theta arithmetic14][theta].

### Empirical correlations of the actual zeros

The closest predecessor is [OC Note14][oc14]. It carefully distinguishes
the source probability, the deterministic zero measure, and an auxiliary
random-matrix law. A common random height center supplies a genuine
averaging law for the actual zeros; it does not turn them into a proved
Vandermonde ensemble. The factorial pair removes only equal labels.
Distinct labels at a multiple zero remain, and both imaginary coordinates
are retained. A height-difference statistic alone loses this information.

The branch has more than a formal representation. [OC Proof17][oc17]
proves explicit time moduli for the complete first, factorial-pair and
connected smoothed statistics, at fixed finite height and positive blur,
through multiple events without a minimum gap. [Assessment14][ocreview]
accepts all six claims unchanged at that scope; this is internal checking
with shared premises. An independently acquired initial profile can be
transported with an explicit additive allowance. This does not acquire
that initial profile or a useful collision-exclusion margin uniformly in
height and resolution.

[OC Note11][oc11] supplies a common-center mixed-source interface;
[Note18][oc18] pays Gaussian windows and distinguishes averaging H from
averaging log|H|. The missing arithmetic information includes joint
small-value behavior of the field. A finite routine that first certifies
the zero inventory and then computes its correlation is a benchmark,
not an independent restriction on that same inventory.

### Deterministic Vandermonde energy and an actual signed-estimate gain

[Heat-density Note12][clock] obtains complete-source late-time root
localization and a growing-block Vandermonde/Hessian comparison. Its
status is estimates and eventual asymptotics proved in-note, with
same-assistant review; floating finite evaluations are separate. This is
a deterministic clock approximation. It neither posits an ensemble nor
provides a near-zero-time estimate as height tends to infinity. Selected
blocks retain the exterior force. The scalar determinant cannot locate
the center or determine every gap of a larger block.

There is also a positive precedent for the proposed *type* of gain:
[GNP-RS Note29][signedgain] bounds the real quadratic correlation actually
consumed by its residual norm, including all shifts. It improves that
branch's window exponent from 17/72 to 17/81. The recorded status is
proved in-note from named published and inherited inputs, with
same-assistant checking. It gives an averaged high-height estimate, not
an every-center result. It is not a ready-made exterior-count theorem,
but shows why preserving a signed joint expression can outperform
separate absolute bounds in an analytical height argument.

## 2. The precise correlation relevant to the exterior bound

[OG Advisory10, section 4][og10] already identifies the selected-pair
environment. For a finite simple cloud, its exterior coefficient is

    B_ij = -sum_(k != i,j) 1/((z_i-z_k)(z_j-z_k)).

Here `M_ij=-B_ij` has the VCD-GT lower-mass sign. For a bounded pair test
psi, under the displayed kernel's integrability assumptions,

    E sum_(i != j) psi(z_i,z_j) M_ij
      = integral psi(a,b)/((a-z)(b-z)) d alpha_3(a,b,z).

This is the existing finite factorial identity, not a new canonical
infinite-product claim. It explains why the exterior environment of a
pair generally involves three-zero information. Where ordinary factorial
densities and disintegration exist, the informal conditional interface
is rho_3/rho_2. The empirical measure may instead be singular; no
Lebesgue density or division at an exact double is assumed. Requiring
adjacency or isolation additionally conditions the rest of the cloud.

One need not acquire an entire three-point function. A bound for this
specific signed integral, a deterministic exterior-field inequality, or
an integrated count/distance estimate may suffice. This is the useful
narrowing relative to a general full-correlation program.

For the current real exterior groups, the contribution of a real root r
is `1/((r-c)^2-D)`. [Synthesis32][s32] already combines lower counts and
distance allowances in this kernel. It also demonstrates a finite gain
from retaining common time cells rather than independent global extrema.
A broader question is whether the incoming gap, center and exterior
population satisfy a common structural bound. Independent worst-case
bounds may combine geometries that cannot occur together.

A conditional *average* would initially provide an average statement.
To exclude every forbidden pair it needs either a bound valid throughout
the conditioned class, or a dynamical lower bound on the detectable
contribution of any exception. The conditioning denominator, spatial and
temporal visibility, imaginary marks, smoothing bias and complete exterior
remain part of that comparison. In particular, the existing OC example
has an O(d^2) difference between fixed-blur profiles near a double while
inverse-square energy is O(d^-2). Exact Gaussian smoothing is injective;
the obstruction is a fixed positive error at shrinking separation.

## 3. Why multiplicity two is the appropriate first case

Use the VCD-GT Newman-time convention `H_t=-H_zz`. On a paid isolated
count-two tube write

    H_t(z) = ((z-c(t))^2-D(t)) A_t(z),
    D' = 2 - 4 M D.

The deflated factor A is holomorphic and nonzero on the required region.
The center and polynomial coefficient D continue through the double
without choosing singular individual root trajectories. For a real pair
D>0; for a conjugate pair D<0. At a double D=0. No positive lower gap is
assumed as part of the putative exclusion.

The algebraic squared Vandermonde of these two roots is exactly

    (z_+-z_-)^2 = 4D.

Thus the deterministic two-root discriminant and the existing gap
variable coincide up to normalization. This is distinct from the
nonnegative modulus-square Vandermonde in an ensemble: for a conjugate
pair the latter is 4|D| and loses the sign of D. The advantage of the
two-root case is that internal separation has one scalar coefficient,
so the unpaid quantitative issue can be concentrated on its environment
and the time budget.

The useful initial question is therefore: **what quantitative constraint
does the unchanged theta source impose on the exterior population and
signed interaction of an isolated close pair, jointly with its gap?**
An answer could be a pointwise joint inequality or a directly useful
integrated estimate. Merely renaming this interaction a conditional
expectation does not answer it. The same complete-source information
must remain available to the direct evaluator in any comparison.

The extension to larger clusters requires more than changing a factor
of two. It needs symmetric cluster coefficients, control of internal
near-collisions and their coupling to the exterior, and treatment of loss
of the separating collar. A single discriminant does not determine the
individual gaps or the realness of a general larger cluster. The useful
part of the pair proof to generalize would be its exterior estimate and
collective cancellation. A proof for isolated double events alone would
not exclude a positive-threshold event of higher multiplicity. Time-zero
multiple real zeros also remain compatible with the positivity objective;
their exclusion would add a simplicity claim.

Across height a frozen spacing scale ell gives normalized variables
`D_hat=D/ell^2`, `M_hat=ell^2 M`, and time increment `dt/ell^2`.
The same gap equation then applies. The question is whether a joint
estimate survives that scaling as the target time approaches zero, not
whether the fixed physical number 0.59 recurs. Late-time clock estimates
and fixed-blur finite-height moduli must retain their distinct regimes.

## 4. Controls and classification

This is a documentary replay of the [controls12][controls] premise
screen, not a new numerical control campaign. No favorable new inequality
has been proposed as proved. Heat evolution, positive source probability,
factorial bookkeeping and the pair equation permit collisions.

| Recorded control class | Failed canonical premise; what remains untested here |
| --- | --- |
| Euler-factor controls | Unchanged theta source/completion fails. Positivity and the heat identities survive; no proposed joint bound has been evaluated. |
| Weighted-square and finite-source controls | Complete unit-weight theta expansion and full normalized reflection fail. Finite endpoint and support constraints do not replace them; finite-source controls need not have any all-real time. |
| Reflected super-Gaussian and smooth positive-primitive controls | Exact theta arithmetic fails; reflection, positive carriers, shape or primitive positivity alone are insufficient. |
| Finite-prime and finite-canonical-score controls | Full reflection/source data fail despite agreement with selected finite information. Finite score or prime matches cannot supply the missing all-source estimate. |
| Fixed-event and finite-profile threshold controls | Unchanged coefficients, full profile growth and eventual simplicity fail in their recorded classes. Finite conditioning data alone do not bound the deflated jet. |
| RH-conditional normalized endpoint controls | Ordinary Dirichlet-series arithmetic fails. Their escaping time-zero doubles concern a different quantifier from compact positive-time exclusion. |
| Raw-kernel and actual-profile relaxations | Respectively the full cosine/Newman equation and complete integer-divisor structure fail; the relaxed examples are not physical theta collisions. |

Failure of literal source identity does not prove failure of a future
derived inequality. A successful quantitative argument must identify the
premise it actually uses and show its allowance distinguishes the relevant
controls. The fixed-blur, order-four monotonicity and finite-polynomial
closure obstructions above retain their separate, explicitly limited
classes. They do not exclude correlation methods as a whole.

## 5. Discussion disposition

The preferred direction is to reuse the existing selected-pair and
conditional-source interfaces, first for a count-two cluster, and seek
one signed exterior or joint count/distance estimate that has a plausible
scaled-height form. This is a choice of a question, not an acquired
mechanism. A later execution request should specify its domain, extra
source input, numerical margin or asymptotic rate, control separation,
and comparison with the equally informed direct route. No task is issued
here. No manuscript, result-summary status or DRAFT_HISTORY milestone is
changed by this discussion.
