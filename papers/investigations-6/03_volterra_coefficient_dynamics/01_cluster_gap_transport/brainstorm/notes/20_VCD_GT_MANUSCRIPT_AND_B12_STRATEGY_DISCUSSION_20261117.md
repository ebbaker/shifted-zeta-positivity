## What the manuscript has accomplished

The branch's manuscript is the [existing parent master][master],
*Jacobi dynamics, signed error, and cluster transport for the Newman heat
flow*. Its [reading guide][guide] identifies the integrated results through
Notes40–41. [Assessment20][assessment] accepts that integration within
scope after corrections, with internal LLM review and shared inherited
premises. This discussion reads the TeX sources and supporting status
records; it does not replay certificates or supply specialist validation.

The development has three useful layers.

1. **Approximation and regular geometry.** The complete signed quadrature
   error is controlled, and contour moments define a pair's center and
   discriminant through a double. The actual finite Jacobi heat defect is
   retained. These prevent approximation errors and singular individual
   root labels from being mistaken for physical information. They do not
   themselves distinguish theta from every colliding positive source.
2. **Canonical estimates with quantitative gains.** The local certificate
   acquires exterior magnitude on one already controlled tube. At growing
   height the far sum has a favorable main term. Transverse source moments
   improve the ordinary-center mean from C/a to C/a^3, and density plus
   isolation gives an exceptional-pair upper count. These are actual
   estimates at their stated scopes, rather than new names for positivity.
3. **Common-time information.** B11 retains conjugate dissipation and
   derives the exact complete interaction evolution, including multiple
   roots. The resulting occupation and Riccati allowances improve the
   earlier estimates. They also locate precisely why those allowances
   still permit individual exceptions.

For fixed microscopic collar parameter a, L=log T and ell=1/L,

| --- | --- | --- |
| Total time occupied by eligible pairs with negative interaction | O(TL), integrating a slice bound over fixed positive physical duration | O(T/L), with the displayed boundary term; this is occupation, not an event count or a fraction of the eligible pairs |

These comparisons do not say that the true interaction or multiplier has
the size of its upper allowance. The source constants and finite-height
onsets are not numerically certified by these analytical results.

## Where the unresolved problem now lies

The distant zeros have favorable sign, but their normalized contribution
tends to zero for the manuscript's R=sqrt L cutoff. The leading unresolved
environment is therefore nearby, and must be controlled at the center of
the selected pair. The identical-horizontal-count construction shows that
even exact projected counts, plus the stated upper density and moment
bounds, leave opposite signs possible in its fixed-slice analytic class.
It supplies neither a canonical source nor a common physical heat tube.

An average can improve substantially while allowing one exceptional pair.
B11's O(T/L) occupation allowance grows even when compared with the entire
available physical duration. Better visibility alone cannot turn that
specific aggregate allowance into a count below one. This is a limitation
of the displayed argument, not a theorem against all averaging methods.

There is a separate distinction between favorable interaction and a
positive gap. The exact law is D'=2-4MD, and backward passage compares an
independent incoming D value with a weighted time cost. At D=0 it still
has D'=2. The manuscript's even quartic has positive M and an actual
collision: it fails the strict incoming-cost condition. A theorem giving
M>=0 would not alone settle the original objective.

The global difficulty also concerns small time jointly with height.
Polymath's Theorem1.5(i)–(ii) already gives eventual real, isolated zeros
above an exp(C/t) scale for fixed positive t. That onset recedes as
t approaches zero. An additional statement confined to that already
controlled regime needs an independently useful new rate or consumer.
The statement does not settle the finite exceptional region at a positive
threshold, and a general threshold argument must still cover higher
multiplicity. The pair case remains a reasonable first case, not a
reduction of every multiple event to an isolated double.

## Recommended purpose of B12

The best-supported next question is still the selected-pair, complete-source
question. The new equation provides a concrete consumer:

    m_sigma = 2 m^2 - Fcal,
    Fcal = 12 sum_j mu_j^2 + 16 delta sum_j mu_j^3,
    delta = D/ell^2,  mu_j = ell^2/((rho_j-c)^2-D).

The currently proved payment is |Fcal|<=C_F L. A lower barrier for m
uses an **upper** bound on Fcal. Thus “sign-sensitive” must specify the
useful direction; a favorable-sounding sign or a lower bound on Fcal is
not automatically what this comparison needs. This observation is read
directly from the accepted scalar equation, not a proposed new theorem.

My recommendation is to assess candidate estimates by their consequence
for the joint gap and interaction problem. A height-independent forcing
allowance would be a worthwhile intermediate gain, but the same Riccati
comparison would still permit negative O(1) interaction after the buffer.
It would not acquire a tube, initial interaction control or the incoming
gap. The initial layer also remains part of the integrated cost. Do not
describe this prospective improvement as a completed positivity mechanism.

Before an execution request, B12 should identify:

- the additional quantitative information from the unchanged theta source,
  beyond the counts and broad moments already consumed;
- a one-sided, joint, or time-integrated estimate with an explicit effect
  on the existing interaction or gap budget, including its initial layer;
- the range where it holds for both signs of D and through D=0, with
  isolation, time duration and incoming data either acquired or explicitly
  conditional.

Retaining the dependence between D, m and the exterior sums may avoid
unnecessary separate worst-case bounds. This is a preference for the next
comparison, not a selected inequality or a claimed proof route. It need
not require reconstruction of a whole three-zero correlation function:
the [earlier discussion12][discussion12] already explains why a bound for
the particular contracted signed observable can suffice. A conditional
mean still needs its measure and an argument connecting it to the selected
pair; renaming it does not supply that argument.

Use the existing at-most-two-proposal B12 limit. A concrete source estimate
with a new rate or useful margin would complete an intermediate milestone;
a scoped failure of a precisely specified information class can also be
useful. Another exact identity without a quantitative improvement would
leave the main issue unchanged. The equally informed direct method receives
every new input. A computational speed advantage is not required for an
analytical advance and has not been established here.

I would keep generic aggregate refinement, another fixed-pilot certificate,
and the already excluded separate-phase allowances secondary unless an
explicit new connection to the selected-pair question is supplied. Tube
acquisition and higher clusters remain visible downstream obligations.
This recommends sequencing; it does not declare those broader approaches
impossible or close any additional branch.

## Reapplied control screen

This is a documentary application of [Decision19's screen][decision19]
and [controls12][controls], not a new numerical control campaign. No new
candidate inequality is asserted or selected; its actual numerical or
analytical test remains part of B12 when defined.

| Control class | Premise or inference it prevents here |
| --- | --- |
| Quadratic and even quartic heat fields | They can obey the local dynamics through a collision; the quartic can have positive M. Their finite divisor lacks canonical growing counts, and the collision exhausts or defeats the incoming passage budget. |
| Euler-factor translates | They change the source and add a linear-in-height term to the projected count, failing the canonical logarithmic discrepancy; their positivity and heat law survive. |
| Documented endpoint-defective weighted-square, finite-source/score and finite-prime changes | They fail full reflected unit-weight theta equality and the fixed global strip; selected finite matches do not restore these premises. |
| Reflected super-Gaussian | Fails order at most one; canonical count/resolvent and source-moment inputs are not assigned to it. |
| Smooth shape and positive-primitive controls | Change the arithmetic; the required strip/count and transverse-density transfer remain untested. |
| Fixed-event atomic and compact-profile controls | Fail unchanged theta equality and canonical growing counts; generic heat identities can still apply. |
| RH-conditional endpoint mergers | Keep their RH premise and changed arithmetic; count/moment transfer remains untested. Time-zero D=0 is permitted by the reality objective. |
| Varied observation and fractional-profile relaxations | Fail, respectively, the literal cosine/Newman field or the complete integer zero divisor. |
| Note34 opposite-sign examples | Pass the specified slice count, collar, density and moment upper bounds with enlarged constants; no common canonical heat tube is supplied. They disprove a count-only sign inference in that class, not a future full-source dynamic estimate. |

An intermediate bound need not reject every colliding control. A proposed
collision exclusion must distinguish its relevant controls using a premise
actually present in its proof. Literal difference from theta is not by
itself proof that a particular quantitative premise fails.
