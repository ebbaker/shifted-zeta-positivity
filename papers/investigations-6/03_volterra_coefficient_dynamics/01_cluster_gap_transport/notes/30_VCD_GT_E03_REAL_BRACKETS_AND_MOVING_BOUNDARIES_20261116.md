## 1. Sign brackets supply the lower count actually needed

Keep the complete canonical field, physical equation
\(\partial_tH=-\partial_z^2H\), and the target pair from [Note21][divisor]
on \(I=[0,1/10]\), centered at \(a=140101/10\). Its absolutely convergent
divisor identity and the independently paid radius-\(8/5\) target-only disk
give

\[
 -K(t)=\sum_{\rho\ {\rm exterior}}
       \frac1{(z_1-\rho)(z_2-\rho)}.
 \tag{1}
\]

After conjugate grouping, every omitted group contributes positively, by
the canonical strip and [Note21 §2][divisor]. This statement does not assume
all exterior zeros real. The reflected target pair is retained separately.

Let \(J_j(t)=(\ell_j(t),u_j(t))\) be finitely many pairwise disjoint
positive real intervals outside the target disk, with continuous boundaries
and \(\ell_j(t)<u_j(t)\). Suppose each boundary is nonzero for every
\(t\in I\) and

\[
 H_t(\ell_j(t))H_t(u_j(t))<0\qquad(t\in I).
 \tag{2}
\]

For each fixed \(t\), the real entire function is continuous on the real
interval. The intermediate value theorem supplies a real zero in every
\(J_j(t)\). Select one zero occurrence from each interval. Disjointness
makes these occurrences distinct. Each selected occurrence is itself
conjugation invariant, so deleting it leaves a conjugation-invariant
remainder; any unselected multiplicity at the same real zero stays in that
remainder and remains favorable in (1).

This proves **a lower count of at least one real zero occurrence per
bracket on every slice**. It proves neither an exact count nor simplicity.
There is no need to choose labels continuously in \(t\), establish an
individual trajectory, or assume that the selected occurrence is the same
one on adjacent slices. The bound below is proved pointwise for every
slice, which suffices for a uniform estimate. In particular, finitely many
sampled sign changes alone do not establish (2).

## 2. Weights with independently paid target coordinates

Suppose on a closed time cell \(T\subset I\) that independent enclosures
give

\[
 c_-\le c(t)\le c_+,
 \qquad D_-\le D(t)\le D_+.
 \tag{3}
\]

For bracket \(j\), let
\(l_j=\inf_{t\in T}\ell_j(t)\),
\(u_j=\sup_{t\in T}u_j(t)\), and put

\[
 U_j=\max\{|l_j-c_+|,|u_j-c_-|\}.
 \tag{4}
\]

Every selected real \(r_j(t)\) has \(|r_j-c|\le U_j\). Verify also that
the bracket is separated from the target so that
\((r_j-c)^2-D>0\). One sufficient independent check is a lower distance
\(L_j>0\) with \(L_j^2>D_+\). The inherited exclusion by radius \(8/5\),
\(|c-a|\le7/100\), and \(|D|\le1/5\) already supplies
\(|r_j-c|>153/100\) for brackets strictly outside that real diameter.
If \(U_j^2-D_->0\), then

\[
 \frac1{(r_j-c)^2-D}
 \ge\frac1{U_j^2-D_-}>0.
 \tag{5}
\]

The sign of \(D_-\) is not restricted. With only the original coarse
bounds, \(D_-=-1/5\) and
\(U_j=\max(|l_j-a|,|u_j-a|)+7/100\) give
\(1/(U_j^2+1/5)\). A stronger lower bound on \(D\) improves the
denominator in the indicated direction.

The independently acquired [geometry09][geometry-record] permits the
following outward decimal endpoints, interpreted as exact rationals:

\[
 \begin{split}
 c_-&=a+0.0594957328,\qquad c_+=a+0.0637631130,\\
 D_-&=0.0007983892,\qquad D_+=0.1791166200.
 \end{split}\tag{6}
\]

These contain the recorded balls in [Note22][geometry]. Consuming (6)
must be explicit: this version does use the independently paid positive
lower \(D\), and therefore already has target-pair reality from that input.
It seeks an additional magnitude estimate for \(-K\), not a new proof of
that already known reality. The old measured \(K\) and the gap equation
are not premises for acquiring exterior locations or counts.

Evenness also provides the exterior reflected occurrence \(-r_j(t)\).
For positive brackets, their negative reflections are disjoint from the
positive brackets and one another. Excluding the target disk also excludes
overlap with its reflected pair after reflection. Since \(c,r_j>0\),

\[
 \frac1{(-r_j-c)^2-D}
 \ge\frac1{(u_j+c_+)^2-D_-},
 \tag{7}
\]

provided its denominator is positive, as it is in this domain. Each
selected occurrence and its reflection are counted once only. No extra
field evaluation is needed for (7). The original target reflection gives
\(\kappa_0=5000/1962848636289\); alternatively (3) gives the stronger
\(\kappa_T=[2(c_+^2-D_-)]^{-1}\), provided \(c_->0\) and
\(c_-^2>D_+\). This follows from its exact contribution
\(1/[2(c^2-D)]\), not from a new zero count.

Combining (1)–(7), a sufficient cell test is

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 -K(t)\ge\kappa_T+
 \sum_j\left\{\frac1{U_j^2-D_-}
       +\frac1{(u_j+c_+)^2-D_-}\right\}
 \ge\frac{59}{100}\quad(t\in T).
 \quad}\tag{8}
\]

Use \(\kappa_0\) instead if the stronger target-reflection bound is not
consumed, and omit the second summand if selected reflections are not
retained. The remainder is favorable and absolutely convergent; no
quantitative absolute tail penalty is necessary. Verify (8) on every cell
of a finite cover of \(I\). Different brackets may have overlapping
unions of locations over a time cell; it is their **same-time disjointness**
that prevents duplicate occurrences. The union bounds in (4) only enlarge
the denominator and do not change that count argument.

This information class includes real selected zeros and possibly the
stronger \(D_-\) in (6). The width-one rectangular count barriers of
Note27 do not apply unchanged to it. Neither (8) nor the bracket mechanism
asserts that any proposed finite manifest passes its threshold.

## 3. Complete-source bounds for every boundary center

Retain [Note12][oracle]'s literal complete theta source, normalization
\(H_0(z)=\xi((1+iz)/2)/8\), physical heat equation, and exact convolution

\[
 H_t(z)=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-r^2}
                  H_0(z+2i\sqrt t\,r)\,dr.
 \tag{9}
\]

Its absolute-integrability proof is unchanged. Here the real local center
may be any \(b\in[13840,14180]\), and the local positive gauge is
\(g_b=e^{\pi b/8}\). This section extends the explicit domain of its
majorants; it does not substitute a finite theta source.

For the radius-two oracle, take \(|z-b|\le2\),
\(r=\nu+i\eta\), \(|\eta|\le2\), \(q=\sqrt t<8/25\), and reflect
\(s_0=(1+i(z+2iqr))/2\) to \(s\in\{s_0,1-s_0\}\) with
\(\Re s\ge1/2\). Set \(\omega=s/2=X+iY\), \(V=|\nu|\).
The same coordinate formulas as Note12 give

\[
 \tfrac14\le X\le\tfrac34+\tfrac4{25}V,
 \quad \bigl||Y|-b/4\bigr|\le\tfrac12+q,
 \quad 3459<|Y|<3547.
\]

Consequently
\(|\omega|\le3550+(4/25)V\) and
\(|s-1|\le|s|\le7100+(8/25)V\).
The previously proved Binet bound applies with \(X\ge1/4\).
Euler summation gives \(|\zeta(s)|\le3|s|\), since
\(|s-1|\ge|\Im s|>6918\) and \(1/\Re s\le2\).
The local gauge leaves the same factor
\(g_be^{-\pi|Y|/2}\le e^{(\pi/2)(1/2+q)}<4\).
Thus Note12's calculation, with \(3505,7010\) replaced by
\(3550,7100\), has constant prefactor at most
\(27(7100)^3<10^{13}\). Its log-linear coefficient is bounded by

\[
 \tfrac4{25}\tfrac{409}{50}
 +\frac{(1/4)(4/25)}{3550}
 +\frac{3(8/25)}{7100}<\tfrac{131}{100},
\]

and its log-quadratic coefficient is
\((4/25)^2/3550<1/10000\). The exact finite-series inequality
\(\sum_{j=0}^{24}(409/50)^j/j!>3552\) pays both logarithm bounds
needed here and below. Therefore, with
\(C=10^{13},\alpha=9999/10000,\beta=131/100\),

\[
 |g_be^{-r^2}H_0(z+2iqr)|
 \le C e^{\eta^2-\alpha V^2+\beta V}.
 \tag{10}
\]

The contour-shift and lattice-tail proof in Note12 §3 now applies verbatim.
For \(h=1/3\) or \(1/4\), cutoff \(|kh|\le12\), its complete-source
absolute error in this local gauge is

\[
 E_h=\frac{4C e^{4+\beta^2/(4\alpha)}}
 {\sqrt\alpha(e^{4\pi/h}-1)}
 +\frac{2C e^{-144\alpha+12\beta}}
 {\sqrt\pi(24\alpha-\beta)}.
 \tag{11}
\]

Finite-sum arithmetic and any rounding require their own outward
enclosures. At \(t=0\), direct \(\xi\) evaluation may replace the lattice
with its actual arithmetic error; it introduces no quadrature error.

For the radius-five bound, let \(|z-b|\le5\) and \(r\) be real.
Then \(X\le3/2+(4/25)|r|\),
\(\bigl||Y|-b/4\bigr|\le5/4\),
\(3458<|Y|<3547\), and it is sufficient to use

\[
 |\omega|\le3552+\tfrac4{25}|r|,
 \qquad |s|\le7104+\tfrac8{25}|r|.
\]

The Binet/Euler bounds still apply, and the local gauge factor is below
\(e^{5\pi/8}<8\). The constant prefactor is at most
\((27/4)(7104)^3(3552)<10^{16}\). The log-linear coefficient is bounded by

\[
 \tfrac4{25}\tfrac{409}{50}
 +\frac{4/25}{3552}+\frac{3(8/25)}{7104}<\tfrac{131}{100},
\]

and its quadratic coefficient is below \(1/10000\).
Integration in (9) proves, with \(C_5=10^{16}\),

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 |g_bH_t(z)|\le M_5=
 \frac{2C_5}{\sqrt\alpha}e^{\beta^2/(4\alpha)},
 \qquad |z-b|\le5, t\in I, b\in[13840,14180].
 \quad}\tag{12}
\]

The arithmetic prefactor checks are exactly

\[
 27(7104)^3=9679939043328<10^{13},\qquad
 \tfrac{27}{4}(7104)^3(3552)=8595785870475264<10^{16}.
\]

These constants bound the true complete field. They are not measured
values, boundary-sign floors, or external-zero counts.

## 4. Affine boundaries and the full time derivative

Let a real boundary be
\(b(t)=B+v(t-1/20)\), with fixed real \(B,v\), and require
\(b(t)\in[13840,14180]\) for every \(t\in I\).
Use the **constant gauge** \(g_B=e^{\pi B/8}\), and write
\(F_B(t)=g_BH_t(b(t))\). Since
\(|b(t)-B|\le|v|/20\), the ratio of constant to local gauges satisfies

\[
 g_B/g_{b(t)}=e^{\pi(B-b(t))/8}
 \le A_v:=e^{\pi|v|/160}.
 \tag{13}
\]

The PDE and affine chain rule give, for every integer \(m\ge0\),

\[
 \begin{split}
 F_B^{(m)}(t)
 &=g_B\,(-\partial_z^2+v\partial_z)^mH_t(b(t))\\
 &=g_B\sum_{j=0}^m\binom mj(-1)^jv^{m-j}
                         \partial_z^{m+j}H_t(b(t)).
 \end{split}\tag{14}
\]

The operators commute and \(v\) is constant, which proves the expansion
inductively; a nonaffine boundary would require extra terms.
Cauchy's estimate on the full radius-five disk in (12), followed by
(13), yields

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 |F_B^{(m)}(t)|\le A_v M_5
 \sum_{j=0}^m\binom mj|v|^{m-j}
                       \frac{(m+j)!}{5^{m+j}}=:R_m(v).
 \quad}\tag{15}
\]

For \(v=0\) this reduces to \(M_5(2m)!/5^{2m}\), as required by the
fixed-point heat derivative. If source values are first computed in local
gauges, convert them to \(g_B\) before forming the time interpolant.
Interpolating \(g_{b(t)}H_t(b(t))\) instead would add a scalar derivative
term \(\pi v/8\); (14) would no longer be its formula.
Equation (11) pays constant-gauge sample error at most \(A_vE_h\), plus
arithmetic; using each actual node's gauge ratio can give a smaller bound.

## 5. Time interpolation and a closed-cell sign test

Fix an integer \(n\ge1\), Lobatto nodes
\(x_l=\cos(\pi l/n)\), \(t_l=(1+x_l)/20\), and real recorded node
values \(\widehat F_l\) with
\(|F_B(t_l)-\widehat F_l|\le\varepsilon_s\).
The finite-sum approximation can be complex in interval arithmetic;
its real part encloses the real true value, with a retained modulus error.
Let \(p_B(x)\) be the exact degree-at-most-\(n\) interpolant of these
recorded real values.

As proved in [Note14 §2][tensor], discrete cosine orthogonality gives
interpolation norm at most \(2n\), hence the convenient allowance
\(2n+1\). The monic node product satisfies

\[
 \max_{t\in I}\left|\prod_{l=0}^n(t-t_l)\right|
 \le2^{1-n}(1/20)^{n+1}.
\]

Apply the ordinary real interpolation remainder to the true real function
\(F_B\), using (15) with \(m=n+1\). Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 |F_B(t)-p_B(20t-1)|\le
 E_B:=(2n+1)\varepsilon_s+
 \frac{R_{n+1}(v)}{(n+1)!}
                2^{1-n}(1/20)^{n+1}.
 \quad}\tag{16}
\]

Enclose coefficient construction and evaluation outward. If rounded
coefficients define a different exact polynomial, add
\(\sum_{m=0}^n e_m\) to (16), where \(e_m\) bounds each coefficient
change in the ordinary Chebyshev representation
\(p_B(x)=\sum_{m=0}^n a_mT_m(x)\). Endpoint half factors from a primed
cosine convention must be included in the actual \(a_m\).

For \(x\in[-1,1]\),
\(|T_m'(x)|=m|U_{m-1}(x)|\le m^2\); the inequality follows from
\(\sin(m\theta)/\sin\theta\) being a sum of \(m\) unit-modulus terms,
with endpoints obtained by continuity. Thus

\[
 |p_B'(x)|\le V_B:=\sum_{m=1}^n m^2|a_m|.
\]

Divide \([-1,1]\) into \(N\) equal closed cells of radius \(1/N\),
with midpoints \(x_k\). For desired sign \(\sigma_B\in\{-1,1\}\),
the computable strict test

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \sigma_Bp_B(x_k)-V_B/N-E_B>0
 \quad\hbox{for every }k
 \quad}\tag{17}
\]

proves that sign for \(F_B\), hence \(H_t(b(t))\), on the entire slab.
All terms in (17) require appropriate outward lower/upper enclosures.
A refined cellwise derivative bound may replace the global \(V_B\) only
when it is itself valid throughout that cell. Two boundary polynomials
with opposite certified signs give (2). An unresolved node, cell or
threshold is a failure of the attempted certificate, not a claimed zero.

The bound (16) allows degree eight, ten, or another frozen degree; it
does not assert success at any such choice. Velocity can enlarge both
the derivative sum and the gauge factor substantially. Both payments
must be evaluated in the numerical contract.

## 6. Exact affine geometry and acquisition gates

For affine boundaries, each of the following is an affine inequality,
so verifying it at \(t=0,1/10\) proves it throughout the slab:

- lower boundary below upper boundary for each bracket;
- an ordering \(u_j(t)<\ell_{j+1}(t)\) for brackets listed left to right;
- each bracket wholly left of \(a-8/5\) or wholly right of \(a+8/5\);
- positive boundaries, and all boundary centers in \([13840,14180]\).

Strict inequalities ensure the target and bracket intervals never overlap.
Selected negative reflections are automatically distinct under these
conditions. On any time cell, the extrema of each affine boundary occur
at the cell endpoints, paying the union bounds used in (4), (7) and (8).
If independently paid target coordinates are cell-dependent, use their
enclosures on the same cell. Every denominator and the final lower-mass
threshold must pass with exact or outward arithmetic.

The frozen acquisition must name all actual rational boundary coefficients,
target-coordinate inputs, node degree, source precision/step/cutoff,
arithmetic and interpolation errors, sign-cell schedule, weight-cell
schedule, and resource cap before the certificate executes. The proof
here neither chooses unmeasured signs nor upgrades Stage A's three-time
candidate matching to trajectories. Refinements and alternate manifests
belong to that separately declared finite contract.

## 7. Controls, comparison and scope

The [full Note21 premise table][premise-table] and [Note27 §6][prior-screen]
are retained. The new premise is a complete-field, whole-slab strict
boundary-sign payment. It supplies a lower count, not a generic source
property. In particular:

| Control family | Consequence for the bracket/mass mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Exact quadratic and even quartic | Outside the target (and, for the quartic, its reflection) they have no real zeros. A supposed additional exterior sign bracket must fail; their existing target geometry can still pass. They test no-extra-mass failure without contradicting the lower-count theorem. |
| Nonzero Gaussian-factor controls | Fail the order-one divisor premise; real sign changes alone do not remove the extra exponential curvature term. |
| Euler-factor translates | Share the physical equation/order; width one can hold or fail as described in Note21. Full-time signs and sufficient mass in these literal windows are untested. |
| Weighted-square, finite-source/canonical-score and finite-prime orbit changes | The specified endpoint-defective classes fail a global finite strip despite physical heat/order and finitely matched data. Any individually verified bracket still supplies a real lower count, but the favorable global remainder is not inherited. |
| Reflected super-Gaussian | Fails order at most one. The sign-bracket counting fact survives, but the present divisor conclusion does not follow. |
| Damped smooth-shape / positive-primitive controls | Share the specified order/physical flow; the required strip, target geometry, actual boundary signs and useful mass remain untested. |
| Reflection rigidity and four-family comparison | Preserve their scoped arithmetic distinctions; no additional sign certificate follows merely from those qualitative identities. |
| BW-01 and finite-profile threshold controls | Their threshold information does not establish this earlier slab's strip or selected bracket signs. The lower-count theorem is generic wherever actual strict signs are paid. |
| RH-conditional endpoint merger | Retains RH and its stated order/strip; added remote doubles do not certify these brackets. A qualifying interval beginning at a target double still has zero exact passage margin. |
| Changed observation / fractional-profile relaxation | The former need not satisfy the literal physical equation; the latter need not furnish an integer divisor. Neither supplies the required complete-field sign oracle merely from compatibility data. |
| Five- and four-atom controls in Note24 | Both fail the frozen width-one strip at their displayed nonreal zeros, as detailed in Note27. Positive weights or undeflated Laguerre positivity do not certify the present boundary signs/mass. |

These are premise screens, not a claim that every altered source fails
every bracket. Source equality pays the canonical oracle; it is not used
as a substitute for checking a derived inequality on controls. The
intermediate-value assertion applies to any continuous real function;
the source and physical-PDE assumptions enter the complete numerical
sign payment, divisor sum and favorable remainder at their stated steps.

The direct method receives all source samples, brackets, target-coordinate
bounds, strip information, reflections and time payments. Its existing
endpoint/minimum certificate remains available. In particular, using (6)
does not acquire new target reality. This route seeks the finite magnitude
\(-K\ge59/100\) through independently verified real exterior lower counts;
no superior evaluation cost or new endpoint enclosure is claimed. A failed
boundary test or failed finite mass allowance stops only that tested
manifest. No source samples, zero searches, or acquired canonical counts
are part of this analytical note.
