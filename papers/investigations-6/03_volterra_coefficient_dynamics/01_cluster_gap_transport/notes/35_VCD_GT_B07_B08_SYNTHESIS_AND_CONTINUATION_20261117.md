## The selected estimate and its actual gain

For the unchanged canonical H_t, 0≤t≤1/2, let a paid isolated pair
have real c,D with c≥16π and |D|≤1. On each slice put x=Reρ−c,
y=Imρ and retain the full nearby exterior sum

\[
 S_R=\sum_{\rho\ \mathrm{exterior},\ |x|\le R}
 \frac{x^2-y^2-D}{(x^2-y^2-D)^2+4x^2y^2},\qquad T_R=M-S_R.
\]

All sums count multiplicities and conjugate groups. At 3≤R≤c/2,
[Note33][far] gives the canonical quantitative estimate

\[
 \left|T_R-\frac{\log(c/(4\pi))}{2\pi R}\right|
 \le C\left[\frac{\log(2+c)}{R^2}
          +\frac{\log(2+c)}{R^3}
          +\frac{\log(2+c)}c\right].
\]

C is absolute but unevaluated. Conjugation is used before approximation,
giving a fourth-power kernel error. The cumulative divisor discrepancy
pays the cutoff and its atoms; remote zeros on both sides are included.
Compact count squeezing extends the input through t=0. There is no
all-real exterior assumption, exp(C/t) onset, singular internal-gap
division, or requirement that D be positive.

Writing L=log(c/(4π)), R=√L and frozen ℓ=1/L, L≥9 gives

\[
 T_R=\frac{\sqrt L}{2\pi}+O(1),\qquad
 \ell^2T_R=\frac1{2\pi L^{3/2}}+O(L^{-2}).
\]

This is a uniform far-divisor main term, beyond the inherited shell
upper payment. The raw contribution grows while its normalized size
vanishes. A fixed sufficiently large physical R instead has positive
mass of order log(c)/R; its normalized size still tends to zero. None
of these statements bounds S_R or establishes a useful full M lower
bound. Sharp-cutoff membership may change with time; the theorem is
slice-wise uniform, and does not differentiate that cutoff.
