## The question at one time

Given the complete theta source at one large physical time \(T\), how many consecutive zeros of \(H_T\) can we locate, with a proved error smaller than their spacing? Can we retain the dependence between their position errors well enough to determine the first departure from equally spaced gaps? The ambition is a useful formula for arbitrarily high labels at a specified \(T\). An intermediate success would be an explicit larger range of labels, a sharper position or separation bound throughout an existing range, or the same accuracy from less source information. The time evolution comes after this comparison.

Use \(H_T(x)=\int_0^\infty e^{Tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(xu)\,du\) and let \(x_k(T)\) be the \(k\)-th positive zero, counted with multiplicity at an independently known all-real anchor. Write \(\mu_T=\mathbb E_T U\) for the mean under the normalized tilted source and \(q_k=(k-\tfrac12)\pi\). The large saddle solves \(4\pi e^{4L_T}=2TL_T+9\), its curvature is \(\kappa_T=8TL_T-2T+36\), and \(\ell_T=\mu_T\sqrt{\kappa_T}\). The physical position is \(x_k\). The variables \(q_k\) and \(\mu_Tx_k\) are dimensionless. ## What the published benchmarks measure

**Polymath supplies an individual-location comparator.** At \(0<t\le1/2\) its high-zero clock has centres \(a_j\) satisfying
\[
 \frac{a_j}{4\pi}\log\frac{a_j}{4\pi}-\frac{a_j}{4\pi}
      +\frac{11}{8}+\frac{t}{16}\log\frac{a_j}{4\pi}=j.
\]
Theorem 1.5 separates the thresholds for the real height and the clock index. Above the respective \(e^{C/t}\) thresholds it accounts for every high zero and gives one zero per counted disk. Section 9 after (104) bounds displacement from the closest centre by \(O(a_j^{-ct}/\log a_j)\). The local spacing is asymptotic to \(4\pi/\log(a_j/(4\pi))\), by differentiating the clock. Thus there is already a decreasing error in units of spacing. Identify the clock index with the branch's rank by a count before comparing either positions or adjacent differences. For finite explicit estimates the paper's Theorem 1.3 has its own domain and errors. [Polymath, Theorems 1.3 and 1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438v2#S1) · [Proof after (104)](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438v2#S9)

There is a concrete intersection at \(t_*=1/2\). All zeros are known to be real there, so the exact product recovery applies, and Polymath's theorem applies at high labels. Test whether finite-order recovery can supply narrower location or spacing intervals, a lower effective height onset, or independent information about common errors. Price any coarse clock or count used to initialize it. For the saddle inverse, the established \(T\to\infty\) theorem gives no common asymptotic range with Polymath's stated time interval. The immediate comparison at large \(T\) is with direct counted localization from identical source data. An overlap for the inverse would itself require proof. [Product hypotheses](../manuscript/anchors.tex) · [Polymath, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438v2#S1)

**Platt–Trudgian supplies finite-height coverage and a bound to improve.** At \(t=0\) their Theorem 1 verifies the critical-line count through zeta ordinate \(3{,}000{,}175{,}332{,}800\). The corresponding physical height is twice this number since \(H_0(x)=\xi((1+ix)/2)/8\). Their Section 2 describes the precision used to ensure the count. Individual large-time position errors are a different observable. Corollary 2 uses the verified height with Polymath's Table 1 to obtain \(\Lambda\le0.2\), improving the \(0.22\) established in the Polymath paper. This sets a published benchmark for a later global consequence. [Platt–Trudgian, Theorem 1 and verification method](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.09765v1#page=2) · [Corollary 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.09765v1#page=5) · [Polymath, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438v2#S10)

## How better positions could improve the dynamics

A common-time list supplies both marked endpoints and their separations from the exterior zeros. For integer \(p,q\ge0,\ p+q\ge2\), estimate the complete mixed moments
\[
 S_{n,p,q}=\sum_{\rho\ {\rm exterior}}
 (\rho-x_{n+1})^{-p}(\rho-x_n)^{-q},\qquad M_n=S_{n,1,1}.
\]
Use \(g_n=x_{n+1}-x_n,\ D_n=g_n^2/4\) in \(D_n'=2-g_n^2M_n\). Existing work supplies every fixed-order row in an interior portion of the growing window, sums the unseen exterior from radial source traces at an all-real anchor, and finds \(\ell_T^2(g_n^2M_n-2)\to\pi^2\) for \(n+1=o(\sqrt{\ell_T})\). The last estimate sets a concrete error scale. Its integrated form uses new information at each time. The desired extension starts with data from just one \(T\), preserves the count, and pays for the higher moment orders entering over time.
