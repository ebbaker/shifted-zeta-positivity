## Direct polynomial and an effective error test

Let \(F(x)=H_t(x)/H_t(0)\), \(e_m=\mathbb E_t U^{2m}/(2m)!\), and \(P_N(x)=\sum_{m=0}^N(-1)^m e_m x^{2m}\). For real \(x\),
\[
 |F(x)-P_N(x)|\le e_{N+1}|x|^{2N+2}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Apply Taylor's formula through degree \(2N+1\) to the real cosine inside the positive source integral; every derivative has magnitude at most one. Thus one more supplied coefficient pays for the entire omitted tail. On \(|x|\le X\) add \(\sum_{m=0}^N|\delta e_m|X^{2m}\) for uncertain inputs, using an upper bound on \(e_{N+1}\). This gives a practical sign test wherever the resulting interval excludes zero. Only the normalized even moments already used by the product enter.

## How fine a localization that budget buys

Let \(u_k\) be the matched high clock centre and \(\Delta_k=u_{k+1}-u_k\asymp1/\log k\). The argument works as well with any fixed rank shift once that shift is known. For large \(k\), the real interval \(I_k=[u_k-d\Delta_k,u_k+d\Delta_k]\), say \(d=1/8\), contains just \(x_k\), and \(|x_k-u_k|=o(\Delta_k)\). Set \(X=u_k+d\Delta_k\). The only additional data used in the asymptotic estimates below are the two-sided coarse clock geometry, including spacing over the full high tail and coarse growth, as also used for the sharp product error. These follow from the labelled estimate after (104) and the derivative of the clock. [Polymath, §9](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438v2#S9)

On \(I_k\) there are constants \(c,C>0\), independent of \(k\), such that
\[
 |F(x)|\ \ge\ e^{-Ck}\frac{|x-x_k|}{k\Delta_k},\qquad
 F(2iX)\le e^{Ck}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Here and below a larger \(C\) can absorb fixed multiplicative constants. To see the lower bound, separate \(1-x^2/x_k^2\) from the normalized even product. It has magnitude at least \(c|x-x_k|/(k\Delta_k)\) since \(x_k\asymp k\Delta_k\). For \(k/2\le i\le2k,\ i\ne k\), coarse spacing and the small radius of \(I_k\) give \(|1-x^2/x_i^2|\ge c|i-k|/k\); for the closest neighbor use the spacing asymptotic \((x_{k+1}-x_k)/\Delta_k\to1\). The product of these lower bounds is at least \(e^{-Ck}\) by the factorial bound \(m!\ge(m/e)^m\). For \(i<k/2\) the factors have magnitude at least one eventually since \(x_{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}/x_k\to1/2\). For \(i>2k\), \(x^2/x_i^2\) stays uniformly below one and its sum is \(O(k)\), using \(x_i\asymp i/\log(2i)\) and \(\sum_{i>2k}\log^2(2i)/i^2=O(\log^2 k/k)\). This proves the lower bound including every exterior root. For the upper bound, sum \(\log(1+4X^2/x_i^2)\). For \(i\le2k\) use \(X/x_i\le Ck/i\), decreasing the lower comparison constant for the finite prefix if necessary. The factorial estimate gives \(O(k)\). The tail is again \(O(k)\). No lower bound on the derivative supplied by field evaluations was used.

All \(e_m\) are positive and \(e_m(2X)^{2m}\le F(2iX)\). Thus (1) is bounded by \(4^{-(N+1)}e^{Ck}\) with exact inputs. For \(0<\epsilon\le d/4\), a field-error allowance less than \(e^{-Ck}\epsilon/(4k)\) suffices to isolate a root within \(\epsilon\Delta_k\) for a sufficiently large constant in (2). For example, bisect the known interval. If an evaluation interval of radius at most that allowance contains zero, (2) bounds the distance of the evaluation point to \(x_k\) by \(\epsilon\Delta_k\). Otherwise retain the bracket with the sign change. The endpoint signs are opposite by the one-root count and simplicity and can be certified by (1)–(2). Bisection takes \(O(1+\log(1/\epsilon))\) tests. With unknown quantitative constants one can instead certify opposite signs on a subdivided mesh, using (1) and refining coefficient accuracy; no value of \(C\) is needed to accept such a bracket.

Consequently \(N=O(k+\log(1/\epsilon))\) suffices, with even moments through \(2N+2\). Uniform relative coefficient errors \(\delta\) contribute at most \(\delta F(iX)\le\delta e^{Ck}\). Sufficient relative input precision is \(O(k+\log(1/\epsilon))\) bits per normalized coefficient, including the final upper allowance; an interval implementation must also enclose polynomial evaluation roundoff. This counts source orders and sufficient coefficient precision. It does not estimate the arithmetic work to generate those moments or prove a necessary condition number. Adjacent intervals of radius \(\epsilon\Delta_k\) give gap uncertainty \(O(\epsilon\Delta_k)\).

For fixed \(\epsilon\), the exchange bound for the product at \(h\asymp k\) asks for moments through \(2kh\), of order \(k^2\). Direct real polynomial evaluation uses a prefix of those same inputs of order \(k\). If both methods receive the full published clock displacement, it already gives \(O(u_k^{-ct})\) in spacing units without moment acquisition. To prescribe smaller errors both methods must price additional information. With \(\epsilon=k^{-A}\) for fixed \(A>0\), the direct sufficient moment order is still \(O_A(k)\). These are comparisons of demonstrated sufficient bounds, with no claim of a lower bound for other product algorithms. The matched-rank derivation and the time-zero verification are in [Note 13](13_EZ_CLOCK_RANK_20261120.md). The same proof needs fixed-time spacing/growth information before use above the last counted large-\(T\) zero.

## Ranked direct comparison at any fixed positive all-real time

Ki–Kim–Lee supply the missing geometric input for each fixed \(T>0\): their consecutive high positive zeros obey \((x_{i+1}-x_i)\log x_i\to4\pi\) in the present physical coordinate. The original formulas use \(\Xi_\mu(v)=8H_{4\mu}(2v)\). The [repository's inspected primary passages, Theorems 1.3–1.4, pp. 282–284](../../../../investigations-5/08_theta_observables_newman_dynamics/03_operator_imaginary_time_evolution/references/07_TND_O_B03_COMPLEX_ZERO_STATISTICS_SOURCE_USE_20261106.md) and [physical normalization](../../../../investigations-5/08_theta_observables_newman_dynamics/03_operator_imaginary_time_evolution/manuscript/sections/11_complex_statistics.tex) record this import. Telescoping \(x\log x-x\) at successive roots gives \(x_i\log x_i\sim4\pi i\), hence \(x_i\sim4\pi i/\log i\). An independently all-real anchor supplies the full positive product, including its finite exceptional part. The proof of (2) now applies in a small neighborhood of the actual \(x_k\) with constants depending on \(T\). The gap theorem alone does not name an isolated centre at the accuracy of one spacing. The direct comparator must pay for rank identification.

One can identify the rank from the same moment polynomial. For \(|z|=r\), each real product factor satisfies \(|1-z^2/x_i^2|\ge |1-r^2/x_i^2|\). Thus \(\min_{|z|=r}|F(z)|=|F(r)|\). Take a search interval of radii with endpoints fixed multiples of \(k/\log k\) bracketing \(x_k\) for all sufficiently large \(k\), and denote its upper endpoint by \(X\). The absolute Taylor tail throughout \(|z|\le X\) is at most \(4^{-(N+1)}F(2iX)\le4^{-(N+1)}e^{C_T k}\), by positivity of the coefficients at imaginary arguments. It also has a finite test using the next moment. The \(e_m\) are elementary symmetric sums of positive inverse squares, hence log-concave. For completeness, for a finite partial product \(f(v)=\prod(1+b_i v)\), Rolle gives real zeros of \(f^{(m-1)}\), so \((f^{(m)})^2-f^{(m-1)}f^{(m+1)}\ge0\) on the real line by its logarithmic derivative. Evaluate at zero and pass to the coefficient limits to get \(e_m^2\ge e_{m-1}e_{m+1}\). Therefore if \(\eta=X^2e_{N+1}/e_N<1\) with interval upper bound \(\bar\eta<1\), the full tail is at most \(e_{N+1}X^{2N+2}/(1-\bar\eta)\), using its upper endpoint. For \(N\ge C'_T k\), \(\eta\le(e_N X^{2N})^{1/N}\le 4^{-1}\exp(C_Tk/N)<1/2\); relative input errors leave a strict ratio test for large \(k\).

Seek two radii \(r_-<r_+\) enclosing the desired position. On each circle certify that the minimum modulus of the centre polynomial exceeds the sum of its coefficient uncertainty and omitted-tail bound. Rouché then identifies its polynomial root count with the complete count. Half this count equals the number of positive roots inside the radius, by evenness, all-reality and \(F(0)=1\). Counts \(2(k-1)\) and \(2k\) before halving give a ranked interval with no input clock offset. Such circles exist on a grid of mesh \(c\epsilon/\log k\) with sufficiently small fixed \(c\): take points on both sides of \(x_k\) between one and three mesh widths away, where the product lower bound (2) is at least \(e^{-C_T k}\epsilon/(C_T k)\). For each such circle the exact minimum occurs at its real endpoints, so polynomial and tail errors smaller than a quarter of this lower bound suffice. A strict lower bound on the centre polynomial or its winding can be determined by certified finite polynomial arithmetic, for example by interval subdivision on the circle with a derivative bound, refining only candidates with clearance. One can enumerate candidates/subdivisions by interleaving trials. This gives the same sufficient \(O_T(k+\log(1/\epsilon))\) moment order and relative input bits, charging for the rank through polynomial counts. Root-counting operations and the cost of producing the moments are unestimated. No exceptional height or onset uniform in \(T\) follows. At \(t=1/2\), the matched clock in [Note 13](13_EZ_CLOCK_RANK_20261120.md) supplies a shorter search interval and the real sign test already suffices. [Ki–Kim–Lee, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2009.04.003)

**What is effective at a specified time.** The circle test itself can be run without a high-clock threshold. For any specified simple neighboring roots of a known all-real field, exact counts on finitely many sufficiently close radii isolate both in arbitrarily narrow intervals. To turn that existence statement into an adaptive procedure, enumerate rational radii and increasing moment orders and precisions in parallel. On every fixed circle avoiding the roots, the polynomial converges uniformly. Its positive-coefficient tail test eventually succeeds: the log-concave ratios tend to zero because the series is entire. With arbitrarily refinable certified moment inputs the strict comparison on that circle is eventually verified. Rational radii avoiding the divisor are dense. Thus a search for bracketing ranks with width at most a prescribed fraction of a simultaneously certified nonzero gap terminates for each such requested rank. The comparable fixed-(T) asymptotic budgets above are stronger information about large ranks; neither supplies an evaluated resource bound at the transition between the two asymptotic regimes.

## Scope and controls

This comparison concerns an all-real product with coarse information on its entire high tail and the real cosine moments. A lattice cosine satisfies (1) and the general product comparison with its own geometry. Weighted-square and finite-tail controls with nonreal divisors fail the all-real premise; a changed observation kernel need not satisfy (1). The other controlled families have different arithmetic inputs or different tail geometry. Whenever an altered source does satisfy the displayed assumptions the enclosure applies to its own roots at the selected time.
