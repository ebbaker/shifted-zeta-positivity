# Where the half-line phase stops counting adjacent zeros

20 November 2026. Prepared for Edward Baker with OpenAI Codex assistance. Conceptual argument and synthesis gpt-6.2-astra/ultra; bounded analytical cross-checks gpt-6.2-sol/ultra. Status **proved in-note** for the canonical half-line lift specified below, using the cited classical square-variable concavity theorem. [Result index](../../../../../result-summaries/README.md) · [Heat-flow results](../../../../../result-summaries/11_newman_heat_flow_reductions_and_certificates.md) · [Patterns](../../../../../avoid/README.md) · [Scoped exclusions](../../../../../avoid/07_scoped_exclusions_read_as_universal.md).

Write \(f_t(u)=e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\),
\[
 B(t,x)=\int_0^\infty f_t(u)e^{ixu}\,du=H(t,x)+iS(t,x),\qquad J=\Im(\overline B B_x).
\]
These are the branch's literal half-line lift and the physical field in [Polymath v2, §1 (3)–(4)](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438v2#S1). The question is whether \(J\) can keep one sign between two distinct positive real zeros. It concerns a *coordinate used to transport a pair*. It makes no assumption about the reality of other zeros.

**Theorem.** Let \(t_{\rm m}=-\Phi''(0)/(2\Phi(0))\). Then \(t_{\rm m}>5/2\). For every \(t\le t_{\rm m}\), \(S(t,x)>0\) for every \(x>0\). Between any two distinct positive real zeros of \(H(t,\cdot)\), the current \(J(t,\cdot)\) takes both signs. This includes every real edge at \(0\le t\le1/2\), with no height cutoff, simplicity hypothesis, or assumption of RH.

**Proof.** The complete theta source is positive, even, smooth and rapidly decreasing. The classical input is strict concavity of \(L(v)=\log\Phi(\sqrt v)\) for \(v>0\), in this normalization. This is the square-variable theorem of Csordas and Varga, Theorem 2.1, also restated by Csordas, Theorem 4.2(b); the latter's (4.2) gives the literal source. Evenness gives \(L'(0+)=\Phi''(0)/(2\Phi(0))\). For \(t\le t_{\rm m}\), \(t+L'(v)<0\) for every \(v>0\). Hence \(f_t(u)\) is strictly decreasing on \(u>0\), also at \(t=t_{\rm m}\). For \(t>t_{\rm m}\) its derivative is positive near the origin. The threshold exactly characterizes this monotone-source argument; it need not be the threshold of any zero phenomenon. [Csordas–Varga, pp. 177–178](https://www.math.kent.edu/~varga/pub/paper_161.pdf#page=3) · [Csordas, (4.2), Thms. 4.1–4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.0055v2#page=10).

Here is an explicit analytical margin. For \(Y=\pi n^2\) the contribution to \(\Phi''(0)\) is \(e^{-Y}(32Y^4-224Y^3+330Y^2-75Y)\). The elementary first-mode and positive-tail bounds in [BW10 Note 05, §1 (2)](../../../../investigations-3/13_contradiction_analysis/04_boundary_winding/threshold_routes/10_rh_implies_simple_zeros/notes/05_CLOSE_POSITIVE_SOURCES_AND_NORMALIZED_DOUBLE_ZEROS_20261020.md) give \(\Phi''(0)<-711/25+18<-10\). Only this unconditional estimate from that note is used. Also \(\Phi(0)<32\sum_{n\ge1}n^4 e^{-3n^2}<2\): consecutive terms have ratio at most \(16e^{-9}<1/100\) and \(e^3>20\). This proves \(t_{\rm m}>5/2\).

For \(x>0\) let \(p=\pi/x\). Pair consecutive half-periods, justified by absolute integrability:
\[
 S(t,x)=\sum_{k\ge0}\int_0^p\!
   \{f_t(2kp+u)-f_t((2k+1)p+u)\}\sin(xu)\,du>0 .
\]
The same classical half-period argument was used for the unheated source in [BW10 Note 05, §2 (4)–(5)](../../../../investigations-3/13_contradiction_analysis/04_boundary_winding/threshold_routes/10_rh_implies_simple_zeros/notes/05_CLOSE_POSITIVE_SOURCES_AND_NORMALIZED_DOUBLE_ZEROS_20261020.md). Now \(B\) lies in the open upper half-plane on \(x>0\). Its continuous argument \(\theta\in(0,\pi)\) equals \(\pi/2\) at both ends of any such pair. Since \(\theta'=J/|B|^2\), its derivative integrates to zero between the endpoints. An identically zero derivative there would force \(H\) to vanish on the whole interval, contrary to analyticity and \(H(t,0)>0\). Continuity gives strictly positive and strictly negative values in the interior. This proves the theorem, including multiple or nonconsecutive endpoints.

**A second obstruction quantifies the height dependence at any fixed time.** With \(I\) any compact interval of real times and \(A_I=\sup_{t\in I}\|f_t''\|_{L^1(0,\infty)}\), two real integrations by parts give
\[
 S(t,x)=\frac{\Phi(0)}x-\frac1{x^2}\int_0^\infty f_t''(u)\sin(xu)\,du .
\]
All boundary terms at infinity vanish; the derivative boundary at zero is multiplied by \(\sin0\). The differentiated square expansion gives \(A_I<\infty\), uniformly, by the double-exponential bound on \(\Phi,\Phi',\Phi''\) (Csordas Thm. 4.1(iv)). For example, writing \(M=\max_{t\in I}|t|\), a majorant for its integrand is \(e^{Mu^2}(|\Phi''|+4Mu|\Phi'|+(2M+4M^2u^2)\Phi)\). Thus the same sign-change proof applies to **every** distinct pair whose two endpoints exceed \(A_I/\Phi(0)\). On each \(0<\tau\le t\le1/2\), Polymath's Theorem 1.5(i)–(ii) and §9 actually give arbitrarily high simple real zeros in the far tail. The two scale statements are compatible with the opening large-time theorem: there \(t\) grows and \(x\) stays within a fixed multiple of a growing saddle window; here \(I\) is fixed. [Source derivatives](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.0055v2#page=10) · [Polymath, Thm. 1.5 and §9](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438v2#S9) · [Opening uniformity assumptions](../manuscript/transport.tex).

**Consequence for propagation.** A more precise 28th moment, a different division of time, or a movable origin for the *argument* cannot make this same \(J\) one-signed across the pair. Adding a fixed multiple of \(2\pi\) to a continuous branch leaves the two endpoint phases equal. Pointwise nodal velocities survive: at a simple zero in \(S>0\), \(J=-SH_x\ne0\), and \(J_x=-SH_{xx}\) there since \(J=HS_x-SH_x\). Thus \(J_x/J=H_{xx}/H_x=\dot x\) at each actual simple node even when the logarithmic curvature averaged across their gap is singular. Regular contours of the physical field, or estimates in separate labelled node neighborhoods with a proof of the count and coverage between them, remain possible. Inherited physical positive-time clock results already use such geometry in a far tail. **Control check.** The sign change is a scoped exclusion of one increasing-phase method. It makes no claim that a physical pair collides. A smooth decreasing colliding control also has \(S>0\) and passes the same sign-change argument; this explains why \(S>0\) supplies no physical zero-exclusion criterion. The pure cosine and the atomic lift fail the density and its endpoint assumptions. Weighted-square, finite-tail and Euler-factor controls alter the canonical \(L\) and endpoint calculation and must check any monotonicity of their own heat weights. Reflection, positive primitives, finite-prime/profile agreement and the RH-conditional normalized perturbations do not supply that calculation. An altered observation fails the given cosine readout. Every sufficiently smooth decaying half-line source with a positive value at zero separately obeys the compact-time endpoint argument once its common derivative bound is paid.
