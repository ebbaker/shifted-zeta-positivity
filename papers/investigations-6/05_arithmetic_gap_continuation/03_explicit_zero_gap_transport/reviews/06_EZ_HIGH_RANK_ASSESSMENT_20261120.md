## Product error and coefficient conditioning

At an all-real anchor the paired factors index the positive roots with their multiplicities. A term in \(E_{j,h}\) removes and inserts the same number of indices across \(j\). The corresponding powers of the threshold cancel. The elementary symmetric sums of the positive lists are bounded by powers of their sums divided by the factorials, giving Note 12 (1) including the infinite tail by monotone convergence. Dividing the resulting enclosures of \(B_{k-1}^h,B_k^h\) in the correct order gives (2). Its logarithmic width in (2) is exact for those endpoints. To convert to spacing units, \(x_k/\Delta_k\asymp k\), and the small logarithmic width then costs \(O(k/h)\) times the combined excitation and relative coefficient errors.

For \(j/2\le i\le2j\), the log of the inverse-square ratio across one high spacing is bounded below by \(c/j\). The geometric midpoint of the threshold makes the nearest term on each side pay half such a step. The remote head contributes at most \(j q^h\) with \(q<1\), and the remote tail is at most a constant times \(j q^{h-1}\) using \(\sum_{i>2j} x_i^{-2}=O(\log^2 j/j)\). Both are bounded by \(O(e^{-c'h/j})\) uniformly for \(h\ge j\). Thus the product width is \(O((k/h)(e^{-ch/k}+\tau))\) in spacing units when both coefficients have relative radius \(O(\tau)\). This uses a strict high-rank split. For a general certified head, the tail bound from \(m_2/2\) and its monotone bound on the first unknown factor are sufficient for the stated truncation of the tail sum.

The \(h\) rotations multiply each factor to \(1-b_i^h v^h\). Truncation through \(kh\) commutes with taking the coefficients used at ranks \(k-1,k\). The rotated coefficient norm is invariant under each rotation and submultiplicative. Multiplying one perturbed factor at a time produces the factor \(h U_R^{h-1}d_R\) in (6), before division by \(R^{jh}\). Since \(E_{j,h}\ge B_j^h\), it suffices to bound \(M_R/(R^j B_j)\) at \(R\asymp (k/\log k)^2\). Its head consists of \(O(k)\) factors bounded by fixed constants; its tail logarithm is at most \(R\sum_{i>j}b_i=O(k)\). Raising to \(h\) genuinely costs the \(e^{O(kh)}\) allowance in this estimate. It is covered by uniform relative coefficient precision \(O(kh+\log(h/\tau))\) bits, and moment order \(2kh\). This is a sufficient conditioning allowance in the given norm. It is not an estimate of necessary precision or of moment generation and polynomial arithmetic work.

## Absolute rank at \(t=1/2\)

Polymath's Theorem 1.5 and the refined bound after (104) give disjoint switching intervals from each clock centre to its actual root. Their contribution to the integral of the error against \(dx/x\) on \([R,2R]\) is \(O(R^{-ct})\), using the \(O(R\log R)\) centres and the \(O(R^{-ct}/\log R)\) interval lengths. These contributions sum dyadically. The full divisor is real and the origin is zero-free by source positivity. Thus (105) integrates to the paired normalized product, with no free linear term. On the imaginary axis, \(K_Y(x)=2Y^2/[x(x^2+Y^2)]\) integrates to \(\log(1+Y^2/x_k^2)\) above each root. Positivity justifies interchange with the count. The finite head and switching error cost \(O(1)\). The zero-mean floor remainder also costs \(O(1)\), since \(K_Y/g'\) is eventually decreasing and uniformly bounded at its starting point.

After averaging the floor, the clock constant becomes \(11/8-1/2+d=7/8+d\), where \(d\) is the fixed rank offset. Integrating against \(K_Y\) doubles this coefficient of \(\log Y\), yielding \(7/4+2d\). The difference weight in the integral of the clock's \(t\log(x/(4\pi))/16\) term has zero total mass by inversion and finite absolute logarithmic moment. This checks that this term contributes \(t\log^2(Y/(4\pi))/16+O(1)\) with no extra coefficient of \(\log Y\) when the same \(4\pi\) is kept inside the squares. The linear-in-\(x\) part gives \(Y(\log(Y/(4\pi))-1)/4\).

Independently, the normalized zero-time source uses \(s=(1+Y)/2\). In \(\log\xi(s)\), the two polynomial powers of \(s\) and the \(-\tfrac12\log s\) from Stirling leave \(3\log s/2\); shifting \(Y\) to \(Y+1\) adds another \(\log Y/4\). The positive Gaussian average has variance \(2t\) and multiplies the central estimate by \(e^{tA_Y^2}\), where \(A_Y=\log(Y/(4\pi))/4+7/(4Y)\). The shifted center \(2tA_Y=O_t(\log Y)\) remains in the \(|v|\le Y^{1/4}\) interval with probability tending to one. Taylor there costs \(O(Y^{-1/2})\), and the two Gaussian tails in Note 13 account for the rest. The independent \(\log Y\) coefficient is \(7/4\). Equality of the two evaluations forces \(d=0\). The midpoint parity check follows from Proposition 9.1(i) and Lemma 9.2, but is unnecessary for this conclusion. Platt–Trudgian's zero-time count and width conversion were also checked. Their count alone does not transfer the rank through a chosen positive-time vertical boundary.
