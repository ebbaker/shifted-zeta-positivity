### Tail, parity and count

Write \(a=x_n<b=x_{n+1}\), \(s=p+q\ge2\), and let \(r\ge R>b\) denote the positive zeros beyond the counted head. The complete even product gives \(u_j=\sum_{r>0}r^{-2j}\) from the coefficients of the logarithm of the normalized theta field. In particular \(u_1=\mathbb E_T U^2/2\). Subtracting the whole positive head gives the residual \(U_j\). This subtraction includes both positive targets. The finite **exterior** sum, by contrast, includes their two negative reflections. Each multiplicity counts separately. [Complete product and coefficient formula](../manuscript/anchors.tex)

Let \(h_d=[v^d]\,(1-bv)^{-p}(1-av)^{-q}\). In a reflected pair's contribution, the \(d\)-th term has coefficient \(1+(-1)^{s+d}\). Thus only \(s+d\) even survives. For \((1,1)\) the leading term is \(2U_1\). For \((2,1)\) it is \(2(2b+a)U_2\). These two rows check the parity and the assignment of the first pole to the upper target. Axis rows use the same expansion with one factor equal to one.

With \(b\le A<R\), each coefficient lies in \([0,\binom{s+d-1}{d}A^d]\). For \(z=A/R<\sigma<1\), every omitted \(d\ge L\) pays \(z^d\le(z/\sigma)^L\sigma^d\), while \(r^{-s}\le R^{2-s}r^{-2}\). Summing the binomial series gives the remainder \(2R^{2-s}U_1(z/\sigma)^L(1-\sigma)^{-s}\). The same argument justifies interchanging the two sums. A uniform ratio \(z\) bounds the geometric rate at fixed order. Uniform evolution of increasing orders would also have to absorb the order-dependent prefactor.

For \(M_n\) one can see the direction of the error directly. The \(j\)-th surviving term is \(2U_{j+1}\sum_{i=0}^{2j}a^ib^{2j-i}\). Truncating after \(j=J\) gives a lower bound. With \(z=b/R\) the remainder is at most
\[
2b^{2J+2}U_{J+2}
\left(\frac{2J+3}{1-z^2}+\frac{2z^2}{(1-z^2)^2}\right).
\]
Indeed the inner sum is at most \((2j+1)b^{2j}\) and each further power of \(r^{-2}\) costs at most \(R^{-2}\). Thus a certified head and complete \(U_j\) give a two-sided estimate for the missing exterior in the gap law. Enclosures for the head positions, their separation, and the source coefficients also enter a usable error budget. Subtracting two nearly equal power sums can consume precision even when the analytic series is rapidly convergent.
