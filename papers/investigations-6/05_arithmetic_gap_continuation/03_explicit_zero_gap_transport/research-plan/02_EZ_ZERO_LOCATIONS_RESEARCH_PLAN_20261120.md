## First deliverable

Write a comparison of the counted loop, the odd-cumulant inverse and cubic, and the full-product recovery at a single physical time. For each give the exact identity, what is known before locating the root, the root count, the proved domain in \((T,k)\), and the error after a specified amount of source information. Display three different quantifiers explicitly: fixed \(k\) as \(T\to\infty\), a growing family \(k\le K(T)\), and \(k\to\infty\) at fixed \(T\). Mark computable sufficient tests separately from asymptotic thresholds with unspecified constants. For the full product distinguish existence of the infinite limit from a finite-order enclosure.

For a proposed formula \(F_T(q_k)\) in physical position units, write \(e_k=x_k-F_T(q_k)\). The two observations are \(e_k\) and \(e_{k+1}-e_k\). A bound on the second can retain a shared mean, source coefficient or phase error that cancels across a gap. Report the uniform position error and adjacent-difference error divided by a stated local spacing, with an independent lower bound if using the actual gap. Give the counted prefix \(K(T)\), the corresponding physical height, and the source orders, tails and auxiliary localization used. Compare observed bounds with direct localization using the *same available inputs*. Compare published theorem bounds on their own hypotheses and identify any additional inputs on either side.

### AGC-EZ-B07 and E10 · Understand and improve the large-time formula

**B07 · Astra/ultra.** Start with the first deliverable above and the [founding resummation](../../brainstorm/notes/08_SUMMING_THE_LARGE_TIME_ZERO_LOOP_20261120.md). The choice is whether better control of the same residual yields more spacing information or whether the first loss is the size of the counted window. Compare at most two candidates. For the first, set \(R_T(q)=Y_T(q)-Y_3(q)\). The physical gap error of the cubic is exactly \([R_T(q_k+\pi)-R_T(q_k)]/\mu_T\). Test this against the best bound obtainable by differencing the known estimates or applying a Cauchy bound to the same analytic data. For the second, try to track \(C\) in the complex saddle argument behind \(q_k\le C\ell_T\). Identify where the complete-source remainder ceases to control the Gaussian relative error, the logarithm, or the inverse. An explicit \(T_0(C)\) or a proved increasing \(C(T)\) would quantify range. Choose one target, comparator, input budget and analytic domain for E10. Permit one revision if the first obstruction suggests a different estimate.

**E10 · Sol 6.2/ultra.** Derive the chosen fixed-time estimate with the stated counts and scaling. Price uncertainty in \(\mu_T\), the retained coefficients, the complex boundary or source tail, and the omitted terms. Report a bound on an indexed family, with the scale of the comparator and the leading distortion it resolves. One proof and one correction or refinement are in scope. Choice of a smaller interior disk or equivalent remainder formula is routine. If the argument needs a different source, a new reference function or an unproved inverse domain, return to B07. A finite check is useful only if it distinguishes two proposed remainder estimates, with that decision stated in advance. ### AGC-EZ-B08 and E11 · Locate high ranks at one specified time

**B08 · Astra/ultra.** Use the validity comparison and E10's result or documented limitation to choose a route for the ranks beyond the effective prefix at a *fixed* upper time. The first candidate is the complete factor formula. Let \(B_j=\prod_{i\le j}x_i^{-2}\). The proof of factor recovery already gives \(B_j^h\le E_{j,h}\le B_j^{h-1}E_{j,1}\) for \(h>1\). This is an input for enclosing \(B_{k-1}/B_k=x_k^2\) with finite data. Test how its width scales with rank, moment order and coefficient errors. A second candidate is to centre a counted contour on a separate high-index approximation and match that labelling to the prefix. Compare at most these two mechanisms and allow one revision. A failed estimate should locate the loss in conditioning, boundary clearance or label identification. [Full-product identity and existing separation-dependent estimate](../../01_jacobi_integer_hierarchy/brainstorm/notes/07_LARGE_TIME_FACTORIZATION_AND_BACKWARD_PROPAGATION_20261120.md)

**E11 · Sol 6.2/ultra.** On the selected route derive one uniform finite-data localization estimate or quantify its failure. Record how \(h\), the required input precision or contour error, and \(k\) relate if the desired error is a specified fraction of the local spacing. State which indices remain outside the proved range and whether their collective effect is still controllable. For the product, use \(t_*=1/2\) as a separate common-time comparison with Polymath at matched sufficiently high labels, in addition to stating what the estimate yields at the large upper \(T\). Give the clock its own count and precision as a direct baseline if it supplied coarse localization. If the chosen formula has no proved overlap with Polymath, document the two domains and the missing inequality. One analytical derivation, one published comparison, one refinement. A change of physical time family or high-index mechanism returns to B08. [Primary benchmark passages](../references/README.md#published-comparisons-for-the-fixed-time-zero-formulas) · [Polymath, §9](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438v2#S9)

**E12 · Sol 6.2/ultra, assessment.** Check an issued position result against the original formula, the analytic constants or quantifiers, the pole/zero count, the error in spacing units and the strongest direct comparison. Verify which published result actually applies. One assessment per substantive position milestone, with one correction pass. Record shared inputs and reviewer provenance. If a claimed gain disappears, return the specific comparison to B07 or B08. The lead may integrate a useful limitation and continue the other position route. The output for dynamics is a counted family at one anchor, position and difference errors with common uncertainties retained, and a bound on every unlocated contribution at the needed moment orders.
