## The exact object carried by the tree

The polynomial of a block counts its roots with multiplicity. The upward message multiplies two such polynomials. For a child, the downward message adds the sibling's logarithmic derivative to the parent's exterior logarithmic derivative. Writing the child's monic polynomial as \(P\) and its exterior field as \(R\), its fixed-coordinate instantaneous velocity is
\[
 P_t=-\operatorname{rem}_{P}\!\left(P_{zz}+2RP_z\right).
\]
At a root of multiplicity \(q\), \(P_z\) already vanishes to order \(q-1\). Consequently exactly one value of \(R\) per distinct root suffices for this velocity. The next derivative needs further spatial data. This distinction persists when two roots meet. The full shared contour trace gives the same polynomial law and the same pair and moment equations, including the products between different exterior blocks. ## Why a pair of exterior marks is informative

Take consecutive real targets \(a<b\) and \(g=b-a\). The complete dimensionless forcing \(K=24D^2 S_2+32D^3 S_3\), \(D=g^2/4\), governs \(g_{tt}=16(K-1)/g^3\). A real exterior occurrence at outward distance \(xg\) from the nearer endpoint contributes
\[
 k(x)=\tfrac12\{x^{-3}-(x+1)^{-3}\}.
\]
If two exterior marks of nominal rank \(m\) straddle the target, their mean normalized distance is determined by their outer span and \(g\). Since \(k\) is convex, opposite first-order displacements cancel in their sum. The remaining Taylor error costs at most \(15\lambda^{-6}[(x_--m)^2+(x_+-m)^2]/m^6\) for \(x_\pm\ge\lambda m,\ 0<\lambda\le1\). Keeping this correlation improves on bounding both distances by the larger one. Direct summation supplied the same correlated inputs attains the same estimate. For a nonreal mark write \(r\) for the complex displacement measured outward on its side. Its forcing is the integral of \(3g^3(r+s)^{-4}/2\) over \(0\le s\le g\). Thus \(\Re r\ge(1+\sqrt2)|\Im r|\), \(\Re r>0\), suffices for a nonnegative real contribution. In a known horizontal strip the potentially adverse marks are confined to a finite target-centered box.
