## A merge tensor whose output is the squared spacing

Fix a real time and a height window. Give each subinterval a thin rectangular box symmetric about the real axis. On zero-free boundaries the literal field gives the number of zeros and their first two power sums about a common real origin \(o\),

\[
(N_B,P_B,Q_B)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial B}
 (1,z-o,(z-o)^2)\frac{\partial_z H(t,z)}{H(t,z)}\,dz .
\]

The argument principle counts multiplicities. Parent moments are the sums of child moments because internal edges have opposite orientations. Moving a split away from a zero, or combining cells and using their outer contour, permits a count even when the original split is unusable. If each cell stores its own origin, set \(h=o_{\rm child}-o_{\rm parent}\) and translate its moments by \(P\mapsto P+hN,\ Q\mapsto Q+2hP+h^2N\) using the original \(P\). [AGC counted-pair coordinates](../../05_arithmetic_gap_continuation/manuscript/sections/02_pair_chain.tex)

Set \(S_B=N_BQ_B-P_B^2\). Expanding shows that \(S_B=\sum_{i<j}(z_i-z_j)^2\) over the zero occurrences in the box. In a common coordinate the merge is

\[
 S_{L\cup R}=S_L+S_R+N_RQ_L+N_LQ_R-2P_LP_R.
\]

The cross term sums the squared differences across the dividing line. With one zero on either side, both \(S_L\) and \(S_R\) vanish, and the cross term gives the entire squared gap. With messages \((1,N,P,Q,S)\), the displayed merge and the additions for \(N,P,Q\) are bilinear in the two child messages. They define a literal fixed-size tensor at each merge.

At a symmetric count-two box, \(S_B=4D\) in the manuscript's notation. Its sign distinguishes a separated real pair, a double and a nonreal conjugate pair. For \(S_B>0\), \(\sqrt{S_B}\) is the Newman spacing and \(\sqrt{S_B}/2\) the corresponding zeta-ordinate spacing at \(t=0\). For a box with more than two zeros the same channel sums over *all* pairs. To request a particular adjacent gap, use counts to locate the two ranks and combine cells covering just those roots, including any empty cells between them. ## Where a tensor approximation could earn its cost

The five-channel merge works for any holomorphic field with cleared contours. For one preselected pair, cancellation of the internal contours gives exactly the direct outer-contour calculation. The opportunity is to compute the expensive boundary data once across **many** neighboring pairs. Giving the tree tabulated zeros would only measure compression of an existing gap table.

A specific ansatz is to factor the table \(H(t,X_j+s_\ell)\) across box centers \(X_j\) and common local offsets \(s_\ell\) on their boundaries. For example, truncate the literal source at \(v=V\), call the resulting field \(H_V\), and use

\[
 H_V(t,X_j+s)\ \simeq\ \sum_{k=0}^{p}\partial_x^k H_V(t,X_j)\,s^k/k!.
\]

All boxes share the same functions of \(s\). For real \(X_j,t\), Taylor's integral remainder gives an entrywise bound
\[
 \frac{(|s|V)^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}\int_0^V W_t(v)e^{|\Im s|v}\,dv,\qquad
 W_t(v)=e^{tv^2}\Phi(v).
\]
The omitted source costs at most \(\int_V^\infty W_t(v)e^{|\Im s|v}\,dv\). These are absolute bounds. To use the factors for counts or logarithmic derivatives requires bounds relative to the field on the walls, derivative control (for example by a wider collar) and working-precision errors. The coefficients themselves cost arithmetic evaluations. This gives a concrete factorization to compare, including against scalar Taylor evaluation using the same coefficients. The worthwhile question is how that cost and the required degree grow at a fixed *gap accuracy*.

**Original fixed-window proposal, retained as a possible later example.** At \(t=0\), fix the window \(40\le x\le80\) before inspecting any zero ordinates. Request every internal consecutive real gap with a certified interval of width at most \(10^{-6}\) in Newman units. Acquire the counts and boundary clearances from the source. Give the direct root/contour method the same geometry, caching, source information and precision choices. Compare total acquisition and evaluation work and peak storage at this accuracy. A lower cost for the batch, or tighter final gap intervals at equal cost, would give a measured reason to develop the ansatz. This is a proposed test. A harder external-to-the-QTT-box benchmark already has a [count and direct endpoint interval near \(14010.1\)](../../03_volterra_coefficient_dynamics/01_cluster_gap_transport/notes/16_VCD_GT_CANONICAL_SIGNED_GAP_SYNTHESIS_20261116.md). Increasing depth in a fixed window controls resolution. Covering increasing heights is a separate limit requiring rank, tail, conditioning and cost estimates.
