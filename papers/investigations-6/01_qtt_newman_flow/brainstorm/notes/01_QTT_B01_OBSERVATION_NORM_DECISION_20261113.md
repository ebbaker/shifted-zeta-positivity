## The bounded comparison

All three candidates are compared with the same scalar polynomial baseline:
approximate the full positive-half-line source on [0,2], multiply by a fixed
heat polynomial, and integrate the resulting polynomial against literal
cos(zu). The baseline keeps the full continuum tail. Polynomial Fourier
integrals can be evaluated exactly as mathematical expressions; no lower
bound on practical baseline cost is assumed.

1. **Ordinary source polynomial QTT, selected.** Analyticity on one explicit
   ellipse gives an elementary source approximation rate; the inherited
   heat Taylor polynomial gives a uniform orbit; the same polynomial has
   explicit QTT rank at most its degree plus one. The complete signed
   observation pays the exponential transverse weight, a height-dependent
   scaling factor and midpoint error. All needed constants follow from
   the literal theta series. The method can lose badly against direct
   polynomial integration; an honest unfavorable comparison is useful.
2. **Observation-weighted source QTT, deferred.** On this fixed box the
   natural sufficient norm is integral e^(8u)|error(u)| du. Factoring
   e^(8u) into the state changes which approximation is built but does not
   remove Fourier cancellation or the height scaling. No sharper
   approximation constant or complete-cost gain has yet been supplied.
   This is a selection judgment about this first round, not an obstruction
   to adaptive or cancellation-aware weighted approximation.
3. **QTT of the normalized complete arithmetic expression, deferred.**
   It could remove gamma-scale cancellation analytically, but requires
   choosing an arithmetic tensor ordering and retaining both arms and
   remainder. No transformed source-coordinate generator or all-time
   contract is currently frozen. That conceptual development would exceed
   this one round. No impossibility claim is made about this candidate.

## Why the first target is concrete

Use Newman heights 40<=x<=80, offsets |y|<=8 and 0<=lambda<=1/2.
Set S(x)=exp(pi*x/8)/(1+x)^(7/4) and measure
sup S(x)|H_lambda(x+iy)-Htilde_lambda(x+iy)|. This is an explicit
nonvanishing scale, justified by the inherited real-axis time-zero gamma
scale. It is **not** exact division by the completed gamma factor at every
offset or time, and is never division by H. It therefore remains defined
at zeros. The box includes the inherited offsets 0,4,8 without importing
the Green logarithm/anchor/deflation theorems as acquired conclusions.

The first trial is U=2, ellipse rho=5/4, source degree d=512, heat degree
m=64, and L=72 binary sites. The nominal rank is at most 641, independent
of 2^72 formal sample locations. These deliberately conservative values
are proposed sufficient parameters, not measured optimal ranks. E01 must
prove or refute their 10^-8 allowance. It must not allocate the dense grid.
One bounded correction may use d<=640,m<=80,L<=80 on the same box.

For the scalar baseline the same initial and heat polynomials give exactly
the same source/evolution allowance, and exact polynomial integration
removes the midpoint contribution. Thus this method offers no stronger
error guarantee than its scalar baseline. Whether construction or repeated
contraction becomes advantageous is a separate unmeasured cost question.

## Collision-control screen

This is a controlled approximation, not a noncollision estimate. An actual
colliding analytic control may satisfy the generic approximation theorem;
no premise of that theorem must fail at its event. This is essential:
accurate representation must retain a collision, not exclude it. The
theta-specific numerical constants are only derived for the complete
series, and are not assigned to other sources without checking them.

| Catalog family | Actual failed premise if one tried to import the literal theta calculation; interpretation here |
| --- | --- |
| Euler-factor/translated sources | Unchanged theta/xi source fails. Their smooth-source approximation can still hold, including on event-containing boxes; no sign contradiction. |
| Weighted-square, finite-prefix and finite-prime-orbit controls | Complete coefficients together with full reflection fail. A half-line analytic bound may still apply with their own constants. Their event times are not asserted finite thresholds. |
| Reflected super-Gaussian | The canonical integer-square series fails. Smoothness/positivity alone do not distinguish its event. |
| Smooth shape/positive-primitive controls | Complete canonical arithmetic fails; matching shape, endpoints and primitive asymptotics do not imply our particular theta constants. No approximation theorem is claimed to reject them. |
| Atomic and finite-profile threshold controls | Atomic sources fail analytic density assumptions; continuous extensions need their own regularity constants. Complete theta profile/arithmetic and eventual threshold simplicity are absent. The inherited rank-one event already defeats a low-rank sign argument. |
| RH-conditional endpoint controls | Ordinary Dirichlet-series arithmetic fails; conditional existence is kept conditional. Approximation near their added doubles remains compatible with them. |
| Fixed source/variable readout controls | Literal cos(zu) and its Newman evolution fail. This request keeps that observation exactly. |
| Actual-profile positive-measure relaxation | Its measure is not an integer zero configuration or the literal theta source; its feasible objective is not a value of this field. |

This analytical screen uses the catalog's existing witnesses, not rerun
certificates. No favorable inequality contradicted by one of those
witnesses is asserted. The only prospective numerical gain is a complete
compact absolute-error/rank allowance. Working precision, signed Green
acquisition, useful all-height ranks and practical speed remain unclaimed.
