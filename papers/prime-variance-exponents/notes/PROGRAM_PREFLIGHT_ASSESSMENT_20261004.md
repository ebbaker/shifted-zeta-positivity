## Recommendation

The best three programs remain **01 signed arithmetic covariance**, **02
source-selective Sonin loss**, and **03 signed prime-pair dispersion**, in
that order. This ranking concerns readiness and the information likely to
come from the next bounded investigation; it is not a probability of proving
a new constant-width zero-free strip.

**Target program 01 next, specifically the centered Vaughan block covariance
and its scalar mismatch.** Its exact interface is now derived, and the
Möbius cofactor identity supplies a second coordinate system for testing an
actual arithmetic cross-term mechanism. Program 02 is the strongest
parallel option. Program 03 is the best alternate arithmetic formulation.

The [original overview](PROJECT_OVERVIEW_20261004.md) correctly identifies
the common difficulty: one fixed kappa>0 must work on every sufficiently
large real shell. Logarithmic savings in a cubic variance estimate,
positive density exponents, finite positivity, and better constants alone
do not meet that target. Preliminary work below sharpens the interfaces
and rejects some easy approaches; it does not resolve the missing estimate.

## Why these three

**01 has the best-defined arithmetic object.** Easy sectors have already
been removed with proved norm budgets. This pass finishes the exact matrix
interface and shows that the linear mismatch cannot be hidden by projection
or by changing identities. It gives a focused way to accept or reject a
new covariance mechanism. Its main weakness is decisive: no genuinely
power-saving estimate for that signed form is currently supplied.

**02 gives a source-dependent alternative to an obstructed ambient bound.**
The allowance is weaker than the historical RH target, and the reference
energy stays bounded on the actual translated sources. The required
head/residual and mixed costs can be stated in one exponential budget.
It ranks below 01 because the arithmetic approximation needed in the
inverse metric is less explicit. Growing heads remain possible; fixed
finite-rank repairs and full ambient positive absorption do not solve it.

**03 makes exceptional costs and cancellations transparent.** This pass
shows exactly how much of the shift range can be left to elementary bounds,
and how a hypothetical almost-all estimate would transfer. This avoids
asking for unnecessarily strong uniform RH-scale pair errors. It still
needs an actual signed prime-pair theorem at proportional shifts; the
available logarithmic almost-all input does not reach the gate.

Programs 04 and 05 should be used when they expose a specific advantage
for 01 or 03. Program 06 is the most distinct analytic fallback. Programs
07–09 currently offer reformulations or scouts, with no identified input
that beats the more concrete first three. Program 10 should follow the
active analytic question.

## Concrete next investigation: centered arithmetic overlap

Use the exact notation and capped coefficients in
[program 01](programs/01_signed_arithmetic_covariance/PRELIMINARY_INVESTIGATION_20261004.md).
For B_(U,V)=sum_j B_j, U=V=X^(11/24), let G-perp be the Gram matrix
of B_j projected orthogonally off x, and lambda_j=<B_j,x>/||x||².
As a concrete accounting target, take kappa=0.01:

\[
\mathbf1^TG^\perp\mathbf1\ll X^{2.99+o(1)},\qquad
\left|c_wM_1(U)+\sum_j\lambda_j\right|
\ll X^{-0.005+o(1)}.
\]

This value is an audit budget, not a prediction. Any other fixed positive
saving would meet the first milestone. These two estimates together would
give the exact variance exponent delta=0.99 and zero boundary beta<=0.995
through the inherited endpoint theorem. They are not proved here.

The next bounded deliverable is one complete arithmetic-overlap identity
after projection, together with the size of its scalar and cutoff-annulus
terms. Use the exact prime-place relation only if it produces information
beyond its full-sum identity; otherwise test the K=3 multilinear blocks.
Keep the entire signed sum before applying Cauchy–Schwarz.

Continue that mechanism only if it isolates a new product-level arithmetic
inequality with a plausible positive exponent budget. Record failure if
it reduces to a power Mertens assumption, a uniform factorwise bound
already implying a strip, a reconstruction of the original response, or
uncontrolled low frequencies/mismatch. The task is to identify and audit
the missing inequality before enlarging computations.
