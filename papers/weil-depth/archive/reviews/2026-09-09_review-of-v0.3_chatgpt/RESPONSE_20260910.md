## 2. The finite profile does not prove super-exponential decay — restated

The abstract now reads as the review suggested (even sector at every horizon; rapidly
increasing logarithmic decay rate across the sampled horizons, consistent with the
empirical Landau–Widom picture; no decay law at unbounded depth proved). The "three
features" paragraph of the introduction keeps the finite-difference slopes (about 11 and 33
per unit horizon) as observations on seven horizons, says explicitly that they prove no
decay law as `L → ∞`, and describes Zhu's law as an empirical fit stated as a conjecture,
whose only proved large-window form is conditional on RH (checked against the abstract and
Sections 12–13 of arXiv:2608.24827v2). The shift intervals are described as shrinking with
the floors along the sampled horizons; the end of Section 7.1 no longer says they collapse
"super-exponentially". `README.md`, `STATUS.md` (new row "Decay of the enclosures with
`L`: observed, not proved") and `CONTINUATION.md` were aligned.

## 3. The estimated horizon near 2.7 is not a proved ceiling — restated

The abstract now says the paper estimates the rapidly increasing computational cost of
extending the scalar tail bound; "cannot reach … beyond 2.7" is gone. Section 7.2 opens by
declaring its figures resource projections for the present implementation, not
impossibility bounds, and records the reviewer's observation: for fixed `0 < L < 3` and any
admissible `M`, `K_L` and `Σ_L` are finite, so `a_{N,L} → +∞` as `N → ∞` and the scalar
floor alone never obstructs the test at a fixed horizon. It states that whether a floor
near 0.6 suffices at an uncomputed horizon is not known (the required coupling has risen
with each new prime), keeps the `N` estimates as "would need", qualifies the `10^-34` per
unit figure as a local extrapolation of Table 1, keeps `L < 3` as the domain of the proved
profile remainder, and replaces "can reach perhaps log 13 to log 16 with effort and stops
there" by the suggested wording (demanding for the present implementation; no prediction of
success or failure). `STATUS.md` and `CONTINUATION.md` ("Beyond log 8") carry the same
qualification.

## 4. Denominator bound in the Laplace-line proof — corrected

The proof of Lemma 3.3 now writes the rational factor explicitly,
`ρ_s(p) = (p+α)(p−β)/((p−α)(p+β))` with `α = 1/2 − s ∈ [0,1/2]`, `β = 1/2 + s ∈ [1/2,1]`,
and bounds only the denominator factors: `|p−α| ≥ Re(p−α) ≥ η − 1/2` and
`|p+β| ≥ η + 1/2` on the line. It notes that the numerator factor `p−β` can have modulus as
small as `η − 1`, which is harmless. The false statement `|p±α|, |p±β| ≥ η − 1/2` is
removed. The conclusion of the lemma and everything downstream are unchanged; `STATUS.md`
records the correction.

## Additional clarifications — done

* **Classical comparison.** The related-work paragraph now states the hypotheses as
  printed: Yoshida (as quoted in the introduction of Connes–Consani) for smooth
  positive-definite functions supported in `(1/2, 2)` with Fourier transform vanishing at
  `±i/2`; Connes–Consani Theorem 1 for `g` supported in `[2^{-1/2}, 2^{1/2}]` with Fourier
