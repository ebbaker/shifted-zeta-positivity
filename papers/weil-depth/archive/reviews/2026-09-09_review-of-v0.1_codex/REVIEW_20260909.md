# Review of the finite-horizon Weil draft

9 September 2026. Baseline: branch `finite-horizon-weil`, commit
`2ca3c96b374a06c86819cdc665b22bdb3fc54299`, and the supplied v0.1 review ZIP.

## Assessment

I found no invalidating error in the main analytic reduction or in the
`Q_(0,9/5) >= 1e-26 I` certificate. The full calculation reproduces, and a
second implementation written from the printed equations also passes. The
continuation guide's proposed `log(7)` experiment succeeds at its initial
parameters. Version 0.2 includes that result, more explicit proof details,
stronger verification code, and precise formulations of the proposed recursion.

This assessment is a machine-assisted mathematical and computational review,
not an external human referee report or proof-assistant verification.

## New endpoint result

At total input horizon `L = log(7)`, with 128 Legendre modes, degree 220, and
1792-bit Arb arithmetic, both full-operator Schur inequalities pass at
`m = 1e-34`. The retained dimension is 64 in each parity sector.

| Quantity | Independently recomputed value or outward bound |
|---|---:|
| Infinite-tail floor | `> 0.60748624181467` |
| Smooth-profile variation | `< 5.839924` |
| Profile norm remainder | `< 9.342e-42` |
| Total analytic Schur error | `< 3.737e-37` |
| Full-output Gram trace | `< 1712` |
| Minimum even LDL pivot (approximate) | `1.6916652823e-5` |
| Minimum odd LDL pivot (approximate) | `9.0360554449e-5` |
| Generator-change constant | `< 13.541169 < 14` |

The pivots establish signs; they are not spectral lower bounds. The certified
central floor is the explicitly subtracted `1e-34`, justified by the infinite
tail and exact parity leakage argument.

The exact rational continuation check is

`1e-34 - (14/3)(3e-18)^2 = 5.8e-35 > 5e-35`.

It proves `||V_(omega,L)|| <= exp(-5e-35 omega)` for
`0 < omega <= 3e-18` and `0 < L <= log(7)`, with reflection lower bound
`1-exp(-5e-35 omega)` and cumulative storage lower bound
`1-exp(-1e-34 omega)`.

The endpoint is exact: `n=7` has zero delay operator almost everywhere.
The generator indices remain 2, 3, 4, 5; the transfer includes 6; the reflected
ordered pairs remain (2,2), (2,3), (3,2). No extra retained modes were needed.
The diagnostic required tail floors are about 0.59479118 and 0.58991735,
close to the available 0.60748624. This supports trying a better tail treatment
before assuming that the same dimension will suffice at the next prime.

## Analytic checks and manuscript changes

1. **Normalization.** I checked Suzuki's printed equations (2.1)–(2.4),
   including the factor `n^(-1/2)` introduced by logarithmic coordinates.
   The finite Euler product gives the stated sign and factor 2 in the
   causal derivative. The pole term is `2|C|^2-2|S|^2` for arbitrary complex
   tests; writing it in autocorrelation form makes its translation invariance
   and the distinction between total length and half-width explicit. See
   [Suzuki v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.1827v2).
2. **Domains.** The polynomial form-core proof is sound: dilation and
   mollification give compact smooth approximants; uniform approximation of
   the derivative then gives polynomial approximation in the logarithmic
   norm. The finite-rank projection is bounded in that norm. I found no step
   that substitutes convergence in ordinary L2 for form-norm convergence.
3. **Energy argument.** On a right Laplace line, positive-shift decay gives
   the smoothing needed for the logarithmic generator. Restriction uses a
   Sobolev exponent strictly below 1/2, avoiding endpoint extension problems.
   Strong convergence at zero is sufficient for Gronwall; the proof does not
   require an operator-norm derivative there. Definition 2.1 now explicitly
   uses `0 < omega <= 1/2`, matching the construction actually given.
4. **Entire tail.** The fractional estimate controls arbitrary combinations,
   rather than individual modes. I added the exact weighted Jacobi norm and
   the elementary inequality `x(L-x) <= L^2/4` to expose the constants. The
   gamma ratio and differentiated harmonic-number floor agree. Relevant
   reference formulas are in [DLMF 18.3](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.3) and
   [DLMF 18.17](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.17).
5. **Remainders.** The radius-3 Cauchy bound and the integrated variation
   bound reproduce the numerical constants. Finite ball radii do not replace
   this analytic remainder. I added a proved refinement: bounding the change
   of the omitted-output column directly reduces the Schur error to
   `(|a-m|+2*kappa_r)*eta + eta^2`, with
   `kappa_r >= ||(I-P) Q_tilde P_r||`. A certified upper bound on
   `sqrt(trace(E_r))` is sufficient. Existing certificates retain the original
   conservative error, so no numerical conclusion depends on this refinement.
6. **Parity and storage.** The full-output identity
   `E_r = (F_r+r*C_r)/2-q_r^2` is correct, including both mixed-prime overlaps.
   The storage bound follows by minimizing the complete defect form. Neither
   identity alone proves that a new arithmetic slab preserves positivity.
7. **References and scope.** Zhu's current v2 metadata and half-width 0.8
   were verified; the reported total length is 1.6. Its certificate is not
   used as an input. I did not independently audit its numerical claims or
   perform an exhaustive novelty search. See
   [Zhu v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24827v2).

## Computational checks

All original ZIP checksums matched. The standalone and packaged PDFs have
identical extracted text on all 14 pages despite different binary file sizes.

The original source was rebuilt at `L=9/5`. A separate implementation was
started from the PDF before the ZIP became available, then completed without
using the original builder's reflected tail-logarithm assembly. It normalizes
the basis before matrix products and integrates reflected gamma-delay terms
over the opposite end of the interval. At each horizon, all 16,384 entries of
each of the head, causal Gram and reflected Gram overlap between the two
implementations. Both independently saved Schur matrices also pass replay.

Additional checks include 45 high-precision moment quadratures, 51 direct
arithmetic coefficient-derivative identities (including zero shift and mixed
prime integers), three multi-mode fractional-tail checks, and a finite
Toeplitz parity-identity control. These numerical identity checks are
diagnostics; the certification itself uses Arb throughout. The independent
quadratures agree with the moment formulas to better than `1e-70`.

The code now also tests failing cases: indefinite and singular LDL matrices,
arithmetic threshold activation, optimized-Python rejection, stale certificate
hash rejection, and rejection of a negative proposed shift.

## Reproducibility issues corrected

These were weaknesses in future/replay use of the code, not evidence that the
recorded v0.1 positive tests were false.

- The analyzer used `9/5`-specific rounded constants for every archive. It now
  derives a checked generator bound from the loaded horizon and verifies the
  shift/decay inequality with exact rational arithmetic. Explicit inputs
  reproduce the two paper statements; default inputs use a rational halving
  rule to find a sufficient positive shift.
- The analyzer trusted a saved `PASS` string without binding it to the actual
  matrix archive. It now verifies the hash and metadata and reruns the ball
  sign tests before reporting a continuation bound.
- Replay relied on saved scalar bounds and leakage. It now recomputes the
  analytic bounds, checks the full Gram trace, and reconstructs leakage from
  the saved head and full Grams, checking consistency with the archived data.
- The builder warned users not to use `python -O` but allowed it to disable
  assertions. It now refuses optimized Python. A failed sign test now returns
  a nonzero exit status.
- The builder unnecessarily restricted the analytic model to `1<L<2`. Its
  mathematical domain is now `0<L<3`, with exact finite-path cosine norms and
  strict threshold handling. This change makes future experiments possible;
  it does not certify every accepted horizon.
- Source hashes and verification status accompany the revised deliverables.

## Generalizing the recursion

The paper now derives an exact factorization of the new defect `I-V*V` for
`V=[[X,0],[Y,Z]]`. If the previous defect is strictly positive and `||Z||<1`,
the next step is governed by

`C = (I-ZZ*)^(-1/2) Y (I-X*X)^(-1/2)`.

Contraction is equivalent to `||C||<=1`. Strict inequality gives a new
invertible defect factor that can be carried into the next append. Equality
can leave a singular defect, so an inverse-based recursion cannot silently
continue. The full factor records how the next coupling meets the weak old
directions. A two-dimensional exact counterexample in the paper shows that
equal old minimum eigenvalues can lead to opposite update signs.

A second derivation divides degree space into an old head, a finite buffer,
and a far tail. It eliminates the far tail and then the buffer, retaining the
cross-Gram between their couplings. With the same scalar far-tail bound this
is algebraically the existing larger-head test, organized for factor reuse.
It becomes a stronger estimate only if one proves a more informative lower
bound for the tail or reduces the certified remainder.

The priority order I recommend is:

1. Try the next prime-active horizon `log(8)` with a fresh model/error budget;
   it includes 7 in the generator and excludes the endpoint delay 8. Do not
   reuse the `log(7)` shift interval or assume 128 modes still suffice.
2. Implement the proved smaller leakage error and compare it on the already
   certified cases before using it at greater depth.
3. Track the full normalized coupling and defect factors across horizons;
   inspect which old directions the new prime couples to. Candidate weak
   directions may come from ordinary numerics, but final comparisons require
   enclosed basis transformations, residuals and complete output.
4. Test a finite buffer and a structured tail lower bound. Preserve cross-Grams;
   replacing them by separate scalar norms erases the information sought.
5. For longer intervals, replace the origin-centered gamma profile by validated
   Taylor or Chebyshev pieces. The logarithmic singular kernel can remain
   exact; all new piecewise-polynomial breakpoints and reflected overlaps must
   be included. This addresses the radius restriction, not positivity itself.

The missing theorem remains an arithmetic bound that preserves these
conditions for an unbounded sequence of depths. The diagonal RH implication
in the original draft is correct as an implication, but this review does not
construct that sequence. The earlier second-slab shift gap and first-slab
primary-certificate audit remain separate tasks.
