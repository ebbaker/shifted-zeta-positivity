# The 128-mode central certificate at total horizon 1.8

9 September 2026. Research continuation and reproducibility record, not a manuscript.

## 1. Result

**The previously unresolved full-operator estimate at total horizon \(L=9/5\) now closes.** A new implementation using Arb ball arithmetic throughout gives

\[
\boxed{Q_{0,9/5}\succeq10^{-26}I.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Here \(Q_{\omega,L}\) is the symmetric logarithmic shift generator of the causal transfer \(V_{\omega,L}\), with the normalization of `ARITHMETIC_DEPTH_RESULTS_20260906.md`. The estimate includes the infinite-dimensional tail and all output outside the retained polynomial space. It is not a finite-section eigenvalue claim.

The analytic continuation lemma then gives

\[
\boxed{\|V_{\omega,L}\|\le e^{-5\times10^{-27}\omega}<1,
\quad 0<\omega\le3\times10^{-14},\quad 0<L\le9/5.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

The precise status is a **computer-assisted working certificate**: all finite arithmetic in the new construction and sign tests is enclosed by Arb, while the passage to the full operator uses the analytic estimates proved below and in the preceding report. Independent mathematical and code review is still appropriate. There is no all-depth theorem or RH conclusion.

Since \(9/5>\log6\), this crosses the first mixed-prime transfer threshold. The active prime powers in the generator are \(2,3,4,5\). The transfer also contains the \(n=6\) contribution, generated by composing the delays for 2 and 3; there is no \(\Lambda(6)\) term in the generator.

## 3. Operator and finite-model definitions

On \(L^2(0,L)\), write \(T_df(x)=\mathbf1_{x>d}f(x-d)\), and let \(R\) be reflection about the midpoint. The generator is

\[
Q_{\omega,L}=Q^\gamma_{\omega,L}
-\sum_{\log n<L}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
\cosh(\omega\log n)(T_{\log n}+T_{\log n}^*).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let \(U=\partial_\omega V_{\omega,L}|_{0}\) on the common core. Then
\(Q_0=-(U+U^*)/2\), and causal convolution gives \(U^*=RUR\). In unit coordinates \(u=x/L\), the central gamma derivative on monomials is

\[
U^\gamma u^k=u^k(\log u+\ell-H_k)
+\sum_{j\ge1}g_jL^j\frac{(j-1)!k!}{(j+k)!}u^{j+k},
\qquad \ell=\gamma+\log(2\pi L),
\tag{3.2}
\]

where

\[
G_0(t)=\frac{e^{t/2}t}{\sinh t}-4t\cosh(t/2)
=\sum_{j\ge0}g_jt^j,
\qquad (g_0,g_1,g_2)=(1,-7/2,-1/24).
\]

The arithmetic part of \(U\) is
\(2\sum\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}T_{\log n}\). All profile coefficients and polynomial coefficients are constructed as exact rationals or integers before conversion to balls. The model \(\widetilde U\) retains \(j\le M=180\).

Let \(P=P_{128}\) project onto the first 128 normalized shifted Legendre polynomials, of degrees 0 through 127. Each reflection sector has 64 retained modes. The new code works directly with the causal derivative, storing

\[
\widetilde Q_P=-\tfrac12P(\widetilde U+\widetilde U^*)P,
\quad F=P\widetilde U^*\widetilde U P,
\quad C=P\widetilde U^*R\widetilde U P.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The matrices \(F\) and \(C\) integrate the complete polynomial/logarithmic/delayed outputs. For input reflection parity \(r=\pm1\),

\[
\widetilde Q_0f=-\tfrac12(\widetilde Uf+rR\widetilde Uf),
\qquad
F_{Q,r}=\tfrac12(F_r+rC_r),
\]

\[
E_r=F_{Q,r}-\widetilde Q_{P,r}^{\,2}
=P_r\widetilde Q_0(I-P)\widetilde Q_0P_r.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus \(E_r\) is the exact omitted-output coupling squared for the truncated analytic profile. It is not obtained by squaring a finite compression and treating that as a full Gram.

The reflected delayed-output overlaps are included exactly when \(\log(n_i n_j)<L\). At this horizon the ordered overlap pairs are \((2,2),(2,3),(3,2)\). In particular, both mixed-prime cross terms are present. Threshold decisions are strict and fail if an interval comparison is unresolved; logarithmic-horizon equalities use exact integer comparisons.

## 4. Analytic bounds on the infinite tail and omitted profile

Put \(w(t)=(G_0(t)-1)/t\). The gamma derivative splits as

\[
U^\gamma=\left.\partial_\nu[(2\pi)^\nu I_\nu]\right|_{\nu=0}+S_w,
\]

where \(I_\nu\) is causal fractional integration and \(S_w\) is causal convolution. The logarithmic part is unbounded; only the displayed smooth correction and subsequent generator differences are treated as bounded operators.

For a tail combination of Legendre modes of degree at least \(N\), the Jacobi fractional-integration identity and weighted orthogonality give

\[
\|I_\nu(I-P_N)\|\le(L/2)^\nu
\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(N+1-\nu)}{\Gamma(N+1+\nu)}},
\qquad 0<\nu<1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

To see the operator, rather than individual-mode, estimate: fractional integration maps the degree-\(n\) Legendre polynomial to
\(n!x^\nu P_n^{(-\nu,\nu)}(2x/L-1)/\Gamma(n+1+\nu)\). Bound
\(x^{2\nu}\le(L/2)^{2\nu}x^\nu(L-x)^{-\nu}\), apply Jacobi orthogonality to the entire linear combination, and use the decreasing gamma ratio in \(n\). Differentiating the squared inequality at zero on polynomial tails gives the lower bound
\(H_N-\gamma-\log(\pi L)\) for the negative symmetric logarithmic derivative. The endpoint \(\nu\uparrow1\) gives
\(\|I_1(I-P_N)\|\le L/(2\sqrt{N(N+1)})\). Closure extends the form inequality.

Because \(S_w=(w(0)I+S_{w'})I_1\), a bound

\[
K_L\ge|w(0)|+\int_0^L|w'(t)|\,dt
\]

costs at most \(K_LL/(2\sqrt{N(N+1)})\) on the tail. Finite delay chains give norm \(\sqrt2\) for \(T_{\log2}+T_{\log2}^*\) at \(L=9/5\), and norm 1 for the corresponding \(n=3,4,5\) operators. Hence

\[
a_{N,L}=H_N-\gamma-\log(\pi L)
-\frac{K_LL}{2\sqrt{N(N+1)}}
-\sum_{\log n<L}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\|T_{\log n}+T_{\log n}^*\|
\tag{4.2}
\]

is a full infinite-tail lower bound.

For completeness, the Cauchy majorant used here is \(|G_0(z)|<256\) on \(|z|=3\). The Euler product of \(\sinh\) gives \(|z/\sinh z|\le3/\sin3<24\); also \(e^{3/2}<5\), so \(|G_0(z)|<5(24+12)<256\). The code verifies the two elementary strict inequalities with balls. Thus, for \(L<3\),

\[
\|U-\widetilde U\|\le\eta_M
=\frac{256(L/3)^{M+1}}{(M+1)(1-L/3)},
\tag{4.3}
\]

and

\[
K_L\le\sum_{j=1}^{M}|g_j|L^{j-1}
+\frac{256}{3}\frac{(L/3)^M}{1-L/3}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Equation (4.3) refers to the bounded difference of the unbounded derivatives. It follows by integrating the absolute omitted convolution kernel and applying Young's inequality.

At \(N=128,M=180,L=9/5\), outwardly checked rounded bounds are

\[
a>0.69124708426711,\qquad K_L<5.482684,
\qquad \eta_M<2.477\times10^{-40}.
\]

The arithmetic term in (4.2) is approximately 2.39376738699.

## 5. Decisive matrix tests and arithmetic accounting

Write the central form in retained/tail blocks. If the tail is bounded below by \(aI\), then \(Q_0\succeq mI\) follows from

\[
(a-m)(Q_{P,r}-mI)-E_{Q,r}\succeq0
\qquad(r=\pm1),
\tag{5.1}
\]

where the exact coupling squared is used. This is the form Schur complement with the tail inverse bounded by \((a-m)^{-1}I\).

The full model-output Gram trace is enclosed and checked below \(10^7\), implying \(\|\widetilde U P\|<10000\). Symmetry and parity projection give the same bound for \(\widetilde Q_0P\). Consequently, replacing exact head and coupling by their degree-180 model changes (5.1) in norm by at most

\[
\epsilon_M=(|a-m|+40000)\eta_M+2\eta_M^2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Indeed, the head contributes \(|a-m|\eta_M\); the full-output Gram and the squared head each contribute at most \(20000\eta_M+\eta_M^2\). The code takes an outward lower value of \(a\), an outward upper value of \(\eta_M\), and subtracts an outward upper value of \(\epsilon_M I\) before LDL. Here

\[
\epsilon_M<9.908\times10^{-36}.
\]

| Retained dimension | 64 | 64 |

Pivots are not eigenvalue lower bounds. The central floor is the explicitly subtracted \(m\), justified by the full Schur reduction.

The run used **1536-bit working precision**, Python 3.12.14, python-flint 0.9.0, and FLINT 3.6.0. The largest radius among the four constructed matrix enclosures was below \(9.341\times10^{-215}\). Such finite-arithmetic uncertainty is propagated throughout LDL; it is not silently dropped or replaced by the analytic profile error.

The matrix archive stores dyadic midpoint and radius pairs. Every export checks that reconstructing the ball encloses the original ball; reconstruction may enlarge a radius outward. A separate execution loaded the saved archive and repeated both sign tests successfully. Decimal displays in this report are summaries, not inputs to the sign decisions.

The new construction was also compared with all shared entries of the previous 96-mode head and the two full-output Grams, after converting the old Hankel sign convention to the causal convention. All differences were below \(3\times10^{-217}\). This is a useful check on normalization, logarithmic moments, and reflected-delay algebra. The old point matrices are diagnostic comparison inputs only; the new certificate does not depend on their rounding budget.

## 6. Small-shift and storage consequences

The established bounded-difference estimate is

\[
\|Q_{\omega,L}-Q_{0,L}\|\le C_L\omega^2,
\qquad 0\le\omega\le1/2,
\]

where one may take

\[
C_L\le\cosh(L/2)\left(e^{L/2}\frac{L^3}{3}+\frac{L^2}{4}\right)
+\frac12\sum_{\log n<L}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
(\log n)^2\cosh(\log n/2)\|T_{\log n}+T_{\log n}^*\|.
\tag{6.1}
\]

At \(L=9/5\), direct ball evaluation gives \(C_L<10.298615<11\). Applying the energy identity on the common core at positive shift, integrating, and using the strong zero-shift limit yields

\[
\|V_{\omega,L}\|\le\exp(-m\omega+C_L\omega^3/3).
\]

With \(m=10^{-26}\) and \(h=3\times10^{-14}\), the exact rational check is

\[
m-11h^2/3=6.7\times10^{-27}>5\times10^{-27},
\]

which proves (1.2) at the outer horizon. Causal compression proves it at every smaller horizon.

For any spatial split, write
\(V=\begin{pmatrix}X&0\\Y&Z\end{pmatrix}\). Then the cumulative storage Schur complement satisfies

\[
\boxed{\mathcal S=I-X^*X-Y^*(I-ZZ^*)^{-1}Y
\succeq(1-e^{-10^{-26}\omega})I.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

This follows by minimizing the quadratic form of
\(I-V^*V\succeq(1-e^{-10^{-26}\omega})I\) over the second block. In particular, the split \(9/5=\log5+(9/5-\log5)\) crosses the new arithmetic region. This is a storage consequence of separately certifying the larger operator; it is not an inductive preservation theorem derived from the previous slab's storage alone.

## 7. Implications and next target

This result confirms that the earlier failure at \(L=1.8\) was a limitation of the head/tail reduction at 96 modes, not evidence of nonpositivity. Retaining more polynomial directions captures enough additional cancellation that exact parity coupling plus a scalar tail floor suffices.

It also establishes a more auditable computational method: future changes to depth or retained dimension no longer require a guessed extension of the earlier aggregate construction-error budget. The analytic tail and profile remainder remain essential, and increasingly small central margins may require greater precision or stronger relative error estimates.

**The next concrete depth target is \(L=\log7\)**, covering the full interval after the first mixed-prime threshold. Use the same ball implementation and exact parity reduction, adapting \(N\) only if the Schur test requires it. A subsequent horizon strictly larger than \(\log7\) activates a new prime delay. The continuing conceptual target is a structured tail/storage estimate that explains positivity across these updates, rather than only a growing list of independently certified horizons.

The earlier second-slab positive-shift gap remains open. The first-slab primary-Arb audit is a separate unresolved calculation; this new central calculation does not close that audit. No uniform-in-depth estimate or all-depth sequence has been established.
