\begin{abstract}
We study the central Weil form $Q_{0,L}$ on complex test functions supported
in an interval of total length $L$, together with the causal finite-horizon
transfer $V_{\omega,L}$ of Suzuki's shifted zeta ratio. A computer-assisted
argument that uses no information about zeta zeros encloses the bottom of the
spectrum of $Q_{0,L}$ from both sides at the seven horizons
$L=\log2,\log3,\log4,\log5,\log6,9/5,\log7$; in particular
\[
 1.37\times10^{-28}\le\lambda_{\min}(Q_{0,\log7})\le6.81\times10^{-28},
\]
so that $Q_{0,L}\succeq1.37\times10^{-28}I$ for every $0<L\le\log7$. The
horizon $\log7$ lies beyond $\log6$: the generator contains the prime-power
delays $2,3,4,5$, and the transfer contains the first mixed-prime composition
$6=2\cdot3$. Lower bounds combine an analytic lower bound on the infinite
Legendre tail, obtained by fractional integration, with full-output Gram
matrices that retain the exact reflection-parity coupling; the resulting
$64\times64$ Schur inequalities are verified in ball arithmetic with an
explicit analytic profile remainder. Upper bounds are ball Rayleigh quotients
of retained polynomial vectors. At every certified horizon the bottom of the
spectrum lies in the even reflection sector, and across the sampled horizons
the two-sided enclosures exhibit a rapidly increasing logarithmic decay rate,
consistent with the empirical Landau--Widom picture; no decay law at unbounded
depth is proved. An even-in-shift generator identity converts each floor into
a small-shift contraction, for instance
$\norm{V_{\omega,L}}\le\exp(-6\times10^{-29}\omega)$ for
$0<\omega\le4\times10^{-15}$ and $L\le\log7$, with quantitative
reflection-positivity and cumulative-storage bounds. A second implementation
reproduces every certificate, and the matrices were checked against the
Fourier-side definition of the form. The results are finite-depth statements:
they give neither a zero-free region nor an all-depth positivity theorem, and
we estimate the rapidly increasing computational cost of extending the present
scalar tail bound to larger horizons.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Weil positivity; Riemann zeta function; causal
convolution; Hankel operators; Schur complement; validated computation.

\section{Introduction and main result}

Weil's positivity criterion expresses the Riemann hypothesis as positivity of
an explicit quadratic form on all compactly supported test functions. Fixing
a bounded support produces a separate, finite-horizon question: is the
restricted form coercive, and can an explicit positive lower bound be proved
without information about zeta zeros? Such a result controls only a bounded
part of the criterion, but provides a test of methods intended to extend to
larger arithmetic depth.

This paper addresses that question using the causal transfer associated with
the shifted quotient
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta}
 \Theta_\omega(z)=
 \frac{\xi(\tfrac12-\omega-iz)}{\xi(\tfrac12+\omega-iz)},\qquad
 \xi(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s).
\end{equation}
Suzuki introduced this family and its arithmetic Hankel kernel
\cite{Suzuki2016}. Our statements concern the finite causal convolution
defined in Section~\ref{sec:operators}; no global innerness assumption is made.

Throughout the paper, $L$ is the \emph{total length} of the input interval.
Its centered version is $(-L/2,L/2)$, and its autocorrelation is supported
in $[-L,L]$. The corresponding multiplicative cutoff is $a=e^{L/2}$.
This convention is important when comparing arithmetic thresholds or
compact-window bounds.

\begin{theorem}[Two-sided finite-horizon bounds and contraction]\label{thm:main}
For each row of Table~\ref{tab:main}, with horizon $L_j$, floor $m_j$, upper
bound $\mu_j$ and constants $C_j,h_j,d_j$, the central form \eqref{eq:weil}
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mainenclosure}
 m_j\le\lambda_{\min}(Q_{0,L_j})\le\mu_j,\qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(Q_{0,L}):=\inf\{Q_{0,L}[f]:f\in\DD_{\log,L},\ \norm f_2=1\},
\end{equation}
and the lower bound holds in each reflection sector separately, with the
sector-wise floors and upper bounds of Table~\ref{tab:sectors}. Consequently,
for every $0<L\le L_j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maincentral}
 Q_{0,L}[f]\ge m_j\norm f_2^2\qquad(f\in\DD_{\log,L}).
\end{equation}
For the transfer $V_{\omega,L}$ of Definition~\ref{def:transfer},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maintransfer}
 \norm{V_{\omega,L}}\le e^{-d_j\omega}<1,
 \qquad 0<\omega\le h_j,\quad 0<L\le L_j,
\end{equation}
and $H_{\omega,L}=V_{\omega,L}R_L$ is self-adjoint with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mainreflection}
 I\pm H_{\omega,L}\succeq(1-e^{-d_j\omega})I.
\end{equation}
The bounds \eqref{eq:mainenclosure} are computer-assisted
(Theorem~\ref{thm:computation} and Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}); the
remaining implications are analytic.
\end{theorem}

In particular $Q_{0,L}\succeq1.37\times10^{-28}I$ for $L\le\log7$ and
$Q_{0,L}\succeq3.38\times10^{-23}I$ for $L\le9/5$. The total horizons $9/5$
and $\log7$ are strictly larger than $\log6$. There the logarithmic generator
has precisely the delays $\log2,\log3,\log4,\log5$, while the coefficient of
$n=6$ occurs in the transfer as a product of two prime factors, although
$\Lambda(6)=0$; in the full-output calculation the ordered mixed overlaps
$(2,3)$ and $(3,2)$ are retained explicitly. At the exact endpoint $L=\log7$
the delay $T_{\log7}$ vanishes on $(0,L)$, so $\log7$ is the largest horizon
with this generator.

Three features of Table~\ref{tab:main} deserve comment. First, the enclosures
are tight: the ratio $\mu_j/m_j$ is below $1.3$ through $9/5$ and below $5$ at
$\log7$, so the floors are within a small factor of the true bottom of the
spectrum, and the smallness of the constants is a property of the form, not of
the method. Second, the enclosures fall rapidly along the profile, at an
increasing logarithmic rate: $-\log_{10}\mu_j$ grows by about $11$ per unit
horizon between $\log2$ and $\log3$ and by about $33$ per unit horizon between
$9/5$ and $\log7$. These finite-difference slopes are observations on seven
horizons. They prove no decay law as $L\to\infty$, which is exactly the regime
that remains open; they are consistent with the empirical Landau--Widom law
that Zhu \cite{Zhu2026} fits to his data and formulates as a conjecture, and
whose only proved large-window form is conditional on RH. Third, the shift
intervals $h_j$ are fixed by $m_j$ and the quadratic perturbation bound of
Lemma~\ref{lem:change}, $h_j\approx\sqrt{3m_j/2C_j}$, and therefore shrink
with the floors along the sampled horizons; only at $\log2$, where no prime
term is present, is the interval macroscopic.

The proof has three components. First, differentiating the finite Euler
product gives a generator even in $\omega$ and a bounded perturbation
$Q_{\omega,L}-Q_{0,L}=O_L(\omega^2)$. Second, a fractional-integration estimate
gives a lower bound on the infinite Legendre tail. Third, full-output Grams
control the coupling between that tail and the retained space, separately in
each reflection sector. The finite comparison is a Schur inequality at the
claimed positive level $m_j$; positivity of a finite compression alone
would not suffice. Upper bounds need only the retained head.

\subsection{Relation to earlier work}

Positivity of the Weil form on windows of total length at most $\log2$, where
no prime term is present, is classical, with the following hypotheses. Yoshida
\cite{Yoshida1992} proved, in the formulation quoted in the introduction of
\cite{ConnesConsani2020}, that the Weil functional is nonnegative on smooth
positive-definite functions supported in $(1/2,2)$ whose Fourier transform
vanishes at $\pm i/2$. Connes and Consani \cite[Theorem~1]{ConnesConsani2020}
prove, for $g$ supported in $[2^{-1/2},2^{1/2}]$ with Fourier transform
vanishing at $i/2$ and at $0$, the stronger inequality bounding the functional
of $g*g^*$ below by the trace of a manifestly nonnegative operator built from
the compressed scaling action, as part of the operator-theoretic program
pursued with Moscovici and van Suijlekom. In the coordinates of
Section~\ref{sec:operators} the support condition is total length $\log2$,
and the vanishing conditions at $\pm i/2$ remove the pole contribution of the
explicit formula, which in the normalization of \eqref{eq:weil} is the
indefinite term $2|C(f)|^2-2|S(f)|^2$. Our $\log2$ row concerns the full form
\eqref{eq:weil} on all of $\DD_{\log,\log2}$, pole term included and with no
vanishing condition. On test functions for which the pole term vanishes,
\eqref{eq:weil} reduces to the pole-free functional, so there the row
restricts, up to the normalization of the functional, to a quantitative
version of the classical statement; elsewhere it is a different statement,
and we have not matched the two normalizations term by term.
Beyond $\log2$ prime terms enter, and the positivity statements we are aware
of are computer assisted. Zhu \cite{Zhu2026} certifies
$Q\succeq8.9\times10^{-18}$ at total length $1.6$ (his window parameter is a
half-width, so his $L=0.8$ is total length $1.6$ here) through a pointwise
envelope for the Weil symbol and a single finite matrix, together with the
certified upper bound $2.27\times10^{-17}$; he observes that the window ground
state is simple and even, and argues that any such one-stroke certificate must
resolve frequencies up to a threshold that is doubly exponential in the
horizon. Our $\log5$ row has the same active primes as his window and, since
$\lambda_{\min}$ is nonincreasing in $L$, implies
$\lambda_{\min}(1.6)\ge8.95\times10^{-18}$ by a different reduction, consistent
with his reported enclosure. The closeness of the two lower bounds is
compatible evidence, not a cross-check of either implementation: lower bounds
are not estimates with two-sided error bars, and the two windows differ. We do
not use his certificate. The specific contributions here
are the fractional tail bound of Section~\ref{sec:tail}, the full-output
parity reduction of Section~\ref{sec:schur}, two-sided certificates at seven
horizons through $\log7$, and the explicit positive-shift consequences. The
doubly exponential threshold appears in our setting as the growth of the
arithmetic loss in the scalar tail floor; Section~\ref{sec:limits} quantifies
it. We make no claim that finite-matrix approaches to Weil positivity are
themselves new.

The estimates are finite-depth. They neither establish global innerness of
\eqref{eq:theta} nor prove a new zero-free region, and the storage corollary
of Section~\ref{sec:storage} does not supply an induction in arithmetic depth.

\section{Causal transfer and the central form}\label{sec:operators}

\subsection{Finite-horizon definitions}

Let $\HH_L=L^2(0,L;\CC)$, with inner product linear in the second argument,
and define
\[
 R_Lf(x)=f(L-x),\qquad T_df(x)=\mathbf1_{x>d}f(x-d).
\]
In particular, $T_d=0$ for $d\ge L$ and
$T_dT_e=T_{d+e}$. Every adjoint and norm below refers to unweighted
$L^2(0,L)$.

Put $\gam(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$. For $0<\omega\le1/2$
the archimedean causal convolution has Laplace transfer
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gammaK}
 K^\gamma_\omega(p)=
 \frac{\gam(\tfrac12+p-\omega)}{\gam(\tfrac12+p+\omega)}.
\end{equation}
It may be defined on a sufficiently far right Laplace line, followed by
restriction to $(0,L)$. For an explicit impulse, set
$\alpha=\tfrac12-\omega$, $\beta=\tfrac12+\omega$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gammafactor}
 K^\gamma_\omega(p)=\pi^\omega
 \frac{(p+\alpha)(p-\beta)}{(p-\alpha)(p+\beta)}
 \frac{\Gamma((p+\alpha)/2)}{\Gamma((p+\beta)/2)}.
\end{equation}
The last ratio is the transform of
$2e^{-\alpha t}(1-e^{-2t})^{\omega-1}/\Gamma(\omega)$.
The rational factor has an impulse consisting of a delta mass and smooth
exponentials (with the usual limiting interpretation at $\alpha=0$).
Thus the full impulse $\kappa_\omega$ is locally integrable, behaves as
$O(t^{\omega-1})$ at the origin, and defines a bounded finite-horizon
convolution $V^\gamma_{\omega,L}$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:transfer}
For $0<\omega\le1/2$, define
\begin{align}
 b_\omega(n)&=n^{\omega-1/2}\prod_{p\mid n}(1-p^{-2\omega}),
 & b_\omega(1)&=1,\label{eq:bcoeff}\\
 B_{\omega,L}&=\sum_{\log n<L}b_\omega(n)T_{\log n},
 &V_{\omega,L}&=B_{\omega,L}V^\gamma_{\omega,L}.\label{eq:Vdef}
\end{align}
Set $V_{0,L}=I$ in the strong-limit sense proved below.
\end{definition}

The sum is finite and contains all integers below $e^L$. This is the
logarithmic-coordinate form of Suzuki's arithmetic kernel. The Euler product
identity for its Dirichlet series also identifies the full Laplace transfer,
on a sufficiently far right line, as
$\xi(\tfrac12+p-\omega)/\xi(\tfrac12+p+\omega)$.
Real causal convolution gives $V_{\omega,L}^*=R_LV_{\omega,L}R_L$;
therefore $H_{\omega,L}=V_{\omega,L}R_L$ is self-adjoint and
$\norm{H_{\omega,L}}=\norm{V_{\omega,L}}$.

\subsection{The central Weil form}

For $f\in\HH_L$, let $f_c(x)=f(x+L/2)$ on $(-L/2,L/2)$ and zero elsewhere.
Write
\[
 \widehat f_c(\tau)=\int_{\RR}f_c(x)e^{-i\tau x}\,dx,\qquad
 C(f)=\int_{\RR}f_c(x)\cosh(x/2)\,dx,\quad
 S(f)=\int_{\RR}f_c(x)\sinh(x/2)\,dx.
\]
The logarithmic form domain is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:domain}
 \DD_{\log,L}=\left\{f\in\HH_L:
 \int_{\RR}\log(2+|\tau|)|\widehat f_c(\tau)|^2\,d\tau<\infty\right\}.
\end{equation}
With $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$, define
\begin{align}
 Q_{0,L}[f]={}&\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\RR}
 \bigl(\Rea\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{i\tau}{2})-\log\pi\bigr)
 |\widehat f_c(\tau)|^2\,d\tau\notag\\
 &+2|C(f)|^2-2|S(f)|^2
 -\sum_{\log n<L}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
 \ip f{(T_{\log n}+T_{\log n}^*)f}.
 \label{eq:weil}
\end{align}
Here $\Lambda(p^k)=\log p$ and is zero otherwise. The last scalar is real.
The Fourier multiplier differs from $\log(2+|\tau|)$ by a bounded function,
by the digamma asymptotics \cite[\S5.11]{DLMF}. All remaining terms are
bounded. Hence \eqref{eq:weil} is a closed semibounded form on
\eqref{eq:domain}. It is the central geometric Weil form in this normalization.
For example, if $g=f_c*\widetilde f_c$ with
$\widetilde f_c(x)=\overline{f_c(-x)}$, its pole contribution is
$\int g(t)(e^{t/2}+e^{-t/2})\,dt=2|C(f)|^2-2|S(f)|^2$.
Its arithmetic contribution is
$-\sum\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}(g(\log n)+g(-\log n))$, exactly the delay term
above. This also makes translation invariance explicit for complex tests.
It commutes with reflection and with complex conjugation. The same notation
$Q_{0,L}$ denotes its associated self-adjoint operator where appropriate.

\begin{lemma}[Polynomial core]\label{lem:core}
Polynomials restricted to $(0,L)$ form a core for $Q_{0,L}$.
If $P_N$ is the orthogonal projection onto the polynomials of degree less
than $N$, polynomial combinations in $(I-P_N)\HH_L$ are a form core for
the restriction to that subspace.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Equip \eqref{eq:domain} with its weighted Fourier norm, equivalent to a
shifted form norm. First shrink the centered zero extension by a dilation
about the origin so that its support lies in a smaller interval. Dilations
tending to the identity converge in this weighted norm: their norms are
uniformly bounded, since the logarithmic weights at dilated frequencies are
comparable, and strong convergence follows first on smooth Fourier data and
then by density. Mollification with support smaller than the resulting
boundary gap converges in the weighted norm by dominated convergence.
Consequently $C_c^\infty(0,L)$ is a form core.

For $h\in C^1[0,L]$, integration by parts in its zero extension gives
\[
 |\widehat h(\tau)|\le L\norm h_\infty,\qquad
 |\widehat h(\tau)|\le
 \frac{2\norm h_\infty+L\norm{h'}_\infty}{|\tau|}\quad(\tau\ne0).
\]
These bounds imply continuity from $C^1[0,L]$ to the logarithmic form
domain. Approximate the derivative of a smooth function uniformly by a
polynomial and integrate, matching one value. This gives polynomial
approximation in $C^1$, hence in form norm. Endpoint jumps of the zero
extension cause no difficulty.

Finally $P_N$ is bounded in form norm: it has finite rank, its range consists
of form-domain polynomials, and its coefficient functionals are bounded in
$L^2$. Apply $I-P_N$ to the polynomial approximants of a vector orthogonal
to the retained space.
\end{proof}

\section{The prime-delay generator and continuation}\label{sec:generator}

\subsection{Exact logarithmic differentiation}

Write $\ell(p)=\log\gam(\tfrac12+p)$ on a far right half-plane. The
archimedean logarithmic generator has symbol
\begin{align}
 a^\gamma_\omega(p)&=\ell'(p-\omega)+\ell'(p+\omega)\notag\\
 &=\frac1{p+\alpha}+\frac1{p-\alpha}
 +\frac1{p+\beta}+\frac1{p-\beta}-\log\pi\notag\\
 &\hspace{18mm}+\frac12\psi((p+\alpha)/2)+\frac12\psi((p+\beta)/2).
 \label{eq:agamma}
\end{align}
It defines a causal logarithmic operator $A^\gamma_{\omega,L}$ on its natural
core. Its symmetric form at zero is the gamma part of \eqref{eq:weil}.
For example, the two central rational terms give the symmetric kernel
$2\cosh((x-y)/2)$, whose form is $2|C|^2-2|S|^2$; the digamma term gives
the multiplier in \eqref{eq:weil}.

\begin{proposition}[Prime-power generator]\label{prop:generator}
On the causal generator core,
\begin{align}
 \partial_\omega V_{\omega,L}&=-A_{\omega,L}V_{\omega,L},\label{eq:derivative}\\
 A_{\omega,L}&=A^\gamma_{\omega,L}
 -2\sum_{\log n<L}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
 \cosh(\omega\log n)T_{\log n}.\label{eq:Ageneral}
\end{align}
Thus its symmetric form is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qgeneral}
 Q_{\omega,L}=Q^\gamma_{\omega,L}
 -\sum_{\log n<L}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
 \cosh(\omega\log n)(T_{\log n}+T_{\log n}^*).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The commuting nilpotent delay algebra gives
\[
 B_{\omega,L}=\prod_{\log p<L}
 (I-p^{-1/2-\omega}T_{\log p})
 (I-p^{-1/2+\omega}T_{\log p})^{-1}.
\]
Each inverse is a terminating geometric series. Expanding the logarithm
of one factor, the coefficient of $T_{k\log p}$ is
$(p^{-k/2+k\omega}-p^{-k/2-k\omega})/k$.
Its derivative is
$2(\log p)p^{-k/2}\cosh(k\omega\log p)$.
Differentiating the product in \eqref{eq:Vdef} and using commutation with
causal gamma convolution proves \eqref{eq:Ageneral}. Symmetrization gives
\eqref{eq:Qgeneral}. Only the bounded arithmetic factor has been inverted;
no bounded inverse of the smoothing transfer is asserted.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A bounded quadratic change in the shift}

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rkernel}
 r(t)=e^{t/2}-\frac{e^{-5t/2}}{1-e^{-2t}},\qquad t>0.
\end{equation}
\begin{lemma}[Bounded shift difference]\label{lem:change}
The forms $Q_{\omega,L}$, $0\le\omega\le1/2$, have the common domain
$\DD_{\log,L}$. Their gamma difference has symmetric kernel
\[
 (\cosh(\omega|x-y|)-1)r(|x-y|).
\]
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:changebound}
 \norm{Q_{\omega,L}-Q_{0,L}}\le C_L\omega^2,
\end{equation}
as a bound on the bounded form difference, where
\begin{align}
 C_L={}&\cosh(L/2)\left(e^{L/2}\frac{L^3}{3}+\frac{L^2}{4}\right)\notag\\
 &+\frac12\sum_{\log n<L}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
 (\log n)^2\cosh(\log n/2)\norm{T_{\log n}+T_{\log n}^*}.
 \label{eq:CL}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In the digamma integral \cite[\S5.9]{DLMF}, form
$a^\gamma_\omega-a^\gamma_0$ before separating the divergent terms. Its
digamma part has inverse Laplace kernel
$-2e^{-t/2}(\cosh(\omega t)-1)/(1-e^{-2t})$.
The rational terms contribute
$4\cosh(t/2)(\cosh(\omega t)-1)$.
Since
$2\cosh(t/2)-e^{-t/2}/(1-e^{-2t})=r(t)$,
symmetrization gives the asserted kernel. It is $O(|x-y|)$ at the diagonal
and extends continuously in $\omega$ through $1/2$. This physical-space
identity avoids taking an incomplete pointwise digamma limit at the endpoint.

Now $|r(t)|\le e^{t/2}+1/(2t)$ and
$0\le\cosh(\omega t)-1\le\omega^2t^2\cosh(t/2)/2$.
The row-integral bound for the symmetric kernel is at most
$\omega^2\cosh(L/2)\int_0^L(t^2e^{L/2}+t/2)\,dt$.
Bounding each arithmetic difference in \eqref{eq:Qgeneral} in the same
way gives \eqref{eq:CL}. Bounded perturbations preserve the closed-form
domain.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Laplace-line estimates]\label{lem:laplace}
Fix $\eta>1$ and write $p=\eta+i\tau$, $\tau\in\RR$. For $0\le s\le\tfrac12$ let
\[
 K_s(p)=\frac{\xi(\tfrac12+p-s)}{\xi(\tfrac12+p+s)},\qquad
 a_s(p)=-\partial_s\log K_s(p).
\]
Then $a_s(p)=a^\gamma_s(p)-2\sum_{n\ge1}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\cosh(s\log n)\,n^{-p}$
with an absolutely convergent series, $K_0\equiv1$, and there is a constant
$C_\eta$ such that for all $0\le s\le\tfrac12$ and $j\in\{0,1,2\}$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laplace-bounds}
 |\partial_s^jK_s(p)|\le C_\eta(1+\log(2+|\tau|))^j(2+|\tau|)^{-s},
 \qquad |a_s(p)|\le C_\eta(1+\log(2+|\tau|)).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $K_s=K^\gamma_sK^\zeta_s$ with
$K^\zeta_s(p)=\zeta(\tfrac12+p-s)/\zeta(\tfrac12+p+s)$. Since
$\Re(\tfrac12+p\mp s)\ge\eta>1$, both zeta values are absolutely convergent
Dirichlet series, bounded above and below in modulus uniformly in $\tau$ and
$s$, and their $s$-derivatives are Dirichlet series with coefficients
$O(\log^jn)$, also absolutely convergent and bounded. The logarithmic
derivative gives the displayed arithmetic part of $a_s$. For the gamma factor,
\eqref{eq:gammafactor} gives $K^\gamma_s=\pi^s\rho_s(p)\,
\Gamma(\tfrac{p+\alpha}2)/\Gamma(\tfrac{p+\beta}2)$ with the rational factor
$\rho_s(p)=(p+\alpha)(p-\beta)/((p-\alpha)(p+\beta))$, where
$\alpha=\tfrac12-s\in[0,\tfrac12]$ and $\beta=\tfrac12+s\in[\tfrac12,1]$. Its
denominator factors satisfy $|p-\alpha|\ge\Re(p-\alpha)\ge\eta-\tfrac12$ and
$|p+\beta|\ge\eta+\tfrac12$ uniformly on the line (the numerator factor
$p-\beta$ can be as small as $\eta-1$ in modulus, which is harmless), so
$\rho_s$ is bounded with bounded $s$-derivatives. By the
uniform Stirling expansion \cite[\S5.11(iii)]{DLMF},
$|\Gamma(z+a)/\Gamma(z+b)|$ is comparable to $|z|^{\Re(a-b)}$ uniformly for
$z=p/2$ on the line and $a,b$ in a compact set; here $\Re(a-b)=-s$, so the
gamma ratio is comparable to $(2+|\tau|)^{-s}$. Its $s$-derivatives are the
ratio times polynomials in $\tfrac12\psi(\tfrac{p+\alpha}2)$,
$\tfrac12\psi(\tfrac{p+\beta}2)$ and their derivatives, each
$O(\log(2+|\tau|))$ by \cite[\S5.11(i)]{DLMF}. Combining the factors gives
\eqref{eq:laplace-bounds}; $K_0=1$ is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Central coercivity implies contraction]\label{prop:continuation}
If $Q_{0,L}\succeq mI$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:continuation}
 \norm{V_{\omega,L}}\le
 \exp(-m\omega+C_L\omega^3/3),\qquad 0<\omega\le1/2.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We give the argument in detail because the generator is unbounded and
$V_{0,L}=I$ is a strong, not a norm, limit.

\emph{Weighted realization.} Fix $\eta>1$ and let $M_\eta$ be multiplication
by $e^{-\eta x}$, a bounded operator on $\HH_L$ with bounded inverse. For a
distribution kernel $k$ supported in $[0,\infty)$ and $g$ supported in
$[0,L]$, $M_\eta(k*g)=(M_\eta k)*(M_\eta g)$, and multiplication by the
indicator $P_L$ of $(0,L)$ commutes with $M_\eta$. Hence the compression to
$(0,L)$ of the causal operator with Laplace symbol $\kappa(p)$ equals
$M_\eta^{-1}P_L\,\mathcal M[\kappa(\eta+i\cdot)]\,P_LM_\eta$, where
$\mathcal M[\phi]$ denotes the Fourier multiplier by $\phi$ on $L^2(\RR)$. In
particular $V_{s,L}=M_\eta^{-1}P_L\mathcal M[K_s(\eta+i\cdot)]P_LM_\eta$,
and by Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace} $\norm{V_{s,L}}\le C_\eta\norm{M_\eta}\norm{M_\eta^{-1}}$
uniformly in $s$.

\emph{Norm differentiability for positive shift.} Fix $\varepsilon>0$. By
\eqref{eq:laplace-bounds} with $j=2$,
$|K_{s+h}(p)-K_s(p)-h\,\partial_sK_s(p)|\le\tfrac{h^2}2C_\eta(1+\log(2+|\tau|))^2(2+|\tau|)^{-\varepsilon}
\le C_{\eta,\varepsilon}h^2$ uniformly in $\tau$ for $s,s+h\in[\varepsilon,\tfrac12]$.
Therefore $s\mapsto V_{s,L}$ is norm-$C^1$ on $[\varepsilon,\tfrac12]$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dV}
 \partial_sV_{s,L}=M_\eta^{-1}P_L\mathcal M[\partial_sK_s(\eta+i\cdot)]P_LM_\eta
 =-M_\eta^{-1}P_L\mathcal M[(a_sK_s)(\eta+i\cdot)]P_LM_\eta .
\end{equation}

\emph{Smoothing and the generator.} Fix $s\in[\varepsilon,\tfrac12)$, $f\in\HH_L$,
and put $g=V_{s,L}f$. By \eqref{eq:laplace-bounds} with $j=0$,
$\mathcal M[K_s]M_\eta f\in H^r(\RR)$ for every $r<s$. Multiplication by the
indicator of an interval is bounded on $H^r(\RR)$ for $0\le r<\tfrac12$
\cite{Strichartz1967}, and on functions supported in $[0,L]$ multiplication by
$e^{\eta x}$ coincides with multiplication by a smooth compactly supported
function, also bounded on $H^r$. Hence $g\in H^r(\RR)$ for every
$r<\min(s,\tfrac12)$, $g$ is supported in $[0,L]$, and in particular
$g\in\DD_{\log,L}$. Since $a_sK_s$ is a bounded symbol on the line,
$u:=M_\eta^{-1}P_L\mathcal M[(a_sK_s)(\eta+i\cdot)]M_\eta f$ lies in $\HH_L$,
and by the convolution identity above $u$ is the restriction to $(0,L)$ of
$k_{a_s}*g$, where $k_{a_s}$ is the causal distribution kernel with Laplace
transform $a_s$: indeed $k_{a_s}*P_L(k_{K_s}*f)$ differs from
$(k_{a_s}*k_{K_s})*f$ by $k_{a_s}$ applied to a function supported in
$[L,\infty)$, whose output is supported in $[L,\infty)$. Thus
\eqref{eq:dV} reads $\partial_sV_{s,L}f=-A_{s,L}V_{s,L}f$ with
$A_{s,L}g:=P_L(k_{a_s}*g)$, which is the identity \eqref{eq:derivative}, and
$A_{s,L}g\in\HH_L$.

\emph{The symmetric form of the generator.} We claim
$\Re\ip g{A_{s,L}g}=Q_{s,L}[g]$ for the vector $g$ above. The arithmetic part
of $A_{s,L}$ is the finite sum
$-2\sum_{\log n<L}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\cosh(s\log n)T_{\log n}$, since delays
with $\log n\ge L$ vanish on $(0,L)$; it is bounded and its real part is the
arithmetic part of \eqref{eq:Qgeneral}. The four pole terms of
\eqref{eq:agamma} have causal kernels $e^{\mp\alpha t}$, $e^{\mp\beta t}$,
bounded on $(0,L)$, and their symmetrizations are the corresponding
$\cosh$ kernels (at $s=0$, the kernel $2\cosh((x-y)/2)$ of the pole term of
\eqref{eq:weil}). The digamma part
$\tfrac12\psi(\tfrac{p+\alpha}2)+\tfrac12\psi(\tfrac{p+\beta}2)-\log\pi$ has a
tempered causal kernel $k_\psi$ (a multiple of $\delta$, a finite part of
$1/t$, and a bounded function); for $g\in L^2$ with compact support,
$k_\psi*g$ is tempered with Fourier transform $\hat k_\psi(\tau)\hat g(\tau)$,
where $\hat k_\psi(\tau)=\tfrac12\psi(\tfrac{i\tau+\alpha}2)+\tfrac12\psi(\tfrac{i\tau+\beta}2)-\log\pi$
is the Laplace symbol on the imaginary axis; for $s<\tfrac12$ its arguments
stay away from the poles of $\psi$ and it is $O(\log(2+|\tau|))$. Since
$g\in H^r$ with $r>0$, $k_\psi*g\in L^2$, and Plancherel gives
$\Re\ip g{k_\psi*g}=\frac1{2\pi}\int\Re\hat k_\psi(\tau)|\hat g(\tau)|^2\,d\tau$,
the digamma term of $Q_{s,L}$. Summing the three parts proves the claim; this
is the extension of Proposition~\ref{prop:generator} from the smooth core to
$g$, and the same computation shows that the form $Q_{s,L}$ so obtained is the
one whose difference from $Q_{0,L}$ is bounded by Lemma~\ref{lem:change}.

\emph{Energy inequality and Gronwall.} With $g_s=V_{s,L}f$, norm
differentiability gives, for $s\in[\varepsilon,\tfrac12)$,
\[
 \frac{d}{ds}\norm{g_s}^2=2\Rea\ip{g_s}{\partial_sg_s}
 =-2\Rea\ip{g_s}{A_{s,L}g_s}=-2Q_{s,L}[g_s]
 \le-2(m-C_Ls^2)\norm{g_s}^2,
\]
using the hypothesis and Lemma~\ref{lem:change}. Integrating from
$\varepsilon$ to $\omega<\tfrac12$,
\[
 \norm{V_{\omega,L}f}^2\le\norm{V_{\varepsilon,L}f}^2
 \exp\Bigl(-2\int_\varepsilon^\omega(m-C_Ls^2)\,ds\Bigr).
\]

\emph{The zero-shift limit.} Finally
$\norm{V_{\varepsilon,L}f-f}\le\norm{M_\eta^{-1}}\,\norm{(\mathcal M[K_\varepsilon(\eta+i\cdot)]-I)M_\eta f}_{L^2(\RR)}$,
and the right side tends to $0$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ by Plancherel and
dominated convergence, since $K_\varepsilon(\eta+i\tau)\to1$ pointwise with
$|K_\varepsilon|\le C_\eta$. Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves
\eqref{eq:continuation} for $0<\omega<\tfrac12$; the case $\omega=\tfrac12$
follows by the norm continuity of $s\mapsto V_{s,L}$ established above. No
norm derivative at zero and no bounded inverse of the smoothing transfer are
used.
\end{proof}

\section{An analytic lower bound on the infinite Legendre tail}\label{sec:tail}

Let $\phi_n(x)=\sqrt{(2n+1)/L}\,P_n(2x/L-1)$ and let $P_N$ retain
$\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{N-1}$. Define causal fractional integration by
\[
 I_\nu f(x)=\frac1{\Gamma(\nu)}\int_0^x(x-y)^{\nu-1}f(y)\,dy.
\]
The following estimate applies to all combinations in the infinite tail.

\begin{lemma}[Fractional tail estimate]\label{lem:fractional}
For $N\ge1$ and $0<\nu<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fractail}
 \norm{I_\nu(I-P_N)}\le
 (L/2)^\nu\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(N+1-\nu)}{\Gamma(N+1+\nu)}}.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:I1tail}
 \norm{I_1(I-P_N)}\le\frac{L}{2\sqrt{N(N+1)}}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Termwise beta integration of the terminating Legendre polynomial gives
\[
 I_\nu P_n(2x/L-1)=
 \frac{n!}{\Gamma(n+1+\nu)}x^\nu
 P_n^{(-\nu,\nu)}(2x/L-1).
\]
The polynomial and orthogonality identities may also be read from
\cite[\S\S18.3,18.5,18.17]{DLMF}. For a finite tail combination, use
$x^{2\nu}\le(L/2)^{2\nu}x^\nu(L-x)^{-\nu}$ and Jacobi orthogonality.
Indeed $x(L-x)\le L^2/4$, and the required Jacobi norm is
\[
 \int_0^L x^\nu(L-x)^{-\nu}
 \bigl[P_n^{(-\nu,\nu)}(2x/L-1)\bigr]^2\,dx
 =\frac{L\,\Gamma(n+1-\nu)\Gamma(n+1+\nu)}
 {(2n+1)(n!)^2}.
\]
The squared norm becomes bounded by
\[
 (L/2)^{2\nu}\sum_{n\ge N}|c_n|^2
 \frac{\Gamma(n+1-\nu)}{\Gamma(n+1+\nu)}.
\]
Successive gamma ratios decrease by the factor
$(n+1-\nu)/(n+1+\nu)<1$. Density and boundedness of $I_\nu$ prove
\eqref{eq:fractail}. Letting $\nu\uparrow1$, using convergence of the
fractional kernels in $L^1(0,L)$, gives \eqref{eq:I1tail}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Sharpness on single modes]\label{rem:sharp}
The bound is essentially optimal in its leading term. By the
Weber--Schafheitlin integral \cite[\S10.22(ii)]{DLMF}, differentiated in the
exponent, the exact value of the logarithmic part on a single normalized
Legendre mode is
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_{\RR}\log\frac{|\tau|}{2\pi}\,|\widehat{(\phi_n)_c}(\tau)|^2\,d\tau
 =\log2+\tfrac12\bigl(\psi(n+\tfrac12)+\psi(n+\tfrac32)\bigr)-\log(\pi L),
\]
which exceeds the floor $H_N-\gamma-\log(\pi L)$ at $n=N$ by
$\log2+O(1/N)$. The floor must hold for all tail combinations, and the
logarithmic multiplier is not diagonal in the Legendre basis, so a loss of this
order is expected. The identity was also used to check the computed head
matrices independently of the formulas of Section~\ref{sec:computation}.
\end{remark}

The central gamma derivative $U^\gamma=\partial_\omega V^\gamma_{\omega,L}|_0$
has the decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Udecomp}
 U^\gamma=J_L+S_w,\qquad
 J_L=\left.\partial_\nu[(2\pi)^\nu I_\nu]\right|_0,
 \qquad w(t)=\frac{G_0(t)-1}{t},
\end{equation}
where $S_w$ is causal convolution and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G0}
 G_0(t)=\frac{e^{t/2}t}{\sinh t}-4t\cosh(t/2)
 =\sum_{j\ge0}g_jt^j.
\end{equation}
To verify \eqref{eq:Udecomp}, the inverse kernel of $-a^\gamma_0$, away
from the origin, is
$e^{t/2}/\sinh t-4\cosh(t/2)$; subtracting the finite-part kernel of
$J_L$ leaves $w$. The constant term agrees with the Laplace asymptotic
$-a^\gamma_0(p)=\log(2\pi/p)+O(p^{-1})$.
In particular $g_0=1$, $g_1=-7/2$, and $g_2=-1/24$.

\begin{proposition}[Central infinite-tail floor]\label{prop:tail}
Suppose
$K_L\ge |w(0)|+\int_0^L|w'(t)|\,dt$. Then, on
$(I-P_N)\DD_{\log,L}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:afloor}
 Q_{0,L}\succeq a_{N,L}I,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
 a_{N,L}={}&H_N-\gamma-\log(\pi L)
 -\frac{K_LL}{2\sqrt{N(N+1)}}\notag\\
 &-\sum_{\log n<L}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
 \norm{T_{\log n}+T_{\log n}^*}.
 \label{eq:tailformula}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:fractail} to a polynomial tail vector, multiply by
$(2\pi)^\nu$, square, and differentiate at $\nu=0$. Both sides have the
same value there. The result is
$-\Rea\ip f{J_Lf}\ge(H_N-\gamma-\log(\pi L))\norm f^2$.
The differentiation is legitimate on each polynomial, whose derivative
contains only polynomials and logarithmic endpoint terms in $L^2$.

Next $S_w=(w(0)I+S_{w'})I_1$. Young's inequality and \eqref{eq:I1tail}
give
\[
 \norm{S_w(I-P_N)}\le\frac{K_LL}{2\sqrt{N(N+1)}}.
\]
Since $Q^\gamma_0=-\Rea U^\gamma$ in form sense, this is a lower-bound
loss. Bound the remaining symmetric delays individually in norm. The
resulting inequality $Q_{0,L}[t]\ge a_{N,L}\norm t^2$ holds for every
polynomial tail combination $t$. Both sides are continuous in the form norm of
$\DD_{\log,L}$ (the left side because $Q_{0,L}$ is closed, the right side
trivially), and by Lemma~\ref{lem:core} polynomial combinations in
$(I-P_N)\HH_L$ are a form core for the restriction of $Q_{0,L}$ to
$(I-P_N)\DD_{\log,L}$; hence the inequality extends to the complete tail form
domain.
\end{proof}

For clarity, the delay norm in \eqref{eq:tailformula} is not automatically 2.
Decompose $x$ into $x=r+kd$, $0<r<d$. On each fiber,
$T_d+T_d^*$ is the adjacency matrix of a finite path. If
$q=\lceil L/d\rceil$, its norm is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chainnorm}
 \norm{T_d+T_d^*}=2\cos\frac{\pi}{q+1}
 \quad(d<L),
\end{equation}
with threshold equality using the shorter path, since the exceptional fiber
has measure zero. In particular the norm is 1 for $d<L\le2d$ and
$\sqrt2$ for $2d<L\le3d$. The reviewed implementation evaluates the
cosine formula for every finite path length. Logarithmic endpoints use
exact integer-power comparisons; unresolved ball comparisons at other
horizons stop the calculation.

\subsection{A uniform analytic profile remainder}

\begin{lemma}[Profile enclosure]\label{lem:profile}
For $0<L<3$ and an integer $M\ge1$, let $\widetilde U$ retain the terms
$g_j$, $j\le M$, in \eqref{eq:Udecomp}, leaving $J_L$ and the arithmetic
delays exact. Then the bounded difference satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta}
 \norm{U-\widetilde U}\le\eta_M,
 \qquad \eta_M=\frac{256(L/3)^{M+1}}{(M+1)(1-L/3)}.
\end{equation}
One may use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Kbound}
 K_L=\sum_{j=1}^M|g_j|L^{j-1}
 +\frac{256}{3}\frac{(L/3)^M}{1-L/3}
\end{equation}
in Proposition~\ref{prop:tail}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On $|z|=3$, the Euler product for $\sinh z/z$
\cite[\S4.36]{DLMF} gives $|z/\sinh z|\le3/\sin3<24$.
Since $e^{3/2}<5$, \eqref{eq:G0} implies
$|G_0(z)|<5(24+12)<256$. Thus $|g_j|\le256\,3^{-j}$.
The $L^1$ norm of the omitted convolution kernel is bounded by
$\sum_{j>M}|g_j|L^j/j\le\eta_M$, proving \eqref{eq:eta}.
For $w$, the constant term contributes $|g_1|$, while integration of each
derivative term gives $|g_j|L^{j-1}$. Summing the omitted geometric series
proves \eqref{eq:Kbound}.
\end{proof}

The majorant $256$ is deliberately loose: numerically
$\sup_{|z|=3}|G_0(z)|\approx28.3$ and $\max_j|g_j|3^j\approx15.2$. Since
$\eta_M$ is already far below every quantity it is compared with
(Table~\ref{tab:certificate}), nothing is gained by tightening it.

\section{Exact reflection-parity coupling and the Schur test}\label{sec:schur}

Write $P=P_N$, let $P_r$ be its parity-$r$ part ($r=\pm1$), and denote
the central model form by $\widetilde Q=-(\widetilde U+\widetilde U^*)/2$.
The polynomial space lies in the operator domain: the fractional derivative
of a polynomial has only square-integrable logarithmic endpoint terms,
and all other terms are bounded convolutions or delayed polynomials.
Thus $Q_0P$ and $\widetilde QP$ are bounded finite-column operators.

Define the retained matrix and two full-output Grams by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grams}
 q=P\widetilde QP,\qquad
 F=(\widetilde UP)^*(\widetilde UP),\qquad
 C=(\widetilde UP)^*R_L(\widetilde UP).
\end{equation}
In this notation, $F$ and $C$ are finite matrices but the output in their
definition has \emph{not} been projected onto $P\HH_L$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact central-parity leakage]\label{lem:parity}
If $q_r,F_r,C_r$ denote the same-parity blocks in \eqref{eq:grams}, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leakage}
 E_r=\frac12(F_r+rC_r)-q_r^2
 =\bigl((I-P)\widetilde QP_r\bigr)^*
   \bigl((I-P)\widetilde QP_r\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Causal convolution gives $\widetilde U^*=R_L\widetilde UR_L$.
For $R_Lf=rf$,
$\widetilde Qf=-(\widetilde Uf+rR_L\widetilde Uf)/2$.
Taking the full-output squared norm yields
$\norm{\widetilde Qf}^2=\ip f{(F_r+rC_r)f}/2$.
Subtract the retained-output norm $\norm{q_rf}^2$ and polarize.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Certified finite test]\label{prop:schur}
Suppose the exact central tail is at least $aI$, $a>m$, and
$\norm{\widetilde UP}<K$. If both finite inequalities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-test}
 (a-m)(q_r-mI)-E_r-\epsilon I\succeq0,
 \qquad r=\pm1,
\end{equation}
hold, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eps}
 \epsilon=(|a-m|+4K)\eta_M+2\eta_M^2,
\end{equation}
then $Q_{0,L}\succeq mI$ on the full form domain.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The profile difference has norm at most $\eta_M$, as does its symmetric
part. On each input parity, $\widetilde QP_r$ is an output-parity projection
of $-\widetilde UP_r$, and hence has norm below $K$. The full squared-output
Gram and the squared retained matrix each change by at most
$2K\eta_M+\eta_M^2$. The linear head term changes by at most
$|a-m|\eta_M$. Therefore \eqref{eq:finite-test} implies
\[
 (a-m)(Q_{P,r}-mI)-B_r^*B_r\succeq0,
 \quad B_r=(I-P)Q_0P_r,
\]
for the exact central form.

For an arbitrary input in one sector, write it as $h+t$ with $h$ retained
and $t$ in the form-domain tail. The quadratic form of $Q_0-mI$ is bounded
below by
\[
 \ip h{(Q_{P,r}-mI)h}
 +2\Rea\ip{B_rh}t+(a-m)\norm t^2.
\]
Completing the square, or minimizing over $t$, leaves
$\ip h{(Q_{P,r}-mI-(a-m)^{-1}B_r^*B_r)h}\ge0$.
The two orthogonal reflection sectors exhaust the complex Hilbert space.
\end{proof}

The use of the exact parity leakage is consequential. Replacing it by
the full leakage of $\widetilde U$ also bounds the coupling, but charges
irrelevant output parity against the available tail margin. Equation
\eqref{eq:leakage} removes that loss without discarding any coupling of
the central form.

\begin{remark}[A smaller error for future calculations]\label{rem:sharper-error}
The certificates below keep the conservative error \eqref{eq:eps}.
One can instead set
\[
 \epsilon_r=(|a-m|+2\kappa_r)\eta_M+\eta_M^2,
 \qquad \kappa_r\ge\norm{(I-P)\widetilde QP_r}.
\]
Indeed the omitted-output column operator changes in norm by at most
$\eta_M$, so its squared Gram changes by at most
$2\kappa_r\eta_M+\eta_M^2$ directly. There is no need to bound the full
Gram and the squared head separately. A certified upper bound on
$\sqrt{\tr E_r}$ supplies $\kappa_r$, since $E_r$ is an exact positive
semidefinite Gram. Any numerical upper bound used here must include the
ball uncertainty; this refinement is not used to justify the reported runs.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite construction and the certificates}\label{sec:computation}

\subsection{Polynomial and logarithmic output formulas}

Use the unitary coordinate $u=x/L$, so the normalized basis is
$\sqrt{2n+1}\,p_n(u)$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:legpoly}
 p_n(u)=\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^{n+k}\binom nk\binom{n+k}k u^k.
\end{equation}
With $\ell_L=\gamma+\log(2\pi L)$, the gamma derivative of a monomial is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monomial}
 U^\gamma u^k=u^k(\log u+\ell_L-H_k)
 +\sum_{j\ge1}g_jL^j\frac{(j-1)!k!}{(j+k)!}u^{j+k}.
\end{equation}
This follows by differentiating the beta integral for $I_\nu u^k$ and
integrating each term of $S_w$. For the model, the sum stops at $j=M$.
Every output therefore has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:output}
 \widetilde Up_n(u)=p_n(u)\log u+q_n(u)
 +\sum_{\log j<L}\frac{2\Lambda(j)}{\sqrt j}
   \mathbf1_{u>\delta_j}p_n(u-\delta_j),
 \quad \delta_j=\frac{\log j}{L}.
\end{equation}
The polynomial $q_n$ is supplied by \eqref{eq:monomial}.
The head, $F$, and $C$ are integrated using exact formulas for moments of
$1,\log u,(\log u)^2,\log(1-u)$, and $\log u\log(1-u)$, including
truncated integration intervals. Appendix~\ref{app:moments} lists them.
There is no quadrature error and no finite-output truncation.

For the reflected delayed products, the nonempty integration window is
$(\delta_i,1-\delta_j)$, equivalently $\log(ij)<L$.
At $L=9/5$ and at $L=\log7$ the active generator indices are $2,3,4,5$, and
the ordered overlap pairs are exactly $(2,2),(2,3),(3,2)$; at $L=\log5$ and
$L=\log6$ only $(2,2)$ is present, and there is none at $\log4$ and below.

\subsection{Enclosed arithmetic}

The standalone implementation uses Arb real balls \cite{Johansson2017}
through python-flint \cite{PythonFlint}. Integer and rational coefficients
in \eqref{eq:G0}, \eqref{eq:legpoly}, and \eqref{eq:monomial} are formed
exactly. All subsequent conversions, elementary constants, logarithms,
square roots, moments, polynomial reflections, and matrix operations are
performed with balls, at 1536-bit working precision for the horizon $9/5$
and 1792 bits at the logarithmic horizons. Exact symmetries are imposed only
after checking consistency of the enclosures.

The matrix $F$ encloses the model-output Gram, and its trace is checked
below $10^7$. Hence $\norm{\widetilde UP}<10000$, allowing $K=10000$
in \eqref{eq:eps}; the computed traces are below $2400$ at every horizon, and
the deliberately loose bound avoids making this part of the proof delicate.
An outward lower value of \eqref{eq:tailformula} and an outward upper value
of \eqref{eq:eta} are used. An outward upper value of \eqref{eq:eps} is
then subtracted from each model Schur matrix.

For a symmetric ball matrix $M$, the sign test uses the recurrence
\begin{align*}
 d_i&=M_{ii}-\sum_{k<i}\ell_{ik}^2d_k,\\
 \ell_{ji}&=\frac{M_{ji}-\sum_{k<i}\ell_{jk}\ell_{ik}d_k}{d_i}\quad(j>i).
\end{align*}
Every pivot must be strictly positive as a ball. All divisions then have
denominators excluding zero. Induction encloses the exact $LDL^*$ factors
of every symmetric matrix contained in $M$; positive pivots imply its
positivity. Ordinary floating-point eigenvalues never enter this decision.

\subsection{Certified upper bounds}

\begin{proposition}[Upper bounds]\label{prop:upper}
For each horizon and each reflection sector, $\lambda_{\min}$ of the sector
form is at most the upper bound listed in Table~\ref{tab:sectors}; the even
values are the $\mu_j$ of Table~\ref{tab:main}. At every horizon the even
upper bound is smaller than the odd floor, so the bottom of the spectrum of
$Q_{0,L_j}$ lies in the even sector.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Computer-assisted proof]
The retained polynomials lie in the operator domain and
$\norm{Q_{0,L}-\widetilde Q}\le\eta_M$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:profile}), so for
every vector $v$ in the retained space of parity $r$,
\[
 \lambda_{\min}\bigl(Q_{0,L}|_{\text{parity }r}\bigr)\le
 \frac{\ip v{q_rv}}{\ip vv}+\eta_M .
\]
For each sector, a floating-point eigenvector of the ball midpoints of $q_r$
for its smallest eigenvalue is rounded to a rational vector $v$ with forty
digits per coordinate, and the right side is evaluated with balls, using the
saved enclosure of $q_r$ and the outward value of $\eta_M$; its upper end,
rounded up, is the tabulated bound. The last assertion is the comparison of
two exact numbers in each row of Table~\ref{tab:sectors}.
\end{proof}

The vectors $v$ are diagnostics; the certified content is the ball evaluation.
Table~\ref{tab:spectra} lists the lowest head eigenvalues in each sector at
the two leading horizons. Two regularities are visible at every horizon: the
spectrum alternates between the sectors, the odd ground state lying between
the first two even eigenvalues, and consecutive same-parity eigenvalues are
separated by factors of about $10^{5.5}$ to $10^{6}$ at the deeper horizons.
These are floating-point observations; the certified statements are the
enclosures.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Theorem~\ref{thm:computation} and Proposition~\ref{prop:upper} give
\eqref{eq:mainenclosure} at $L=L_j$. Zero extension into a larger interval
preserves the central form: the Fourier representation and its
translation-invariant kernels, including the delay correlations, do not depend
on the chosen containing horizon. Thus the lower bound holds at every shorter
horizon, which is \eqref{eq:maincentral}.

Direct ball evaluation of \eqref{eq:CL} gives $C_{L_j}<C_j$ for the integers
$C_j$ of Table~\ref{tab:main}; at the two leading horizons
$C_{9/5}<10.298615<11$ and $C_{\log7}<13.541169<14$. The exact rational
inequalities $m_j-C_jh_j^2/3\ge d_j$ are verified for every row by the
supplied analyzer; for instance
\begin{align*}
 3.38\times10^{-23}-\tfrac{11}{3}(2\times10^{-12})^2
 &>1.91\times10^{-23}\ge10^{-23},\\
 1.37\times10^{-28}-\tfrac{14}{3}(4\times10^{-15})^2
 &>6.2\times10^{-29}\ge6\times10^{-29}.

Proposition~\ref{prop:continuation} then proves \eqref{eq:maintransfer} at

\end{proof}

\section{Storage, scope, and the next depth problem}\label{sec:storage}

\begin{corollary}[Cumulative storage]\label{cor:storage}
Fix a row of Table~\ref{tab:main}. For any spatial split of $(0,L)$ with
$L\le L_j$, write
\[
 V_{\omega,L}=\begin{pmatrix}X&0\\Y&Z\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For $0<\omega\le h_j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:storage}
 I-X^*X-Y^*(I-ZZ^*)^{-1}Y
 \succeq(1-e^{-2d_j\omega})I.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $\rho=e^{-d_j\omega}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives
$I-V^*V\succeq(1-\rho^2)I$, and $\norm Z\le\rho<1$.
The Schur complement of the lower-right block $I-Z^*Z$ is
\[
 I-X^*X-Y^*Y-Y^*Z(I-Z^*Z)^{-1}Z^*Y
 =I-X^*X-Y^*(I-ZZ^*)^{-1}Y.
\]
For a fixed first-block vector, minimizing the defect quadratic form over
the second-block vector leaves a value at least
$(1-\rho^2)$ times the first-block norm squared. This proves
\eqref{eq:storage}.
\end{proof}

In particular, the split $\log7=\log5+\log(7/5)$ includes the first
mixed-prime region. The corollary is obtained by certifying the whole larger
operator first. It does not show that positivity of a previous slab alone
forces the next storage update to be positive; that distinction separates
the present finite-depth results from an induction in depth. The algebraic
identities that such an induction would have to carry are recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:recursion}; they specify bookkeeping, not a mechanism.

\subsection{A diagonal formulation of the all-depth target}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:diagonal}
Suppose there exist $L_j\to\infty$ and $\omega_j\downarrow0$ such that
$\norm{V_{\omega_j,L_j}}\le1$ for every $j$. Then the central Weil form
is nonnegative on every compactly supported smooth test function.
By Weil's criterion, such a sequence would imply RH.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a finite horizon $L$ and a smooth test $f$ compactly supported inside it.
For all sufficiently large $j$, causal compression gives
$\norm{V_{\omega_j,L}f}\le\norm f$.
For such $f$ the map $s\mapsto\norm{V_{s,L}f}^2$ is differentiable at $s=0$
with derivative $-2Q_{0,L}[f]$: in the weighted realization of the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:continuation}, $\widehat{M_\eta f}$ is rapidly
decreasing while $\partial_sK_s(\eta+i\tau)$ grows only logarithmically, so
dominated convergence applies to the difference quotient on the Laplace line,
and the identification of the resulting pairing with $Q_{0,L}[f]$ is the case
$s=0$ of the form identity proved there. Hence
\[
 \lim_{s\downarrow0}\frac{\norm f^2-\norm{V_{s,L}f}^2}{2s}
 =Q_{0,L}[f].
\]
The quotients along $s=\omega_j$ are nonnegative, so their limit is
nonnegative. The horizon and compact test were arbitrary; the final
implication is the classical Weil criterion
\cite{ConnesConsani2020,Conrey2019}.
\end{proof}

The proposition requires neither a depth-independent coercivity margin nor
a fixed positive shift interval. Nevertheless, constructing the entire
sequence remains the missing all-depth statement; the seven horizons certified
here are not such a sequence, and Table~\ref{tab:main} shows the shift
intervals $h_j$ that the present method certifies shrinking rapidly along the
sampled horizons. The finite data establish no rate for that collapse at
unbounded depth.

\subsection{Limitations: the scale of the next horizons}\label{sec:limits}

The scalar tail floor \eqref{eq:tailformula} is
\[
 a_{N,L}\approx\log N-\log(\pi L)-\Sigma_L,\qquad
 \Sigma_L=\sum_{\log n<L}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\norm{T_{\log n}+T_{\log n}^*},
\]
up to the small smooth-profile term. It pays separately for every arithmetic
delay norm, and $\Sigma_L$ grows like a multiple of $e^{L/2}$ by the prime
number theorem. What follows are resource projections for the present
implementation, not impossibility bounds. Indeed the scalar floor alone never
obstructs the test at a fixed horizon: for $0<L<3$ and any admissible profile
degree $M$ the constants $K_L$ and $\Sigma_L$ in \eqref{eq:tailformula} are
finite, so $a_{N,L}\to+\infty$ as $N\to\infty$. The retained head, the
coupling, the profile error and the cost of the ball arithmetic decide whether
a certificate is obtained, and the arithmetic loss governs how fast that cost
grows. The floor the finite test needed at the certified horizons is the
relative-coupling diagnostic of Table~\ref{tab:certificate}, which rises from
$0.17$ at $\log2$ to $0.59$ at $\log7$ while $a_{128,L}$ falls from $4.07$ to
$0.61$; whether a floor near $0.6$ suffices at an uncomputed horizon is not
known, since the required coupling has risen with every new prime. At the
next prime-active horizon $\log8$, where the generator acquires the delay
$\log7$ and the endpoint delay $\log8$ vanishes, $\Sigma_{\log8}\approx3.13$
and the tail floor at $N=128$ is negative ($\approx-0.20$); it is $0.52$ at
$N=256$ and $1.22$ at $N=512$. Keeping the floor near $0.6$ would need roughly
$N\approx310$ at $\log8$, $950$ at $\log11$, $1900$ at $\log13$, $4400$ at
$\log16$, and $27{,}000$ as $L\to3$, the boundary of the domain of the proved
profile remainder (Lemma~\ref{lem:profile}). A local extrapolation of
Table~\ref{tab:main} suggests that the certified margin shrinks by a factor of
roughly $10^{-34}$ per additional unit of horizon, and the model error must be
kept below it. Matrix assembly scales like $N(N+M)^2$ ball products.
Extrapolating these figures, the cost of the present implementation rises
rapidly beyond the certified range, and horizons near $\log13$ to $\log16$
would be demanding for it; none of this predicts whether a certification there
succeeds or fails. The growth of $\Sigma_L$ is the tail-side appearance of the
doubly exponential resolution threshold that Zhu \cite{Zhu2026} derives for
one-stroke certificates.

The small-shift intervals in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} are consequences of the
stated coercivity floors and the quadratic perturbation estimate; they are
not asserted to be sharp, and they do not connect to the interior shift
ranges studied in the companion manuscripts of this repository on total
horizons at most $\log2$ and $\log3$.

For horizons beyond the radius of the present origin-centered profile,
one can seek validated piecewise polynomial approximations to $w$ on the
positive real axis, retaining the logarithmic kernel exactly. The complex
poles of $G_0$ lie at nonzero integer multiples of $i\pi$, whereas $w$ is
smooth at every nonnegative real argument. Taylor expansions about positive
centers or Chebyshev pieces can therefore cover any fixed finite interval,
provided their errors and derivative variations are enclosed. This removes
the specific $L<3$ profile restriction in principle; it does not prove
coercivity at larger depth or bound the cost of doing so. The one idea that
could change the scaling is a structured tail bound that keeps the
prime--gamma cancellation instead of paying $\Sigma_L$ in full, for instance
on frequency bands; whether that is easier than the original problem is
exactly the question, and nothing in the present data decides it.

\section{Moment formulas and assembly conventions}\label{app:moments}

The code constructs all polynomial coefficients and integrates them against
the moments below. For an integer $k\ge0$, put $s=k+1$,
$H_s=\sum_{j=1}^s1/j$, and $H_s^{(2)}=\sum_{j=1}^s1/j^2$.
The full-interval formulas are
\begin{align}
 \int_0^1u^k\,du&=\frac1s,&
 \int_0^1u^k\log u\,du&=-\frac1{s^2},\notag\\
 \int_0^1u^k(\log u)^2\,du&=\frac2{s^3},&
 \int_0^1u^k\log(1-u)\,du&=-\frac{H_s}{s},\label{eq:moments}\\
 \int_0^1u^k\log u\log(1-u)\,du
 &=\frac{H_s}{s^2}-\frac{\pi^2/6-H_s^{(2)}}s.\notag
\end{align}
They follow by differentiating the beta integral; the first three also
follow directly by differentiating $1/s$.
For $0<\delta<1$,
\begin{align}
 \int_\delta^1u^k\,du&=\frac{1-\delta^s}s,\notag\\
 \int_\delta^1u^k\log u\,du
 &=-\frac{1-\delta^s}{s^2}-\frac{\delta^s\log\delta}s,\label{eq:tailmoments}\\
 \int_\delta^1u^k\log(1-u)\,du
 &=\frac{(1-\delta^s)\log(1-\delta)-H_s+\sum_{j=1}^s\delta^j/j}{s}.
 \notag
\end{align}
Integration by parts gives the last formula. Products of delayed
polynomials use
$\int_\delta^b u^k\,du=(b^s-\delta^s)/s$ on the appropriate nonempty
window. For reflection, expand $(1-u)^k$ by the exact binomial theorem.

To recover the gamma polynomial $q_n$ in \eqref{eq:output}, multiply each
coefficient of $u^k$ in \eqref{eq:legpoly} by $\ell_L-H_k$, and add
its contributions $g_jL^j(j-1)!k!/(j+k)!$ at degree $k+j$.
The beta factor is computed rationally with the recurrence
$\beta_{1,k}=1/(k+1)$,
$\beta_{j,k}=\beta_{j-1,k}(j-1)/(j+k)$.
The series $g_j$ is obtained by reciprocal power-series inversion of
$\sinh t/t$, multiplication by $e^{t/2}$, and subtraction of
$4t\cosh(t/2)$, all over the rationals.

The unit-coordinate integrals acquire factors $\sqrt{(2i+1)(2j+1)}$ from
normalization. The saved matrices use the causal derivative $U$.
If instead $H_1=UR_L$ is used, the two full-output Grams acquire the
entry sign $(-1)^{i+j}$; this sign is 1 within either same-parity block.
The central head and central leakage are unchanged.

\section{Algebraic identities for a possible depth recursion}\label{app:recursion}

The following identities are elementary and are recorded only because an
induction in arithmetic depth would have to carry them. Nothing in
Sections~\ref{sec:computation}--\ref{sec:storage} depends on them.

\paragraph{An exact storage update with memory.}
Fix a positive shift and suppose $D_X=I-X^*X\succ0$ and $\norm Z<1$,
with bounded positive inverses. Put
\[
 W=I-Z^*Z,\qquad
 \mathcal C=(I-ZZ^*)^{-1/2}YD_X^{-1/2},\qquad
 S=D_X^{1/2}(I-\mathcal C^*\mathcal C)D_X^{1/2}.
\]
For $V=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}X&0\\Y&Z\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ one has
the exact factorization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:storage-factor}
 I-V^*V=
 \begin{pmatrix}I&-Y^*ZW^{-1}\\0&I\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}S&0\\0&W\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}I&0\\-W^{-1}Z^*Y&I\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
verified by multiplication using $I+Z(I-Z^*Z)^{-1}Z^*=(I-ZZ^*)^{-1}$.
Thus contraction is equivalent to $\norm{\mathcal C}\le1$; if
$\norm{\mathcal C}<1$ the two triangular factors and $S$ provide the complete
inverse defect for the next append operation, while if only equality is
certified the defect may be singular and an inverse-based recursion cannot
automatically continue. A scalar lower bound on $D_X$ discards orientation:
for $Z=0$ and $Y=(c,0)$, the defects $\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon,1)$ and
$\operatorname{diag}(1,\varepsilon)$ have the same least eigenvalue, but their
updates are positive precisely when $c^2\le\varepsilon$ and $c^2\le1$,
respectively; $\varepsilon=1/4$, $c^2=1/2$ gives a failing and a passing
update with identical old scalar margins.

\paragraph{Nested Schur elimination for a structured tail.}
Split a form into an old head, a finite buffer, and the remaining tail,
\[
 Q_0-mI=\begin{pmatrix}
 A&B^*&C^*\\B&D&E^*\\C&E&F
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad F\succeq\alpha I,\quad\alpha>0.
\]
Replacing $F$ by $\alpha I$ and eliminating the tail gives
$\widehat A=A-C^*C/\alpha$, $\widehat B=B-E^*C/\alpha$,
$\widehat D=D-E^*E/\alpha$, and the two tests
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nested-schur}
 \widehat D\succ0,\qquad
 \widehat A-\widehat B^*\widehat D^{-1}\widehat B\succeq0
\end{equation}
are sufficient for $Q_0\succeq mI$, by two completions of the square. The
cross-Gram $C^*E$ must be retained; full-output parity Grams for the enlarged
retained space supply $C^*C$, $C^*E$ and $E^*E$. The blocks displayed here are
those of the exact form $Q_0$, whereas constructed Grams enclose the model
form $\widetilde Q$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:profile} in ball arithmetic; a
certificate built on \eqref{eq:nested-schur} would therefore have to carry the
profile error $\eta_M$ and the ball radii into the head, the buffer and every
coupling product, as \eqref{eq:eps} does for the present two-block test. With
the same enlarged space and scalar far-tail floor this is a reordering of the
existing test, not a stronger theorem; it becomes one only with a proved
structured lower bound for $F$.

\section{Reproducibility and review map}\label{app:reproduce}

The repository folder \path{papers/weil-depth} contains the LaTeX source,
bibliography, compiled manuscript, continuation guide, and the full numerical
supplement under \path{numerics/}; older drafts and the reviews that produced
each version are kept under \path{archive/}. The core numerical files are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \path{certify_arb.py}: exact-rational profiles, full-output ball
  construction, analytic tail bounds, and ball LDL;
\item \path{enclose_certificate.py}: sector-wise floor bisection, ball
  Rayleigh upper bounds, and exact continuation constants, producing
  \path{enclosure.json} in each output folder and the summary table;
\item \path{analyze_certificate.py}: hash-bound replay, rational continuation
  checks and the diagnostic relative coupling;

  complete pivot lists with the archive hash and a content hash of the matrices
  (\path{central_certificate.json}), the enclosure record, and the continuation
  analysis. The saved ball matrices themselves (\path{central_matrices.json.gz},
  about 13~MB per horizon) are deterministic output of the builder and are
  distributed separately rather than versioned; their expected layout and
  hashes are documented in \path{ARCHIVES.md}. In the tested environments
  (three Python versions with the same python-flint and FLINT releases) a

\item \path{independent_arb.py}: the separately written construction from
  the printed formulas, with its pivot records at all seven horizons and saved
  matrices at $9/5$ and $\log7$;
\item \path{review_checks.py} and \path{review_claude/}: moment,
  arithmetic-generator, parity and failure controls, and the Fourier-side
  checks described in Section~\ref{sec:computation}.
\end{itemize}
