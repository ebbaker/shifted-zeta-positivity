\title{Finite response matrices for Weil positivity}
\author{Draft for author review}

\begin{abstract}
We formulate an explicit matrix conjecture equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis.
For each bounded interval, a digamma resolvent expansion and a Neumann comparison
give an unconditional positive lower bound on a specified high-frequency sector
of the Weil form. Eliminating that sector leaves a finite Hermitian response
matrix, including every coupling to the low modes. We derive an explicit bounded,
noncompact boundary correction to the diagonal Neumann model and convergent
bounds for its action on finite input spaces. These yield rigorous lower and
upper bounds on the response matrix and an equivalent formulation with arbitrarily
small positive tolerances. The unresolved conjecture is positivity of these
matrices on an unbounded sequence of intervals. The equivalence and the analytic
reduction do not establish that positivity. No priority is claimed for the
general finite-codimension or Schur-complement principles.
\end{abstract}

\section{The proposed conjecture and its scope}
The useful question from the longer investigation \cite{Investigation} is whether
all possible negativity of the localized Weil form can be isolated in an
explicit, finite response problem. The answer to the reduction question is yes.
The arithmetic sign of the resulting matrices remains open.

The ingredients below define a matrix $S_n$ for every integer $n\ge1$ without
using zeta zeros or assuming their location. Its dimension is a prescribed
integer $N(n)$ obtained from an explicit high-mode estimate.
\begin{conjecture}[Finite response]\label{conj:main}
For every positive integer $n$, the matrix $S_n$ defined in
\eqref{eq:schur} is positive semidefinite.
\end{conjecture}
In energy language, allowing the positive high modes to respond to a low-mode
input never removes more than the low input's original energy. Theorem
\ref{thm:equiv} proves that this assertion is equivalent to RH.

Localized Weil positivity, positivity on suitable subspaces of finite
codimension, and variational formulations have substantial antecedents in
Yoshida and Bombieri, as reviewed in \cite[Section 1]{Suzuki}. Explicit finite
matrices also appear in \cite{CCM}. The content to assess here is the particular
Neumann boundary representation, the retained mixed response, and its controlled
remainder. The Schur complement itself is standard. A comparison with those
antecedents is necessary before making a novelty claim.

\section{The arithmetic operator and an unconditional cutoff}
Use $X_L=L^2(0,L)$, zero extension $E_L$, and
$\widehat F(\tau)=\int_\R F(x)e^{-i\tau x}\,dx$. Inner products are
antilinear in the first slot. Set
\begin{align}
a_k&=2k+\tfrac12,&
b(s)&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac2{a_k}\frac{s}{a_k^2+s}
=\re\psi(\tfrac14+i\sqrt{s}/2)-\psi(\tfrac14),\label{eq:b}\\
w_0&=\psi(\tfrac14)-\log\pi,&
\mathcal D_L&=\left\{f\in X_L:
\int_\R\log(2+|\tau|)|\widehat{E_Lf}(\tau)|^2\,d\tau<\infty\right\}.
\end{align}
Let $T_L$ be the nonnegative operator associated with the closed form
\[
t_L[f]=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\R b(\tau^2)|\widehat{E_Lf}(\tau)|^2\,d\tau.
\]
For $d>0$, $U_dF(x)=F(x-d)$ and $V_d=E_L^*U_dE_L$. Put
$c_L(x)=\cosh((x-L/2)/2)$ and $s_L(x)=\sinh((x-L/2)/2)$.
The bounded remainder and the full operator are
\begin{align}
R_L={}&w_0I+2c_Lc_L^*-2s_Ls_L^*
-\sum_{\substack{p\ \mathrm{prime},\ m\ge1\\m\log p<L}}
 (\log p)p^{-m/2}(V_{m\log p}+V_{m\log p}^*),\label{eq:remainder}\\
W_L={}&T_L+R_L,\qquad Q_L[f]=t_L[f]+\ip f{R_Lf}.\label{eq:weil}
\end{align}
Here $vv^*f=v\ip v f$. The sum includes every active prime power.
All sums in $R_L$ are finite. In the additive normalization of the Weil
functional in \cite[Section 1.1]{Suzuki}, $Q_L$ is exactly the Weil form of
$E_Lf$. The constant, both poles and all prime powers are essential.

The digamma expansion \cite{DLMF} gives
$b(\tau^2)=\log|\tau|-\log2-\psi(1/4)+O(\tau^{-2})$.
Consequently $\mathcal D_L$ is the form domain and $C_c^\infty(0,L)$ is
a form core. The support contraction followed by mollification proves the
core assertion in this logarithmic Fourier norm. Bounded perturbation then
defines the self-adjoint, semibounded $W_L$ on $\Dom T_L$.

Let $H_{\rm N}$ and $H_{\rm D}$ denote the Neumann and Dirichlet interval
Laplacians. Minimizing
\[
\frac2a\left(\norm{E_Lf-u}_{L^2(\R)}^2+a^{-2}\norm{u'}_{L^2(\R)}^2\right)
\]
over $u\in H^1(\R)$ gives $(2/a)s/(a^2+s)$ in Fourier space.
Restricting $u$ to the interval lowers this minimum; allowing only zero
extensions of $H_0^1(0,L)$ competitors raises it. Summation proves the
closed-form bracket
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bracket}
b(H_{\rm N})\le T_L\le b(H_{\rm D}).
\end{equation}
The positive resolvent representation is a concrete case of the extension
framework in \cite{KM}. The comparison does not assign Neumann boundary
conditions to the actual whole-line fields.

Write
\[
e_j(x)=\nu_j\cos(j\pi x/L),\quad
\nu_0=L^{-1/2},\quad\nu_j=\sqrt{2/L}\ (j>0),\quad
b_j=b((j\pi/L)^2).
\]
For $q\in(0,1)$ define
\[
r_h(q)=\max_{1\le i\le h}
\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}q^j+\sum_{j=1}^{h-i}q^j\right),
\]
and specify
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
\beta_L=-w_0+2\sinh(L/2)-L+
\sum_{\log p<L}(\log p)r_{\lceil L/\log p\rceil}(p^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
The prime bound follows by decomposing translation by $\log p$ into finite
chains and bounding each symmetric correlation matrix by its largest row
sum. The odd pole costs at most $2\norm{s_L}^2=2\sinh(L/2)-L$;
the even pole is nonnegative. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
W_L\ge b(H_{\rm N})-\beta_LI.
\end{equation}
Fix $\delta_0=1/16$ once and for all and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:N}
N(L)=\min\{N\ge1:b_N\ge\beta_L+\delta_0\},\qquad
P=\sum_{j=0}^{N(L)-1}e_je_j^*,\quad Q=I-P.
\end{equation}
The set is nonempty because $b_j\to\infty$. Any larger certified cutoff
gives an equivalent criterion. The high compression, defined by restricting
the closed form to $Q\mathcal D_L$, obeys $H:=W_L|_{QX_L}\ge\delta_0I$.
This bound is unconditional and makes $H^{-1}$ a known bounded response.
It says nothing yet about the low/high coupling.

\section{An explicit boundary operator}
The following representation makes that coupling accessible. For $a>0$ set
\[
u_a(x)=e^{-ax},\quad v_a(x)=e^{-a(L-x)},\quad r_a=e^{-aL},\qquad
K_a=\frac{u_au_a^*+v_av_a^*+r_a(u_av_a^*+v_au_a^*)}{1-r_a^2}.
\]
\begin{theorem}[Boundary representation]\label{thm:boundary}
The positive finite sums $K^{(J)}=\sum_{k<J}K_{a_k}$ converge strongly to
a bounded positive operator $K_L$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary}
T_L=b(H_{\rm N})+K_L,\qquad
\Dom T_L=\Dom b(H_{\rm N}),\quad
\mathcal D_L=\Dom b(H_{\rm N})^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Finite cosine sums are an operator core. The essential norm is
$\norm{K_L}_{\rm ess}=\pi/2$, so finite mass sums do not converge in
operator norm. Nevertheless, for the first $h$ cosine modes,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
\norm{(K_L-K^{(J)})P_h}\le
\eta_{h,J}:=\sqrt{\frac{2(2h-1)}L}
 (a_J^{-1/2}+a_J^{-3/2})\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The compressed free Green function is $e^{-a|x-y|}/(2a)$; the Neumann
Green function is
\[
\frac{\cosh(a\min(x,y))\cosh(a(L-\max(x,y)))}{a\sinh(aL)}.
\]
Their difference multiplied by $2a$ is $K_a$. Subtracting the corresponding
resolvent energies proves the finite-tower version of \eqref{eq:boundary}.
The coefficient matrix of $K_a$ is positive.

Let $n(t)=e^{-t/2}/(1-e^{-2t})$. Summing the kernels gives
$n(x+y)+n(2L-x-y)$ plus a bounded kernel. Since
$n(t)=(2t)^{-1}+O_L(1)$ on $(0,2L]$, this is one half of each endpoint
Carleman kernel, modulo a bounded-kernel, hence compact, operator. The
weighted Schur test bounds the Carleman operator by $\pi$. The partial
towers have the same uniform kernel bound, so monotone convergence yields
a bounded strong limit and the asserted domains.

Splitting the interval in half makes the two endpoint operators a direct
sum modulo compact operators. Each has essential norm $\pi$ before the
factor $1/2$: normalized functions proportional to $t^{-1/2}$ on $[1,R]$,
then dilated toward zero, have limiting Rayleigh quotient $\pi$ and tend
weakly to zero. This also proves the lower essential-norm bound; see the
Carleman background in \cite{Yafaev}.

Direct integration, with $\omega_j=j\pi/L$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:column}
K_ae_j=\frac{\nu_j a}{a^2+\omega_j^2}(u_a+(-1)^jv_a).
\end{equation}
Its norm is at most $\sqrt2\nu_j a^{-3/2}$. Sum the decreasing series
using $\sum_{k\ge J}a_k^{-3/2}\le a_J^{-3/2}+a_J^{-1/2}$, then sum squared
column bounds over $j<h$ to obtain \eqref{eq:tail}.
\end{proof}

In particular all low basis vectors lie in $\Dom W_L$. The boundary entries
vanish for opposite parities; for equal parities they are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entries}
\ip{e_i}{K_Le_j}=2\nu_i\nu_j\sum_{k\ge0}
\frac{a_k^2(1-(-1)^j e^{-a_kL})}
{(a_k^2+\omega_i^2)(a_k^2+\omega_j^2)}.
\end{equation}
The remaining entries are elementary integrals of the rank-two pole
kernel and of translated cosines on their overlap intervals. These formulas
specify the operator using gamma and prime data, independently of RH.

\section{Exact elimination and the equivalence theorem}
For each $L$, use the prescribed $P,Q$ and define
\[
A=PW_LP,\qquad B=QW_LP,\qquad H=W_L|_{QX_L}.
\]
Here $A$ is an $N(L)$ by $N(L)$ Hermitian matrix, $B:PX_L\to QX_L$ is
bounded, and $H$ is the positive operator already bounded below in
\eqref{eq:lower}. Define the finite matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schur}
S_L=A-B^*H^{-1}B,\qquad S_n:=S_L\big|_{L=n}.
\end{equation}
Its entries involve an infinite-dimensional inverse. It is a finite matrix
with explicitly specified, approximable entries, not a formula involving
only finitely many elementary operations.

\begin{theorem}[Exact RH equivalence]\label{thm:equiv}
For each $L>0$, $Q_L\ge0$ on $\mathcal D_L$ if and only if $S_L\succeq0$.
Consequently Conjecture \ref{conj:main} is equivalent to RH.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $f=p+h$, $p=Pf$, $h=Qf$. Completing the high square gives the
identity of closed forms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square}
Q_L[p+h]=\norm{H^{1/2}(h+H^{-1}Bp)}^2+\ip p{S_Lp}.
\end{equation}
Every term is defined: $p\in\Dom W_L$, $h\in\Dom H^{1/2}$, and
$H^{-1}Bp\in\Dom H$. If $S_L\succeq0$, this proves positivity. Conversely,
choose $h=-H^{-1}Bp$ to recover exactly $\ip p{S_Lp}$.

RH implies Weil positivity on smooth compactly supported functions.
The form core extends it to $\mathcal D_L$, so every $S_n\succeq0$.
For the converse, every compact support can be translated into $(0,n)$
for some integer $n$. The Weil form is translation invariant: its gamma
multiplier and prime correlations are invariant, and the pole kernel is
$2\cosh((x-y)/2)$. Zero extension to a larger interval preserves all these
terms. Thus positivity of all $S_n$ implies Weil positivity on all required
test functions, and hence RH by the classical criterion \cite{Suzuki}.
\end{proof}
No uniform gap in $n$, common source on all intervals, or compatible limit
of the matrices is required. The dimensions can grow without bound.

\subsection{A comparison between two positive matrices}
To retain the positive-source language of the investigation, put
$\alpha=\beta_L+1$. Equation \eqref{eq:lower} proves
$W_L+\alpha I\ge I$ without RH. Define
\begin{align}
G_L&=A+\alpha I-B^*(H+\alpha I)^{-1}B,\label{eq:G}\\
D_L&=\alpha I+B^*\bigl(H^{-1}-(H+\alpha I)^{-1}\bigr)B.\label{eq:D}
\end{align}
Minimizing the positive shifted form gives $G_L\ge I$, and resolvent
monotonicity gives $D_L\ge\alpha I>0$. Their difference is exactly $S_L$.
Thus Conjecture \ref{conj:main} can equivalently be written
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contraction}
\lambda_{\max}(G_n^{-1/2}D_nG_n^{-1/2})\le1\qquad(n\ge1).
\end{equation}
The positivity of $G_L$ and $D_L$ separately supplies no ordering between
them. Changing an admissible scalar shift changes the two matrices but
cancels from $S_L$.

\section{Bounds on the unresolved matrix}
Put $d_j=b_j-\beta_L$ for $j\ge N$, and let $\Lambda e_j=d_je_j$ on
$QX_L$. Then $H\ge\Lambda\ge\delta_0I$. For any finite-input trial map
$Y:PX_L\to\Dom H$, set $R_Y=B-HY$. Expansion gives
\begin{align}
B^*H^{-1}B&=B^*Y+Y^*B-Y^*HY+R_Y^*H^{-1}R_Y,\label{eq:residual}\\
0&\le R_Y^*H^{-1}R_Y\le R_Y^*\Lambda^{-1}R_Y.
\end{align}
For $K>N$, let $P_{N,K}$ project onto $e_N,\ldots,e_{K-1}$ and define
the Galerkin trial by the positive finite system
\[
(P_{N,K}HP_{N,K})Y_K=P_{N,K}B,\qquad
\operatorname{ran}Y_K\subset P_{N,K}X_L.
\]
Its residual $R_K=B-HY_K$ is supported in cosine modes $j\ge K$.
\begin{theorem}[Convergent enclosures]\label{thm:enclosure}
The finite Hermitian matrices
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-matrix}
L_{L,K}=A-B^*Y_K-d_K^{-1}R_K^*R_K,\qquad
U_{L,K}=A-B^*Y_K
\end{equation}
satisfy $L_{L,K}\preceq S_L\preceq U_{L,K}$ and converge to $S_L$ in norm
as $K\to\infty$. Their gap is exactly $d_K^{-1}R_K^*R_K$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Galerkin orthogonality gives
$Y_K^*HY_K=B^*Y_K=Y_K^*B$ and $P_{N,K}R_K=0$.
Apply \eqref{eq:residual} and
$\Lambda^{-1}\le d_K^{-1}I$ on the remaining modes to obtain the bounds.
For convergence, write $H=\Lambda+E$ with $E$ bounded using
Theorem \ref{thm:boundary}. The cosine core and coercivity imply
$Y_K\to H^{-1}B$ in the form norm. If $P_K$ contains the first $K$ modes,
\[
R_K=(I-P_K)(B-EY_K)\longrightarrow0
\]
in operator norm on the finite input space. Therefore the enclosure gap
tends to zero, proving the assertion.
\end{proof}
The column tail \eqref{eq:tail} controls finite mass approximations to $B$
and $HY_K$. The residual Gram must include their interference with the
pole and prime columns. Positive eigenvalues of an upper enclosure alone
are not a positivity certificate.

\begin{corollary}[An equivalent tolerance formulation]\label{cor:epsilon}
Conjecture \ref{conj:main}, and hence RH, is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epsilon}
\forall n,m\in\N\ \exists K>N(n):\quad
L_{n,K}+2^{-m}I\succ0.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $S_n\succeq0$, choose $K$ with
$\norm{L_{n,K}-S_n}<2^{-m}$, using Theorem \ref{thm:enclosure}.
Conversely $S_n\succeq L_{n,K}\succ-2^{-m}I$ for every $m$ implies
$S_n\succeq0$. This avoids assuming that a semidefinite limit is detected
by an unshifted, finite strict certificate.
\end{proof}
The strict margin makes each true instance amenable to sufficiently
accurate enclosures. The companion derivations give a version using arbitrary
finite trials and explicit column-error allowances. No finite collection of
successful intervals or tolerances establishes \eqref{eq:epsilon}.

\section{What makes the remaining conjecture arithmetic}
The reduction works for many semibounded operators. For example
$W=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1/2&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has
a positive high block $H=1$ and $W+I>0$, but its low Schur complement is
$-1/2$. A positive shifted source and a positive high sector alone therefore
do not imply Conjecture \ref{conj:main}.

There is a more relevant negative control. Define the Chebyshev function
$\psi_{\rm Ch}(x)=\sum_{p^m\le x}\log p$ and
$E(r)=\psi_{\rm Ch}(e^r)-e^r+1$. For
$h_f(r)=\re\ip{E_Lf}{U_rE_Lf}$, cancellation of the smooth prime density
against the poles and the lowest gamma mass gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discrepancy}
Q_L[f]=t_{\ge1,L}[f]+(4+w_0)\norm f^2
-2\int_0^L e^{-r/2}h_f(r)\,dE(r).
\end{equation}
The Stieltjes form holds on $\mathcal D_L$; derivatives of $h_f$ are not
required. If the prime measure is replaced by $e^r\,dr$, the last term
vanishes. On the unit input $f(x)=\sin(\pi x/2)$ on $(0,2)$,
\[
Q_2^{\rm dens}[f]<-1+\frac{26}{125}\frac{(22/7)^2}{4}
=-\frac{2979}{6125}<0.
\]
Here $w_0<-5$ and
$\sum_{k\ge1}2/a_k^3\le26/125$; smooth approximation preserves negativity.
The actual prime discrepancy carries information that the leading density
and the positive response architecture cannot replace.

At $L=1$, the longer manuscript gives a rational certificate allowing
$N=4$ and $\delta_0=1/16$. That is a certificate for the high sector; it
does not evaluate or establish the sign of $S_1$. Present bounds may require
very large dimensions as $L$ increases. No practical complexity estimate,
new positivity interval, or zero computation is asserted here.

The proposed short paper consists of the unconditional boundary and response
theorems, the exact equivalence, and the open matrix conjecture. The extended
supersymmetric models motivate the investigation but are not hypotheses of
this equivalence. In particular, no unproved physical $tt^*$ identification
is being used to infer an arithmetic sign.
