## 2. The exact target and its meaning

Let $I_L=(-L/2,L/2)$ and $f,g\in C_c^\infty(I_L;\mathbb C)$. Capital letters $F,G$ denote zero extension to the whole line. Use

\[
\widehat F(\tau)=\int_{\mathbb R}F(x)e^{-i\tau x}\,dx,\qquad
\langle G,F\rangle={1\over2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
\overline{\widehat G(\tau)}\widehat F(\tau)\,d\tau.
\]

Inner products are conjugate-linear in the first entry. The whole-line translation is $U_dF(x)=F(x-d)$; the compressed translation is $T_df(x)=\widetilde f(x-d)$ on $I_L$. The adjoint of $T_d$ translates backwards and has the corresponding endpoint cap. For $d\ge L$, $T_d=0$.

To avoid confusing channel masses with primitive lengths, use

\[
a_k=2k+\tfrac12,\quad
\ell_p=\log p,\quad r_p=p^{-1/2},
\quad
B(s)=\sum_{k\ge0}{2\over a_k}{s\over a_k^2+s}.
\]

The kinetic part and fixed contact constant are

\[
K[F]={1\over2\pi}\int B(\tau^2)|\widehat F(\tau)|^2d\tau,
\qquad
w_0=\psi(1/4)-\log\pi
=-\gamma_E-\pi/2-3\log2-\log\pi
\approx-5.372183419225665.
\]

Write $C(f)=\int f(x)\cosh(x/2)\,dx$ and $S(f)=\int f(x)\sinh(x/2)\,dx$. The central target is

\[
\boxed{
Q_{0,L}[f]=K[F]+w_0\|f\|^2+2|C(f)|^2-2|S(f)|^2
-\sum_{\substack{p\ {\rm prime},\,n\ge1\\n\ell_p<L}}
\ell_pr_p^n\langle f,(T_{n\ell_p}+T_{n\ell_p}^*)f\rangle .
} \tag{2.1}
\]

The corresponding cosine coefficient is $-2\ell_pr_p^n$, while the coefficient of each one-sided delta translation is $-\ell_pr_p^n$. Keep this factor of two explicit.

The digamma partial fractions give

\[
B(\tau^2)=\operatorname{Re}\psi(1/4+i\tau/2)-\psi(1/4).
\tag{2.2}
\]

The positive-kernel expression uses

\[
j(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}e^{-a_kt}
={e^{-t/2}\over1-e^{-2t}},\quad t>0,
\qquad
B(\tau^2)=2\int_0^\infty j(t)(1-\cos(\tau t))\,dt.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The pole kernel is $2\cosh((x-x')/2)$, which has rank two on a finite interval. The negative contact $w_0$ and both pole signs are essential.

The natural closed form domain is

\[
\mathcal D_{\log,L}=\left\{f\in L^2(I_L):
\int_{\mathbb R}\log(2+|\tau|)|\widehat F(\tau)|^2d\tau<\infty\right\}.
\]

The full form is closed and semibounded on this domain independently of whether it is nonnegative: all its nonkinetic terms are bounded at fixed $L$. Smooth compact tests are a form core. Nonnegativity for every complex test at every length, or an unbounded collection of covered lengths, is the RH-equivalent target. No positive gap uniform in $L$ is required. A single short window, a parity sector, or positivity on a numerical mesh is insufficient. The shift parameter $\omega$ in the background is auxiliary; it is not the position coordinate or the new evolution coordinate below.

For external normalization, the earlier source audit compares (2.1) with the printed Weil functional in [Suzuki, v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09096v2). Its different archimedean subtraction changes the displayed contact by $\psi(1/4)-\psi(1/2)$ and gives exactly $w_0$. This pass uses that recorded normalization and the shared background; it has not independently recertified every result in Suzuki's paper.

## 3. Established boundaries of the investigation

These distinctions should survive any continuation.

* The gamma kinetic tower is positive at every length. It is not the whole gamma form, which also has $w_0$ and the two pole amplitudes.
* The earlier explicit full short-interval factor covers $L\le1/4$ and $0\le\omega\le1/2$. The later round-4 construction factors the shifted odd gamma form for $L\le7/10$, $0\le\omega\le1/2$, with floor $1/200$; it is a full-Weil factor only on the prime-free range $L\le\log2$. The central even mean-zero gamma block has a lower bound $11/100$ for $L\le7/10$; the remaining even scalar Schur complement is still unresolved.
* The pairwise positive-network comparison fails already at $L=2/3$ in the specified class. This excludes pairwise independently positive edges and the finite hidden-node comparison class, not arbitrary coherent field theories or arbitrary continuum limits.
* At $L=1$, a normalized odd linear input has negative gamma form but positive full form after the first prime. The recorded rational checks give gamma $<-0.12$, full form $>0.26$, and the gamma remainder after a forced independent-edge debit $<-0.58$. These are useful controls showing that the prime contribution can stabilize an input while the naive positive-edge accounting fails.
* A simultaneous full factor that is closable on ordinary whole-line $L^2$ and covers all compact tests is excluded by the growing-support zero-sampling argument. First the alleged norm identity would imply RH; then $f_N=N^{-1}e^{i\gamma_0x}\phi(x/N)$ tends to zero in $L^2$ but its zero-sampling vector tends to a nonzero coordinate. This is a closability contradiction, not a prohibition on fixed-interval factors or stronger test-function topologies.
* A topological boundary theory does not automatically specify a positive boundary metric. A supertrace, index, ghost pairing, or intersection form is not an ordinary positive Hilbert norm. A positive Hamiltonian $\{\mathsf q,\mathsf q^*\}$ does not by itself identify a particular boundary pairing with (2.1).
* If an amplitude $Af$ is inserted into the differential built from that same factor, the proposed degree-one state can be exact. The relative complex below avoids that specific problem by using a different differential and a quotient source.
* Representative independence is useful but weaker than a Ward identity that fixes the arithmetic observable. Neither primes, their lengths and weights, the oscillator offset, nor the finite contact are selected by the presently specified supersymmetry.

These are prior results or direct structural distinctions. Their historical certificates were not rerun in this pass. The candidate package records the earlier successful replays and their precise scope.

## 4. The positive relative complex to retain

For one mass $a>0$, take $E^0=L^2(\mathbb R)$, $E^1=L^2(\mathbb R;\mathbb C^2)$, and

\[
D_a u=(u,a^{-1}u'),\qquad \operatorname{Dom}D_a=H^1(\mathbb R).
\]

This is closed and has closed range because $\|D_au\|\ge\|u\|$. Its actual adjoint is

\[
D_a^*(v,w)=v-a^{-1}w',\qquad
\operatorname{Dom}D_a^*=L^2\oplus H^1.
\]

The two-term supercharge sends $(u,w)$ to $(0,D_au)$. It squares to zero, and the partner Hamiltonian is the positive operator built from the actual Hilbert adjoint. The physical degree-one space is $E^1/\operatorname{ran}D_a$, identified with $\ker D_a^*$ using the ordinary quotient norm.

Inject one input by $JF=(F,0)$. Its harmonic representative is

\[
\Psi_a(F)=\sqrt{2/a}\,(F-u_f,-u_f'/a),\qquad
\widehat u_f={a^2\over a^2+\tau^2}\widehat F.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus $u_f=(a/2)e^{-a|\cdot|}*F$. The state is nonzero for nonzero $F$, is harmonic, and its norm is

\[
\|\Psi_a(F)\|^2
={1\over2\pi}\int {2\over a}{\tau^2\over a^2+\tau^2}|\widehat F|^2d\tau.
\]

The polarized kernel is $(2/a)\delta_0-e^{-a|x-x'|}$. Summing the projected states over $a_k$ yields $K$ and is finite exactly on the logarithmic form domain.

Do not first form the unprojected infinite source $\big(\sqrt{2/a_k}\,JF\big)_k$: it has infinite squared norm for every nonzero $F$. Prepare each harmonic state, then take its square-summable direct sum. This remains true after the new coupling.

Changing $JF$ by an element of $\operatorname{ran}D_a$ preserves the class. Reparametrizing $D_a$ by an invertible operator with the same range preserves the quotient. Heat evolution preserves an already harmonic state. Raw-source heat evolution has a nonconstant response, so it cannot be cited as a deformation-invariance proof for the boundary observable.

A geometric realization of a single channel uses a positive segment energy with end value $F$, free end value $u$, segment length $a/2$, and end stiffness $2/a^3$. Eliminating the segment gives $(2/a)(\|F-u\|^2+a^{-2}\|u'\|^2)$. Subdivision at fixed total resistance preserves this response. Changing resistance changes it.

Whole-line output matters. Eliminating exterior tails at interval ends gives the channel's Robin data $u'(l)=a u(l)$, $u'(r)=-a u(r)$ and exterior energy $(2/a^2)(|u(l)|^2+|u(r)|^2)$. The candidate note also gives the exact cross-interface exponential kernel. Independent interval pieces cannot be glued by dropping these terms.

One geometric source for the mass labels is the ordinary oscillator
$A_z=-\partial_z^2+z^2-1/2=2N+1/2$. Its eigenvalues are $a_k$ and its ordinary heat trace is $j(t)$. This explains how to realize the assigned channel spectrum; it does not explain why arithmetic selects that offset, spacing, or the coupling $2/a_k$. Replacing the heat trace by a supersymmetric supertrace would change the calculation.

The other candidate families supply useful controls. For a half-space Dirac model with tangential operator $M_a=-i\sigma_1\partial_x+a\sigma_3$, the positive energy is $\int_{y>0}(|u_y|^2+|u_x|^2+a^2|u|^2)$. At frequency $\tau$, $M_a$ has eigenvalues $\pm\rho$, $\rho=\sqrt{a^2+\tau^2}$. The first-order saturation equation produces $e^{-y\rho}P_+F+e^{y\rho}P_-F$. Finite energy requires $P_-F=0$, which fails for every nonzero compact scalar injection $F=(f,0)$. The unrestricted positive minimum instead has multiplier $\rho$. Choosing a responding second component $\tau\widehat f/(\rho+a)$ restores saturation and arbitrary-input coverage, but gives multiplier $2\rho^2/(\rho+a)\sim2|\tau|$. Finite positive collections of these channels do not produce logarithmic growth. An infinite string/tower can change that order, as the relative complex demonstrates.

For abelian CS theory, quotienting only gauge transformations trivial at the physical boundary leaves a chiral edge field. With a chosen positive boundary velocity its classical Hamiltonian is a positive multiple of $\|\partial_x\phi\|^2$. Interpreting $f$ as a profile, as a derivative profile, or as a smeared current gives respectively a $\tau^2$ weight, a constant weight, or the one-sided current pairing $(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^\infty\tau|\widehat f(\tau)|^2d\tau$. Opposite chiralities yield $|\tau|$, not $\log|\tau|$. The current-smearing map allows arbitrary smooth complex $f$; a false mean-zero restriction should not be imported from a periodic classical derivative profile. The bulk supplies surviving edge degrees of freedom and a symplectic structure, but does not fix the needed boundary Hamiltonian.

There is a related smearing caveat for any proposed local supersymmetric insertion. If its charge variation is only a total derivative $\partial_x\mathcal O(x)$, smearing by $f$ gives $-\int f'(x)\mathcal O(x)\,dx$. Compact support removes endpoint terms but does not remove this term. Arbitrary-input closure must therefore be proved, not inferred from a constant-smearing or homogeneous-vacuum Ward identity.

## 5. Exact loops and why the previous scalar coupling needed revision

### 5.1 The primitive return already has the right arithmetic weights

Translate $I_L$ to $(0,L)$ and call the translated input $h$. For one prime put $T=T_{\ell_p}$, $r=r_p$, $P=T^*T$, and $\Pi=I-P$. Since $T$ is nilpotent on this finite interval,

\[
W=(I-rT)^{-1},\qquad
Yh=(\sqrt{1-r^2}\,Wh,\ r\Pi Wh)
\]

are explicitly defined by finite sums. Direct algebra gives

\[
Y^*Y=W^*(I-r^2T^*T)W
=W+W^*-I
=I+\sum_{n\ge1}r^n(T_{n\ell_p}+T_{n\ell_p}^*).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The sum terminates on the interval. Consequently the complete prime contribution is

\[
\ell_p\big(\|h\|^2-\|Yh\|^2\big).
\tag{5.2}
\]

This fixes repetitions and the escape cap exactly, but it is a difference of norms.

The associated passive scattering map $S=(rI-T)W$ satisfies
$I-S^*S=(1-r^2)W^*\Pi W$. On the whole line the scattering map is unitary and its defect vanishes. The positive residence/Poisson response, not that vanishing defect, is the relevant nontrivial loop object:

\[
W_p^\infty=(I-r_pU_{\ell_p})^{-1},\qquad
\mathcal P_p(\tau)
={1-r_p^2\over1-2r_p\cos(\ell_p\tau)+r_p^2}
=1+2\sum_{n\ge1}r_p^n\cos(n\ell_p\tau).
\tag{5.3}
\]

The prime symbol is $\ell_p(1-\mathcal P_p)$. Its sign is not supplied by passivity.

The primitive length arises when differentiating a loop logarithm: the $1/n$ in a logarithmic expansion cancels the $n$ from differentiating the repeated length. An ordinary logarithm alone retains the wrong $1/n$. A fixed finite matrix cannot have $\operatorname{Tr}M^n=-r^n$ for every repetition: its determinant would have to be $1/(1-rz)$ rather than a polynomial. The positive control $M=r$ gives $+r^n$, and a negative scalar alternates signs rather than reversing every repetition.

There is also an exact full-form bookkeeping identity. Let $\theta_L=\sum_{\ell_p<L}\ell_p$, and set

\[
\mathcal Bf=(\Psi_{\rm kin}f,\sqrt{\theta_L}f,\sqrt2 C(f)),
\quad
\mathcal Rf=(\sqrt{-w_0}f,\sqrt2 S(f),(\sqrt{\ell_p}Y_ph)_p).
\]

Then $Q_{0,L}=\|\mathcal Bf\|^2-\|\mathcal Rf\|^2$. Deriving a contraction between these states from an independent conservation law would be useful. Declaring it contractive is precisely the missing inequality.

### 5.2 Coherent histories carry mixed terms

For two forward delays $a,b$, a common amplitude such as $h-\alpha T_ah-\beta T_bh$ generates $T_a^*T_b$, with its actual cap. For $b>a$ on $(0,L)$,

\[
T_a^*T_b=P_aT_{b-a},\qquad
\text{output support }(b-a,L-a).
\]

At $L=5/4$, $a=\log2$, $b=\log3$, the forward sum is inactive but this mixed adjoint term is active. Replacing compressed translations by whole-line unitaries changes the answer.

A finite Gram block with $\langle v_0,v_p\rangle=-c_p$, $\langle v_0,v_q\rangle=-c_q$, and $\langle v_p,v_q\rangle=0$ pays the Schur cost $d_0\ge c_p^2/d_p+c_q^2/d_q$. Loop graphs sharing a return denominator can also generate non-prime-power cycles. Orthogonality, cancellation, or gluing constraints must be proved; they are not consequences of naming edges by primes.

### 5.3 Tangent success is not a finite-coupling identity

The previous positive derivative family uses

\[
M_\eta=I-\eta\sum_j b_jU_{d_j},\qquad
Z_\eta[F]={1\over2\pi}\int B(\tau^2|m_\eta(\tau)|^2)|\widehat F|^2d\tau.
\]

The hypothesis $|\eta|\sum b_j<1$ gives bounded invertibility. Put
$h(\tau)=2\tau^2B'(\tau^2)$; then $h(0)=0$, $h(\tau)\to1$ at high frequency. The first variation is
$-h(\tau)\sum b_j\cos(d_j\tau)$.

For the first prime, $b=2\log2/\sqrt2$ produces the correct first delta pair plus $b(1-h)\cos(\ell_2\tau)$. Its regular kernel is expressed using

\[
R_h(x)=j(t)+t j'(t),\quad t=|x|,\quad R_h(0)=1/4,
\qquad \mathcal F^{-1}h=\delta_0+R_h.
\]

The delta is generated by the jump of $xj(|x|)$ at zero and must be retained. The first residual uses $k=-R_h$, with $k(0)=-1/4$ and changing sign. A finite mass tower misses this contact because its high-frequency limit and the infinite-channel limit do not commute.

Additive two-prime coupling generates an unwanted leading $\cos((\ell_p+\ell_q)\tau)$. Product composition removes that leading mixed delta, but leaves the mixed regular kernel

\[
V(x)=-\tfrac14[j(t)+3t j'(t)+t^2j''(t)],\quad V(0)=-1/16,
\]

in four translates. These first/mixed derivatives are informative but are not the exact finite-coupling remainder.

Indeed, for $m=\prod_p(1-\alpha_pe^{-i\ell_p\tau})$, $0<\alpha_p<1$,

\[
B(\tau^2|m|^2)-B(\tau^2)
=-\sum_{p,n\ge1}{\alpha_p^n\over n}\cos(n\ell_p\tau)+O(\tau^{-2}).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Matching $p=2,n=1$ sets $\alpha_2=2\log2/\sqrt2\approx0.980258$; the second cosine coefficient then has magnitude $(\log2)^2$, not $\log2$. At the first 3-delay the required $\alpha_3=2\log3/\sqrt3\approx1.268568$ is already outside the stipulated range. Taking $\alpha>1$ gives a constant plus a series with $\alpha^{-n}/n$, whose first coefficient still has magnitude below one. Thus a single stable scalar factor per prime cannot meet these tests. Multiple factors and length-dependent realizations are outside that particular argument.

For the first-prime factor, the actual remainder is

\[
r_\alpha(\tau)=B(\tau^2|1-\alpha e^{-i\ell_2\tau}|^2)
-B(\tau^2)-\log|1-\alpha e^{-i\ell_2\tau}|.
\]

At the matching $\alpha_2$, $r_\alpha(0)\approx3.925014$, whereas its first-order prediction is $\alpha_2\approx0.980258$. On the permissible complex bump

\[
f(x)=e^{-1/(1-(x/.49)^2)}(1+.3ix)e^{3ix},\quad |x|<.49,
\]

extended by zero, the prior review computes the actual kinetic change as $+0.00346853325$ and its tangent prediction as $-0.00405814876$. Two quadrature resolutions agree on the actual change to about $4.2\times10^{-11}$. This is a floating-point diagnostic, not a rigorous enclosure, but it is a clear reason to use the exact family below rather than patch tangent kernels.

## 6. New finite-coupling construction: evolve by the exact loop return

Fix a finite prime set $\mathcal P$ and a real contact parameter $c$. For matching $Q_{0,L}$, $\mathcal P$ must include every prime with $\ell_p<L$; it may include extra inactive primes, although that changes the residual.

On whole-line $L^2$ define the norm-convergent series

\[
\mathcal A_{\mathcal P}
=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\ell_p(W_p^\infty-I)
=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\ell_p\sum_{n\ge1}r_p^nU_{n\ell_p},
\qquad
M_t=\exp\!\left[t(cI-2\mathcal A_{\mathcal P})\right].
\tag{6.1}
\]

Here $t\in[0,1]$ is an auxiliary evolution length. Its operator exponential is defined by an absolutely convergent power series in a bounded operator. All translations commute with differentiation and are isometries on every ordinary Sobolev space. Therefore $M_t$ and $M_t^{-1}$ are bounded on each such space. For example,

\[
\|M_t^{\pm1}\|_{H^s\to H^s}
\le \exp\left[t\left(|c|+2\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}
{\ell_pr_p\over1-r_p}\right)\right].
\tag{6.2}
\]

Let

\[
g_{\mathcal P,c}(\tau)
=c-2\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}
\ell_p\,{r_pe^{-i\ell_p\tau}\over1-r_pe^{-i\ell_p\tau}},
\quad v(\tau)=\operatorname{Re}g_{\mathcal P,c}(\tau).
\]

The multiplier of $M_t$ is $m_t=e^{tg}$, so, exactly,

\[
\boxed{
\log|m_t(\tau)|=t\,v(\tau),\qquad
v(\tau)=c+\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\ell_p(1-\mathcal P_p(\tau))
=c-2\sum_{p,n\ge1}\ell_pr_p^n\cos(n\ell_p\tau).
} \tag{6.3}
\]

The real logarithm of the modulus has no branch ambiguity. Do not replace it by a principal complex logarithm of $m_t$, whose imaginary part can jump.

At $t=1$ all repetition weights are correct. The first 3 coefficient is allowed: no condition forces $2\log3/\sqrt3<1$. There are no mixed-prime Fourier coefficients in $v$, even though the amplitude $M_t$ itself contains mixed histories. This is an additive logarithmic response produced by an exponential of the primitive-return generator.

The phase of $m_t$ supplies a causal whole-line realization; the reduced gamma energy below depends only on $|m_t|$. Positivity therefore does not uniquely select this phase. The arithmetic lengths and strengths in (6.1), and the choice $c=w_0$, remain prescribed input data.

This construction works just as well with an assigned non-arithmetic delay, reflection coefficient and real weight. The checker includes such a control. That is a limitation of its explanatory force: it solves an implementation problem but supplies no prime-selection principle.

## 7. The exact positive state and an auxiliary-field description

Replace $D_a$ by

\[
D_{a,t}u=(u,a^{-1}M_tu'),\qquad \operatorname{Dom}D_{a,t}=H^1.
\]

Closedness follows because $M_t$ is boundedly invertible. The first component again gives closed range. Its actual adjoint is

\[
D_{a,t}^*(v,w)=v-a^{-1}M_t^*w',\qquad
\operatorname{Dom}D_{a,t}^*=L^2\oplus H^1.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The adjoint reverses all translations. There is an ordinary positive two-term Hilbert complex with this differential; no indefinite metric or graded trace is used.

The positive energy, before elimination, is

\[
\mathcal E_t[F,(u_k)]
=\sum_{k\ge0}{2\over a_k}
\left(\|F-u_k\|^2+a_k^{-2}\|M_tu_k'\|^2\right).
\tag{7.2}
\]

For each channel, minimization gives

\[
\widehat u_{k,t}
={a_k^2\over a_k^2+\tau^2e^{2t v(\tau)}}\widehat F,\qquad
\Psi_{k,t}(F)=\sqrt{2/a_k}
(F-u_{k,t},-a_k^{-1}M_tu_{k,t}').
\tag{7.3}
\]

For each fixed $k$, $u_{k,t}\in H^2$ when $F\in L^2$, using the upper/lower multiplier bounds. Equations (7.1)–(7.3) directly show that the state is harmonic. Completing the square at each frequency and using monotone convergence of the nonnegative channel sums proves the exact full pairing

\[
\boxed{
Z_t(g,f)
=\langle\Psi_t(G),\Psi_t(F)\rangle
={1\over2\pi}\int
B(\tau^2e^{2t v(\tau)})
\overline{\widehat G(\tau)}\widehat F(\tau)\,d\tau .
} \tag{7.4}
\]

At fixed $\mathcal P,c,t$, the difference between this multiplier and $B(\tau^2)$ is bounded. Thus the form domain remains $\mathcal D_{\log,L}$, and the projected direct sum exists for every admitted input. The same raw-source divergence as in §4 remains; it is not repaired by the evolution.

There is a constrained auxiliary-field implementation of $M_t$. For an initial derivative field $v_0(x)=u'(x)$, use a field $v_\xi$ and prime histories $R_{p,\xi}$ satisfying

\[
R_{p,\xi}=v_\xi+r_pU_{\ell_p}R_{p,\xi},
\qquad
\partial_\xi v_\xi
=c\,v_\xi-2\sum_p\ell_p(R_{p,\xi}-v_\xi).
\tag{7.5}
\]

Solving the first equation gives $R_{p,\xi}=W_p^\infty v_\xi$; hence $v_t=M_tu'$. The histories can themselves be written on transport segments:

\[
\chi_p(\xi,x,s)=R_{p,\xi}(x-s),\quad
(\partial_s+\partial_x)\chi_p=0,\quad 0<s<\ell_p,
\quad
\chi_p(\xi,x,0)=v_\xi(x)+r_p\chi_p(\xi,x,\ell_p).
\]

This makes the translations local in an additional transport coordinate, with explicit return data. The energy (7.2) is an ordinary positive norm restricted to these uniquely solvable constraints.

This is not yet a local superspace action with a proved positive physical measure, or a TQFT with an arithmetic Ward identity. Introducing unconstrained Lagrange multipliers for (7.5) does not automatically make an off-shell action positive. The evolution need not be unitary or contractive, and changing $t$ changes the observable. A proposed superspace completion must derive its boundary conditions, actual adjoints, physical quotient, and observable separately.

## 8. What is matched exactly, and the exact remainder

The high-frequency expansion needed here is

\[
B(\tau^2)
=\log|\tau|-\log2-\psi(1/4)-{1\over24\tau^2}
+O(\tau^{-4}).
\tag{8.1}
\]

It follows by substituting $z=1/4+i\tau/2$ into the digamma asymptotic [DLMF 5.11.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11.E2); the partial fraction identity used in (2.2) is [DLMF 5.7.6](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.7.E6). The displayed inverse-square coefficient was also checked numerically against the implementation's large-frequency expansion.

Define the exact, fully integrated remainder

\[
\rho_t(\tau)
=B(\tau^2e^{2t v(\tau)})-B(\tau^2)-t\,v(\tau).
\tag{8.2}
\]

For finite $\mathcal P$ it is continuous, even and real, with

\[
\rho_t(0)=-t\,v(0),\qquad
\rho_t(\tau)
=-{e^{-2t v(\tau)}-1\over24\tau^2}+O(\tau^{-4}).
\tag{8.3}
\]

The remainder estimate is uniform in real $\tau$ for this fixed family, since $v$ is bounded. It is not a uniform estimate over all prime cutoffs.

Consequently $\rho_t\in L^1(\mathbb R)$. Its inverse Fourier transform $R_t(x)$ is bounded, continuous and tends to zero at infinity. Its restriction as a difference kernel to $I_L\times I_L$ is Hilbert–Schmidt, hence compact. “Regular” here means continuous; it does not mean infinitely differentiable. Section 10 exhibits an actual cusp.

Let $\mathscr R_{t,L}(g,f)$ denote the pairing of that restricted convolution kernel. Since all shifts of length at least $L$ vanish on supported input pairs, (6.3) and (7.4) yield the exact equality

\[
Z_t(g,f)=K(G,F)+t\,c\langle g,f\rangle
-t\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P,n\ge1\\n\ell_p<L}}
\ell_pr_p^n\langle g,(T_{n\ell_p}+T_{n\ell_p}^*)f\rangle
+\mathscr R_{t,L}(g,f).
\tag{8.4}
\]

Thus at $t=1,c=w_0$, with all active primes included,

\[
\boxed{
Q_{0,L}[f]=Z_1[f]+2|C(f)|^2-2|S(f)|^2-\mathscr R_{1,L}[f].
} \tag{8.5}
\]

This is an exact decomposition, not a positive completion. The new family supplies all the prescribed delta and contact coefficients, with a computable compact error.

One can compute the remainder through a full evolution identity rather than a tangent approximation:

\[
\rho_t(\tau)
=\int_0^t v(\tau)
\left[h\!\left(\tau e^{u v(\tau)}\right)-1\right]du,
\qquad h(\tau)=2\tau^2B'(\tau^2).
\tag{8.6}
\]

The checker's independent quadrature of this evolution agrees with direct subtraction to about $6.4\times10^{-14}$ on its listed frequencies. The value of the integrand at $u=0$ is only a derivative, not the remainder at $t=1$.

If independent prime parameters enter $v=\sum_p t_pv_p+c$, mixed differentiation still gives

\[
\partial_{t_p}\partial_{t_q}B(\tau^2e^{2v})
=4v_pv_q\,[s_vB'(s_v)+s_v^2B''(s_v)],
\quad s_v=\tau^2e^{2v}.
\tag{8.7}
\]

It has no nondecaying mixed delta term but is not zero. Nonlinear gamma response still mixes the loop histories in the continuous residual.

An exact elementary control is constant scaling without primes. Put $b=e^c>0$. Then for $x\ne0$ the regular kernel is

\[
R_c(x)=j(|x|)-b^{-1}j(|x|/b),\qquad
R_c(0)={1\over4}(1-b^{-1}).
\tag{8.8}
\]

The contact is $\log b=c$, and $\widehat R_c(0)=-c$. The identity follows from (2.3), including the finite contact left by rescaling the singular $1/(2t)$ term. Even this simplest realization of a constant changes more than the contact: its regular response must be retained.

## 9. A scoped rational-realization obstruction

For one prime let $z=e^{-i\ell\tau}$ and $0<r<1$. Suppose a scalar causal transfer $m(z)$ is rational, has no poles on the closed disk, and is nonzero on the unit circle. Suppose its exact logarithmic modulus were

\[
\log|m(e^{i\theta})|
=c-2\ell\,\operatorname{Re}{r e^{i\theta}\over1-r e^{i\theta}}
\tag{9.1}
\]

for all $\theta$. Remove any finite Blaschke factors corresponding to zeros inside the disk; they have modulus one on its boundary. The remaining rational factor $m_{\rm out}$ has no zeros or poles on the closed disk and has an analytic logarithm. Normalize its constant phase so $m_{\rm out}(0)>0$.

The real part of that logarithm is determined by its boundary values through the harmonic uniqueness theorem. Therefore inside the disk,

\[
m_{\rm out}(z)=
\exp\left(c-{2\ell rz\over1-rz}\right).
\tag{9.2}
\]

This is impossible for a rational function when $\ell r\ne0$: its logarithmic derivative would be

\[
{m_{\rm out}'(z)\over m_{\rm out}(z)}
=-{2\ell r\over(1-rz)^2}.
\]

A rational function's logarithmic derivative has only simple poles, with integer zero/pole multiplicities as residues. The right side has a double pole at $z=1/r$. Equivalently (9.2) has an essential singularity there.

This is an elementary obstruction to a **fixed finite scalar rational response in the one primitive-delay variable, matching every repetition simultaneously**. It is not a no-go theorem for matrix-valued observables, continuous fields, nonrational transfer functions, or finite-horizon matching of finitely many coefficients.

Controls matter here. The rational transfer $m(z)=1-\alpha z$, $|\alpha|<1$, does give exactly $-\alpha^n/n$ in its logarithmic cosine expansion; the checker verifies its second coefficient. The nonrational exponential (9.2) gives the desired $\ell r^n$ sequence, as proved above. Moreover, sufficiently long Taylor polynomials of (9.2) approximate it uniformly on the closed unit disk and are zero-free there, while agreeing with any prescribed finite initial set of Taylor and logarithmic coefficients. Thus permitting complexity to grow with horizon genuinely evades this all-repetition obstruction.

The calculation is not asserted to be new. Standard minimum-phase/all-pass spectral factorization is relevant context; [Oppenheim–Verghese, MIT notes, §§3.3–3.4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-011-introduction-to-communication-control-and-signal-processing-spring-2010/a6bddaee5966f6e73450e6fe79ab0566_MIT6_011S10_notes.pdf) discusses that framework. The obstruction itself is proved here rather than attributed to that source.

## 10. A sharper residual obstruction: one prime already has infinite rank

This argument concerns the exact finite-coupling remainder, not its tangent. Set $t=1$, take a single prime, and assume $c\le0$. Write $v(\tau)=v(\theta)$ with $\theta=\ell\tau$, and expand the real analytic periodic function

\[
H(\theta)=e^{-2v(\theta)}-1
=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\beta_ne^{-in\theta}.
\]

Its Fourier coefficients decay exponentially. Since the mean of $v$ is $c$, strict Jensen gives

\[
\beta_0={1\over2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{-2v(\theta)}d\theta-1
>e^{-2c}-1\ge0.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Strictness follows because the nontrivial prime response is nonconstant. From (8.3),

\[
q(\tau):=\rho_1(\tau)+{H(\ell\tau)\over24(1+\tau^2)}
=O(\tau^{-4}).
\]

This function is bounded near zero, and $\tau^2q\in L^1$. Its inverse transform $Q_{\rm reg}$ is therefore $C^2$. Using $\mathcal F^{-1}(1+\tau^2)^{-1}=\tfrac12e^{-|x|}$ gives

\[
R_1(x)
=-{1\over48}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\beta_n e^{-|x-n\ell|}
+Q_{\rm reg}(x).
\tag{10.2}
\]

The series converges with its derivatives away from the points $n\ell$. In a neighborhood of zero shorter than one primitive length, only its $n=0$ term has a derivative jump. Consequently

\[
R_1'(0+)-R_1'(0-)={\beta_0\over24}>0.
\tag{10.3}
\]

Thus the regular kernel is continuous but has a diagonal cusp.

This proves that its compression has infinite rank on every nonempty open interval. To see this directly, choose a subinterval of length less than $\ell$ and distinct interior points $y_1,\ldots,y_N$. The columns $R_1(x-y_j)$ each have a nonzero first-derivative jump at their own $x=y_j$ and no such jump at the other chosen points. They are linearly independent, since taking derivative jumps of any alleged linear relation forces every coefficient to vanish. If the integral operator had finite rank, approximate delta inputs at each $y_j$ would put all these continuous columns in its finite-dimensional, hence closed, range. Arbitrarily many independent columns contradict that.

Subtracting the rank-two smooth pole kernel does not remove the cusp or the infinite rank. Therefore no finitely many scalar amplitudes of the form
$\sum_{i,j\le N}h_{ij}\overline{\lambda_i(g)}\lambda_j(f)$ can exactly supply $2\overline{C(g)}C(f)-2\overline{S(g)}S(f)-\mathscr R_{1,L}(g,f)$ for this fixed one-prime family.

This conclusion does not exclude one extra field with infinitely many degrees of freedom, or a modification of an existing gamma channel's differential. A “single block” can be infinite-dimensional. It also does not claim that every finite-rank coupling to an infinite bath leaves its response unchanged; the statement applies when the net proposed correction is a finite-rank boundary operator.

Controls: with no prime and $c=0$, the residual is identically zero, so no cusp obstruction remains. A kernel made from the two pole amplitudes really does have rank at most two. With no prime and $c\ne0$, (8.8) has the expansion

\[
R_c(x)={1\over4}(1-e^{-c})
-{1\over48}(1-e^{-2c})|x|+O(x^2),
\]

which gives the same derivative-jump rule. The nonzero cusp is a property of the changed kinetic response, not an assumption about RH.

For two incommensurate prime lengths, the Fourier expansion of $e^{-2v}$ has mixed integer combinations of lengths. These can approach zero. The isolated-cusp proof above has not been extended to that setting in this pass; do not silently replace its one-prime hypothesis by an arbitrary finite prime set.

## 11. New numerical checks and what they establish

All numbers here are double-precision diagnostics, without certified tail or rounding enclosures. The exact coefficient and rank arguments above do not depend on them.

The new checker uses NumPy only and no zeta zeros. Digamma/trigamma are evaluated by recurrence to real part $20.25$ followed by an eight-term Bernoulli asymptotic. Checks against a 200,000-channel positive partial sum are recorded, with the expected finite-tail discrepancy. No target matrix square root, spectrum fit, or full-form eigenvalue sweep is used.

The primitive Fourier tests check $p=2,3,5$ and repetitions $n=1,2,3$. The maximum coefficient discrepancy is $3.4\times10^{-16}$. A two-angle torus check finds mixed sum/difference coefficients of the logarithmic response below $1.2\times10^{-16}$. These support the algebraic identities rather than replace them.

For continuum input checks define

\[
b_L(x)=
\begin{cases}
\exp[-1/(1-(x/(.49L))^2)],& |x|<.49L,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Use $f_{\rm complex}=b_L(x)(1+.3ix)e^{3ix}$ and
$f_{\rm odd}=b_L(x)x/L$. Both are smooth compact tests. The prime pairing is computed independently by spatial integration over its actual overlap. The kinetic and deformed pairings are computed in Fourier space.

Two configurations use $(N_x,N_\tau,T,\Delta)=(192,32,140,1)$ and $(288,48,210,1/2)$: $N_x$ spatial Gauss nodes, $N_\tau$ Gauss nodes per frequency panel, cutoff $[-T,T]$, and panel width $\Delta$. Arrays are transient. Fine-resolution values for $c=w_0,t=1$ are:

The largest coarse/fine difference in these defect values is approximately $2.1\times10^{-9}$. These observed agreements are not error bounds.

The negative defects in the table are strong evidence against completing this fixed $Z_1$ merely by adding an independent positive norm. Such an addition would require $Q_{0,L}-Z_1\ge0$ on every input. A formal exclusion based on these particular numerical witnesses still requires a rigorous enclosure. It is not presented as certified here. The exact finite-rank exclusion in §10 is separate and does not require these signs.

As a positive control for the logic, a form $Z_1+\epsilon|\lambda(f)|^2$, $\epsilon\ge0$, is realized by adjoining $\sqrt\epsilon\,\lambda(f)$ to the state. Independent positive addition can work; it just cannot produce a negative defect.

## 12. What a completion would actually have to do

The reduced problem is now precise:

\[
\text{derive an independently positive constrained system whose boundary pairing is }
Z_1+2C^*C-2S^*S-\mathscr R_{1,L}.
\tag{12.1}
\]

Writing this expression or taking its unknown square root does not solve the problem. Neither does defining a boundary metric to equal it.

A plausible next architecture is a **field-valued relative-complex modification**, mixing the low gamma modes, the pole observables and the primitive-history fields before minimization. It must change the equilibrium response; it cannot be just a finite set of appended pole variables or an independent nonnegative output.

For example, an explicit block energy with response variables $u,w$ could have the schematic form

\[
\|JF-Du-Ew\|_{\mathcal H}^2+\|Aw\|_{\mathcal K}^2
\]

with all component spaces, maps, adjoints, domains and trace conditions specified before calculation. Eliminating $w$ can reduce part of an existing response while the bare energy remains positive. That mechanism is different from subtracting a desired kernel after the fact. It still needs an independently justified choice of $D,E,A$, and the computed response must equal (12.1) on all complex inputs, including the diagonal and endpoints.

This schematic family is not a constructed solution. Choosing $E$ or $A$ through a spectral decomposition of the desired defect would conceal the missing sign/realization problem. A proposed correction should come from an independently defined field equation, geometric incidence rule, or conservation identity that can be tested even on non-arithmetic data.

The gamma mass $a_0=1/2$ is worth examining because it lies at the same exponent as the hyperbolic poles. However, replacing a decaying propagator by a growing one in an isolated channel can introduce a negative Robin mode. A common coupled stability calculation is needed; merely identifying the exponent does not establish positivity.

The topological/SUSY part of a successful theory would need to supply more than the operator algebra:

1. A specified local or constrained field content, boundary trace and physical positive Hilbert space, with every smooth complex input covered.
2. An actual nilpotent charge and Hilbert adjoint on closed domains, with physical boundary states closed and not trivially exact.
3. A protected observable whose Ward identities control its **full** Hermitian pairing, including contact and pole terms, not only an index or leading singularity.
4. A rule for the arithmetic labels and weights, or a clearly stated weaker theorem treating them as external background data.
5. A gluing law that keeps mixed histories, interface fields, exterior response and finite parts.
6. A positivity proof for the jointly coupled bare system before elimination, and an exact elimination identity giving (2.1).

The current model meets the Hilbert-complex positivity and arbitrary-input requirements at fixed finite prime set. It has a constrained transport realization and exact singular coefficients. It does not yet meet the full protected-pairing, arithmetic-selection, or compatible all-length completion requirements.

## 13. Prime cutoffs, interval compatibility and genericity

The choice of a finite prime set is not innocuous for the candidate norm. If $\ell_q\ge L$, adding prime $q$ leaves its compressed delta contribution zero. Nevertheless it changes $v$, $Z_1$, and $\mathscr R_{1,L}$, which cancel each other in (8.4). The checker includes the same $L=1$ complex input with $\mathcal P=\{2\}$ and $\{2,3\}$ to expose this distinction: at $c=w_0$, the fine-resolution $Z_1$ changes from about $0.001051486128$ to $0.002111388176$, although the target $Q_{0,1}$ is unchanged.

Likewise, replacing a full return series by only the powers active at $L$ gives

\[
\mathcal A_L^{\rm trunc}
=\sum_{n\ell_p<L}\ell_pr_p^nU_{n\ell_p},\qquad
M=\exp(cI-2\mathcal A_L^{\rm trunc}).
\]

It matches the same active delta coefficients but has a different continuous remainder. This finite-polynomial-generator variant is mathematically admissible, not automatically physically equivalent to the full primitive-loop model.

Thus agreement on active arithmetic coefficients does not select a unique positive bulk norm or a unique regular correction. A gluing-compatible theory must explain which completion outside the interval is intended. Choosing the active-prime set anew at each $L$ makes the candidate family change at prime thresholds even when the target's entering overlap tends to zero.

There is no boundedly invertible all-prime limit supplied by (6.2). Already at $\tau=0$,

\[
g_{\mathcal P,c}(0)=c-2\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}{\ell_pr_p\over1-r_p}
\longrightarrow-\infty
\]

as the prime cutoff increases. The divergence follows, for example, from the classical divergence of $\sum_p1/p$, since these positive summands dominate it eventually. For each finite cutoff the symbol is continuous, so its value at zero bounds its essential minimum from above; the uniform inverse bound is lost, not just a value at one measure-zero point. No assertion about a complete strong-operator limit is made.

An all-prime interpretation would require a new topology, renormalized equation, or compatible interval construction. No such limit is necessary merely to formulate separate exact finite-interval identities, but those identities must still cover unbounded lengths. None is completed here.

Finally, (6.1) illustrates a general caution: a positive logarithmic kinetic form can encode any assigned bounded real even delay symbol in its high-frequency change by exponentiating a suitable generator. That freedom is why a principal coefficient match cannot itself be evidence that the remaining sign is easy. The hard information is now carried by the exact regular correction and its relation to the poles.

## 14. Concrete continuation tasks and stopping tests

The next pass should pursue one specified field-valued completion, rather than enlarge a positivity sweep.

**First, fix the exact model and its horizon convention.** Start with $\log2<L\le\log3$, $\mathcal P=\{2\}$, $c=w_0$, $t=1$. State whether full primitive histories or the horizon-truncated generator are used. Keep the exact $\rho_1$ of (8.2), not the earlier tangent $k$.

**Second, verify the two structural lemmas.** Independently check the rational logarithmic-derivative argument in §9 and the Fourier-tail/column-independence argument in §10. These are compact analytic tasks suitable for specialist or formal review. Check the derivative-jump sign and the precise one-prime scope.

**Third, certify one defect witness if pursuing a no-addition statement.** Turn the $L=1$ complex-bump value in §11 into an interval enclosure for $Q-Z$. This needs controlled Fourier tails, quadrature error, and digamma or positive-series bounds. A single rigorous negative defect is more useful than many unverified eigenvalues. Until then keep the result labelled numerical evidence.

**Fourth, specify one field correction before solving it.** A worthwhile candidate is a low-mass/history mixing field whose equations naturally produce a continuous, infinite-rank response and whose pole trace is fixed by the same boundary condition. Write its positive bare energy or its positive quotient construction explicitly. Calculate the entire resulting response, including its diagonal cusp, not just one residue or asymptotic coefficient.

**Fifth, use decisive tests.** A successful restricted model must equal (2.1) on all smooth complex inputs in the chosen interval range. A useful failure can be an unmatched delta, a wrong cusp coefficient, a nonzero residual kernel, an endpoint cap error, a negative bare mode, or a domain failure. State exactly which construction is excluded and run a control where the mechanism does work.

**Sixth, only then test multiple labels and gluing.** At $L=5/4$, both 2 and 3 act but the forward $\log6$ shift does not. Check the capped $\log(3/2)$ adjoint interaction and all continuous mixed residuals. Above $\log4$, check the repeated-2 coefficient using an input with appreciable overlap or directly as a distribution. Test whether a prime inactive on a smaller subinterval changes the claimed protected pairing.

**Seventh, separate positivity from arithmetic selection.** Even a successful fixed-label realization should say whether it is an identity for assigned prime data or a theory deriving that data. A non-arithmetic control helps identify how much the symmetry actually determines.

If this field program stalls, the existing even-sector scalar is a well-defined smaller theorem:

\[
s_L=a_L-\langle b_L,F_L^{-1}b_L\rangle,\qquad F_L\ge11/100
\]

on the central even mean-zero subspace for $L\le7/10$. The linked even-sector report provides a response-ansatz error method. Certifying that scalar would be genuine restricted progress, but it is a different task and does not solve the bulk arithmetic-selection problem.

Do not restart these unsuccessful shortcuts without a changed hypothesis: assigning the target square root; assuming the norm-difference map is a contraction; reversing every orbit repetition by one fixed finite trace; treating a position-space logarithm as the required Fourier multiplier; dropping endpoint/exterior energy; equating a supertrace with an ordinary norm; or cancelling only first-order residuals at unit coupling.

This pass created only text, source code and small diagnostic records. No matrix archive, sweep output, third-party PDF, image bundle, or zero cache was placed in the repository. All numerical arrays are generated in memory and discarded. No external archive is required to reproduce this pass, and no empty archive guide or loader was invented.

Run from this directory with a Python environment containing NumPy:

~~~bash
python3 calculations/check_loop_evolution.py \
  --output /tmp/topological-susy-loop-evolution.json
python3 review/check_finite_coupling.py
~~~

The first command writes a small fresh diagnostic record to the chosen path. The preserved review checker writes its diagnostic record beside itself; run a temporary copy of the review directory if the checked-in record must be preserved byte for byte. The environment used here was Python 3.12.14, NumPy 2.3.5, from the bundled Codex Python runtime. The scripts do not hard-code that runtime or an archive root.

Compare scientific quantities with appropriate floating-point tolerances. A matching stored hash identifies the recorded bytes, not a proof or a promise of bitwise numerical reproducibility across systems. The package record includes both stored-file and canonical-JSON-content hashes for the small JSON records, plus the exact input-source hashes. Its manifest does not hash itself.

If a later calculation needs large derived data, place it under szp-archive/susy-positivity/numerics-archives with the appropriate mirrored relative layout. At the first such file, add the paper's ARCHIVES.md and ignore rules, exact regeneration parameters, stored and canonical-content hashes, and a fail-closed consumer using the policy's local-path-then-environment-variable lookup. A proposed variable is SUSY_POSITIVITY_ARCHIVES; it is not needed or implemented by this small calculation. Do not hard-code the maintainer's archive location into a consumer. Third-party PDFs remain temporary reference material regardless of size.
