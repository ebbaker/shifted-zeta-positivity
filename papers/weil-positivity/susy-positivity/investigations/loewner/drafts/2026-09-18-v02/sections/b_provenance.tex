\section{Provenance, and the use of language models}\label{app:provenance}

This manuscript was drafted for Edward Baker by language models of the Claude
family (Anthropic), at his direction and in dialogue with him: working draft
0.1, on 17 September 2026, by Claude Fable 5.1 (model
\texttt{claude-fable-5-1}); drafts 0.2 and 0.3, both on 18 September 2026, by Claude
Opus 5 (model \texttt{claude-opus-5}). Draft 0.2 added the Gauss--Jacobi
assembly and its two propositions, the first registered check programme, the
horizons beyond $\log3$, the Cayley coordinate of
Section~\ref{sec:cayleycoord} and the lattice comparison of
Section~\ref{sec:lattice}, and corrected two numbers of \cite{ContractionNote}.
Draft 0.3 added the horocycle representation of
Section~\ref{sec:horocycle}, the exclusion of the archimedean factor from a
Loewner realization in Section~\ref{sec:nobeta}, the unconditional endpoint
(Proposition~\ref{prop:endpointfree}), and the second registered check
programme.

The investigation was opened by Edward Baker from a research note of the
Wilson-lines investigation \cite{LoewnerNote}; the questions it pursues --- what
exactly is abelian in the shift flow, whether flatness excludes curved
realizations, and whether a Loewner-type evolution could construct the
deformation paths independently --- are his, and the answers here were developed
in response to them. The mathematical content consolidates five research notes
of the program
\cite{LoewnerNote,OpeningNote,ContractionNote,RegisteredNote,HalfDimension},
each written by a model named at its head, and the numerical programmes and
records cited in Appendix~\ref{app:numerics}, also written by them. The proofs
are short and are meant to be checked line by line by a human reader; the
registered computation is replayed by \texttt{validation/drafts.py} and the
remaining numerical statements are reproducible from the programmes and records
in the repository; the readings are labelled as such. Nothing in this document
should be cited as established beyond what its own proofs establish.
