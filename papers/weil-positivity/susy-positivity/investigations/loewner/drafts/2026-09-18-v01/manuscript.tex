\begin{abstract}
The shifted Weil family turns the Riemann hypothesis into a statement about a
one-parameter family of causal all-pass filters: for each shift $\omega>0$ the
transfer $K_\omega(p)=\xi(\frac12+p-\omega)/\xi(\frac12+p+\omega)$ has unit
modulus on the critical line, its compressions to windows of every length are
contractions if and only if no zero lies further than $\omega$ from the line,
and the first-order defect of the compression is the localized Weil form. A
proposal to realize this flow as the deformation of a Wilson line, and a
subsequent proposal to construct the deformation paths by a Loewner-type
evolution, both ask the same question: what, exactly, would a realization have
to produce? This manuscript answers it by factoring the transfer. Writing
$\xi(u)=\frac12u(u-1)\Lambda(u)$, the ratio $\Lambda(s-\omega)/\Lambda(s+\omega)$
is completely monotone, hence the Laplace transform of a positive measure ---
an explicit one, the convolution of a Beta-distributed continuous delay with a
comb on the integer ratios whose weights are the Dirichlet coefficients of
$\zeta(s-\omega)/\zeta(s+\omega)$ --- and the polynomial ratio is a rational
all-pass factor forced by unimodularity. Cleanly: $K_\omega=B_b\Kh_\omega$ with
$B_b$ one Blaschke factor at the pole of $\zeta$ and $\Kh_\omega$ completely
monotone, so the causal kernel is \emph{a positive measure minus twice its
exponential moving average}. The Beta part is Bessel additivity in dimensions
$\frac12\mp\omega$; the comb is the Hecke semigroup of $\N^\times$ acting by
dilations, which no Loewner chain produces in any reading of the variables; the
correction carries no arithmetic; at $\omega=\frac12$ the positive part is the
Eisenstein scattering matrix of the modular surface and the comb weights are
Euler's totient. None of this is a positivity mechanism --- the subtraction
cancels the growth of the positive part exactly and what remains is the
fluctuation the zeros control --- but it makes the transfer, its compression,
its norm and its defect elementary functions of the integers below $e^L$. We
build that assembly and validate it end to end. Written in the right
coordinate the single-atom kernel is $\tau^{\omega-1}$ times a function
holomorphic on $|\tau|<\pi$, so the spike belongs in a Gauss--Jacobi weight
rather than in an evaluated quantity; the assembly then runs in double
precision, is certified against a closed-form Laplace transform involving only
$\Gamma$, and is registered as a replayed check. At $L=\log3,\log5,\log7$ the
compression is a strict contraction whose defect eigenvalue reproduces the
margin of the Weil form in the same basis --- margins from $6\times10^{-8}$ to
$2\times10^{-22}$ --- up to a deviation that is linear in $\omega$ and is
exactly the Galerkin truncation the theory predicts; the exact defect expands as
$2\omega Q-\omega^2(2Q^2+[Q,A])$, whose commutator vanishes on the minimizer;
the nested defect overshoots as predicted; and the first-order mass beyond the
horizon has a closed form from $\xi'/\xi$ alone that the assembled kernel
matches at every horizon. In the Cayley coordinate $P=\frac2\omega\operatorname{Re}(I-V)(I+V)^{-1}$,
where contraction is positivity, $P$ is \emph{even} in $\omega$ --- because the
transfer inverts under $\omega\mapsto-\omega$ and compression respects products
--- so the first-order law holds there with no first-order correction at all.
Finally, in one fixed basis, the best phase-matched lattice surrogate for the
margin is off by factors between $0.76$ and $2.19$ across the three horizons
where the transfer, which contains no zero and no lattice, is off by
$0.54\omega$, $1.20\omega$ and $1.85\omega$. Nothing here constructs a source,
proves positivity, or assumes the Riemann hypothesis except where a statement is
labelled conditional.
\end{abstract}
