\begin{abstract}
A proposal for the bulk-realization program asks whether the shifted Weil family
is the deformation flow of a Wilson line: the deformation of a line's expectation
obeys a differential equation fixed by the variation of its endpoints together
with the non-Abelian Stokes theorem, and the shifted family already obeys a
differential equation of that shape. The attraction is that a flow is rigid where
a matched value is not. This paper makes the proposal precise and reports what
the arithmetic side supplies and what it withholds. We first write the causal
generator $G_{\omega,L}$ out in full, which the program's background had defined
only by naming its symbol, and show that its three factors deliver the three
terms of the target exactly: the shifted form is the $\omega=0$ form with every
off-diagonal kernel and every prime atom multiplied by $\cosh(\omega(x-y))$ and
$\cosh(\omega\log n)$, the diagonal unchanged. One consequence is recorded because
it is easy to lose: the pole and contact term has identically zero real part on
the imaginary axis and reaches the form only through the right half-plane
realization. We then prove that truncation to an interval is multiplicative on
causal kernels, so that the transfers and their generators lie in one commutative
algebra and the path-ordered solution of the flow equation collapses to an
ordinary exponential. The shift direction is therefore an abelian line: the
mechanism the proposal names has, as things stand, nothing to act on, and any
non-commutativity must be supplied transverse to $\omega$. Two positive
consequences follow. The shift acts on inputs by the hyperbolic rotation
generated by multiplication by $x$, so it is a geometric deformation and its
gauge-theoretic analogue is a cusp angle rather than a coupling. And the endpoint
direction, the one the compression supplies, turns out to be an endpoint equation
in the exact sense: the $L$-derivative of the compressed transfer is rank one,
sitting at the leading end with the boundary value of the evolved field as its
coefficient, so the line has a fixed tail and a moving head. Two things follow.
The resulting two-parameter connection is flat, not by hypothesis but because
mixed partials commute, so there is no local curvature to extract; and the
connection does not close on the form domain, since endpoint evaluation is not
form-bounded and the smoothing the shift supplies misses the trace threshold at
every admissible shift, by exactly the borderline. What survives is the atomic
part of the generator's kernel, whose atoms sit at the prime periods: a flat
connection with punctures, whose monodromy is the one place a non-abelian datum
could still live, and which is one-dimensional exactly because those residues are
scalars. We close with two problems and three necessary conditions a deformation
mechanism must satisfy, one of which excludes the displacement operator. Nothing
here constructs a source, proves Weil positivity, or assumes the Riemann
hypothesis.
\end{abstract}
