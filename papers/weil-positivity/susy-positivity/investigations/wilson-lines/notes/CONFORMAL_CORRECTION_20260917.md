# Conformal invariance is not a liability: a correction

**Author: Claude Opus 5 (Anthropic).** 17 September 2026. Correction raised by
Edward Baker against manuscript 0.2 Section 9.4 and the corresponding item in
[the continuation note](CONTINUATION_20260917.md). **The objection is right, and
the error is a specific one: the no-go was stated for a transformation that is
not a conformal transformation of the relevant line.** Working it out reverses
the conclusion and closes a gap in the principal-series reading.

Changes to the manuscript were made in the same pass; Section 6 records them.

---

## 0. Summary

1. **The Weil functional is exactly invariant under translations in $x$**, and
   this is manifest and unconditional --- every kernel in the target depends on
   $x-y$, and the remaining term is a multiple of $\lVert f\rVert^2$.
2. **With $x=\log r$, translation in $x$ is dilation in $r$.** The conformal
   transformations of the $r$-line preserving the two points $0$ and $\infty$ are
   exactly the dilations, together with the inversion $r\mapsto1/r$. In $x$ those
   are translations and reflection --- and **both are already program facts**: the
   Toeplitz structure the numerics exploit, and the first selection rule. So

   > **the Weil form's exact symmetry group is precisely the residual conformal
   > group of a ray with one end pinned at the origin and the other at
   > infinity** --- which is the configuration of arXiv:1102.4948.
3. **The no-go was about the wrong map.** "Dilation destroys the prime atoms"
   refers to dilation in $x$, that is $r\mapsto r^\lambda$, which is not a Möbius
   map and is not in the conformal group of the $r$-line at all. The atoms sit at
   fixed *ratios* $r_2/r_1=p^m$, and ratios are dilation-invariant. **In the ray
   picture the Weil form has no absolute scale to protect.**
4. **Positivity is congruence-invariant**, which is Baker's third point and is
   correct: a conformal reparametrization acts on the form by congruence with a
   positive Jacobian multiplier, and by Sylvester's law of inertia that preserves
   the sign. So when the goal is positivity rather than exact realization, the
   whole conformal orbit of the target is an equally good target.
5. **Flatness does not trivialize shape**, which is his first point and is also
   correct: Proposition 8.4 concerns a two-parameter slice, while the space of
   contours is infinite dimensional. The fibre --- contour deformations at fixed
   $(\omega,L)$ --- is untouched, and it is exactly where the shape term of the
   non-Abelian Stokes variation acts.

The requirement therefore changes shape. It is not "the theory must break
conformal invariance". It is "the theory must be conformal with the right
continuum of dimensions", and the principal series is a continuum, so the two are
consistent.

## 1. The symmetry, stated properly

Every term of the target \eqref{eq:target} is either a multiple of
$\lVert f\rVert^2$ or an integral against a kernel depending on $x-y$: the
archimedean energy through $\gammak(|x-y|)$, the pole form through
$2\cosh\frac{x-y}2$, and the prime sum through translations. Hence

> **The Weil functional is invariant under $f\mapsto f(\cdot-a)$**, exactly and
> unconditionally. (For the localized form this means: translating $f$ within a
> larger interval changes nothing, which is the statement that $Q_L$ is the
> restriction of one functional by support.)

This is not new --- it is the Toeplitz structure the companion investigation's
numerics exploit, and it is visible in the spectral form
$\sum_\rho|\widehat F(\gamma_\rho)|^2$, which sees only $|\widehat F|$. What is
new is the reading.

Put $x=\log r$, so that the arithmetic coordinate is the logarithm of a radial
coordinate on a ray. Then:

| in $x$ | in $r$ | status for the Weil form |
|---|---|---|
| translation $x\mapsto x+a$ | dilation $r\mapsto\lambda r$ | **exact symmetry** |
| reflection $x\mapsto-x$ | inversion $r\mapsto1/r$ | **exact symmetry** (rule 1) |
| dilation $x\mapsto\lambda x$ | $r\mapsto r^\lambda$ | not a symmetry --- *and not conformal* |

The Möbius maps of the line preserving $\{0,\infty\}$ are exactly
$r\mapsto\lambda r$ and $r\mapsto\mu/r$. So the stabilizer of the ray's two
endpoints is generated by dilation and inversion, and **the Weil form is
invariant under all of it.** The special conformal generators move the endpoints
and so are broken by the configuration, not by the form; there is no conflict.

### 1.1 The weight is forced, and the critical line appears

On a ray, a field of weight $\Delta$ has Mellin transform with variable
$s=\Delta+i\tau$, and $\tau$ is the dilation eigenvalue. With $\Delta=\frac12$
this is $s=\frac12+i\tau$: **the critical line.** Condition 9.3 of the manuscript
independently requires effective defect dimension $\Delta=\frac12$, from the
borderline Gagliardo exponent.

So Remark 9.5's principal-series reading no longer rests on a bare
identification. It is now *derived from a symmetry match*: if one asks for a
geometry whose residual conformal symmetry is the Weil form's exact symmetry
group, the ray with a defect at one end is that geometry; the dilation eigenvalue
is then the spectral variable; and the weight that puts it on the critical line is
the same $\frac12$ that Condition 9.3 requires for an unrelated reason. The
program's standing caveat --- that the arithmetic coordinate is not automatically
a physical one --- is not removed, but it is no longer being assumed away: there
is now a reason to make the identification rather than a convenience.

## 2. What the old no-go was about, and what replaces it

The exclusion carried in \cite{ExclusionMap} reads: any renormalization-group
fixed point is scale invariant, and the Weil form fixes an absolute scale twice
over, since dilation destroys the prime atoms and shifts the density symbol. Both
clauses concern **dilation in $x$**, $r\mapsto r^\lambda$, which is not a
conformal map. Under the conformal dilation $r\mapsto\lambda r$ the atoms are at
fixed ratios $p^m$ and are preserved, and the density symbol is unchanged.

So the Weil form has no absolute scale in the ray picture, and conformal
invariance is not in conflict with it. What the atoms do require is a preferred
set of *ratios*, which is a statement about the spectrum of dimensions rather than
about scale: in Mellin variables the prime part carries weight $-\zeta'/\zeta$,
a continuum. A conformal system with a continuous spectrum of dimensions is not
exotic --- the principal series is exactly that --- so the requirement is
consistent. **The correct statement of the obstruction is therefore not "must
break conformal invariance" but "must carry this continuum of dimensions", which
is a sharper and more useful demand.**

I do not claim the exclusion in \cite{ExclusionMap} is wrong for its own purpose;
it is stated there for an RG fixed point in the field theory's own coordinates,
where the relevant dilation may well be the one that breaks the atoms. What is
wrong is my use of it in manuscript Section 9.4 to exclude conformal geometries
for the line, where the relevant coordinate is $r$ and the relevant dilation is
the harmless one.

## 3. Positivity is congruence-invariant

> **Observation.** If $M$ is invertible on the form domain, then
> $Q\succeq0\iff M^*QM\succeq0$. A conformal reparametrization of the line acts
> on a weight-$\Delta$ two-point kernel by congruence with multiplication by a
> positive power of the Jacobian. Hence positivity of the target is a
> **conformal-orbit invariant.**

That is Sylvester's law of inertia, and it is the content of Baker's remark that
only the sign matters. Two consequences and two guards.

*Consequence.* When the goal is positivity rather than exact realization, any
member of the conformal orbit of $Q_L$ is an equally good target. The
ray-to-semicircle map of arXiv:1102.4948 is then not merely a reframing: the
semicircle form is a genuinely different quadratic form with the same positivity
content, on a **compact** contour.

*Consequence.* This is also what the program already does once. The compression
presentation $Q_L=K_+-T_L$ with positivity read as $T_L\preceq K_+$, and the
generalized eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(Q_L;K_+)$, are a congruence restatement of
the same sign. Conformal congruences are a second, geometrically natural class,
and they have not been used.

*Guard.* "Up to congruence" is vacuous if the class is unrestricted, since every
positive form is congruent to the identity. The freedom is only worth something
when the congruence is specified in advance --- a conformal Jacobian, not an
arbitrary invertible operator --- and any use of it must say which.

*Guard, and it is a real tension.* The Jacobian of the ray-to-semicircle map
degenerates at the endpoints. So the congruence is boundedly invertible in the
interior and fails exactly at the endpoint --- which is where
Proposition 8.5's trace obstruction already lives. The conformal freedom and the
program's known obstruction meet at the same point, and whether the degeneration
helps (by softening the endpoint) or hurts (by leaving the form domain) is not
something I can settle here. It is the first thing to check if this route is
pursued.

## 4. Flatness does not trivialize shape

Proposition 8.4 says the connection on the **two-parameter** family
$(\omega,L)$ is flat, so its holonomy is path-independent on a simply connected
domain. The space of contours is infinite dimensional, and the conformal group
acts on it with finitely many parameters. Flatness of a two-dimensional slice
says nothing about the rest.

The useful way to put it: if a correspondence between contour deformations and
motions in $(\omega,L)$ exists, it is many-to-one, and the fibre --- contour
deformations that do not move $(\omega,L)$ --- is untouched by
Proposition 8.4. **That fibre is exactly where the shape term of the non-Abelian
Stokes variation acts**, since a deformation at fixed endpoints is an area
deformation. So the correct statement is not that the shape term is absent but
that it acts on the fibre, and Theorem 5.2's abelian result constrains only
motion along the slice.

This is better than it sounds for the proposal, and it is also a constraint.
If the shape term acts trivially on the fibre, the correspondence is consistent;
if it acts nontrivially, the correspondence is overdetermined, and that is a
genuine condition on a candidate rather than a freedom. Either outcome is
information, and neither has been computed.

## 5. What survives

The empirical obstruction of arXiv:1102.4948 stands and is independent of
everything above: in non-superconformal $\mathcal N=2$ theories with fundamental
hypermultiplets, all BPS open Wilson lines have vanishing expectation value, and
nontrivial protected operators appear only in the superconformal case. But its
direction is now reversed. Previously it read as a trap --- protection requires
conformality, conformality forbids the scale the target needs. It now reads as an
alignment: **superconformality is available, and it is not in conflict with the
target**, because the target's exact symmetry group is the residual conformal
group of the geometry in which those operators live.

That makes the superconformal defect setting of arXiv:1102.4948 the natural place
to run the group-delay test, rather than a place where the test was expected to
fail for structural reasons.

## 7. Status

- Section 1 is elementary and unconditional. The symmetry table is a restatement
  of facts already in the program, with a geometric reading that is new here.
- Section 1.1's derivation of the weight is a symmetry match, not a construction;
  the identification of the arithmetic coordinate with $\log r$ remains an
  identification, but is now motivated rather than assumed.
- Section 2 corrects my own use of an exclusion; the exclusion itself, in its own
  setting, is not challenged.
- Section 3's observation is Sylvester's law. The two guards are stated because
  the freedom is easy to over-read, and the endpoint tension is unresolved.
- Section 4 is a written argument. Neither the triviality nor the nontriviality
  of the shape term on the fibre has been computed.
- No source is constructed, no positivity is proved, and nothing here assumes the
  Riemann hypothesis.
