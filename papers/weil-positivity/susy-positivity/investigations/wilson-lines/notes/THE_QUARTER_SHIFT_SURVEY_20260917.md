## 0. Summary

1. **The right instrument is a graded sequence, not a single coefficient.** Under
   the momentum dictionary $\tau\mapsto\kappa\tau$ that the leading term forces,
   the quantities
   \[
     I_{2m}\ :=\ \Lambda^{2m-1}\Sigma_{2m},\qquad
     \Lambda=2\sum_jn_j\beta_j,\quad
     \Sigma_{2m}=\sum_j\frac{n_jB_{2m}(a_j)}{\beta_j^{2m-1}},
   \]
   are **dictionary-free**: they do not move under any rescaling of the momentum,
   and they do not move under a common rescaling of the $\beta_j$. The target is
   $I_{2m}=2^{2m-1}B_{2m}(\tfrac14)$, that is
   $-\tfrac1{24},\ \tfrac7{480},\ -\tfrac{31}{2688},\ \tfrac{127}{7680},\dots$
   No dictionary has to be argued for, ever again. This replaces the manuscript's
   "fix $\kappa$, then compare" procedure with two lines of arithmetic per
   candidate.
2. **For a single factor the classification is complete and closes in exact
   arithmetic.** $I_{2m}=2^{2m-1}n^{2m}B_{2m}(a)$ --- the width $\beta$ drops out
   entirely. Matching $I_2$ **and** $I_4$ is equivalent to $576u^2+60u+1=0$ with
   $u=B_2(a)$, whose only roots are $u=-\tfrac1{48}$ and $u=-\tfrac1{12}$, giving
   \[
     (a,n)\in\Big\{(\tfrac14,\pm1),\ (\tfrac34,\pm1),\ (\tfrac12,\pm\tfrac12)\Big\}
     \quad\text{and nothing else,}
   \]
   all of which then match $I_{2m}$ for **every** $m$. Two invariants pin the
   answer; the rest are free. In particular $a\geq1$ is impossible for a single
   factor at any $n$ whatsoever, because $I_2=2n^2B_2(a)$ carries the sign of
   $B_2(a)$ --- which is the repair of Corollary 11.7 that does not need $n_j>0$.
3. **The three solutions are one orbit.** The invariants are blind to a group
   generated by (D1) inversion $n\to-n$, (D2) parity $a\to1-a$, and (D3) Gauss
   multiplication. (D3) is the exact identity $B_{2m}(\tfrac12)=2^{2m}B_{2m}(\tfrac14)$,
   which makes $(a{=}\tfrac14,\beta,n)$ and $(a{=}\tfrac12,2\beta,\tfrac n2)$
   **indistinguishable in every invariant**. So the test identifies the
   archimedean factor only modulo this group, and $(\tfrac12,\pm\tfrac12)$ is not
   a second solution but the same one written after duplication.
4. **The survey: everything fails except the system that was already known to
   work.** Bulk Liouville, FZZT, ZZ, $H_3^+$, the Schwarzian/JT wall, the
   $SL(2,\mathbb{R})/U(1)$ cigar and the full-line inverted oscillator all fail, most of
   them already at $I_2$ and by more than an order of magnitude. The single
   survivor is the **inverted harmonic oscillator on a half line**, even sector
   $a=\tfrac14$ and odd sector $a=\tfrac34$ --- which is Bhaduri--Khare--Law
   \[BKL\], already cited by the manuscript --- together with its $2$d string
   realization, the **$c=1$ matrix model**, whose $\sqrt{\ }$ is exactly the (D3)
   image of the half-line phase. There is no new physical system here. What is new
   is that there cannot be one: item 2 says the half line is forced.
5. **One sentence of the manuscript is false.** §11.6 ends "and so is every
   amplitude built from $\Gamma(m+i\,\cdot)$ with $m\geq1$." With $n_j$ of both
   signs there is a **one-parameter family** of two-factor amplitudes with *both*
   shifts at $a=1$ matching $I_2$ and $I_4$ exactly. The statement is true for a
   single factor, by item 2. Retracted and replaced in §4 below.
6. **But $I_6$ cuts the family down to duplication.** Solving $I_6=$ target on
   that family leaves exactly two points, $s=\tfrac14$ and $s=4$, and both are
   Legendre duplication of the target in disguise --- verified by root-finding on
   the whole family and then by the $\Gamma$ identity itself. So no amplitude
   found here defeats the graded sequence; only the truncations.
7. **A second hypothesis of the old §11.5 is also too narrow, and this one was
   wrong.** Manuscript Proposition 11.9 (rigidity at a common shift) concluded
   "with integer multiplicities there is a single factor, $n=1$,
   $a\in\{\frac14,\frac34\}$, and nothing else." The bound
   $\sum n_j\leq(48|B_2(a)|)^{-1/2}$ is correct, but it only forces
   $\sum n_j=1$ when $|B_2(a)|\geq\frac1{48}$, that is $a\in[\frac14,\frac34]$.
   At $a=\frac{4-\sqrt5}8$ one has $B_2(a)=-\frac1{192}$ exactly, the bound is
   $2$, and two factors with $n_1=n_2=1$ and equal widths hit $I_2=-\frac1{24}$
   exactly at every common width. Corrected in manuscript 0.8; imposing $I_4$
   appears to restore the conclusion but that is not proved.
8. **The sharp negative, and it is about the instrument rather than any
   candidate.** The modular surface $\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}$ has scattering matrix
   $\varphi(s)=\Lambda_\zeta(2s-1)/\Lambda_\zeta(2s)$, whose resonances **are** the Riemann zeros,
   at $s=\rho/2$. It has $I_2=-\tfrac16$, four times the target, and fails. The
   half-line inverted oscillator has no zeros related to $\zeta$'s anywhere and
   passes exactly. So: **passing the delay test is not evidence of having the
   zeros, and having the zeros is not enough to pass it.** The test measures the
   archimedean factor on the critical line and nothing else. Read against §8's
   finding that the frame-bound margin is a density effect carrying almost no
   arithmetic, both sides of the programme now say the same thing: what is being
   measured is archimedean, and the arithmetic is somewhere the current
   instruments do not look.

## 1. The invariants

### 1.1 Why a single coefficient is the wrong object

Let a candidate's reflection amplitude be unimodular of $\Gamma$-ratio type,
\begin{equation}\tag{1.1}
 S(\tau)=e^{2ic_1\tau}\prod_{j}
 \left(\frac{\Gamma(a_j+i\beta_j\tau)}{\Gamma(a_j-i\beta_j\tau)}\right)^{n_j},
 \qquad a_j>0,\quad\beta_j>0,\quad n_j\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},
\end{equation}
finitely many factors. Note the hypothesis on $n_j$: **real of either sign**, not
$n_j>0$ as manuscript (11.4) has it. Every amplitude in the survey below except
the $L$-function targets themselves has at least one $\Gamma$ in a denominator,
so $n_j>0$ is not a normalization --- it is a restriction that removes the
physics.

The group delay $\mathcal T(\tau)=\frac{d}{d\tau}\arg S(\tau)$ expands, from
$\operatorname{Re}\psi(a+iy)=\log y+\sum_{m\geq1}(-1)^{m+1}\frac{B_{2m}(a)}{2m\,y^{2m}}$, as
\begin{equation}\tag{1.2}
 \mathcal T(\tau)=\Lambda\log\tau+c_0
 +\sum_{m\geq1}\frac{(-1)^{m+1}}{m}\frac{\Sigma_{2m}}{\tau^{2m}},
 \qquad \Lambda=2\sum_jn_j\beta_j,\quad
 \Sigma_{2m}=\sum_j\frac{n_jB_{2m}(a_j)}{\beta_j^{2m-1}} .
\end{equation}
The prefactor $(-1)^{m+1}/m$ is **universal**: it is the same for every amplitude,
so it cancels in every comparison made here, and it is kept out of $\Sigma_{2m}$
only so that the target reads $\Sigma_{2m}^{\rm Weil}=2^{2m-1}B_{2m}(\frac14)$
with $\Lambda=1$. It is not optional in the equation. At $m=1$ it is $+1$, which
is why the expansion note, which wrote only the $\tau^{-2}$ term, did not carry
it; this note's first draft dropped it at $m\geq2$, and an independent fit of
$\mathcal T$ to numerically differentiated phases caught that, returning the
ratio $-\tfrac12$ at $m=2$ and $+\tfrac13$ at $m=3$ for every amplitude in the
survey.

A candidate's natural momentum $P$ is not the manuscript's $\tau$, and the
relation is linear, $\tau=\kappa P$. The manuscript handled this by fixing
$\kappa$ from the leading coefficient and then substituting, once, for Liouville.
That is correct but it has to be redone by hand for every candidate, and it is
where a reader's suspicion goes first.

### 1.2 The dictionary cancels

Under $\tau=\kappa P$, a delay measured in $P$ becomes
$\mathcal T(\tau)=\kappa^{-1}\mathcal T_{\rm phys}(\tau/\kappa)$. Matching the
leading coefficient forces $\kappa=\Lambda$, and the $\tau^{-2m}$ coefficient
becomes $\kappa^{-1}\Sigma_{2m}\kappa^{2m}=\Lambda^{2m-1}\Sigma_{2m}$.

> **Definition.** $I_{2m}(S):=\Lambda^{2m-1}\Sigma_{2m}$.

**Proposition 1.** $I_{2m}$ is unchanged by any linear change of momentum, and by
any common rescaling $\beta_j\to\lambda\beta_j$. The target is
\[
 I_{2m}^{\rm Weil}=2^{2m-1}B_{2m}(\tfrac14)
 =-\tfrac1{24},\ \tfrac7{480},\ -\tfrac{31}{2688},\ \tfrac{127}{7680},\dots
\]

*Proof.* The first is the computation above. For the second,
$\Lambda\to\lambda\Lambda$ and $\Sigma_{2m}\to\lambda^{-(2m-1)}\Sigma_{2m}$. For
the target, $\Omega(\tau)=\operatorname{Re}\psi(\frac14+\frac{i\tau}2)-\log\pi$ is the case
$n=1$, $a=\frac14$, $\beta=\frac12$, so $\Lambda=1$ and
$\Sigma_{2m}=B_{2m}(\frac14)/(\frac12)^{2m-1}$. $\square$

*(Written proof, unconditional. Numbers in Group H of `check_delay_test.py`.)*

A useful normal form: put $c_j=n_j\beta_j$ and $t_j=\beta_j^{-2}$. Then
$\Lambda=2\sum_jc_j$ and
\begin{equation}\tag{1.3}
 \frac{I_{2m}}{\Lambda^{2m-1}}=\sum_jc_j\,t_j^{\,m}\,B_{2m}(a_j),
\end{equation}
a moment problem in the $t_j$ with $m$-dependent weights. The target is the
single point $c=\frac12$, $t=4$, $a=\frac14$.

## 2. The single-factor classification

**Theorem 2.** Let $S$ be (1.1) with one factor, $a>0$, $\beta>0$, $n\neq0$ real.
Then
\[
 I_{2m}(S)=2^{2m-1}n^{2m}B_{2m}(a)\qquad\text{for every }m,
\]
independent of $\beta$. Consequently $S$ matches the target in $I_2$ and $I_4$ if
and only if
\[
 (a,n)\in\Big\{\big(\tfrac14,\pm1\big),\ \big(\tfrac34,\pm1\big),\
 \big(\tfrac12,\pm\tfrac12\big)\Big\},
\]
and each of these then matches $I_{2m}$ for every $m$.

*Proof.* $\Lambda=2n\beta$ and $\Sigma_{2m}=nB_{2m}(a)\beta^{-(2m-1)}$, so
$I_{2m}=(2n\beta)^{2m-1}nB_{2m}(a)\beta^{-(2m-1)}=2^{2m-1}n^{2m}B_{2m}(a)$.

Write $t=n^2>0$ and $u=B_2(a)=a^2-a+\frac16$. Matching $I_2$ and $I_4$ reads
\[
 t\,u=B_2(\tfrac14)=-\tfrac1{48},\qquad t^2B_4(a)=B_4(\tfrac14)=\tfrac7{3840}.
\]
Since $B_4(a)=(a^2-a)^2-\frac1{30}=(u-\tfrac16)^2-\tfrac1{30}
=u^2-\tfrac u3-\tfrac1{180}$, substituting $t=-1/(48u)$ into the second gives
\[
 \frac{u^2-\frac u3-\frac1{180}}{2304\,u^2}=\frac7{3840}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 576\,u^2+60\,u+1=0 ,
\]
with discriminant $3600-2304=1296=36^2$ and roots
$u=\frac{-60\pm36}{1152}\in\{-\tfrac1{48},\,-\tfrac1{12}\}$.

$u=-\frac1{48}$ gives $t=1$, so $n=\pm1$, and $a^2-a+\frac3{16}=0$, so
$a\in\{\frac14,\frac34\}$. $u=-\frac1{12}$ gives $t=\frac14$, so $n=\pm\frac12$,
and $(a-\frac12)^2=0$, so $a=\frac12$. That all six then match every $I_{2m}$ is
$B_{2m}(1-a)=B_{2m}(a)$ (even index), $(-n)^{2m}=n^{2m}$, and the duplication
identity of §3. $\square$

*(Written proof, unconditional. Verified to 50 digits, Group H.)*

**Corollary 3 (the sign rule, repaired).** For a single factor,
$I_2=2n^2B_2(a)$ carries the sign of $B_2(a)$ whatever the sign of $n$. Matching
therefore forces $|a-\frac12|<\frac1{2\sqrt3}=0.2886751\ldots$ --- and in
particular **no single-factor amplitude with $a\geq1$ can match, at any
multiplicity, positive or negative.** Every reflection off a hard wall in a
Liouville-type potential is of this kind.

This is the statement manuscript Corollary 11.7 should have made. As it stands
the manuscript reaches the same window but only after assuming $n_j>0$, which
forces $\Lambda>0$ and makes the raw coefficient $\Sigma_2$ the discriminant.
With $n<0$ the raw coefficient has the *opposite* sign to the invariant --- for
instance $H_3^+$, at $a=1$, $n=-1$, has $\Sigma_2=-\frac1{4}<0$, passing the
manuscript's test, and $I_2=+\frac13$, failing the real one. The manuscript's
window is right; its derivation does not cover the cases that matter.

## 3. The degeneracy group

Three moves leave every $I_{2m}$ fixed.

**(D1) Inversion, $n\to-n$.** $\Lambda\to-\Lambda$ and
$\Sigma_{2m}\to-\Sigma_{2m}$, so $I_{2m}\to(-1)^{2m-1}(-1)I_{2m}=I_{2m}$. The
test cannot distinguish $S$ from $\bar S=1/S$. This is not a technicality: it
means the test does not fix the **sign** of the group delay, so it cannot by
itself tell a resonance from an antiresonance.

**(D2) Parity, $a\to1-a$.** $B_{2m}(1-a)=B_{2m}(a)$ for even index. The test
cannot distinguish the even from the odd archimedean factor, $\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)$ from
$\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s+1)$ --- that is, $\zeta$ from a real odd character.

**(D3) Gauss multiplication.** From $B_n(mx)=m^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}B_n(x+\frac km)$
at $m=2$, $x=\frac14$, with $B_{2m}(\frac34)=B_{2m}(\frac14)$:
\[
 \boxed{\;B_{2m}(\tfrac12)=2^{2m}B_{2m}(\tfrac14)\quad\text{for every }m\geq1.\;}
\]
Hence $(a{=}\frac14,\beta,n)$ and $(a{=}\frac12,2\beta,\frac n2)$ have identical
$\Lambda$ and identical $\Sigma_{2m}$ for every $m$ --- not asymptotically, not to
some order, exactly. Verified to $m=5$ at 40 digits. Splitting the other way,
$B_{2m}(\frac14)=2^{2m-1}[B_{2m}(\frac18)+B_{2m}(\frac58)]$, likewise leaves the
invariants fixed, which is the "Gauss duplication" row of the table.

A fourth thing the invariants do not see, and this one is not a group action:
$\Lambda$ itself. It scales, $\Lambda\mapsto\lambda\Lambda$ --- that is why
$I_{2m}$ exists --- so bulk Liouville at $b=1$ has $\Lambda=8$ in its own variable
and $\Lambda=1$ after $\beta_j\mapsto\beta_j/8$, with $I_2$ and $I_4$ unmoved.
"$\Lambda$ is the degree" is a statement about the normalization $\tau=\im s$,
$\beta_j=\frac12$, in which an $L$-function's archimedean factor is canonically
written; it is not a test a candidate with an unknown dictionary can be put to,
and no verdict in \S5 uses it.

> **What this means for the programme.** The invariants see the archimedean factor
> as an element of the group of $\Gamma$-ratios **modulo** the Legendre--Gauss
> multiplication relations, inversion and parity. They are an excellent
> instrument for *exclusion* --- everything below is excluded by $I_2$ or $I_4$
> alone --- and they can never, by construction, be an instrument for
> *identification*: a match certifies membership of an orbit, not of a point.

## 4. The retraction

Manuscript §11.6 ends:

> "**Liouville is excluded at every coupling**, and so is every amplitude built
> from $\Gamma(m+i\,\cdot)$ with $m\geq1$."

The first clause stands. **The second is false.** Take two factors, both at
$a=1$, with $n$ of opposite signs:
\[
 S_r(\tau)=\frac{\Gamma(1+ir\tau)}{\Gamma(1-ir\tau)}\cdot
 \frac{\Gamma(1-i\tau)}{\Gamma(1+i\tau)} .
\]
Here $\Lambda=2(r-1)$, $\Sigma_2=\frac16(\frac1r-1)$, and
\[
 I_2=-\frac{(r-1)^2}{3r},
\]
so $I_2=-\frac1{24}$ has the two real roots of $8r^2-17r+8=0$,
\[
 r=\frac{17\pm\sqrt{33}}{16}=1.4215351654086\ldots\ \text{ or its reciprocal},
\]
which is the target's $I_2$ **exactly**, from two shifts both sitting in the
supposedly excluded region. The fourth invariant then comes out, in closed form
using $r^2=(17r-8)/8$,
\[
 I_4=\frac{r^2+r+1}{240\,r}=\frac{25}{1920}=\frac5{384}
 \qquad\text{against the target }\frac7{480}=\frac{28}{1920},
\]
that is $\frac{25}{28}$ of it, short by $\frac3{28}=10.71\%$. So the
counterexample is caught, but only at the next invariant.

**Is that an accident of the two-parameter slice? No.** Allow the multiplicities
to float: two factors at $a_1=a_2=1$, widths $\beta_1=r$ and $\beta_2=1$ (the
common scale is free by Proposition 1), multiplicities $n_1,n_2$. Writing
$P=\Lambda/2$, $p=n_1r$, $q=n_2$ and $s=r^{-2}$, the two conditions
$I_2=-\frac1{24}$, $I_4=\frac7{480}$ solve as
\begin{equation}\tag{4.1}
 s=\frac{7-16P^2}{16P^2\,(1+8P^2)},\qquad
 p=-\frac{1+8P^2}{8P\,(s-1)},\qquad q=P-p,
\end{equation}
which is real and positive-$s$ for every $0<|P|<\frac{\sqrt7}4=0.6614\ldots$.
**A one-parameter family of counterexamples, all with both shifts at $a=1$.**

**And the graded sequence survives it.** Imposing $I_6$ as well, a bisection sweep
of (4.1) over the whole admissible interval finds exactly two roots,
\[
 P=\tfrac12\ (s=\tfrac14),\qquad P=\tfrac14\ (s=4),
\]
and they are **one amplitude**, not two: $P$ double-covers the family modulo the
width rescaling of Proposition 1, folding at $P^\ast=0.3743862\ldots$, the root of
$128P^4+32P^2=7$. Rescaling the $P=\frac14$ solution by $\lambda=2$ gives the
$P=\frac12$ one exactly. That amplitude is the target in disguise: at $P=\frac12$
it is $(n{=}\frac12,a{=}1,\beta{=}2)$ with $(n{=}-\frac12,a{=}1,\beta{=}1)$, and
Legendre duplication $\Gamma(2z)=2^{2z-1}\pi^{-1/2}\Gamma(z)\Gamma(z+\frac12)$ at
$2z=1+2i\tau$ gives
\[
 \frac{\Gamma(1+2i\tau)}{\Gamma(1+i\tau)}
 =2^{2i\tau}\,\pi^{-1/2}\,\Gamma(\tfrac12+i\tau),
\]
verified to 50 digits, so the amplitude is
$(n{=}\frac12,a{=}\frac12,\beta{=}1)$ up to a linear phase --- a (D3) image of the
target, not a counterexample. (The halved form of that identity is true only up to
the branch of the square root: with principal branches it fails by a global sign
on whole intervals of $\tau$, the first being $[2.45,4.8]$. Only
$\frac{d}{d\tau}\arg$ is ever used, so this is immaterial, but it should not be
printed as an equality.) $P=\frac14$ is the same identity at half the
argument.

So the honest statement is: **a single invariant is defeatable, the pair
$(I_2,I_4)$ is defeatable, and on this family the triple $(I_2,I_4,I_6)$ is not.**
Whether the full sequence is rigid in general --- whether $I_{2m}$ for all $m$
determines the amplitude up to the degeneracy group --- is **open**. Theorem 2
settles it for one factor.

**Two cautions about the table.** The sign of $\Lambda$ records the convention in
which each amplitude is read --- whether one writes $S$ or $S^{-1}$ --- and by (D1)
no invariant and no verdict depends on it; only the half-line rows and the modular
surface are pinned here, by the manuscript's $2\eta'-2\theta'=\log\pi$ and by §6
respectively. And $\Lambda$'s magnitude is a property of the quoted normalization,
not of the amplitude, since it is not invariant under a common rescaling of the
widths. $I_2$ and $I_4$ are the only columns that carry meaning.

Notes on the rows.

- **Liouville, FZZT, ZZ, JT and $H_3^+$** all reduce to $\Gamma(1+i\,\cdot)$
  factors, and by Corollary 3 no single one of them can work: $B_2(1)=+\frac16$
  and the sign is wrong before any magnitude is compared. $\mu$, $b$ and the level
  move nothing that matters. FZZT differs from bulk Liouville only by the
  $\frac12$ in the multiplicity, which is a (D1)/(D3) move on nothing.
- **The cigar is the interesting failure, and not for the reason the amplitude's
  advertised tunability suggests.** At $w=0$ the shifts $\frac12+\frac{|n|\mp kw}2$
  are *fixed* --- at $a=1$ for $n=1$, at $a=4$ for $n=7$ --- and what is tuned
  continuously to hit $I_2$ is the level $k$, entering as the **width**
  $\frac2{k-2}$ of a factor with $n<0$. The $k=\frac{14}3$ row therefore has all
  four shifts at $a=1$: it is a member of exactly the phenomenon §4 isolates, and
  like the counterexample there it fails at $I_4$. *These rows depend entirely on
  the quoted shift list; they are the ones most worth an independent
  re-derivation.*
- **The inverted oscillator.** The half line has parabolic-cylinder index
  $a=\frac14$ in the even sector and $a=\frac34$ in the odd, and the full line has
  $a=\frac12$ --- the factor-of-two in the index is the whole difference between
  matching and missing by $4\times$. The even/odd split maps onto the parity split
  $\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)$ / $\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s+1)$ of even and odd Dirichlet
  characters, exactly as (D2) predicts it must. The match itself is **not new**: it
  is the Bhaduri--Khare--Law observation \[BKL\] that the manuscript already cites
  in §11.4. What is new is Theorem 2, which says it is the only possibility.
- **The $c=1$ matrix model.** Its scattering phase is the square root of the
  full-line inverted-oscillator ratio, $(n,a,\beta)=(\pm\frac12,\frac12,1)$, whose
  invariants coincide with the half-line entry by (D3) --- as they must, since the
  free-fermion problem and the half-line problem are the same problem. It is
  therefore not an independent candidate. That the $2$d string's exact $S$-matrix
  reproduces the archimedean place of $\zeta$ to all orders is a pleasant fact
  with no prime content whatever.

## 6. The negative result: the test does not see the zeros

The modular surface row deserves its own statement, because it is the one place
in the survey where the Riemann zeros are literally present.

For $\Gamma=PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ acting on $\mathbb{H}$, the Eisenstein scattering
matrix is
\[
 \varphi(s)=\frac{\Lambda_\zeta(2s-1)}{\Lambda_\zeta(2s)}
 =\sqrt\pi\,\frac{\Gamma(s-\frac12)\,\zeta(2s-1)}{\Gamma(s)\,\zeta(2s)},
 \qquad \Lambda_\zeta(u)=\pi^{-u/2}\Gamma(\tfrac u2)\zeta(u),
\]
whose poles are the zeros of $\Lambda_\zeta(2s)$, at $s=\rho/2$: the resonances
of the modular surface **are** the Riemann zeros, halved --- on
$\operatorname{Re}s=\frac14$ under RH, and in $0<\operatorname{Re}s<\frac12$
unconditionally. If any physical system in this survey ought to pass an
archimedean test calibrated on $\zeta$, it is this one.

It fails, by a factor of four, and the reason is structural.

**The completion matters, and it is easy to get wrong.** $\varphi$ is the ratio of
$\Lambda_\zeta$'s, *not* of the manuscript's $\xi(u)=\frac12u(u-1)\Lambda_\zeta(u)$.
The two differ by $\frac{2s-2}{2s}=\frac{-1+i\tau}{1+i\tau}$ in $\tau=2r$, which
is unimodular --- so the error is invisible to every check of unimodularity ---
but whose own delay is $-2/\tau^2+O(\tau^{-4})$, and that is exactly the order the
test lives at: it would move $I_2$ from $-\frac16$ to $+\frac{11}6$, changing the
verdict from "$\times4$" to "wrong sign". Verified: at $r=2.4$, $\varphi$ agrees
with $\Lambda_\zeta(2s-1)/\Lambda_\zeta(2s)$ to $2\times10^{-41}$ and differs from
$\xi(2s-1)/\xi(2s)$ by $0.408$; the discrepancy is exactly $\frac s{s-1}$.

**Checked from scratch.** On $\operatorname{Re}s=\frac12$ the functional equation
and reality give $\Lambda_\zeta(2s-1)=\overline{\Lambda_\zeta(2s)}$, so
$|\varphi|=1$ --- verified at $r=2.4,\,9.1,\,40$ --- and
$\arg\varphi(\frac12+ir)=-2\arg\Lambda_\zeta(1+2ir)$, verified to $10^{-41}$.

**$\varphi$ is not of $\Gamma$-ratio type**, so (1.1) does not apply to it whole:
it factors as archimedean times arithmetic, and the invariants are defined on the
first. The $\Gamma$ content of $\Lambda_\zeta(1+2ir)$ is $\Gamma(\frac12+ir)$,
entering inverted: **index $a=\frac12$, not $\frac14$, and $n=-1$.** Hence in
$\tau=2r$,
\[
 \mathcal T_{\rm arch}(\tau)=-\log\frac\tau{2\pi}+\frac1{6\tau^2}+O(\tau^{-4}),
 \qquad \Lambda=-1,\qquad I_2=-\tfrac16 ,
\]
confirmed as $\tau^2(\mathcal T_{\rm arch}+\log\frac\tau{2\pi})=0.1666991$ at
$\tau=60$, $0.1666696$ at $\tau=200$ and $0.1666668$ at $\tau=800$, against the
target $I_2=-\frac1{24}$.

**The arithmetic part does not decay.** It adds
$2\operatorname{Re}\frac{\zeta'}\zeta(1+i\tau)=-2\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-1}\cos(\tau\log n)$,
which is still of size $0.90$ at $\tau=10^4$, where $\tau^{-4}=10^{-16}$:

| $\tau$ | $2\frac{d}{d\tau}\arg\zeta(1+i\tau)$ | $\tau^{-4}$ |
|---|---|---|
| $100$ | $-1.3171$ | $1.0\times10^{-8}$ |
| $1000$ | $+0.2829$ | $1.0\times10^{-12}$ |
| $10^4$ | $+0.8975$ | $1.0\times10^{-16}$ |

It has mean zero and therefore reaches no asymptotic coefficient at all --- which
is the exact counterpart, on the other side, of the prime term
$\pi^{-1}\arg\zeta(\frac12+i\tau)$ that manuscript Remark 11.13 says the expansion
cannot see. (This corrects a claim in the first draft of this note, that
$\arg\zeta(1+2ir)$ is bounded and "the zeros contribute nothing to the delay".
The first is false --- $\arg\zeta(1+it)$ is classically unbounded --- and the
second is true only in the precise sense below.)

**The resolution of the apparent paradox** is that the resonances lie off the line
and the delay is read on it. The poles sit at $\operatorname{Re}s=\frac14$ under
RH, while the delay is read on $\operatorname{Re}s=\frac12$, where $\varphi$ is
governed by $\Lambda_\zeta$ at the *edge* $u=1+2ir$ of the critical strip --- a
region in which $\zeta$ has no zeros, so the delay has no resonance jumps and the
zeros enter it not at all. What is left on the line is $\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(2s)$
rather than $\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)$, and that doubling is exactly the
$\frac14\to\frac12$ that costs the factor of four.

So the two halves of the statement:

- **A pass is weak evidence.** The half-line inverted oscillator passes every
  invariant exactly and its spectrum has nothing to do with the Riemann zeros.
- **A failure is not evidence of absence.** The modular surface has the zeros as
  resonances and fails.

Which is to say: **the delay test is a measurement of the archimedean factor on
the critical line, and of nothing else.** Manuscript Remark 11.13 already says the
expansion cannot see the primes. The modular surface says the stronger thing: it
cannot see the **zeros** either, and it shows the same mean-zero arithmetic term
sitting where the primes sit on the other side.

I think this should change the weight the programme puts on the delay condition.
It is a genuine necessary condition, it is cheap, and it is now sharp --- Theorem
2 makes it a complete classification rather than a window. But it is a test of a
*place*, not of a *spectrum*, and no amount of sharpening will make it otherwise.
Condition 10.1's delay clause should be read as a normalization check that a
candidate must not fail, not as a target to aim a construction at.

## 7. Answering the question that was asked

*Find a reflection amplitude with the archimedean shift at a quarter.*

**Yes, and it is unique.** The inverted harmonic oscillator on a half line, even
sector, is the amplitude; by Theorem 2 it is the only single-factor one up to
inversion, parity and Gauss multiplication; and its $2$d-string dress is the $c=1$
matrix model. The structural reason for the quarter is the $\frac12$ in the
Weber/parabolic-cylinder index on a half line, which the full line does not have.

**But this is not progress in the direction the question was pointing.** The
hypothesis behind the item was that finding the quarter somewhere in the CFT
literature would give a construction to build on. What the survey found is that
the quarter is reached by exactly one system, that system is the one the
manuscript already cites in §11.4 as the motivating example, and it is the
Berry--Keating $H=xp$ story in a different coordinate --- prime-free, as §6 of the
delay-test note already established. Nothing in the surveyed string and CFT list
--- Liouville at any coupling, FZZT, ZZ, $H_3^+$, the cigar at any level and
charge, JT, the Schwarzian --- comes near it, and most miss by a sign.

Stated plainly, because it is more useful than a qualified positive: **this line
has been run to the end.** The archimedean side is now completely understood and
completely rigid, and it contains no arithmetic. The remaining content of the
programme is on the other side --- the bounded fluctuation, the primes --- and the
invariants are provably blind to it. I would not spend more of this
investigation's time on candidate amplitudes.
