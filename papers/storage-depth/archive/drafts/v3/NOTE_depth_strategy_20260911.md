# Strategies for increasing depth — discussion note (11 September 2026)

Claude (Fable 5.1), for Teddy. Written while the 256+96 first-step rebuild runs; the numbers quoted are from records R14–R17 of storage-depth v0.3 and from the diagnostics of the review. Everything is in the working normalization.

## 1. Where the programme stands

Certified: `2.99e-29 <= lambda_min(Q_{0,L_q}) <= 3.29e-29` at `L_q = (3/4) log 14 = 1.979`, with residual factor 0.78 and shift interval `9e-16`; at `L_2 = log(56)/2 = 2.013`, after the 96-slab rebuild, `1.69e-30 <= lambda_min <= 1.89e-30` with residual factor 0.8 and shift interval `2e-16`. The true ladder of lowest eigenvalues is therefore known to within factors of 1.1–1.6 at three depths: about `6.6e-28`, `3.3e-29`, `1.9e-30` at `log 7`, `L_q`, `L_2` — a factor of about twenty per quarter-step.

Two facts from the v0.3 work govern what "increasing depth" can mean.

*The dimension barrier.* The joint complement floor needs `H_N - gamma - log(pi L) > rho_L`, and near-resonant high-frequency functions show `rho_L` cannot be beaten on polynomial complements; so `N ~ L e^{rho_L}` with `rho_L` growing like the constant function's arithmetic Rayleigh quotient (1.7 at L=2, 2.8 at 2.5, 4.1 at 3.0, 5.6 at 3.5, 7.5 at 4.0). Passing the leakage tests has cost about four times the dimension needed for a positive floor. Realistic: a few hundred modes to `L = 2.5`, three to four thousand at `L = 3`; `L = 3.5` is out of reach with dense 6144-bit Grams, `L = 4` out of reach with the method.

*What a fixed-horizon certificate sees.* Through the explicit formula, the near-null vector of `Q_{0,L_q}` is a function of exponential type `L_q/2` whose transform has been tuned to vanish at the low zeros (`|F_0(gamma_k)| ~ 1e-28 .. 1e-18` for `k <= 13`); the residual `3e-29` is carried by zeros between heights 85 and 130 on the endpoint-generated `1/tau` plateau. Displacing zero `k` off the line contradicts the certificate at `delta* = 1e-12` (k=1) rising to `0.1` at height 70. So a certificate at horizon `L` constrains zeros below height about `e^{2L}` and nothing above; the milestones `L = 2, 3` carry no information about zeros beyond what has been known since Riemann, and no finite `L` reachable numerically ever will (height `3e12` corresponds to `L ~ 14`).

The second fact reframes the first. Pushing `L` numerically is not a route to information about zeros; it is a route to (i) sharper finite-horizon enclosures as objects in their own right, (ii) method development, and (iii) data for the analytic question that actually matters — how `lambda_min(Q_{0,L})` decays with `L` and why. The all-depth argument the conditional theorem asks for must be analytic, and its premises (`M_theta >= m` at every step) are, for all steps, equivalent to Weil positivity itself.

## 2. Options for the next steps in depth

**(A) Continue the quarter-step recursion as designed (three intervals, 96-mode slabs).** Done for the second step: `Q_{0,L_2} >= 1.69e-30`, `theta = 0.8`, shift `2e-16`, with the three-interval floor 0.768 exactly as projected. The chain analysis says a fourth slab is borderline and a fifth fails even with 96 modes, so this buys two or three more quarter-steps (to about `log 8 = 2.079`) before a merge is forced. Cheap, mechanical, and it completes the second step the paper leaves open.

**(B) The two-interval invariant with a fresh global basis at each step.** Rebuild the old/old Gram on `(0, L_j)` with `N_j` modes at every step; the floor stays near 1.0 as long as `g_{L,N} - rho_L` stays near 1.8. Cost `N_j^2` in 6144-bit moment products (21 minutes at N=256 on Teddy's machine, roughly an hour at 512, half a day at 1000). This is the sustainable form of the recursion. Hybrid: append 96-mode slabs while the comparison floor stays above 0.55, merge every three or four steps.

**(C) Larger steps.** The v0.2 argument for quarter-steps was the collapse of the *coupling* slack (`1 - c^2 = 1.8e-17` at a whole step), which mattered for the absolute comparison of cross norm with old energy. The graph route sidesteps it: by the direct-floor lemma the certified floor is `m/tau^2` for *any* passing `theta < 1`, so `theta` near 1 costs nothing, and the only requirements are (i) enough slab modes to keep `g_{h,M}` up as `h` grows (`-log(pi h)` falls by 1.4 from a quarter to a whole step; `H_128 - H_32 = 1.39` compensates) and (ii) a continuation that still matches the join (the Galerkin minimizer does this automatically). A whole step `log 7 -> log 8` with `M = 128` slab modes on a two-interval partition should have a floor near 0.9 and a head of 384. This would make the recursion "one prime power per step", which is the natural unit: each new `n` changes the active set and the arithmetic bound. Experiment: parameterize the slab length in the first-step builder and run `h = log(8/7)/2` and `h = log(8/7)` with `M = 64, 128`. If the whole step works, the natural schedule is `log 7 -> log 8 -> log 9 -> log 11 -> ...` with a global rebuild at each prime power.

**(D) Push to a chosen horizon.** Candidate targets: `log 8` (2.079, the first composite delay 8 = 2^3 and the end of the paper's motivating step), `log 11` (2.398; the first new prime, `alpha_11 = 0.72`, `rho^+ ~ 2.6`, `N ~ 300–500` for the tests), `log 20 ~ 3` (the practical ceiling, `N ~ 3000–4000`). `log 11` is a reasonable next milestone; `L = 3` is a project for a workstation and a few weeks.

**(E) The positive-shift (Cayley) programme.** No certificate exists; the perturbation route `Q_s >= Q_0 - C s^2` already supplies the diagonal argument's needs. Unless a specific reason appears, leave it as motivation.

## 3. The analytic question worth more effort than more depth

`lambda_min(Q_{0,L}) = min_{f in L^2(0,L)} sum_rho |F(gamma_rho)|^2 / ||f||^2` is the extremal problem: how small can the Fourier transform of a function supported on an interval of length `L` be, simultaneously, at all zeros of zeta? Its decay in `L` is what the storage-depth programme has been measuring. Three data points give `ln lambda_min = -62.6, -65.6, -68.4` at `L = 1.946, 1.979, 2.013`. A natural conjecture is that the decay is governed by the number of zeros a type-`L/2` function can annihilate, `N(2 pi e^L) ~ e^L (L - 1)`; the three points give slopes of `-6.4` and `-5.8` per unit of `e^L (L-1)` (that quantity is 6.62, 7.09, 7.58 at the three depths), not far from `-2 pi`; three points prove nothing, but the test is cheap. The Weil-depth enclosures at `log 2, ..., log 7` are the test of this: if `ln lambda_min(L) + 2 pi e^L (L-1)` is nearly constant across the seven horizons, the ladder has a clean law and the finite-horizon programme has produced a genuinely interesting object. This costs an afternoon with the existing enclosures and the Fourier-side scripts (`zero_sum_check.py` generalizes to any head), and is cheaper than any further depth step.

Related cheap items: (i) make the Fourier-side check rigorous (ball transforms, more zeros, a tail bound from the endpoint values `f_0(0) = f_0(L_q) = 1.8e-14`), which would verify the spatial head's normalization at the `1e-29` level — a much stronger check than anything in the normalization audit so far; (ii) compute the near-null vectors at the seven Weil-depth horizons and confirm the "vanish at the low zeros" structure and the height at which sensitivity is lost (`delta*` reaching 0.1) — the classical support/height relation made quantitative.
