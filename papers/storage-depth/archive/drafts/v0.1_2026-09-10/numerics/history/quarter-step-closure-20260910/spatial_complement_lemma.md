# The spatial complement and the full residual test

Working research, 10 September 2026. The normalization is the same working Weil-depth v0.3 convention as in the [quarter-step note](../quarter-step-20260910/quarter_step_analysis.md). This note establishes the analytic reduction used by the accompanying computations. A positive sign test is required for a successful closure; a positive complement floor alone does not suffice.

Put \(a=\log7\), \(h=\tfrac14\log(8/7)\), and \(L=a+h=\tfrac34\log14\). In the old/new spatial split, let

\[
Q=Q_{0,L}=\begin{pmatrix}A&B^*\\ B&F\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Let \(P_o,P_n\) project onto the first \(N,M\) orthonormal Legendre modes on the two intervals. Use \(P=P_o\oplus P_n\) as the finite head and \(K=I-P\) as the joint complement. All form inequalities below extend from a smooth core to the closed logarithmic form domain. Piecewise polynomials lie in the operator domain: their gamma outputs have square-integrable logarithmic singularities and their arithmetic outputs are square-integrable translated pieces.

## 1. A positive weight controls all arithmetic delays together

Write the positive, self-adjoint arithmetic operator as

\[
(Tf)(x)=\sum_{n\in\{2,3,4,5,7\}}\alpha_n
\left(\mathbf1_{x>\log n}f(x-\log n)
+\mathbf1_{x<L-\log n}f(x+\log n)\right),
\qquad \alpha_n=\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n.
\]

Here “positive” means that it preserves nonnegative functions; it need not be positive as a quadratic form. If a bounded weight \(w\), bounded away from zero, satisfies

\[
Tw\le\rho w,
\]

then \(\|T\|\le\rho\). Indeed, apply
\(2|f(x)f(y)|\le |f(x)|^2w(y)/w(x)+|f(y)|^2w(x)/w(y)\) to each symmetric translated pairing. The resulting quadratic-form bound is \(|\langle f,Tf\rangle|\le\rho\|f\|^2\).

The supplied 512-cell piecewise-constant weight has exact decimal rational values and gives

\[
\boxed{\rho<1.949177212012646.}\tag{1}
\]

For a target cell, each translated interval meets at most two source cells. Taking the larger source weight gives a rigorous row majorant. The proposal is generated by ordinary floating-point iteration; its validity is checked afterwards using exact rational weights and Arb inequalities.

A separate verifier partitions the interval at every discontinuity of every shifted weight. Its 2,152 open intervals cover the operator up to a null set and give the same bound. Exact prime-exponent vectors identify coinciding endpoints; every remaining ordering and cell membership is verified with ball arithmetic. Thus (1) bounds the continuum operator, including all input modes. No spectral assertion about zeta zeros is used.

## 2. The joint old/new complement has a positive floor

For an interval of length \(\ell\), the v0.3 gamma tail estimate gives

\[
g_{\ell,k}=H_k-\gamma-\log(\pi\ell)
-\frac{K_\ell\ell}{2\sqrt{k(k+1)}},
\qquad Q^\gamma_{0,\ell}\big|_{P_k^\perp}\succeq g_{\ell,k}I,
\]

where \(K_\ell\) bounds \(|(G_0(t)-1)/t|\) for \(0\le t\le\ell\).

The gamma cross kernel is \(-1/(2s)-w_0(s)/2\), with \(w_0(s)=(G_0(s)-1)/s\). The singular part has norm at most \(\pi/2\), by the Carleman bound. For an old tail input, its primitive vanishes at both endpoints and has norm at most

\[
\frac{a}{2\sqrt{N(N+1)}}\|f\|.
\]

Integrating the smooth cross kernel by parts therefore bounds its norm by

\[
b_{\rm sm}=\frac{a\sqrt{ah}}{4\sqrt{N(N+1)}}
\sup_{0\le s\le L}|w_0'(s)|.
\]

The derivative is bounded by differentiating the exact rational profile coefficients and adding the radius-3 Cauchy remainder. Thus the gamma cross operator on the joint complement has norm at most \(b_\gamma=\pi/2+b_{\rm sm}\).

Combining this with (1),

\[
KQK\succeq\beta I,
\quad
\boxed{\beta=
\frac{g_{a,N}+g_{h,M}-\sqrt{(g_{a,N}-g_{h,M})^2+4b_\gamma^2}}{2}-\rho.}\tag{2}
\]

For \((N,M)=(128,32)\), the enclosed lower bound exceeds \(0.33467\). For \((256,32)\), it exceeds \(0.90675\). The second pair gives more room to absorb the coupling to the retained space.

These bounds jointly cover the old and new infinite-dimensional complements, including their mutual coupling. They do not yet bound the coupling from those complements back into the head.

## 3. Exact output Grams retain the missing coupling

Let

\[
H=PQP,\qquad W=KQP,\qquad
G_{ij}=\langle Qp_j,Qp_i\rangle,
\]

in the orthonormal piecewise-polynomial head. Then

\[
E=W^*W=G-H^2.
\]

For \(0\le m<\beta\), the finite inequality

\[
\boxed{(\beta-m)(H-mI)-E\succ0}\tag{3}
\]

implies \(Q\succeq mI\). To see this, eliminate the complement from \(Q-mI\) and use \((KQK-mI)^{-1}\preceq(\beta-m)^{-1}I\).

The output Gram is integrated over the entire old and new intervals. There is no output cutoff in \(G\). In particular, \(E\) includes head-to-old-tail, head-to-new-tail, and the signed interference between gamma and arithmetic contributions.

The old-input/old-output part reuses the old-depth v0.3 matrix construction. It does not use a global positivity certificate at the new depth. The other blocks use explicit gamma outputs and translated arithmetic polynomials. The adjoint cross gamma output has polynomial coefficients multiplying

\[
1,\quad \log(1-u),\quad\log(1+h/a-u)
\]

on the unit old interval. The old diagonal also contributes \(\log u\). Products involving the exterior logarithm are integrated by exact recurrences, with dilogarithms for the initial mixed-log moments, all enclosed by Arb. Arithmetic pieces are integrated on their exact support intersections. This retains the endpoint cancellations needed in \(G-H^2\).

If the model output differs from \(QP\) by at most \(\delta\), and the model head differs from \(H\) by at most \(\eta\), then a valid leakage error budget is

\[
e=2\delta\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}\widetilde G}+\delta^2
+2\eta\|\widetilde H\|+\eta^2.
\]

The computation uses a Frobenius bound for \(\|\widetilde H\|\). It subtracts \(((\beta-m)\eta+e)I\) from the model version of (3) before testing all LDL pivots. The analytic profile and the short logarithm-series remainder are included in \(\eta,\delta\). Ball arithmetic encloses all other operations.

## 4. Testing the residual inequality on every old input

Extend the previously constructed continuation by zero outside its original old polynomial space:

\[
J=J_{128,32}P_{128}.
\]

This is a bounded finite-rank map into the new gamma operator domain. The graph congruence

\[
\mathcal T=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\J&I\end{pmatrix}
\]

gives

\[
\mathcal T^*Q\mathcal T
=\begin{pmatrix}H_J&R^*\\R&F\end{pmatrix},
\quad H_J=A+B^*J+J^*B+J^*FJ,\quad R=B+FJ.
\]

For \(0<\theta<1\), put \(\lambda=\theta^{-1/2}\) and consider

\[
M_\theta=\begin{pmatrix}H_J&\lambda R^*\\\lambda R&F\end{pmatrix}.
\]

A coercivity certificate for \(M_\theta\) proves, on the **entire old form domain**,

\[
\boxed{R^*F^{-1}R\preceq\theta H_J,
\qquad S=A-B^*F^{-1}B\succeq(1-\theta)H_J.}\tag{4}
\]

This is a direct test of the desired relative inequality. It does not first assume new-depth coercivity or use a bound derived from such an assumption.

For \(N\ge128\), \(M\ge32\), the graph map is the identity on the joint complement. The modified complement therefore only changes its old/new cross block. The arithmetic cross source windows are disjoint, giving

\[
b_{\rm ar}=\left(\sum_n\alpha_n^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]

A valid modified complement floor is

\[
\boxed{\beta_\theta=
\frac{g_{a,N}+g_{h,M}
-\sqrt{(g_{a,N}-g_{h,M})^2+4\lambda^2b_\gamma^2}}2
-\rho-(\lambda-1)b_{\rm ar}.}\tag{5}
\]

To compute the new leakage, retain the separate old and new output Grams. If \(H_o,H_n\) are the corresponding row blocks of \(H\), put

\[
E_o=T^*(G_o-H_o^*H_o)T,\qquad
E_n=T^*(G_n-H_n^*H_n)T,
\]

where \(T\) is the finite head matrix of \(\mathcal T\). Set

\[
D_o=\operatorname{diag}(I_N,\lambda I_M),\qquad
D_n=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda I_N,I_M).
\]

Then the complete leakage Gram for \(M_\theta\) is

\[
E_\theta=D_oE_oD_o+D_nE_nD_n.\tag{6}
\]

The finite head of \(M_\theta\) is obtained from \(T^*HT\) by multiplying its old/new cross blocks by \(\lambda\). With \(\beta_\theta>0\), the guarded version of

\[
\beta_\theta H_\theta-E_\theta\succ0\tag{7}
\]

is the all-old residual certificate. Its error budget uses
\(e(\|TD_o\|_F^2+\|TD_n\|_F^2)\) for the two leakage Grams and
\(2\lambda\eta\|T\|_F^2\) for the head. All these errors are subtracted before the sign test.

Equations (2), (6), and (7) give a finite, verifiable way to address the old-input complement and its interaction with the retained modes. A successful finite step still supplies no guarantee that the required dimensions or attainable step lengths remain suitable at unbounded depths.

## 5. The energy transported through a successful step

If (4) is certified, Cauchy's inequality in the \(H_J,F\) metrics also gives the full two-input estimate

\[
\boxed{
Q_{0,L}[f,g]\ge(1-\sqrt\theta)
\left(H_J[f]+F[g-Jf]\right).
}\tag{8}
\]

This follows by writing \(g=Jf+v\) in the graph congruence and bounding the remaining cross term by
\(2\sqrt\theta\,H_J[f]^{1/2}F[v]^{1/2}\).

The transported old metric is \(H_J\). Its relation to the preceding energy \(A\) can be extremely small in critical directions; (8) does not promise a fixed percentage of \(A\) at every depth. It specifies precisely which energy a further recursive step can carry, once the candidate continuation and its full residual have both been controlled.
