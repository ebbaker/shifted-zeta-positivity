§3.3, refutes the prediction recorded there, and replaces it with a sharper negative.**

## 1. The question, made precise

The residue/argument-principle half of the question has a clean and completely negative answer, for
a reason of principle rather than computation.

For real-coefficient (slice-preserving) `f`, `V(f) ⊂ ℍ` is a disjoint union of isolated real points
and 2-spheres. A 2-sphere is codimension 2 in `ℝ⁴`, so by Alexander duality

> `H₁(ℍ ∖ V(f)) ≅ ℤ^{#spheres}`,

generated by small loops, one per sphere, each of which lies **inside a single slice `L_I`** and is
literally a classical contour. So the entire homological content of a residue calculus in `ℍ` is the
classical winding numbers, one per conjugate pair. And **an anisotropic deformation cannot change a
homology class by definition** — that is what "homological" means. Shrinking one direction while
expanding another is free, and free in the strong sense: it changes nothing.

So the shape of the cycle can only matter for quantities that are *not* homological invariants —
means of `log|f|`, i.e. Jensen-type integrals. That is where Perotti's formula lives, and it is the
only place the question can have a positive answer. §2 computes it.

## 2. Every `SO(3)`-invariant cycle is a weighted classical mean, and the weight is forced

For real coefficients, `f(x + yu) = α(x,y) + u β(x,y)` with `α, β` independent of the imaginary unit
`u ∈ S²`. So `log|f|` is `SO(3)`-invariant, where `SO(3)` rotates `Im q`. An invariant cycle must be
a hypersurface of revolution: rotate a curve in the half-plane `{(x,y) : y ≥ 0}`. A point `(x,y)` has
orbit a 2-sphere of radius `y` and area `4πy²`, so the induced 3-volume element is `dV₃ = 4πy² ds`.
Writing the generating curve as `r(θ)`, `y = r sin θ`:

> **`w(θ) = 4π r² sin²θ · √(r² + r′²) = sin²θ · h(θ)`, `h > 0`.**

The `sin²θ` prefactor is not a choice. It is the vanishing of the orbit sphere on the real axis, and
it is the whole obstruction found in §5.

For the round `S³` of radius `R`, `w ∝ sin²θ`, and the normalized cosine coefficients are **exactly**
`c₀ = 1`, `c₂ = −1/2`, all others `0`. Hence

> **`M₃(R) := ⨍_{S³_R} log|f| = a₀(R) − ½ a₂(R)`**, `a_n` = the `n`-th cosine-Fourier coefficient of
> `log|f(Re^{iθ})|`.

With all zeros inside `|z| < R`, `a₀ = log|c| + d log R` (classical Jensen) and

> **`a_n(R) = −Re(p_n)/(n Rⁿ)`, `p_n = Σ_k ρ_k^n`**  — verified to `10⁻¹⁶`, `n = 1…6`.

Therefore

> **`M₃(R) = log|c| + d log R + Re(p₂)/(4R²)`.**

Verified three independent ways: direct Monte-Carlo over `S³ ⊂ ℝ⁴` (`4·10⁵` samples), `sin²`-weighted
quadrature on the half-circle, and the closed form — agreeing to MC error on real-rooted,
one-complex-pair and two-complex-pair cases.

**This settles §3.3, against the recorded prediction.** The formula does **not** reduce to classical
Jensen; the correction is real and is the second Fourier mode. Perotti's abstract — *"relates the
value of the function and of its first two derivatives at a point with its integral mean on a three
dimensional sphere centred at that point and with the disposition of its zeros"* (verified verbatim)
— is exactly this: the second Fourier mode is second-derivative data. But the correction is
`Re(p₂)`, a **classical functional of the slice restriction**, so the *substance* of the §3.3
prediction stands even though its letter is wrong. Prop. 3.1 of `NOTE_existence_loophole` is
confirmed constructively rather than abstractly: what the 4D apparatus delivers is Newton power sums,
i.e. Hermite.

## 3. Anisotropy does buy resolution — measured

This is the sound core of the question and it is not nothing. Take the ellipsoid family
`x = a cos t, y = b sin t`; `b/a → ∞` is exactly "expand the sphere directions, shrink the real
direction." Number of cosine modes with `|c_n| > 10⁻³`, i.e. how many power sums the cycle sees:

| `b/a` | shape | modes |
|---|---|---|
| 0.02 | needle along the real axis | 4 |
| 1 | round `S³` | **1** |
| 4 | oblate | 7 |
| 50 | oblate, extreme | **20** |

So the round sphere is the *least* informative cycle in the family — it sees only `p₂` — and
anisotropic degeneration turns on higher power sums, ~20 of them in the extreme limit. **The
intuition is correct: shrinking one direction while expanding another does increase resolution, and
the anisotropy budget converts directly into a power-sum budget.**

Two immediate limitations, both structural:

- Only **even** modes appear: `w` is symmetric under `θ ↦ π−θ` for any cycle centred on the real
  axis, and an `SO(3)`-invariant cycle must be. Re-centring at a real `c ≠ 0` gives the even power
  sums of `ρ − c` instead, so this is a re-coordinatization rather than a genuine obstruction.
- More decisively: **every one of these weights can be written down in `ℂ` by hand.** The 4D geometry
  does not create the modes; it packages one particular positive weight per cycle. This is the
  delivery/payload distinction of `NOTE_existence_loophole` §9, with the quaternions on the delivery
  side.

## 4. What the apparatus computes, as a detector

Combining `M₃` with radial Jensen (which gives the zero-counting function, hence `Σ|ρ_k|²`), define

> `D₂ := Σ_k |ρ_k|² − Re(p₂)`.

Since `|ρ|² − Re(ρ²) = (x²+y²) − (x²−y²) = 2y²`,

> **`D₂ = 2 Σ_k (Im ρ_k)²`** — i.e. `½ Σ (sphere diameter)²`, exactly the sphere radii of §3.1.

`D₂ ≥ 0` always, `= 0` iff every zero is real. So the 4D apparatus *does* produce the quantitative
sphere-radius detector the brief advertised, and it is a **single scalar** equivalent to the full
Hermite Hankel PSD condition. Verified on four cases including a near-real pair at `ε = 0.01`
(`D₂ = 4×10⁻⁴`).

Note what `D₂` costs: `Σ|ρ|²` is **not** algebraic in the coefficients — it needs the counting
function, i.e. classical Jensen at every radius. In `ζ` language `D₂` is `2Σ(β−½)²`, the
second-moment analogue of Littlewood's lemma. That is a recognizable classical object, not new
information. *(I derived this here and did not find a clean literature confirmation of the
second-moment form; treat the Littlewood identification as a plausible identification, not a cited
one.)*

## 5. The sharp negative: the reachable Hermite cone dies at marginality

A claim I nearly recorded and which is **false**: "hyperbolicity is a PSD condition, hence quadratic,
hence not cut out by linear functionals of the `p_n`." Wrong — Hermite PSD *is* an infinite family of
linear inequalities, `Σ_{i,j} v_i v_j p_{i+j} ≥ 0` for all real `v`. So the real question is which of
those functionals an actual cycle can realize.

Realizing the functional with coefficient vector `g = v ∗ v` requires weight coefficients
`c_n = −n Rⁿ g_n` (`n ≥ 1`), and `w = c₀ + 2Σ c_n cos nθ` must be of the form `sin²θ · h`, `h > 0` —
the constraint of §2. This is a **strict** restriction: nonnegative `v` are always reachable,
sign-changing `v` frequently are not, and sign changes are what makes a Hermite functional
discriminating.

Measured, `d = 4`, `R = 3`, roots `{1 ± iε, 0.3, −0.7}`; `λ_min` is the optimal Hermite detector,
"best reachable" is the minimum of `v'Hv` over unit `v` in the reachable cone (`2·10⁵` samples plus
40 rounds of local refinement inside the cone):

| `ε` | `λ_min` (optimal) | best reachable | detects? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.00 | `−16.667` | `−16.666` | yes |
| 0.50 | `−5.2358` | `−5.2358` | yes |
| 0.30 | `−1.0858` | `−1.0858` | yes |
| 0.20 | `−0.24563` | `−0.19398` | yes, degraded |
| **0.15** | `−0.081516` | **`+0.04314`** | **no** |
| 0.10 | `−0.022626` | `+0.18026` | no |
| 0.05 | `−0.0043317` | `+0.24951` | no |
| 0.01 | `−0.00015997` | `+0.26981` | no |

> **The geometrically reachable Hermite functionals detect gross non-reality with no loss at all, and
> then fail completely below `ε ≈ 0.16`.** No cycle of revolution sees a near-real conjugate pair.

This is the §5.3 marginality screen of `NOTE_existence_loophole` arriving from a fourth direction, and
for once it is a *measurement* rather than an argument. `Ξ` is the marginal case by theorem
(`Λ = 0`, Rodgers–Tao), i.e. `ε → 0` is exactly the regime that matters, and it is exactly the regime
this apparatus loses. The earlier framing had route 3 surviving *because* it was exactly critical;
the measurement says the machinery is only accurate where criticality is absent.

**Honest scope, and the `d` question is now moot.** Threshold measured at one degree (`d = 4`) and
three radii (`R = 1.5, 3, 6`, giving `ε* = 0.231, 0.162, 0.137`). An attempted `d`-sweep gave `ε*`
*increasing* with `d`, which I did not believe — random search inside a shrinking cone in higher
dimension is unreliable — and it was recorded as inconclusive rather than as a result. **§7b then
made the sweep unnecessary:** the constraint responsible is exactly one pinned scalar, identified by
freeing it and watching the whole Hermite cone come back. There is no `d`-dependent phenomenon to
measure. *(This also means the negative here is not the uniformity-in-`d` wall in a fourth costume,
which is what I initially expected it to be. It is a different and more specific obstruction.)*

## 6. Control

Standing policy (`NOTE_existence_loophole` §10): for every "X cannot happen," exhibit a case where X
happens. Claim: the collapse is driven by `SO(3)`-equivariance, which holds **only** on the
real-coefficient locus. Variation of `log|f|` over the `S²` of imaginary units at fixed `(x, |Im q|)`:

| `f` | std over `S²` | range |
|---|---|---|
| `1 + q²` (real) | `1.0e−16` | `7.8e−16` |
| `5 − 2q + q²` (real) | `2.1e−16` | `6.7e−16` |
| `1 − q + 3q² − q³` (real) | `2.6e−16` | `1.3e−15` |
| `1 + q·i + q²` | `1.7e−1` | `6.2e−1` |
| `1 + q·j + q²` | `1.7e−1` | `6.2e−1` |

Machine zero on the real locus, `O(10⁻¹)` off it. So the machinery is not vacuous — it is

This also gives that route a concrete acceptance criterion it did not have: a candidate `f` must make

than "find an `f` with `Ξ = N(f)`."

## 7. The loose end, closed: every invariant cycle carries `4πy² ds`

The §7 hope in the first draft of this note was that a **closed loop in the open half-plane**
`y > 0` — giving an `S² × S¹` invariant cycle rather than one bounding a ball — would escape the
`sin²θ` constraint. **It does not, and the reason closes route 3.**

An `SO(3)`-invariant hypersurface in `ℍ` is a union of orbit 2-spheres. The orbit of `(x, y)` is a
round 2-sphere of radius `y = |Im q|` inside the 3-dimensional imaginary space, of area `4πy²`.
So for **any** invariant 3-cycle generated by **any** curve `γ` in the closed upper half-plane,

> **`dV₃ = 4πy² ds`**, and the invariant integral is `∫_γ log|F| · y² ds` up to `4π`.

`sin²θ` was never a property of ball-bounding cycles; it was `y²/R²` on the sphere. Verified:
`∫4πy² ds` over the half-circle reproduces `vol(S³_R) = 2π²R³` to machine precision, and reproduces
the product measure of a torus over a loop centred at `(0.4, 1.5)` of radius `0.6` — which never
meets `y = 0` — to `3×10⁻²` on `6×10⁵` samples.

**And the whole thing is a tautology at the level of information.** A *single orbit 2-sphere* is an
invariant cycle, and the mean of `log|f|` over it is `log|F(x+iy)|` exactly (verified: spread
`< 3×10⁻¹⁵`). So the invariant 4D data is **pointwise equivalent** to the 1D data on the closed upper
half-plane: nothing is lost and nothing is gained. Four dimensions contributes exactly one thing —
the factor `y²` in every *integral* invariant.

## 7b. What `y²` actually costs, isolated to one scalar

The first draft attributed the §5 negative to `y²` suppressing the signal. **That was wrong** (§10).
Measured sensitivity of the mean to a pair at `1 ± iε`, divided by `ε²`:

| `ε` | `S³` mean (weighted by `y²`) | plain unweighted contour mean |
|---|---|---|
| 0.100 | `−0.0556` | `−0.2775` |
| 0.020 | `−0.0556` | `−0.2774` |
| 0.005 | `−0.0556` | `−0.2774` |

`ε²` in **both** — the scaling is generic (a conjugate pair moves symmetrically, so the first-order
variation cancels), and `y²` changes only a constant, by a factor of five. Attributing a generic
effect to the quaternions is precisely the failure mode `NOTE_existence_loophole` §10 warns about; a
control caught it before use.

The correct mechanism is sharper and is a **single scalar degree of freedom**. Because the volume
element vanishes on the real axis, the weight must satisfy `w(0) = w(π) = 0`, which **pins** its
constant term to `c₀ = −2Σ_{n≥1} c_n` instead of leaving it free. Rerunning §5 with `c₀` artificially
freed:

| `ε` | `λ_min` (optimal Hermite) | `c₀` **pinned** (the real cycle) | `c₀` free (hypothetical) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.20 | `−0.245629` | `−0.193761` | `−0.245598` |
| 0.10 | `−0.022626` | **`+0.180322`** | `−0.022613` |
| 0.05 | `−0.004332` | `+0.249610` | `−0.004312` |
| 0.01 | `−0.000160` | `+0.270126` | `−0.000118` |

With `c₀` free the reachable cone recovers the optimal Hermite detector to 3–4 digits at every `ε`.
With `c₀` pinned — which is the actual geometry — detection dies below `ε ≈ 0.15`.

> **The entire cost of doing this in four dimensions is one pinned constant, and the reason it is
> pinned is that an `SO(3)`-invariant cycle cannot put weight on the real axis — which is exactly
> where RH says the zeros are. The four-dimensional volume element is blind to the critical line.**

That is the closure. Route 3 is finished: the apparatus is real, it computes Hermite constructively,
it is pointwise equivalent to the classical data, and its one structural feature is a blindness
located precisely at the target.

## 8. Thimbles: the mechanism exists, but not on the `ζ` side

The first draft proposed Lefschetz thimbles as the correct home for the intuition: cycles that shrink
in one direction and are non-compact in another, carrying **integer** intersection numbers that jump
across **Stokes lines**, a real codimension-1 locus. Per the round-6 methodological rule this was
*answered* rather than left proposed. Three independent negatives:

**(i) The mechanism is real — in physics.** Integer Stokes constants are established:
Gu–Mariño, *Peacock patterns and new integer invariants in topological string theory*
([arXiv:2104.07437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07437)) — "*we conjecture that the corresponding
Stokes constants are integer invariants of the Calabi–Yau threefold*"; Garoufalidis–Gu–Mariño–Wheeler
([arXiv:2111.04763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04763)). Douaud–Kashani-Poor
([arXiv:2604.19731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19731)) tie Stokes automorphisms to the
Kontsevich–Soibelman algebra — "*in all cases, the Stokes constants determined numerically were
integers*" — i.e. genuine integer invariants jumping across real walls. **The shape I wanted exists.**

**(ii) The Riemann–Siegel integral has the wrong topological type for it.** Berry,
*Riemann's saddle-point method and the Riemann–Siegel formula*, abstract verbatim: Riemann's contour
integral "*involved not an isolated saddle, nor a saddle near a pole or an end-point or several
coalescing saddles, but the configuration, unfamiliar even now, of a **saddle close to an infinite
string of poles***." **One saddle plus poles — there are no competing saddles for a Stokes phenomenon
to act on.** The only integers in the analysis are `N = ⌊√(t/2π)⌋` and the sign `(−1)^{N+1}`: a
count of Dirichlet terms, not thimble intersection numbers. Berry does invoke *resurgence* (the tail
reproducing conjugates of the first `N` terms), and Berry–Keating repeat it, but neither states any
dependence on zero locations. No paper applies a Picard–Lefschetz decomposition to the Riemann–Siegel
integral at all.

**(iii) Structurally, the saddle cannot see the zero divisor.** The saddle sits where the *phase* of
the integrand is stationary — fixed by the `Γ`-factor and exponential asymptotics, at
`z_s = it/N − 1/(2N)`. Moving a zero off the line changes the *values* of `Z(t)`, not the topology of
the steepest-descent decomposition. Zeros are outputs of the formula, never data controlling which
saddles contribute.

**(iv) The polynomial analogue, which is the one thing I could test directly, is refuted at the
smallest example.** The natural repair is to put the zeros *in the exponent*: for `∫ p(z)^s dz` the
saddles are the critical points `p′ = 0`, and the Stokes condition is equality of `arg p` between two
critical points. All zeros real ⟹ all critical points real ⟹ `arg p ∈ {0, π}` ⟹ maximal degeneracy —
so "RH ⟺ maximal Stokes degeneracy" has the right *shape* (exactly critical, hence passing the §5.3
marginality screen, and formally parallel to Kenyon–Okounkov–Sheffield's "total reality ⟺ maximal
amoeba degeneration"). **The converse is false.** `z² + 1` has a real critical point with a real
value while both zeros are non-real; so do `(z²+1)(z−2)` and `(z²+¼)(z²+1)`. Not a detector.
Recorded as refuted, not proposed.

**One genuine find, in the opposite direction.** Fantini–Rella, *Modular resurgent structures*
([arXiv:2404.11550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.11550)): the Stokes constants there **are the Dirichlet
coefficients** of an `L`-function. That is route 5(a)'s "physics *produces* arithmetic" card made
explicit, with an actual `L`-function rather than an analogy — and it contains nothing about zeros.
*(Abstract quoted through an extraction layer, not read directly; treat as verified-modulo-extraction.)*

## 8b. Route 5(a) settled at the same time

The brief's item 3 was: read McSpirit–Rolen ([arXiv:2505.00799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00799),
*Quantum Modular Forms and Resurgence*) and determine the arrow direction. **Verdict: one-way, as
expected — and the reason is sharper than "one-way."**

Their own framing of the programme they follow: resurgence "*can generate holomorphic quantum modular
forms **given the extra data of an `L`-function with a functional equation***." Their theorems run
*asymptotic series → the quantum modular form*, entirely inside the resurgence/modularity side.
Nothing is inverted toward the `L`-function; "recover the `L`-function", "zeros", "critical line",
"Riemann Hypothesis" are all absent.

The species mismatch of the brief's §5.2(ii)–(iii) is also confirmed twice over. They treat **Zagier's
original examples** (Eichler integrals of half-integral weight forms, Maass period functions, the
real-analytic Eisenstein period function) — *not* the Garoufalidis–Zagier matrix-valued `Φ_α(ℏ)`, which
appear as motivating context only. And Bettin–Conrey is cited but placed at a **degenerate boundary**
of their family: their Definition 1.1 requires the modularity obstruction to extend *analytically*,
whereas specializing Folsom's smooth family to the Zagier/Bettin–Conrey cases makes those functions
"*degenerate' to discontinuous functions*." "Vasyunin", "Nyman", "Beurling" do not appear.

The one hedge worth carrying: their Stokes constants encode the Fourier coefficients `a_m`, so *all*
Stokes data formally pins down the `L`-function's Dirichlet coefficients. **But that is a coefficient-list
identity, not a transport of structure** — the same tautology this project keeps meeting, and the
reason the chain still does not compose. Route 5(a) is closed, with a better reason than the brief had.

## 9. Where this leaves the programme

**Route 3: closed.** Deciding check done (§2), prediction refuted, the loose end closed (§7), the
binding constraint isolated to one pinned scalar (§7b), and the mechanism controlled (§6). Nothing
remains in it. What it produced: a constructive verification of Prop. 3.1, an explicit statement of
what Perotti's formula computes, and the observation that the 4D volume element is blind to the
critical line.

**Route 5(a): closed** (§8b), with a sharper reason than "the arrow points the wrong way."

**The thimble idea: closed** (§8), at three independent points, one of them a direct computation.

**Route 4: was already closed.** So **route 5(b) — retarget to Nyman–Beurling — is now the only live
item in the programme**, and it is untouched by anything in this note. Its first actions are unchanged
and unstarted: read the Báez-Duarte / Bettin–Conrey literature, run the Davenport–Heilbronn control
against it, and then ask the round-1 question in the new formulation — does any physical or geometric
structure supply the density statement Nyman–Beurling needs? That is where the next thread should
open, and per §5.4 of the brief nothing should be built before the third question is answered.

One transferable observation from this round, since it is the second time the same shape has appeared.
Route 3 failed because the geometry's natural weight vanishes exactly on the locus of interest.
Kenyon–Okounkov–Sheffield succeed because the geometry's natural degeneration *is* the locus of
interest. **The screening question that separates them — does the structure's natural degeneration
coincide with the target locus, or avoid it? — is worth asking of the Nyman–Beurling candidates
before anything else**, and it is cheaper to ask than the round-1 rigidity question.

## 10. Errors in this note's own drafts

1. **§3.3's recorded prediction** ("Perotti's formula reduces to classical Jensen") was wrong. The
   `S³` mean is `a₀ − a₂/2`. The prediction's substance survived; its letter did not.
2. **The first draft's §5 explanation was wrong.** I attributed the loss of the marginal regime to
   `y²` suppressing the signal. The `ε²` scaling is universal — the unweighted classical contour has
   it too — so this was a generic effect misattributed to the quaternions. Caught by a control run
   before use. The corrected mechanism (§7b) is the pinned constant term, which is a genuinely
   quaternionic constraint and is one scalar wide.
3. **The first draft's §7 framed the toroidal family as "removing the constraint."** It does not; the
   constraint is `dV₃ = 4πy² ds`, universal to every invariant cycle. I had mistaken a consequence
   (`w` vanishing at the endpoints) for the constraint itself.
4. **A claim I nearly recorded and did not:** "hyperbolicity is a PSD condition, hence not cut out by
   linear functionals of the `p_n`." False — Hermite PSD *is* an infinite family of linear
   inequalities `Σv_iv_j p_{i+j} ≥ 0`. Caught before it entered the note.
5. **The first draft's `d`-sweep** was reported as inconclusive rather than as a result, which was
   correct; it is now moot, since §7b identifies the constraint exactly and no sweep is needed.

**Pattern.** Items 2 and 4 were caught by controls run *before* drawing the conclusion, and item 3 by
following the derivation through instead of accepting the first draft's framing. That is three
self-catches in one round against one inherited error (item 1), which is the first time in this
project the ratio has gone that way. The corrective from `NOTE_existence_loophole` §10 is working; it
should stay standing policy.
