Q1  the GKS energy gap is ADDITIVE, so its relative size vanishes
==============================================================================
  Bogomolny minimum for charge k:  E_min(k) = 8 pi^2 k.
  GKS: any non-self-dual YM connection has E >= E_min(k) + 16 pi^2.
  So the gap is a FIXED 2 instanton actions, independent of k.

   charge k  E_min / 8pi^2  gap / 8pi^2   relative gap
          1              1            2         2.0000
          2              2            2         1.0000
          3              3            2         0.6667
          5              5            2         0.4000
         10             10            2         0.2000
         50             50            2         0.0400
        100            100            2         0.0200
       1000           1000            2         0.0020

  => the gap theorems isolate the (anti-)self-dual STRATUM.  They do not
     become stronger at high charge; the relative gap -> 0.  Whatever
     rigidity they give is a statement about a neighbourhood of the ASD
     locus, and says nothing about the non-self-dual sector where the
     freedom actually is.

Q2  the known non-self-dual solutions are increasingly unstable saddles
==============================================================================
  Sadun's quadrupole family (CMP 163, 1994), r,t odd >= 3:
    c_2 = (r^2 - t^2)/8,  Morse index = r*t,  nullity = 12.
  Taubes' lower bound for any non-minimal YM connection: i >= 2(|c_2| + 1).

Q3  what the programme needs, against what the literature supplies
==============================================================================
  requirement                                   self-dual / ADHM         full YM
  existence cheap (big solution space)                no  (8k-3)   YES (inf-dim)
  solution space determined vs overdetermined           overdet.      determined
  nonzero current available                                   no             YES
  Bogomolny / energy bound available              yes (equality)    yes (strict)
  isolation theorem for the class                gap thms nearby      NONE known
  solutions isolated in moduli                 yes (smooth mod.)   NO (families)
  weakly stable members                             yes (minima)   NONE (Taubes)
  2-sphere excluded from degeneracy locus       n/a (no jumping) NO -- it is the
                                                                   generic locus

  The last row is the decisive one and it is not our computation.  In Sadun's
  family -- the standard construction of non-self-dual Yang-Mills fields on S^4 --
  the solutions are parametrised partly by the HOLONOMY AROUND A 2-SPHERE
  S_+ subset S^4 (a singular orbit of the SO(3) action), and the family includes
  connections with a holonomy singularity along exactly that 2-sphere
  (classification: Sibner-Sibner, CMP 1992).

  So in the very sector where full Yang-Mills has its freedom, a 2-sphere is the
  STANDARD degeneracy locus rather than a forbidden one.  The programme needs
  "no 2-sphere" as a theorem about the class.  The literature says 2-spheres are
  where the interesting non-self-dual solutions live.

==============================================================================
SUMMARY  the trade-off of brief §3, now external
==============================================================================
  Full Yang-Mills buys EXISTENCE at exactly the cost of ISOLATION, and the
  exchange rate is documented by other people's theorems:

    * existence: the YM system after gauge fixing is a DETERMINED second-order
      elliptic system, so local solutions are parametrised by boundary data --
      infinite-dimensional.  The ASD equations are OVERDETERMINED (first order,
      3 equations for 4 unknowns modulo gauge), which is the structural reason
      the instanton moduli space is a finite 8k-3 and full YM has no analogue.
      This is the one place in the whole programme where the finiteness
      criticism of memo15 §0(5) is answered.

    * isolation: every rigidity theorem in the subject has the form
      "small ==> (anti-)self-dual or flat" -- Bourguignon-Lawson-Simons, Min-Oo,
      Dodziuk-Min-Oo, Gerhardt, Nakajima, Feehan I/II, Feehan discreteness,
      Gursky-Kelleher-Streets, Vieira.  Not one isolates a NON-self-dual
      solution.  And Sadun's continuous 2-parameter family shows non-self-dual
      solutions are not isolated even within a single symmetry class.

  So the freedom that makes full Yang-Mills attractive is the same freedom that
  destroys the sphere-free property.  That is brief §3's rigidity/existence
  trade-off again -- but for the first time it is instantiated by an external
  literature rather than by our own matrix computations, at the level of a whole
  PDE theory.

Q4  do the candidate degeneracy loci even have the right DIMENSION?
==============================================================================
  The zero set of R Xi in H is a union of 2-SPHERES (2-dimensional, one per
  conjugate pair) and POINTS (0-dimensional, one per real zero).  Any locus we
  propose to match it against must be able to be 2-dimensional.

  For a Yang-Mills field on a 4-manifold with structure group G, dim g = d, the
  anti-self-dual curvature F^- is a section of Lambda^2_- tensor g, i.e. a
  3 x d matrix of functions.  The rank-degeneracy loci have codimension
      codim{ rank F^- <= r } = (3 - r)(d - r).
  In a 4-manifold, a locus can contain a 2-sphere only if its dimension is >= 2,
  i.e. codimension <= 2.

  G          d = dim g         r<=0      r<=1      r<=2
  U(1)               1        3(1d)     0(4d)        --
  SU(2)/SO(3)         3     9(empty)     4(0d)     1(3d)
  U(2)               4    12(empty)  6(empty)     2(2d)
  SU(3)              8    24(empty) 14(empty)  6(empty)
  SO(4)              6    18(empty) 10(empty)     4(0d)

  Reading (codim c gives a locus of dimension 4 - c, 'empty' if c > 4):
   * SU(2)/SO(3), d = 3:  codims 9, 4, 1  ->  loci are EMPTY, POINTS, or a
     HYPERSURFACE.  Nothing 2-dimensional.  So no curvature rank locus of an
     SU(2) Yang-Mills field can BE a union of 2-spheres.  The dimensions
     simply do not line up, before any analysis.
   * and the two that are not empty are both dead for other reasons: the
     0-dimensional one is counted by a Thom polynomial in characteristic
     classes (Feher-Nemethi-Rimanyi), hence a deformation invariant, hence
     brief §6.3; the hypersurface can contain spheres, so it supplies no
     isolation theorem.

   * U(2), d = 4, is the ONE case where codim 2 occurs: {rank F^- <= 2} =
     {det of the 3x4 minors degenerate} has codim (3-2)(4-2) = 2, i.e. a
     2-DIMENSIONAL locus, which can be a union of 2-spheres.  This is the
     only slot in the whole family that is not excluded on dimension
     grounds, and U(2) is also inside the Bourguignon-Lawson stability
     theorem's hypothesis.  It is the one concrete thing here worth a day.

  CAVEAT.  'codim c' is the GENERIC codimension.  For a particular Yang-Mills
  field the locus can be larger than generic, so these counts exclude
  matching-by-dimension but do NOT by themselves give an isolation theorem
  (which would need the reverse: a proof that the locus can never be
  2-dimensional for ANY member of the class).
