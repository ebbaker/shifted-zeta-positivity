SUMMARY
==============================================================================
  A slice Pi_e is a Ruling-B line {w = w0} in the incidence quadric Q, whose
  normal direction is ONE COMPLEX dimension (Q is 2-dimensional, the line is 1).
  memo9's two real transverse directions in H are that one complex direction, and

      nu^2  =  r^2 - t^2  =  y^2 sin^2(theta)  =  4 y^2 g_FS(dw,dw) + O(|dw|^4),

  so memo9's transverse Hessian is a POSITIVE HERMITIAN FORM on the normal bundle
  of a Ruling-B line -- a real multiple of the Fubini-Study metric on the sphere of
  complex structures, with coefficient 4 y^2 * 4 R_0 sum_p sigma_p^2 prod(...).
  The isotropy that memo9 observed numerically is exactly the U(1)-invariance of
  the round metric.  "Second transverse jet along a slice" = "second-order normal
  data along a Ruling-B line of Q".

  AND: rho maps {w = w0} to the ANTIPODAL Ruling-B line {w = -1/conj w0}, always
  disjoint from it.  So no slice is rho-real, and the sigma-type invariant -- which
  is a property of RULING-A components and of how rho permutes them -- cannot be
  read off any finite-order jet along a single Ruling-B line, at any order: rho
  moves the base line to a line at finite distance, which no jet reaches.

==============================================================================
the model bundle from twistor_lift: E = O + O(-d,0),  End E > O(d,0) + O(-d,0)
==============================================================================
  twistor_lift §3: the zero divisor of the product-type lift F x id is
  Z(F) x P^1_w = sum over zeros of {zeta = zeta_i}, i.e. the pullback of a degree-d
  divisor from P^1_zeta.  So O(Z) = O(d,0) -- ASYMMETRIC between the rulings, which
  is exactly the sign pattern the table above requires.  Orders m = n+1:

  Convention: the obstruction to extending from Q^(m-1) to Q^(m) involves the
  m-th power of the conormal bundle.  Row m is therefore the obstruction to
  REACHING the m-th formal neighbourhood Q^(m).

   reach Q^(m)   h^1(Q, O(d-2m, -2m))  for d =
                      0     2     4     6     8    10    12    14
         m = 1        0     1     3     5     7     9    11    13
         m = 2        0     0     3     9    15    21    27    33
         m = 3        0     0     0     5    15    25    35    45
         m = 4        0     0     0     0     7    21    35    49
         m = 5        0     0     0     0     0     9    27    45

  nonzero iff d >= 2m.  So:
    reaching Q^(1): needs deg >= 2
    reaching Q^(2): needs deg >= 4
    reaching Q^(3): needs deg >= 6      <-- the Yang-Mills slot in the ambitwistor ladder
    reaching Q^(4): needs deg >= 8

  READING.  The obstruction group is nonzero exactly when the divisor degree
  is large enough relative to the order -- and the THIRD-order slot, the one
  that carries the field equation in the ambitwistor correspondence, first
  opens at degree 6.  So the Jensen truncations J^{d,n} of Xi with d >= 6 are
  the smallest ones for which a third-order obstruction is not excluded on
  dimension grounds alone.  Below d = 6 the route is dead for free.

  What this does NOT say: that any such obstruction is nonzero, or that it
  encodes reality of zeros.  See the caveat in the docstring.

PART 5  why shielding defeats a POINTWISE graded family
==============================================================================
  P_m(x) is dominated, as m -> infinity, by whichever zero is NEAREST to x:
      P_m(x) ~ 2 |x - z_0|^{-2m} cos(2 m phi)   if the nearest zero is a pair at
      angle phi = arg(x - z_0), and  ~ |x - rho|^{-2m} > 0 if it is real.
  So the graded family detects a conjugate pair as soon as that pair is strictly
  nearer to SOME real point than every other zero -- then cos(2 m phi) < 0 for
  infinitely many m and P_m(x) < 0.  A pair a +- i y_0 is nearest at x = a iff
  y_0 < dist(a, other zeros).  If every pair is SHIELDED -- some other zero
  always closer -- the leading term is positive for every real x and every m, and
  the whole pointwise family is blind.  Measured below: the shielding margin.
