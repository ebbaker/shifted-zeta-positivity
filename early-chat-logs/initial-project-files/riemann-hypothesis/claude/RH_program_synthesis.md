# The Rotational-Symmetry Program for RH: Synthesis and a Proposed Function Class

*Research memo — August 17, 2026. Prepared from a close reading of Quat_Riemann.pdf, Thol.pdf, Tconffinal.pdf, Tconf2.pdf, ASDYM_sym.pdf, and Generalized_Isothermal.pdf (all E. B. Baker III).*

---

## 1. The program in one paragraph

Set $s = \tfrac12 + iz$, so that $\Xi(z) := \xi(\tfrac12+iz)$ is entire, even, of order 1, and real on the real axis, with the critical line rotated onto $\mathbb{R}$. RH says all zeros of $\Xi$ are real. The program: extend $\Xi$ (or objects built from it, like $\xi'/\xi$ or $\log\xi$) from $\mathbb{C}$ to the quaternions $\mathbb{H}$ by exploiting the rotational symmetry around the real axis, so that real zeros become **point** phenomena and conjugate pairs of off-axis zeros become **2-sphere** phenomena, and then find a quaternionic/twistor-theoretic structure that treats points and spheres differently — a discriminant that could constrain off-axis zeros out of existence.

## 2. What each paper contributes, and where each stalled

**Quat_Riemann (2015).** Introduces the rotation map $R$ (eq. 2.5): $f(z) = \bar\eta F(\eta z\bar\eta)\eta$, i.e. the axially symmetric extension $F(t+re_q) = u(t,r) + e_q v(t,r)$ of an intrinsic holomorphic $f = u+iv$. The Fueter-type map $Q_0 f = -\tfrac14\Delta R(f)$ produces Fueter-regular functions. Applied to $\xi'/\xi = \sum_\rho (z-\rho)^{-1}$: on-axis zeros map to Cauchy–Fueter kernels $G_{\rho}$ (point singularities), off-axis pairs to $H_\alpha$ (singular on a 2-sphere). **§4 contains the key negative result:** when integrated against rotationally symmetric regular test functions, $I_h(H_\alpha, F) = I_c(h_\alpha, f)$ — the 4D pairings reproduce the 2D contour integrals *exactly*, for all test functions agreeing with a complex function on the axis. The Laurent-expansion argument shows why: the pairing is determined by the complex Laurent data. §§5–8 then build the higher machinery: the Lie algebra $J_i = \tfrac{e_i}{2} + L_i$ of rotations around the real line, the decomposition of harmonic/regular functions into $V_{2\ell+1}$ irreps, quaternionic spherical harmonics $\mathcal{Y}^m_\ell$, the raising/lowering operators $B_\pm$, the maps $Q_\ell = (B_+)^\ell Q_0$, the Bessel-type exponentials $E_\ell(\lambda,z) = i^\ell(j_\ell(\lambda y) + i j_{\ell+1}(\lambda y))e^{\lambda x}$, and a Mellin/plane-wave inversion theory. **The manuscript is unfinished at exactly the critical point:** §9 truncates mid-recursion (the coefficient $\gamma_{n,m,b}$ is never given) and §10, "Application to the Riemann hypothesis," is empty. The constraints on off-axis zeros promised in the abstract were never written down.

**Thol (2017).** Articulates the four deficiencies of Fueter regularity (no multiplicative closure, no geometric interpretation, no analytic continuation, no Riemann surfaces) and pivots to twistor space: coordinates $\mathbb{H}\times I_u$ on $\mathbb{CP}^3\to\mathbb{HP}^1$, with the fiber coordinate $\eta\in I_u$ *being* the complex structure. Defines twistor lines (spheres with center $h$, quaternionic radius $\rho$; the space $\mathrm{TL}\cong\mathbb{H}_\mathbb{C}$ = complexified Minkowski points) and twistor conformal maps. **Rigidity theorem** (Prop. 3.1, reproved in Tconffinal): a holomorphic self-map of twistor space restricting to *degree-one* embeddings on fibers is a Möbius transformation of $\mathbb{H}_\mathbb{C}$. This is the precise form of "if all induced maps on the $S^1$ complex structures are degree one, the function is linear." The generalization to "twistor holomorphic" via an AHS-type connection is begun but the paper is unfinished.

**Tconffinal (2023).** The polished version: frustrated conformal transformations $\bar\sigma\nabla f = 0$ with a self-dual left connection and anti-self-dual right connection; solutions induce holomorphic, composition-closed maps of ambitwistor space. The BPST example yields $f=q$ (conformal downstairs, nontrivial upstairs). Rejected as "bespoke"; in retrospect the equations were probably too close to rigid.

**Tconf2 (2024).** The important structural pivot: stop mapping *points* and map *null curves / self-dual surfaces*. Via the Shaw correspondence, **any degree-$d$ holomorphic endomorphism of $\mathbb{CP}^3$ induces a self-dual morphism** — an infinite nontrivial class at last, with degree one recovering conformal maps. Causal morphisms via biquaternion projective space.

**ASDYM_sym (draft) & Generalized_Isothermal (2026 notes).** Applications to Yang–Mills (pullback of ASDYM under self-dual morphisms) and generalized isothermal coordinates. The footnote in Generalized_Isothermal records the deflating conversation: self-dual morphisms reduce to symmetries already in Mason–Woodhouse, *Integrability, Self-Duality, and Twistor Theory*.

**Diagnosis.** The program forked. The morphism line (Tconf2 → ASDYM_sym → Gen_Isothermal) drifted toward Yang–Mills symmetries — real mathematics, but pointed *away* from RH. The RH line (Quat_Riemann) stalled precisely where a tool distinguishing point-zeros from sphere-zeros was needed, and the §4 negative result explains why linear tools couldn't provide one (see §5 below). The function-class question that caused the twistor detour, however, has a clean answer — and the answer reconnects the two forks.

## 3. The proposed function class: intrinsic slice regular functions and their twistor lifts

**The class you were looking for almost certainly is the slice regular (Cullen regular) functions of Gentili–Struppa — specifically the *intrinsic* ones — together with their twistor lifts.** You cite Cullen and Gentili–Struppa in all the papers for the *definition*, but the theory built on that definition after ~2007, and especially its twistor-geometric branch after ~2012, is exactly the missing infrastructure. Point by point against your desiderata:

**(a) Generated by complex functions.** A slice regular $f$ on an axially symmetric domain satisfies the representation formula: it is determined by its restriction to a single slice $\mathbb{C}_J$. The intrinsic ones (real power-series coefficients, i.e. $f(\bar z) = \overline{f(z)}$ upstairs — precisely your condition below eq. 2.6 of Quat_Riemann) are *exactly* the image of your map $R$. Your $R$ **is** the slice extension; you already had the right class in 2015 and used it only as an intermediate step before applying $\Delta$.

**(b) Closure under addition and multiplication, restricting to the usual operations.** For intrinsic $f$, $f(q)\in\mathbb{C}_{e_q}$, so pointwise products stay in the (commutative) slice: the intrinsic class $\mathcal{N}(\Omega)$ is a **commutative real algebra under pointwise addition and multiplication**, and restriction to $\mathbb{C}_i$ is an algebra homomorphism onto the intrinsic holomorphic functions. (For non-intrinsic slice regular functions the right product is the $\star$-product; on intrinsic ones $\star$ = pointwise.) This is verbatim the "addition and multiplication that restricts to the usual addition and multiplication" requirement. Composition also preserves the intrinsic class.

**(c) Rigidity.** Identity principle (determined by one slice), maximum modulus, open mapping theorem, and a **zero-structure theorem** (Gentili–Stoppato): the zero set of a nonzero slice regular function is a union of isolated points and isolated 2-spheres of the form $x + y\,I_u$. Not "all holomorphic functions of three complex variables" — the class has complex-analysis-grade rigidity because it *is* complex analysis, propagated by the rotation.

**(d) The rotational symmetry and the complex structure.** Axial symmetry is built in: intrinsic functions are the equivariants of your $M_\eta$ action — the $\ell=0$ sector of your own decomposition (4.5). The "appended $I_u$ of complex structures" intuition is realized rigorously by the **twistor lift**: Gentili–Salamon–Stoppato (*Twistor transforms of quaternionic functions and orthogonal complex structures*, J. Eur. Math. Soc. 16 (2014); arXiv:1205.3513) show a slice regular function on a symmetric slice domain lifts to a **holomorphic curve in the Klein quadric** — a holomorphic object on twistor space inducing the map downstairs — and use these lifts to construct and study **orthogonal complex structures (OCS)** on domains of $\mathbb{R}^4\cong\mathbb{H}$. Non-conformal slice regular functions produce non-standard OCS on complements of their degenerate sets; e.g. the complement of a parabola carries an OCS coming from a rational quartic in $\mathbb{CP}^3$. Follow-up work (Altavilla; Altavilla–Sarfatti, *Slice-polynomial functions and twistor geometry of ruled surfaces in* $\mathbb{CP}^3$, Math. Z. 2019) develops exactly the "maps acting on the complex structure" picture. **This is Thol.pdf's program carried out** — with the escape from your rigidity theorem being that the lift is *not* required to send twistor fibers to lines of $\mathbb{CP}^3$ (degree one); fibers may go to higher-degree rational curves. Structurally this is the same escape you found independently in Tconf2 (degree-$d$ endomorphisms instead of degree-one).

**(e) Compatibility with the $\xi$ integral formula.** Riemann's formula (in the rotated variable) is $\Xi(z) = 2\int_0^\infty \Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du$ with $\Phi(u)=\sum_{n\ge1}(2\pi^2n^4e^{9u/2} - 3\pi n^2 e^{5u/2})e^{-\pi n^2 e^{2u}}$, $\Phi$ even, positive, decaying like $e^{-\pi e^{2u}}$. Since $\cos(qu)$ is entire intrinsic slice regular in $q$ (power series with real coefficients) and $|\cos(qu)| \le e^{|{\rm Im}\,q|u}\!\cdot\!O(1)$ while $\Phi$ decays super-exponentially, the integral converges for all $q\in\mathbb{H}$ and defines the **entire intrinsic slice regular extension**
$$\Xi(q) = 2\int_0^\infty \Phi(u)\cos(qu)\,du, \qquad \cos((t+re_q)u) = \cos(tu)\cosh(ru) - e_q\,\sin(tu)\sinh(ru).$$
Equivalently $\Xi(q) = \sum_n c_{2n} q^{2n}$ with the real Taylor coefficients of $\Xi$. No Laplacian, no loss of multiplicativity, no choice of $\ell$: this *is* the canonical rotation of $\Xi$ into $\mathbb{H}$.

**The reformulation.** By the zero-structure theorem:

> **RH $\iff$ the zero set of the quaternionic $\Xi:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}$ contains no 2-sphere** (equivalently: every zero is a real point).

And on the twistor side, using your own dictionary (twistor lines $\leftrightarrow$ points of $\mathbb{H}_\mathbb{C}$ = complexified Minkowski; the sphere $x+y\,I_u$ is the real-slice trace of the complex point $x+iy$ off the real slice): **RH $\iff$ every "zero line" of the lifted $\Xi$ belongs to the real family** (lines over the real axis). Off-axis zeros of $\zeta$ are literally *twistor lines on which the lift of $\Xi$ degenerates*. This makes the program's slogan precise: RH becomes a statement about which twistor lines can lie in a divisor attached to an arithmetic object.

## 4. Relation between the two extension hierarchies

Your two constructions now sit in one diagram. $R$ (slice extension) is where **algebra** lives (products, composition, zero spheres); $Q_0 = -\tfrac14\Delta\circ R$ (Fueter's theorem) and the ladder $Q_\ell = (B_+)^\ell Q_0$ are where **harmonic analysis** lives (Cauchy–Fueter integrals, the $V_{2\ell+1}$ decomposition, Bessel kernels, Mellin inversion). The two are linked by the Fueter map and its inverses ("Fueter primitives"). Modern quaternionic operator theory (Colombo–Sabadini–Struppa and collaborators) is built on precisely this pair: slice hyperholomorphic functions supply the functional calculus, and the associated notion of spectrum — the **S-spectrum**, defined through $q^2 - 2\,{\rm Re}(s)\,q + |s|^2$, i.e. through the minimal polynomial of the sphere through $s$ — is *natively* axially symmetric: it is a union of points on $\mathbb{R}$ and 2-spheres, exactly your dichotomy. For quaternionic **self-adjoint** operators the S-spectrum is real. So the sphere/point distinction the program needs is not just geometric; it is the shape of a spectrum, and "no spheres" is the shape of a *self-adjoint* spectrum. (See Direction B.)

## 5. A structural no-go, and where the hardness must enter

**The no-go behind §4 of Quat_Riemann.** The maps $R$ and $Q_\ell$ are $\mathbb{R}$-linear and injective (on the relevant classes) in the complex input $f$. Hence *any* continuous linear functional of the extension — any pairing against a fixed test function over any contour, in any $\ell$-sector — is a continuous linear functional of $f$ itself, and by density/Laurent arguments (exactly your §4 argument, which generalizes from $\ell = 0$ to all $\ell$ via your Theorem 7.1 reduction to 2D integrals) it is expressible through classical complex-analytic data of $f$. **Conclusion: no rotationally symmetric *linear* pairing can ever distinguish on-axis from off-axis zeros.** This should be stated and proved as a lemma — it dignifies the 2015 negative result as a theorem, explains it, and redirects the search: the discriminant must be **nonlinear** (products — now available in $\mathcal{N}$), **geometric** (degrees, intersection numbers, singularity types of the lift/OCS), **spectral** (self-adjointness, S-spectrum), or a **positivity** (cones, norms), because those are precisely the structures that do not factor through linear functionals of $f$.

**Two hardness checks any candidate mechanism must pass.**
1. **The Davenport–Heilbronn test.** The Davenport–Heilbronn function satisfies a functional equation and reality (so its rotated version has an intrinsic slice extension with the same symmetry as $\Xi$) but **has zeros off the critical line** — and no Euler product. Its quaternionic extension therefore *has* genuine zero spheres. Any proposed theorem "symmetry + growth $\Rightarrow$ no spheres" is false; the mechanism must consume the arithmetic input — positivity of $\Phi$ is not enough by itself either (one can cook even, real, order-1 functions with nonreal zeros), so realistically the Euler product / explicit formula must enter. A criterion that cannot "see" the difference between $\zeta$ and Davenport–Heilbronn is dead on arrival; this is the cheapest falsification test and should be run (numerically if needed) on every candidate.
2. **The small-sphere test.** By De Bruijn–Newman/Rodgers–Tao ($\Lambda \ge 0$, so RH $\iff \Lambda = 0$), the heat-flow deformations $\Xi_t$ for $t<0$ have nonreal zeros — in this language, **zero spheres of arbitrarily small radius**, accumulating along the real axis at height $\to\infty$. RH, if true, is "barely true." So a soft removability/rigidity argument that is stable under small perturbations of the function would prove too much and must fail. The winning mechanism has to be quantitatively sensitive to spheres of radius $\epsilon$ — another argument that the arithmetic must enter with quantitative teeth.

These are not reasons for pessimism so much as a specification: they tell you exactly what shape a viable discriminant must have.

## 6. Three directions worth real effort

**Direction A — Twistor geometry of the lift (the most "you" direction).**
Compute the twistor lift/transform of $\Xi(q)$ — concretely, of the kernels $\cos(qu)$ and of partial products/sums — and study:
- *Local model at zeros:* the local geometry of the lifted curve (and of the induced OCS $J_\Xi$ on $\mathbb{H}\setminus Z(\Xi)$) near a **real point zero** versus near a **zero sphere**. For polynomials this should be completely computable with Altavilla–Sarfatti's ruled-surface technology (start with $q^2+1$ vs $q^2-1$: one sphere vs two points).
- *Global invariant:* is there a degree / self-intersection / Chern-class-type invariant of the lift on $S^4$ (compactifying; $\Xi$ has order 1, so growth control at the line at infinity is the analytic crux) that **counts spheres with a different weight than points**? This is the geometric version of "find $c\neq 2$": the classical argument principle weights a conjugate pair as 2 and a real pair as 2, indistinguishably; an intersection-theoretic count on twistor space could weight a *line in the divisor* differently from *two point-degenerations*. The Salamon–Viaclovsky classification and removable-singularity theorems for OCS on domains of $\mathbb{R}^4$ (Math. Ann. 343 (2009); arXiv:0704.3422) are the model results: OCS with small singular sets are severely constrained. The dream statement: *"an OCS of the type induced by an entire intrinsic function of order 1 with positive cosine-transform density cannot degenerate on a twistor line off the real family"* — with the DH and small-sphere tests forcing where $\Phi>0$/Euler product must be used.

**Direction B — Spectral realization via the S-spectrum (quaternionic Hilbert–Pólya).**
Realize $Z(\Xi(q))$ as the S-spectrum of a natural quaternionic operator built from the explicit formula / from $\Phi$, and aim for: *operator naturally quaternionic-normal; RH $\iff$ it is self-adjoint (real S-spectrum)*. Two concrete bridges: (i) de Branges' canonical systems are $2\times2$ real first-order systems $Jy' = zHy$, $H\ge0$ — quaternions encode these naturally, and the rotation around the real axis becomes a gauge action; a quaternionic rewriting of the de Branges/Hermite–Biehler theory of $\Xi$ may expose structure invisible in the complex form. (ii) An internal omen: your $E_\ell$ kernels are spherical Bessel functions, whose zeros are all real *because* they are eigenfunctions of self-adjoint Sturm–Liouville problems — the $B_\pm$ ladder is already a spectral-theory object. The question is whether the ladder can be attached to $\Xi$ itself rather than to the kernels.

**Direction C — Quadratic pairings and an SU(2)-graded positivity.**
Linear pairings are dead (§5), but **quadratic** ones are not: sector energies $\mathcal{E}_\ell = \langle Q_\ell(\xi'/\xi),\, Q_\ell(\xi'/\xi)\rangle_w$ against suitable weights are *quadratic* in the zeros and do not factor through linear functionals. Heuristic to make precise: a real zero contributes to the extension data through $j_\ell(\lambda\cdot 0)$-type factors that kill $\ell\ge1$ sectors, while a sphere of radius $y$ leaks $j_\ell(\lambda y)$-terms into **all** $\ell$; so "energy in the $\ell\ge1$ sectors" is a candidate sphere-detector. The program: compute $\mathcal{E}_\ell$ via the explicit formula (cross terms = pair correlation flavor), see whether positivity of some combination $\iff$ absence of spheres, i.e. hunt for a **Weil-positivity with a new cone of test functions supplied by the SU(2) decomposition**. First falsification: compute $\mathcal{E}_\ell$ numerically for truncations of $\zeta$ vs Davenport–Heilbronn.

3. **Do the local computation** (Direction A): twistor lift of $q^2+1$ vs $q^2-1$ near their zero sets; then $\cos(qu)$; identify the invariant that differs.
4. **Build the numerics lab**: implement $\Xi(q)$ on slices (via $\Phi$-integral, verified against known zeros), the Davenport–Heilbronn extension, visualization of zero spheres, and a harness for testing candidate discriminating functionals ($\mathcal{E}_\ell$ first). 5. **Write the clean equivalence** ("RH $\iff$ no twistor line in the zero divisor of the lifted $\Xi$ off the real family") as a short, careful note — a well-posed novel equivalence is a legitimate waypoint and a better calling card than a symmetry paper, independent of whether the full mechanism materializes.

## 8. Known landscape (so nothing blindsides us)

- A 2025 MDPI *Symmetry* paper (J. Tang, ["From Complex to Quaternions: Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis..."](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/17/7/1134)) claims a quaternionic proof of RH via λ-regularization and convexity. The quaternionic function theory in it is schematic (no slice/Fueter framework), the key convexity step is asserted rather than proved, and it has no standing in the number-theory community. It is *not* the same program, but it occupies adjacent keyword space — a reason to be crisp about rigor and about the zero-sphere formulation, which that paper lacks entirely.
- Mainstream RH ecosystem this program should interface with, not ignore: Laguerre–Pólya/Jensen (realness classes), Weil positivity/explicit formula (where Direction C lands), de Branges/canonical systems (Direction B), De Bruijn–Newman $\Lambda\ge0$ (Rodgers–Tao) as the "barely true" constraint (§5).
- The slice regular ecosystem: Gentili–Struppa (definition), Gentili–Stoppato (zeros), Colombo–Sabadini–Struppa (*Entire Slice Regular Functions*, S-functional calculus, S-spectrum), Ghiloni–Perotti (stem functions — the cleanest formalization of "holomorphicity on the complexified space induces the extension," which is your $I_u$-appending intuition), Alpay–Colombo–Kimsey (spectral theorem for quaternionic normal operators).

## 9. Bottom line

The 2015–2026 arc makes sense and was not wasted: the rigidity theorem (twistor conformal $\Rightarrow$ Möbius) correctly killed the naive version; the Tconf2 pivot (raise the fiber degree) was the right escape and is the same escape the slice-regular twistor literature uses; and the §4 negative result was the correct discovery that linear tools cannot work — it just needed to be promoted from disappointment to theorem. The function class you were searching for exists, is developed, and is friendly: **intrinsic slice regular functions**, with your own map $R$ as the extension, pointwise algebra restricting to complex algebra, sphere/point zero dichotomy giving *RH $\iff$ no zero spheres*, twistor lifts realizing the "maps acting on the complex structure," and the S-spectrum giving the dichotomy a spectral meaning. What remains — everything, to be honest — is the discriminant, and §§5–6 constrain its shape: nonlinear or geometric or spectral, quantitatively sensitive to small spheres, and consuming the Euler product. That is a real research program with falsifiable first steps, not a scattered bundle of ideas.
